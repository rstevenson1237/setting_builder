# Genre

The world is mostly unpeopled. Settlement is small, lit and far apart, and the
ground between the lights belongs to whatever holds it. An empire covered all of
this once and does not now: its roads, walls, cut stone and shafts are the most
common built thing anywhere, and none of it is maintained. It fell because of
what it dug into. Under its works is something older that was there first, that
was never built, and that is still occupied.

## Register

Plain nouns and stated measurements. A thing is described by what it does and
what it is made of, and never by what it means. The prose withholds conclusions
rather than detail: it states what is there in full, and leaves what that
implies to the table. Distances are stated in feet, yards and miles, and time in
turns, watches and days, with each region counting in the units its type calls
for.

Player register, which shows and never concludes:

> The stair turns four times and ends at dressed stone. The steps are worn
> deepest along the outside edge, where something wide was carried down.

Referee register, which is plain, bounded and spatially precise:

> Forty feet of stair in four turns, wide enough for two abreast. The wall at
> the bottom is a facing rather than a structure, one course thick, and the
> second block from the left sits a finger proud of the rest.

Entry register, which is a fragment read aloud from a table at speed:

> Stair worn at the outer edge. Something wide went down it.

## Play

A session is a trip out and a trip back. A party leaves a lit place, reaches
somewhere the empire built or the older thing occupies, reads it, takes
something out of it, and returns while the return still costs less than the
thing is worth. What is taken out is not always an object: a route, a name, a
working device and a way in are all extractions.

The setting rewards reading a place carefully, asking the specific question,
talking to whoever still lives out there, and turning back on time. It punishes
assuming a place is what it resembles, and staying past the point where the way
home was still affordable.

## Constants

- Settlement is small, lit, and far enough apart that the distance between two
  of them is a journey rather than a walk.
- Anything squared, dressed, levelled or spanned was made by the empire, and
  nothing has been maintained since it fell.
- Under the empire's work is something older that was there before it, and it
  is still there.
- Depth is a direction rather than a distance. The further down a place goes,
  the fewer of the surface's rules it keeps, and light is the intrusion rather
  than the norm.
- Magic is a found technology that nobody now understands. It half-works, works
  differently than it is recorded to work, or works on the wrong thing.
- Nobody is coming. What is done in a place is done by the party, and what is
  left undone stays undone.

## Absences

**No living authority.** Nothing rules any of this, there is nobody to appeal
to, and no power will arrive because a party is in trouble. In its place stand
orders nobody alive can rescind and people still keeping them, and local
agreement that reaches exactly as far as the light does.

**No magic for sale.** Nothing enchants on request and no one stocks a shelf of
it. In its place stand found working things, each already used by somebody else,
each carrying instructions that are wrong in one particular, and none of them
replaceable.

## Naming

Names come in two layers, and which layer a name belongs to tells a referee who
named the place and when.

The living layer is plain compounds of ordinary words, naming what a thing is or
what it does. It is what people out here call somewhere now, and it decomposes
on sight.

The empire's layer is built from the recorded root vocabulary and decomposes
into it. It is older, it is often wrong about what the place is now, and where a
place carries a name in both layers the two rarely agree. That disagreement is
content and is worth writing.

Every proper name in the setting decomposes into roots recorded in `T-LNG`,
which is the registry for both layers. A new root is proposed in a step's
opening question batch and never coined in passing.

## Influences

**B/X, Moldvay and Cook.** Taken: the procedural discipline of the wilderness
and dungeon loops, where the sequence of play is what generates the session; and
points of light as a standing condition of the map rather than a theme the prose
announces.

No second work is named, and no prose model is named at all, because the
register above is the prose model. Nothing generated here reproduces a named
work's places, characters or scenes.
