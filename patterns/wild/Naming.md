# Wild - Naming

## Decides
What a WILD location is called, in whose mouth, and what gets written back to
`setting/Language.md`.

## Read at
Step 4c, for every WILD location, after Dressing. Roots and affixes are in
`setting/Language.md`.

## Spec

```
NAMING
  1     A name, built from roots in setting/Language.md
  1     Whose name it is - who calls it this, and why they had reason to name it
  1     The roots recorded back into setting/Language.md under "Coined here"
  20%   A second name, in another tongue, for the same place
```

**The write-back is not optional.** `setting/Language.md` only stays useful if every
coinage goes back into it. A name that cannot be decomposed into roots already present is
either wrong, or a loan word, and a loan word is marked as one.

## Patterns

**Who names wild country.** Not the people who live in it - nobody lives here. WILD names
are given by people who **pass through and need to talk about it afterwards**, which makes
them practical, descriptive, and often unflattering:

- **Marchspeech**, from the settlements that border the region. Names by what a place is
  for, what it costs, or what happened to somebody there. These are the names a party
  arrives with, and they are usually accurate about danger and wrong about cause.
- **Old Rede**, on stone and nowhere else. A marker, a boundary, a milestone. When a
  natural feature carries an older name, it means somebody once thought it worth claiming
  - which is information.
- **The occupants' tongue**, where something living here has language. Blunt and
  territorial: the taking-place, the deep den, the wet.

**Name shapes** - what it looks like; what it is good for; what it costs to cross; who died
there; what was found there; a distance or direction relative to somewhere that matters; a
warning; a name that is plainly somebody's joke.

**Restraint.** A region holds about as many locations as its die, and most of them earn a
proper name because most of them will be referred to again. But a Hidden or Secret child
often does better with a descriptive label than a coinage - it is *the hollow behind the
fall*, and naming it separately from the fall weakens both.
