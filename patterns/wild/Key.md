# Wild - Key

## Decides
When a WILD location holds an object whose real function is opening something elsewhere.

## Read at
Step 4c, when a spec line draws a key. Criteria are in `patterns/setting/Keys.md`.

## Spec

```
KEY
  1     The object, and what it physically is
  1     What it opens, named by location code and feature
  1     How it came to be out here rather than with its lock
  30%   A clue connecting object to lock
  20%   Somebody else knows it is here
```

**WILD is where a key gets separated from its lock.** That is the tier's natural role in
the hook layer: somebody carried it out and did not arrive, or hid it deliberately at a
distance, or it was buried with the person who held it. The distance between key and lock
is the reason a point crawl is a network - and a key found in open country points a party
at a dungeon they may not have heard of yet.

## Patterns

**How it got out here** - carried by somebody who died on the way; hidden at a distance on
purpose, because keeping it with the lock defeated the point; buried as a grave good;
taken as loot and dropped; given to a person out here for safekeeping, who still has it;
built into something out here as though it were a component; lost, and it is a matter of
looking in the right place.

**Where it sits** - in a barrow, with its holder; in a lair, because something took it;
under water; set into a marker as decoration; held by a hermit or trapper who does not
know what it is; in a cache with unrelated things; in plain sight and unrecognisable as a
key at all.

**Connecting clue** - a maker's mark shared with a door two regions away; a material found
in only one place; a name in the older tongue, per `setting/Language.md`; a measurement; a
matching wear pattern; an inscription that names the lock and not the key.
