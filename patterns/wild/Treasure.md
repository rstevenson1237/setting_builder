# Wild - Treasure

## Provides
What a WILD location holds to be taken - which registry or table it comes from, why it is
still out here, and what reaching it costs. Tables are `setting/Treasure1.md`-`5.md`. The
demand end is the classifier's own registry lines.

## Spec

```
TREASURE
  1     What it is - exactly one, drawn at these rates rather than freely chosen
        {table roll 45% | key 30% | lore 20% | unique treasure 5%}
                        (patterns/setting/Treasure.md,
                         wild/Key.md, wild/Lore.md,
                         patterns/setting/UniqueTreasures.md)
  1     Which table, where the draw was a table roll  (genre: treasure-tables-outdoors)
  1     Why it is still here after weathering        (genre: treasure-survival)
  1     What reaching it costs - climb, dig, wade, carry, or wait
  30%   Something that has a claim on it             (genre: treasure-claims)
```

**The key rate is highest here, and that is the point.** Open country is where a key gets
separated from its lock, which is the one thing a WILD region can do that neither of the
others can, and an unrated menu would never produce it.

**Never name or describe the contents of a table roll.** The cited roll decides them. This
does not apply to a unique treasure, a piece of lore, or a key: each is a stub row needing a
name.

**Weather is the difference.** A pristine find in open country needs a reason.

## Constraints

- **Lore and a Key are things a WILD location holds, not hooks it hands out.** Both are
  objects found in the open and carried away, so both are drawn here rather than as registry
  lines of their own. What stays in registry is the quest a place carries.

- **Carrying is a real cost out here.** Four hours per action, distances in miles, and a
  party already loaded. Bulk that would be trivial in a dungeon is a decision in the field.
