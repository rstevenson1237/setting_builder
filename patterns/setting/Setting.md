# Setting - Setting

## Decides
The setting's name, its three tags, and the referee-facing outline every other artifact
builds on.

## Read at
Step 2a, right after `GENRE.md`, before anything else.

## Spec

```
SETTING
  1     A name, coined from setting/Language.md, and the roots recorded back
  3     Tags - and they must constrain, not decorate
  1     What the party is doing here, in a clause
  1     A pressure - something already going wrong, which is not aimed at the party
  1     Something the setting has that a generic instance of the genre does not
```

**Tags constrain or they are wasted.** *dark, mysterious, dangerous* rules nothing out and
so directs nothing. *timber, debt, boundary* tells every later artifact what to reach for
and what to leave alone. Three words that could describe half the settings in the genre are
three words doing no work.

**Name the pressure, not the plot.** Something is already going wrong here and would go on
going wrong if the party never arrived. Per `GENRE.md` the party are treasure hunters
barely above commoners, not the resolution of anything - the pressure is weather, not a
story.

## Patterns

**What a setting is built on** - a resource somebody is extracting; a boundary between two
kinds of country or two claims; a road, river, or crossing and who controls it; a ruin
being worked; an arrival, recent enough that nobody has settled into it; a withdrawal -
whoever kept order has gone; a debt, held over the whole place.

**Why the party is here** - work, of the kind nobody respectable takes; something owed;
somewhere else was worse; a rumour, from `setting/Rumours.md`; hired, and the hirer is in
`setting/Factions.md`; born here and back.

**What makes it not generic** - one thing that is true here and nowhere else, stated in a
clause. It should be the sort of thing that changes how a party *acts*, not how the place
looks: a custom with teeth, a substance, a rule the land keeps, a thing everyone does that
nobody explains. `setting/Truths.md` develops it; the outline names it.

**Outline discipline.** Two or three sentences for the referee, not for players. What this
place is, how it is run, and what it is for within the module. If a sentence does not help
a referee decide something at the table, it is description and belongs elsewhere.
