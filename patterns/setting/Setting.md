# Setting - Setting

## Decides
The setting's name, its three tags, and the referee-facing outline every other artifact
builds on.

## Read at
Step 2a, right after `GENRE.md`, before anything else.

## Spec

```
SETTING
  1     A name, coined from setting/Language.md, and the roots recorded back
  3     Tags - and they must constrain, not decorate
  1     What the party is doing here, in a clause
  1     A pressure - something already going wrong, which is not aimed at the party
  1     Something the setting has that a generic instance of the genre does not
```

**Tags constrain or they are wasted.** *dark, mysterious, dangerous* rules nothing out and
so directs nothing. *quarry-dust, blood-feud, the shut pass* tells every later artifact what
to reach for and what to leave alone; *garrison, incursion, the old sally-port* does the
same from a different domain entirely. Three words that could describe half the settings in
the genre are three words doing no work - and three words lifted from this file's own
examples are exactly as generic, however specific they sound in isolation. Pick tags from
what *this* setting is built on below, not from the illustrations here.

**Name the pressure, not the plot.** Something is already going wrong here and would go on
going wrong if the party never arrived. Per `GENRE.md` the party are treasure hunters
barely above commoners, not the resolution of anything - the pressure is weather, not a
story.

## Patterns

**What a setting is built on** - a resource somebody is extracting; a boundary between two
kinds of country or two claims; a road, river, or crossing and who controls it; a ruin being
worked, or newly broken open; a keep, garrison, or stockade holding a border alone; a route
a monster or a raiding band has cut, recently enough that nobody has found another way
around it; an arrival, recent enough that nobody has settled into it; a withdrawal -
whoever kept order has gone; an obligation standing over the whole place - a debt, a tithe,
a blood-price, a service owed. **Don't default to the last one.** An economic instrument
(a bond, a ledger, a tally) is one flavor among the many above, not the setting's engine -
the genre's classic answer to "how does this run with no overarching authority" is more
often a fortified point of civilization, a dungeon's reputation, or a route something has
cut, not a financial system layered under everything.

**Why the party is here** - work, of the kind nobody respectable takes; something owed;
somewhere else was worse; a rumour, from `setting/Rumours.md`; hired, and the hirer is in
`setting/Factions.md`; born here and back; a ruin's reputation, and the treasure everyone
says is still in it.

**What makes it not generic** - one thing that is true here and nowhere else, stated in a
clause. It should be the sort of thing that changes how a party *acts*, not how the place
looks: a custom with teeth, a substance, a rule the land keeps, a thing everyone does that
nobody explains. `setting/Truths.md` develops it; the outline names it.

**Outline discipline.** Two or three sentences for the referee, not for players. What this
place is, how it is run, and what it is for within the module. If a sentence does not help
a referee decide something at the table, it is description and belongs elsewhere.

## Constraints

- **Salt/debt/tide convergence, three independent full builds.** Every one landed on a
  coastal or tidal setting built on a debt-ledger economy with a salt-preservation Truth at
  its center. Traced to three places doing more work than intended: this file's own tag
  example (formerly *timber, debt, boundary* - "debt" handed over verbatim), its own
  "built on" list naming *a debt, held over the whole place* as a standalone bullet, and
  `templates/Setting.md`'s worked example for the mood-vs-handle test (formerly a salt/
  tide/bodies image) - the single most concrete, fully-realized image in either file, read
  at exactly the moment tags and outline get drafted. A fourth, larger source was
  `checks/SettingJudgementCheck.md` still holding a complete write-up of an earlier setting
  built on the same motif (Brackvaen) - not itself Context for this step, but the richest
  exemplar in the repo of "what a finished setting looks like" for anything that goes
  looking. Fixed by varying both examples above, folding debt into a broader obligation
  category, adding explicitly non-economic "built on" options, and clearing the stale
  check. If a fourth generation converges again, the fix wasn't the examples - look for
  where else in `GENRE.md`, `STEPS.md`, or another pattern file a single vivid image is
  standing in for a whole category of options.
