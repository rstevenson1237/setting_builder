# Setting - Outline

## Decides
The shape of the setting before any of its content exists: how many regions, of what
ratings, at what dice, and what altitude the party plays at.

## Read at
Step 2a, immediately after `GENRE.md` and before `setting/Setting.md`. Everything
downstream depends on it - most sharply `setting/Bestiary.md`, which cannot scale its
creatures without knowing the region dice.

## Spec

```
OUTLINE
  1     Region count, and the code range they occupy
  1     Rating mix - how many SAFE, WILD, DANGEROUS
  1     A die per region, d4 to d12
  1     Party altitude - what the characters are, and what they can expect to survive
  1     The shortest complete loop: one SAFE, one WILD, one DANGEROUS
  30%   A second SAFE region, where the setting is about somewhere being contested
```

**A region's die is a difficulty die, not a power level** - see `setting/Procedures.md`.
d8 is baseline and means typical for a region of this rating; d6 is slightly tougher, d10
slightly easier, and d4 and d12 are true outliers used deliberately and rarely. The scale
runs opposite to intuition because a smaller die fails more often.

The die also sets location count, and that coupling is worth using: a punishing region is
small and concentrated, a mild one large and sprawling.

**The shortest complete loop is one of each rating.** A setting needs somewhere to
resupply, somewhere to cross, and somewhere to go - and the smallest version of that is
three regions. More is a choice; fewer is missing a leg.

## Patterns

**Rating mixes** - one of each, the default and the shape of a starting setting; two WILD
between one SAFE and one DANGEROUS, where getting there is the point; one SAFE and two
DANGEROUS, where the setting is about a choice between two ways down; two SAFE with one
WILD between them, where the setting is about a road and who controls it.

**Die by rating** - start every region at **d8** and move off it only for a reason. A d6
region is one you want to bite harder than its rating suggests: a settlement under
pressure, country that punishes carelessness, a dungeon that is awake. A d10 region is one
you want to breathe: a settled valley, an approach, a shallow ruin. d4 and d12 are
outliers - at most one of each in a setting, and each needs a stated reason.

The rating already carries most of the danger. The die is a modifier on top of it, not a
second rating.

**Party altitude** - treasure hunters barely above commoners, per GENRE.md. State it in
dice: how many, of what size, for a starting character and for one who has survived a
while. Players run 1 to 6 dice of d4 to d12, d6 average, so "barely above commoners" is a
low count of ordinary dice.

This line is what creature AD is pitched against - **not** the region die. Per
`setting/Procedures.md` the two are separate axes, and treating a d8 region as calling for
an 8 AD creature is a category error.
