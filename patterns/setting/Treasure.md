# Setting - Treasure

## Provides
What the five d20 tables hold, how value is distributed within each, and what money means to
a party at this setting's altitude. Reads `GENRE.md` for party altitude, since value is only
meaningful relative to what the characters have.

## Spec

```
TABLE SHAPE - each of the five
  1     Result 1 is significantly below the table's average value
  2-3   Each either slightly below average, or about double the average weight for its value
  4-20  The remaining spread, in randomised order - never ascending or descending
  1     No result describes how it is stored, found, or carried
  1     A week's ordinary wage stated at the head of Table I, and pointed back to from
        each of the others, so every number has a scale
```

```
TABLE II - quality
  10%   poor
  50%   normal
  30%   fine          - 30% of these carry an effect
  3%    masterwork    - always carries a positive effect
  5%    cursed        - always carries a negative effect, and presents as fine or masterwork
```

```
WEIGHT - bulk and weight together: what it takes to move the thing
  0 wt   Pocketed and forgotten about - no call on anyone's hands
  1 wt   One hand, or about 100 coins
  2 wt   Both hands
  3+ wt  More than one person to carry, and more still the higher it goes
  1      About 100 cn per wt is the average relationship. A find escapes it by rising an
         order of magnitude - a gem at 1 wt and 1000 cn - never by being written light.
         Two orders is table-topping; past that is patterns/setting/UniqueTreasures.md's
```

```
THE FIVE TABLES
  I     Scavenged Loot - everyday debris, minor coin, tools, scraps; average under 500 cn,
        and mostly useful rather than valuable
  II    Equipment and Armaments - weapons, armour, gear, with Quality and Effect columns.
        Normal is 100 cn; fine 3-10x, masterwork 10-100x. A cursed item is described as
        what it presents as - the curse is the trap, not a label
  III   Gems and Jewelry - value-dense and weightless, the band where carrying capacity
        stops constraining. Some pieces carry a provenance a party could trace
  IV    Luxury and Trade Goods - bulk with a function: bolts, casks, ingots, hides, spices,
        salt. The band where weight is the whole problem
  V     Treasure Cache - the payoff, and the only table where one result should change a
        party's plans. Usually more than a party can carry out in one trip, and the wt
        column says so
  1     Materials and coinage drawn from setting/History.md and setting/Truths.md - what
        was minted here, by whom, and whether it is still accepted
```

**Result 1 exists so the roll can disappoint**, and the randomised order so it cannot be
read for the best outcome. Both are anti-optimisation, and both matter more than the
contents.

**Encumbrance is the lever value pushes off of.** A chest of coin worth 4000 cn is roughly
40 wt and several trips; writing it at 6 wt to keep the number tidy deletes the only
interesting thing about finding it.

## Constraints

- **Never decide the container.** These tables are reusable everywhere and tied to no place.
  What holds a find, what conceals it, and what it takes to reach it are the location's job.
