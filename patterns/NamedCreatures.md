# Named Creature Patterns

Guidance for what distinguishes an entry in `setting/NamedCreatures.md` from an ordinary `setting/Bestiary.md` template, stubbed during location generation and fully written afterward (see `templates/NamedCreatures.md`).

- A Named Creature is a specific individual, not a reusable template - it has a name, and its own motivation drives what it does, independent of any one location it's encountered in.
- Stat it the same way `setting/Bestiary.md` does (AD, +/-N, MA), but record it here instead when it's meant to recur - across more than one location, or as a figure a party might hear about (in a Rumour, a piece of Lore) before or after actually meeting it.
- Its Motivation is a standing goal it acts on, not a scripted arc pointed at the party (see `GENRE.md`) - something it's pursuing regardless of whether the party ever shows up.
- A creature earns a slot here when the setting benefits from it staying consistent across appearances - what it wants, how it reacts, what it remembers - rather than being reinvented per location. An ordinary Bestiary creature encountered once doesn't need this.

## Where It Fits
- DANGEROUS, high weight: the natural pairing - a high-weight room's central creature or boss-level threat is usually worth naming and giving a motivation, rather than staying a generic Bestiary encounter.
- DANGEROUS, low or medium weight: uncommon - most creatures there are fine as plain Bestiary encounters; name one only if it's deliberately meant to recur elsewhere.
- SAFE or WILD: valid anywhere, but at a relatively low chance of occurring - a notable NPC or beast can be a Named Creature, but most don't need to be.
