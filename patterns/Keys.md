# Key Patterns

Guidance for what belongs in `setting/Keys.md` - physical, portable objects whose function is unlocking or triggering something at another location, stubbed during location generation and fully written afterward (see `templates/Keys.md`).

- A Key is a physical, portable object - an actual key, a rod, a fitted gemstone, a seal, a token, a specific creature's bone, or similar - whose real function is unlocking or triggering something elsewhere.
- Every Key names exactly what it unlocks and where: a specific Feature at a specific location. A Key with nothing it opens isn't a Key - it's Treasure, and belongs on a `setting/Treasure[I-V].md` roll or in `setting/UniqueTreasures.md` instead.
- A Key doesn't need to look like what it opens; the connection between a Key and its lock can itself be a discoverable Secret (`patterns/Secrets.md`) when it isn't obvious.
- Keys are meant to connect locations, often across regions - use them to reward exploration and give a party a concrete reason to return somewhere or press toward somewhere new. Don't use a Key to gate a single room's own contents - that's a Puzzle's or a Trap's job.

## Where It Fits
- DANGEROUS, low or medium weight: a solid substitute for a Treasure citation when a location's find would read better as something that unlocks somewhere else than something merely valuable.
- DANGEROUS, high weight: rather than replacing that location's main payload, add a Key alongside it - a high-weight room can afford both its Treasure/Unique Treasure and a Key pointing elsewhere.
- SAFE or WILD: valid anywhere, but at a relatively low chance of occurring - most landmarks don't need a Key, so don't force one into every entry.
