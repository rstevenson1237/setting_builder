# Treasure Patterns

Guidance for which `setting/Treasure[N].md` table(s) fit a location, picked at generation time in `templates/Location.md` whenever the chosen location pattern calls for treasure (e.g. "hidden treasure," "a creature guardian of some treasure," "the region's primary treasure"). This file guides *which table*, not how the pull is cited - that format lives in `templates/Location.md`.

A location may cite more than one table when the fiction calls for it - a guarded hoard might cite the guardian's gear from Table II separately from the hoard itself. Don't force a citation where the pattern doesn't call for one; plenty of locations should have none.

- **Table I - Scavenged Loot** - background dressing almost anywhere: SAFE/WILD landmarks, low-weight connective rooms, debris that "rewards careful looking without demanding action."
- **Table II - Equipment and Armaments** - tied to a person or creature: a fallen traveler, a guardian, a bandit's kit. Fits WILD landmarks and medium-weight reactive rooms built around a creature.
- **Table III - Gems and Jewelry** - a found or hidden cache with no guardian attached: fits medium-weight "hidden treasure" rooms and WILD landmarks hiding a resource.
- **Table IV - Luxury and Trade Goods** - bulk goods with a function: a SAFE landmark's stock in trade, a faction's stores, a warehouse or cargo hold.
- **Table V - Treasure Cache** - the payoff of a location: high-weight set-pieces, a region's primary treasure or objective, the room the rest of the dungeon points toward.
