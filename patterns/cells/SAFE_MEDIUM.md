# SAFE_MEDIUM

> The body of a settlement.

The ground a party actually walks: a street, a yard, a landing, a quarter with
more than one use. A `SAFE_MEDIUM` location is where the settlement's ordinary
friction lives — what costs what, who is where at which hour, and which way a
question travels once it is asked.

## What belongs here

- **Two to four services, each priced in a different currency.** Coin, standing,
  obligation and risk are four separate prices, and a location that charges coin
  for everything is a shop list rather than a settlement. Price one thing in a
  favour owed and the location starts working.

- **At least one refusal, and it is structural.** Not one keeper's mood: a rule
  the place keeps. Nothing is carried up past the second landing. Nobody works
  the west side after the light goes. The rule is stated, the reason is not
  volunteered, and a party that asks gets a shorter answer rather than a longer
  one.

- **A schedule the location runs on.** `SAFE` counts time in days, and a
  settlement's day has shape. Name what happens in the morning, what happens
  when the light goes, and what a party finds if they arrive between the two.
  The same location at two hours is two usable locations, and that is the
  cheapest content in the cell.

- **People as a standing arrangement, not a cast list.** Who works here, who
  passes through, and what each of them is owed or owes. Two or three named
  people is plenty. The rest are a trade and a direction.

- **Where a question goes.** A party asking about something out in the region
  asks it here. State who answers, what it costs to be answered, and which way
  the asking travels afterward — a settlement that does not gossip is a
  settlement that has been written as a corridor.

- **Distances and materials, stated.** Feet indoors, yards along the street,
  miles to the next lit place. What the buildings are made of, and which of it
  the empire cut and nobody has maintained since.

- **One hidden thing per two features, roughly.** Named in the Overview, opened
  by asking or by spending time. The settlement is not a dungeon and does not
  reward a fine-toothed search, but it rewards a specific question.

## Where the boundary falls

- **Down to `SAFE_LOW`** when everything here is one transaction behind one
  door. Split it: the premises goes down, and the street it stands on stays
  here.

- **Up to `SAFE_HIGH`** when the location holds someone or something a party
  will plan a return around, or a service that exists nowhere else in the
  region. Convenience is `MEDIUM`. A reason to come back is `HIGH`.

- **Not `SAFE` at all** if the honest answer to "what happens if the party
  refuses to pay" is violence rather than a closed door. That is a `DANGEROUS`
  location that has been mislabelled, and the region type is wrong before the
  cell is.

## Form

- **Player Overview:** six to ten sentences, walked rather than surveyed. Bold
  three or four nouns and give every one of them a feature below.
- **Features:** three to five. Each names its service or its rule in its opening
  line, so a referee reading only the first line of each can still run the
  place.
- **Referee Overview:** the schedule first, then the prices, then the refusal,
  then who answers a question and what it costs. State the extent in yards and
  the walk to the next lit place in miles.
- **Exits:** two or more, and at least one leaving the settlement. Every cue is
  sensory and stated from the inside looking out.
- **No combat statistics.** The cost of pushing is standing, obligation or risk,
  and it is written as a price rather than as a threat.
- **Tables it leans on:** `T-PRC` for what a service actually yields, `T-RUM`
  for what travels and how far, `T-NAM` for people and premises, `T-ARC` for the
  empire stone the settlement is built out of, `T-LOR` where something legible
  is set into a wall.

## Worked example

An illustration of the shape and the register. Nothing in it is canon, and the
codes are stand-ins.

**Player Overview.** The street is the top of a wall, forty feet of dressed
coping wide enough for a cart, with the ground on both sides ten feet below it
and wet. **The winch yard** takes up the whole of the inland end, and its frame
is empire iron rethreaded with rope. Below the coping, on the seaward side,
**the under-stalls** are cut into the wall's face and reached by ladders that
are pulled up at dark. **A stone standard** is set into the coping at the
midpoint, a bar cut with four notches, worn bright at the second.

**Referee Overview.** *Extent:* four hundred yards of wall, one street. *Walk:*
two days inland to the next lit place, on a road that holds. *Schedule:* the
winch works from first light until the tide turns, the stalls open when the
winch stops, and the ladders come up at dark. *Rule the street keeps:* nothing
goes up the winch unweighed at the standard, and nothing at all goes up after
dark. Nobody explains it. *Prices:* weighing costs coin; hauling costs coin or a
turn on the winch; being vouched for at the standard costs standing and cannot
be bought. *Questions:* the weigher answers, once, for the price of a turn on
the winch, and repeats what he is asked to whoever asks him next.

## Excluded patterns

- **A cast list.** Names with trades attached and nothing owed between them are
  a table, and the table is `T-NAM`.
- **A stock list or a price sheet.** Four prices in four currencies beats forty
  in coin.
- **A hook.** No location tells a party what to do next. It states what is here,
  what it costs, and what it will not do. What they do with that is the session.
- **Combat statistics or a guard patrol.** A `SAFE` region has no fight in it.
  If it needs one, it is not `SAFE`.
- **A tavern that exists to hold rumours.** Rumours live in `T-RUM` and reach a
  party through whoever has a reason to repeat them. A room whose only content
  is a table draw has no content.
- **A settlement that explains the region.** What is out there is known here as
  a rumour, a price and a refusal, never as a briefing.
