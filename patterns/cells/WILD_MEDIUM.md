# WILD_MEDIUM

> The body of a road.

A `WILD_MEDIUM` location is a named piece of route with something on it that has
to be read before it is walked: a ford, a bend, a span, a bridge, a cut, a gate
in a wall that no longer has a wall. It is where the region does most of its
work, because it is where a party is still moving and is asked to make a
decision that costs them.

## What belongs here

- **A rate that changes here.** State the region's rate, then state what this
  place does to it, in yards and miles a watch. A stretch where the rate does
  not change is `WILD_LOW`.

- **A non-visual approach cue, and its distance.** Sound, smell, temperature,
  the ground underfoot, a change in what the wind carries. State how far out it
  is noticed and what turning back at that distance costs. This is a
  requirement of the type and not a flourish: it is what makes the region
  survivable and what makes pushing on a choice rather than an ambush.

- **The thing that has to be read.** One feature that behaves differently
  depending on when, how or where it is approached. Water that is shin-deep at
  one hour and waist-deep at another. Stone cambered so a slip goes outward. A
  bend that is overlooked from one side only. Write what a party sees, write
  what is actually true, and write what tells them the difference before they
  commit.

- **Both routes, both priced.** Every gate has an answer that is not the gate.
  Here the gate is usually the ground itself, and the answer is a longer walk, a
  wetter one, or one that is not overlooked. Cost each in watches, in what the
  party arrives without, and in what sees them on the way.

- **Terrain stated as material.** What the ground is made of, what it does to a
  boot, and what the empire built here that is still holding. Plain nouns and
  stated measurements. Depth in hands, width in feet, distance in yards.

- **One hidden thing and, sometimes, one secret.** The hidden thing is named in
  the Overview and pays a question. Where there is a secret, it is gated on a
  named physical action a party has a reason to take here — lying down under
  something, going into the water, going back to a thing they passed — and it
  carries a clue, proximate or through the tables.

- **What the stretch does to the region's tables.** Which Encounter rows are
  likelier while a party is committed to the crossing, and what the weather does
  to the reading.

## Where the boundary falls

- **Down to `WILD_LOW`** when the only content is a rate and a cue. Something to
  read is what makes this cell.

- **Up to `WILD_HIGH`** when a party would come out here for this rather than
  passing it on the way to something else. A crossing is `MEDIUM` however
  elaborate it is. A destination is `HIGH`.

- **Sideways into `DANGEROUS`** if the honest reading is that the ground is
  hunted rather than difficult. `WILD` difficulty measures the treacherousness
  of the terrain and the cost of leaving the road. If the cost is a thing that
  wants the party, the region type is wrong.

## Form

- **Player Overview:** five to nine sentences, written from the road, in the
  order a party moving along it meets things. Bold two or three nouns and give
  each a feature.
- **Features:** two to four. The one that has to be read comes first and carries
  its connection pointer where the route leads on.
- **Referee Overview:** approach cue and distance, then rates on and off the
  route, then what is actually true about the thing being read, then both routes
  priced. A referee runs this location without inventing a measurement.
- **Exits:** two or three, and one of them is usually the harder answer. Cues
  are what a party notices from here.
- **Tokens:** a hazard that can be walked into carries its token beside the
  feature, referee-facing, and never in the Player Overview.
- **Tables it leans on:** `T-ARC` for the empire's roadwork and what recurs,
  `T-HAZ` for what the ground does, `T-PRC` for the crossing itself, `T-KEY`
  where a gate here opens elsewhere, `T-NAM` for what the place is called in
  both layers.

## Worked example

An illustration of the shape and the register. Nothing in it is canon, and the
codes are stand-ins.

**Player Overview.** The road runs out into the water and comes up again two
hundred yards on. Between the two, **the sunk span** is a line of slabs a hand
under the surface, wide enough for two abreast, with the open water either side
of it giving nothing back. **A frame** stands at the near end, two uprights and
a crossbeam, and the crossbeam is bare. The slabs are polished smooth along the
middle and rough at the edges.

**Referee Overview.** *Approach cue:* a quarter of a mile out the party stops
hearing its own steps come back off anything. The silence is complete rather
than muffled, and turning back there costs the watch and nothing else. *Rate on
the span:* one mile a watch, half the road rate, and it cannot be hurried
because the smooth middle is the part that is polished. *Rate round the water:*
four miles of reed and peat at half a mile a watch, arriving after dark with the
light spent. *What is true:* the slabs are cambered a finger toward the open
water, so a slip goes outward rather than onto the stone {TEST: Constitution}
{WOUND: Crushing}. The rough edges hold and the smooth middle does not, and a
party that watches where the wear is can read that before stepping on.
*Distributed clue:* the frame is a straight instance of the region's
architecture and the entry for it names the camber (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN,
T-ARC-02). *Table effects:* nothing on the Encounter table can reach a party in
the middle of the span inside a watch, and everything on it can wait at either
end. The road comes up on dressed floor at the far side -> R04-L09.

## Excluded patterns

- **A crossing with one route.** A gate with no answer is a wall, and a wall
  across a road is a region that cannot be played through.
- **A hidden cost.** The price of each branch is stated. What is withheld is the
  conclusion, never the measurement.
- **A hazard that is only found by walking into it.** Every hazard here has a
  tell a party can read from outside it, and the tell is in the Player Overview.
- **A creature keyed to the location.** Living things reach a `WILD` region
  through its Encounter table. What is keyed here is ground, weather, water and
  stone.
- **An approach cue that is a sight.** The type requires a non-visual one.
  Something seen at a distance is a second cue and never the first.
- **The road's whole story.** What the empire built and why is `S-HIS`, reaching
  a party through `T-LOR` and `T-RUM`. What is written here is the stone as it
  is now.
