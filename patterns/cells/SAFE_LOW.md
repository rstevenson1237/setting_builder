# SAFE_LOW

> Thin by nature. A building with one door.

One premises with one use. A `SAFE_LOW` location exists so that a settlement has
edges, and so that a party crossing it passes something rather than nothing. It
is not a small `SAFE_MEDIUM`. It is a different object: one transaction, one
refusal, and then it closes.

## What belongs here

- **One service, priced.** Every location in a `SAFE` region declares a service
  and its cost, in coin, standing, obligation or risk. Here there is exactly
  one, and the price is stated in something a party can hand over today.

- **One refusal, and it carries the location.** Where there is a single service,
  what the door will not do is most of what the place is. A refusal is specific,
  and it is not hostility: not before the boats are in; not for anyone who came
  up from the water side; not that one, it is spoken for. Name what would change
  the answer, and price that too.

- **One keeper, or nobody, stated.** A keeper has one want, one schedule and one
  thing they will not discuss. Where there is nobody, state the nil and its
  reason: the shutters are down, the tally is chalked to the day before last,
  and the bar is on the inside.

- **An interior read in one look.** Feet indoors. There is one room, so one
  description takes the whole of it, and the party is not searching a building.

- **Hours, and the closed version.** A `SAFE` region counts time in days. One
  door means one schedule. A party arriving outside it gets a different and
  equally usable location: a barred door, a light behind a shutter, a thing left
  out on the step.

- **One thing that is not the service.** A single hidden item: a tally, a
  repair, a stone in the wall cut for something else, a name on the lintel that
  the family inside no longer uses. It pays one question and stops.

## Where the boundary falls

- **Up to `SAFE_MEDIUM`** the moment there are two separate things to do here,
  or the outside is worth walking as well as the inside. Two services is a
  `MEDIUM`. One service and one refusal is a `LOW`.

- **Up to `SAFE_HIGH`** when the premises is only interesting because of who
  stands in it, and that person has standing, a want a party can serve, and a
  reason to be come back to. That is a landmark wearing a small building.

- **Not a location at all** when there is no service, no refusal and nothing to
  ask about. That is a clause in the Player Overview of the `MEDIUM` location it
  stands in. A door on a street is scenery until somebody can be turned away at
  it.

## Form

- **Player Overview:** three to six sentences. Two bolded nouns at most, and
  every one of them appears below as a feature.
- **Features:** one or two. A third is the boundary telling you this is a
  `MEDIUM`.
- **Referee Overview:** the service, the price, the refusal, the hours, and the
  keeper's one want. Plain, bounded, and stated in feet and days.
- **Exits:** usually one, back to the street or road the door stands on, with a
  sensory cue on it.
- **No combat statistics.** Nothing in a `SAFE` region carries them. A threat
  here is priced in standing, obligation or risk, and it is written as a cost.
- **Tables it leans on:** `T-NAM` for the keeper and the premises, `T-PRC` for
  what the service costs and yields, `T-RUM` for what the keeper will and will
  not repeat, `T-ARC` where the building is empire stone put to a second use.

## Worked example

An illustration of the shape and the register. Nothing in it is canon, and the
codes are stand-ins.

**Player Overview.** The lamp bracket over the door is empire work, cut for a
fitting twice the size of the one hanging in it. Inside there is one room, one
bench, and **a rack of dried sedge** stacked to the beams. The woman working it
does not stop when the door opens. **A tally board** by the door is chalked in
two columns, and the right-hand column has not been added to in nine days.

**Referee Overview.** *Service:* dried sedge, bundled, enough to burn for four
days, sold by the armful. *Cost:* coin, or a day's cutting, and she prefers the
day. *Refusal:* she will not sell the far rack, which is spoken for by a
household that has not come for it. She will say who it is spoken for once, if
asked plainly, and not a second time. *Hours:* daylight, every day, and the door
is barred by dark. *Want:* somebody to walk out to that household and come back
with an answer.

**Feature: The Tally Board.** Two columns in chalk, cut and paid. The right-hand
column stops nine days back and the left-hand column has not. She is still
cutting for somebody who has stopped collecting. The road she means runs west
out of the settlement -> R07-L02.

## Excluded patterns

- **A second service.** One door, one thing sold or done. The second belongs to
  the `MEDIUM` location this one stands in.
- **A quest.** The keeper has a want. A want is a thing a party may notice and
  act on. A task assigned, tracked and rewarded is a landmark's business.
- **Combat statistics, a guard, or a fight.** Not in a `SAFE` region, and least
  of all in a room with one door.
- **A stock list.** A referee does not need an inventory. They need one price,
  one refusal, and what changes the answer.
- **A keeper who explains the setting.** They know their trade, their street and
  their own grievance. What they know about anything further away is a rumour
  and it goes in `T-RUM`.
- **Padding to match the neighbours.** A thin location beside a full one is the
  contrast working. Thinness is never fixed by adding a feature.
