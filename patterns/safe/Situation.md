# Safe - Situation

## Decides
How the region's standing Situation shows up at this particular location, and what its
next rung looks like from here.

## Read at
Step 4c, for a SAFE location where the region's Situation is visible - which is most of
them, at least in passing. The Situation itself is authored at region level in
`patterns/region/Safe.md`; this file states how it lands here.

## Spec

```
SITUATION - as seen from this location
  1     What is visibly different here because of it
  1     Who here is worse off, by name
  1     Which rung it is on, from the region's Situation field
  40%   Somebody here who benefits, and would rather it continued
  30%   What this location's people are doing about it, which is usually not enough
  20%   A way the party makes it worse by helping
```

**A situation is a condition, not a plot.** It is true whether or not the party engages,
and it moves on its own. Per GENRE.md the party are treasure hunters, not a resolution
mechanism, and the entry should read as something they walked into rather than something
waiting for them.

**State the next rung.** The most useful line in a Situation entry is what happens if
nobody does anything, because that is what makes a party's inaction a choice. A situation
with no trajectory is scenery.

## Patterns

**Kinds of situation** - armed men camped nearby who have not left; refugees arriving from
somewhere the party may be going; a sickness, and whether it is understood; a shortage of
one specific thing; a death that is being called an accident; somebody missing, and how
long; a debt called in on the whole settlement; a festival that suspends normal rules; a
harvest, good or failed; a rival settlement's claim; a faction quietly buying things up; a
child born, died, or taken.

**Escalation rungs.** Write the ladder at region level and pick a rung. The rungs of an
armed-men situation, as an example of the shape:

1. They are paying for what they take, and the locals are selling winter stores.
2. They are drinking too much, and the locals are swallowing insults.
3. A delegation has gone to the captain about something specific.
4. Somebody local has been hurt, and the captain is deciding how much to care.
5. Somebody local is dead, and discipline is visibly slipping.
6. They are taking what they want, and the settlement is deciding whether to fight.

**Who benefits** - the one selling to them; the one whose rival is worse off; the one whose
debt nobody is collecting while this goes on; the one who wanted an excuse; the one for
whom this is cover.

**How helping makes it worse** - the party's involvement escalates a rung; the person they
help is the one who caused it; the fix costs the settlement something it needed; solving it
removes the reason a faction was tolerating this place; the party leaves and the
consequences do not.
