# Safe - Settlement

## Decides
What kind of settled place this is, which locations it can plausibly hold, and - for each
one - how much it matters.

## Read at
Step 4c, before any other `safe/` file, for every SAFE location. Also read at 4a, which
takes its location list from here.

## Spec

```
SETTLEMENT TYPE
  {steading | thorp | village | town | seat}
```

```
PROMINENCE - decided per location, before anything is written
  liner note   - it does what everyone expects a place like this to do, and no more.
                 One or two features. Most locations in most settlements
  working      - it has a person, a want, or a wrinkle worth remembering. Three or four
  central      - the settlement is partly about this place. Five or more, and it is where
                 the region's Situation is most visible
```

**Decide prominence first, and do not derive it from size.** A crossroads shrine may be
central because the setting is about what is buried under it; a large market may be a liner
note because the party is passing through and the market is only a market. **The variance
is the point** - a settlement where every location is equally detailed reads as a
gazetteer, not a place.

Per D17, extra weight in SAFE arrives as **more locations**, not heavier ones. A settlement
that matters gets a fuller list, not a longer entry per item.

## Patterns

**Settlement types, and what each can hold**

- **Steading** - a single household working land, 5-15 people. Holds: hospitality of a
  sort, one trade at most, a household's authority. No market, no law, no temple.
- **Thorp** - 15-50, a cluster with no walls. Holds: a common hall or a taproom, a smith or
  a miller but rarely both, a headman rather than an office. A shrine, not a temple.
- **Village** - 50-300, a defensible core. Holds: an inn, two or three trades, a chapel, a
  headman or a lord's steward, a notice post, a weekly market.
- **Town** - 300-1000, walls or a keep. Holds: several inns of different classes, a full
  spread of trades, a temple, a court or a garrison, a standing market, a moneylender, at
  least one faction with a local interest.
- **Seat** - 1000+, the region's authority sits here. Everything a town holds, plus
  quarters that differ from each other, guilds, a gaol, and outsiders from far enough away
  to be strange.

**The gate is what a type cannot hold.** A thorp with a moneylender and a standing market
has quietly become a village. If the fiction wants those things, change the type rather
than the list - it is the type that tells the party what to expect from the next place they
find, and breaking it costs more than it buys.

**Which locations to pick.** Cover what a party actually arrives wanting - rest, supply,
information, and somebody to ask - before anything else. A settlement that cannot answer
those has made itself a puzzle when it was supposed to be a comma.

Then spend the remaining slots on what makes this settlement not the last one: the trade it
lives on, the thing it is short of, the argument it is having, the person everyone mentions.
