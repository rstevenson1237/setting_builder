# Safe - Commerce

## Decides
What a location where goods and services change hands offers, at what price, and what it
cannot supply.

## Read at
Step 4c, for a SAFE location whose kind is hospitality, a trade, or a market.

## Spec

```
COMMERCE
  1     What it deals in, and who runs it - drawn from the region's People roster
  1     What is in stock, stated concretely
  1     What it cannot supply, and what it says to do instead
  1     Prices, in cn, for the two or three things a party will actually ask for
  40%   Something unusual in stock, and why it is here
  30%   A condition on trade - credit, membership, a grudge, a shortage
  20%   Something the proprietor wants that money will not buy
```

**The gap is more useful than the stock.** A party can assume a smith sells nails; what
changes their plans is that this smith has no steel until the barge comes, or will not sell
to them, or will trade but not for coin. State what is missing and where it sends them -
that is the line that turns a shop into a decision.

**Prices are stated, not implied.** Two or three real numbers in cn. A location that makes
the referee invent prices at the table has left its job unfinished.

## Patterns

**Hospitality** - a taproom with a floor to sleep on; an inn with rooms of two classes; a
bunkhouse for crews; a widow who takes lodgers; a hall where guests are the headman's
responsibility; a stable that will let you sleep in the loft. State what a night costs,
what a meal costs, whether food is any good, and what the place is known for - a dish, a
standing game, a particular clientele, a rule.

**Trades** - smith, farrier, cooper, tanner, fletcher, wheelwright, carpenter, mason,
miller, baker, brewer, dyer, weaver, potter, chandler, rope-maker, boatwright, apothecary,
herbalist, bone-setter, scribe. Pick what the settlement's living justifies; a place that
mills grain has a miller and probably a baker.

**Dealing** - a general store that carries a little of everything badly; a provisioner for
people going out rather than staying; a trader who buys used gear and odd finds; a
moneylender; a money changer, where the coin here is not the coin elsewhere; a pawn.

**Market** - which day, or daily; who sets up - locals only, or outsiders too; what is
actually on the boards this season; what a stranger is charged over a local; who takes a
cut, and whether that is official.

**Stock worth stating** - what came off the last barge or caravan; what has been sitting
unsold and why; what is rationed; what is only sold to people known here; what was taken
in trade from somebody who needed money badly, and is worth more than the taker knows.

## Constraints
*(Empty. Entries arrive from generation testing, never from anticipation.)*
