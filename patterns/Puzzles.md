# Puzzle Patterns

Interactive set-piece elements pulled specifically into high-weight DANGEROUS locations - statues, altars, fountains, standing magical effects, or similar fixtures that reward investigation. Unlike `Treasure.md`, `Creatures.md`, and `Traps.md`, these don't scale across weights: a puzzle needs the room around it that only high weight provides, and doesn't belong in a low or medium-weight room.

## What a Puzzle Is
A statue, altar, fountain, or standing magical effect/object - something clearly built or placed with purpose, not found debris or a dressing detail. It's a fixture worth the players stopping to investigate.

## Trigger and Effect
- State the trigger explicitly - the specific action that engages it correctly (a phrase spoken, an object placed, a sequence pressed, a specific person or item brought near). Don't leave the correct interaction to be improvised at the table.
- Left uninvestigated, or approached without actually attempting the trigger, the puzzle stays neutral - inspection, theorizing, and a wrong guess that stops short of a real attempt cost nothing.
- An attempt that actually gets the trigger wrong carries a negative effect - state what it is. This is what separates a Puzzle from a Trap: a Trap's negative effect fires on contact or presence; a Puzzle's fires only on a failed attempt to use it.
- Getting the trigger right accomplishes something worth the investigation - reveals a Feature, unlocks an Exit, grants a Treasure citation, or similar. State what the correct trigger does alongside the trigger itself.

## Detail
A Puzzle needs more specified physical detail than a Trap does - at least two distinct details (carvings, wear patterns, mismatched materials, an inscription, prior use) that give players something to reason from rather than guess blindly. A Trap can be stated in a single trigger-and-effect line; a Puzzle can't - the detail is what makes it solvable by investigation instead of luck.
