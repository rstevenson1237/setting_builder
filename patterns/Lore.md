# Lore Patterns

Guidance for what belongs in `setting/Lore.md` - written or otherwise recorded works discoverable within locations, stubbed during location generation and fully written afterward (see `templates/Lore.md`).

- A piece of Lore is a written work - a book, scroll, tapestry, letter, journal, map, ledger, inscription, or similar - discovered within a location, never spoken exposition or something a creature just happens to know.
- Every piece of Lore does at least one of: reveals part of a region or the setting's purpose, connects an event in `setting/History.md` to a Truth in `setting/Truths.md` (or connects two Truths, or two events), or plants an adventure hook a party can choose to chase.
- Lore should read like something an in-setting person actually wrote or made - partial, biased, mistaken, or self-interested where it fits - not an omniscient info-dump. `setting/Rumours.md` already covers the outright unreliable end of this; Lore skews toward a primary source, even an untrustworthy one.
- A piece of Lore belongs to the one location where it's found, but nothing stops a later location or a Named Creature from referencing a Lore entry that already exists.

## Where It Fits
- DANGEROUS, low or medium weight: a solid substitute for a Treasure citation when a location's find would read better as something written than something valuable - not every find needs to be Lore, but it's always a fair swap.
- DANGEROUS, high weight: rather than replacing that location's main payload, add Lore alongside it - a high-weight room can afford both a Treasure/Unique Treasure and a written work explaining or complicating it.
- SAFE or WILD: valid anywhere, but at a relatively low chance of occurring - most landmarks don't need a piece of Lore, so don't force one into every entry.
