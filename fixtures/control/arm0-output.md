# The Sunken Hall of Lord Ashcroft

*A one-page dungeon for four to six adventurers of low level.*

## Background

Three hundred years ago, Lord Ashcroft ruled this valley from a hall built into the
hillside. He was a proud man, and when the river rose and the priests told him it was
judgement, he refused to leave. He sealed the doors and sat on his throne while the
water came in. His household went with him. The hall has been quiet ever since,
though shepherds say lights move behind the hillside on wet nights, and last spring a
boy who went in after a lost ewe came out three days later unable to speak.

The adventurers have come for the Ashcroft silver, which was never recovered. They will
find it, but they will find Lord Ashcroft as well, and he has been waiting a long time
for someone to tell his side of it.

## Wandering Monsters

Check once every three turns. On a 1 in 6, something comes: rats, a drifting cold spot,
or one of the drowned household, depending on where the party is and what seems
appropriate. The dead here are slow but they do not stop.

## The Rooms

**1. The Entrance Hall (20x50).** Stairs come down from the north into a long cold
room. Two pillars stand in the west and east halves, carved with the Ashcroft arms — a
heron and three wheat sheaves — worn almost smooth. The air smells of still water. An
opening heads west to room 2, a door in the south leads to a corridor, and a door east
leads to room 9. Anyone who listens at the east door hears, faintly, the sound of water
lapping.

**2. The Ruined Chamber (30x30).** Debris and broken furniture fill this room —
chairs, a smashed sideboard, the remains of a loom. Something has been through here
and wrecked it thoroughly. Searching the debris for a turn turns up a silver thimble
and a child's shoe. Openings head north to 5, south to 3, and east to 1.

**3. The Tapestry Room (30x30).** A large tapestry covers the south wall, showing a
hunt in a forest. It is rotten and will tear if pulled. Behind it, to the west, is a
secret door (find on a 1-2 in 6) leading to room 4. More debris lies about. An opening
heads north to 2 and a door east to the corridor.

**4. The Hidden Gallery (30x70).** A long narrow room, dry, with a tapestry covering
the east wall. This one shows the same forest, but from the other side, and there is a
figure among the trees that was not in the first tapestry. A short corridor ends at the
secret door back to 3. Here Lord Ashcroft hid the household silver: three chests,
badly tarnished, worth 2,000 gold pieces if the party can carry it all out. Disturbing
the chests wakes the Guardian — a suit of rusted plate that stands and swings a
poleaxe. It is a fearsome thing and fights until destroyed.

> **Rusted Guardian:** a tough opponent, hits hard, immune to sleep and charm and
> poison, and difficult to hurt with ordinary weapons.

**5. The North Tapestry (30x30).** A tapestry covers the north wall, showing Lord
Ashcroft in council. His face has been deliberately cut out of it. An opening heads
south to 2 and a door west to 6. If the party has already been to room 8, the cut in
the tapestry is fresh.

**6. The Antechamber (30x40).** An empty room with a great double door west to 7 and a
smaller door east to 5. The double doors are swollen with damp and take two people to
push open, making a great deal of noise. There are wet footprints on the floor going
west, and they are recent.

**7. The Flooded Colonnade (50x50).** The grandest room in the hall, and now the
saddest. Two rows of columns run east-west in the north and south halves, and a foot
of black water covers the floor. Movement is slow and loud. Beneath the water, among
the silt, are the bones of the household — perhaps twenty of them. If the party
crosses the room noisily, three of the drowned rise out of the water behind them.

> **Drowned Servants (3):** slow, waterlogged, and relentless. They grapple rather than
> strike, and try to pull victims under.

**8. The Throne Room (30x30).** A raised dais with a stone throne, and on it Lord
Ashcroft, dead but not gone. He is dressed in the rags of his robes and his crown has
slipped. He speaks, and he is reasonable, and he wants very much to be listened to. He
will explain that the flood was not judgement, that the priests lied, that he stayed
out of duty. If the party listens and agrees with him, he gives them his ring and lets
them go. If they contradict him or attack, he rises.

> **Lord Ashcroft:** a powerful undead lord, drains the living with his touch, commands
> the drowned, and cannot be turned by a novice.

**9. The Guard Room (50x30).** Doors head west to 1, east to a corridor, and north to
10. Racks that once held spears line the walls, empty now. Someone has scratched a
tally into the plaster by the north door — forty-one marks, then a gap, then one more.

**10. The Well Chamber (40x30).** A tapestry covers the north wall, showing a heron.
In the center of the room is a well, or perhaps a fountain — it is hard to tell which
it was meant to be. It is full of clear water, which is the only clear water in the
hall. Dropping something in produces no sound of a splash. A door heads south to 9 and
east to a corridor.

**11. The Blind Room (30x30).** Bare and cold. An opening heads east to the corridor.
In the north wall is a secret door (find on a 1-2 in 6) heading north to 12. Scratches
around the seam of the door suggest someone tried very hard to open it from this side.

**12. The Sealed Vault (40x50).** Behind the secret door, a large room that has been
sealed since the flood. The air is bad. Here the priests were walled up, and their
bones are stacked against the walls with unusual care. In the middle of the floor is a
circle of salt, unbroken, and in the circle is a book. The book is the record of what
actually happened, and it does not agree with Lord Ashcroft. Removing it from the
circle is a very bad idea.

## Ending

If the party takes the silver and leaves, they are rich and the hall goes quiet again.
If they take the book to Lord Ashcroft, he is destroyed, and the valley sleeps easier,
and there is no reward for it at all. That is the choice the dungeon is really about.
