# THE VAULTS OF MORTHEK THE UNMOURNED

*An Adventure for 4–6 Characters of Levels 2–4*

---

> **A note to the Referee.** No read-aloud text is provided. Describe rooms in your own words using the keyed entries as a foundation. The dungeon does not have a story. It has a history, which is different. Players will reconstruct as much or as little of that history as their curiosity and survival allow. Reward cleverness. Do not protect fools.

---

## BACKGROUND

Two centuries past, a minor warlord-sorcerer called Morthek carved a vault complex into the bluffs above the river Sullen. He used conscripted labor, paid soldiers, and at least one bound entity whose nature is no longer clear. Into this vault he placed stolen wealth, the subjects of his preservation research, and his own contingency against death.

His lieutenants eventually decided the contingency was unnecessary and assisted him into the grave before he could use it. They took what portable wealth remained in the upper level, destroyed or removed his name from every surface they could find, and sealed the lower vault. They did not, or could not, dismiss the undead wardens Morthek had set in place.

The locals call him the Unmourned. They do not go to the bluffs.

**What is known at Cobb's Landing:** The vaults exist. Several parties have entered in living memory. None returned from below the first level. A group of dwarven salvagers has been up there for four days and has not come back, which is beginning to concern the factor who hired them.

**What nobody knows:** Morthek is still present in some form in his quarters on the second level. His preservation ritual was partially successful. He has had two centuries to think about what he did wrong and is very nearly correct.

---

## PART ONE: COBB'S LANDING

*The safe haven. Approximately 3 miles downriver from the bluffs by road, 2 miles by water.*

Cobb's Landing is not a town. It is a necessity — a place where the river bends and the current slackens enough to dock without skill, making it the last reasonable stopping point before the Sullen turns difficult. Eighty souls on a good count. Timber-frame buildings on stilts above the spring flood line. Mud streets. Two docks. A smell of fish, tallow, and whatever the tannery upwind is processing this week.

The locals are neither welcoming nor hostile. Strangers pass through. Most are river traders or trappers. Adventurers are recognized on sight and regarded as a mixed omen — they bring coin but they also sometimes bring back whatever they disturbed.

---

### KEY LOCATIONS IN COBB'S LANDING

---

**A. THE TIPPED BARGE**

*The only inn. Ground floor common room, four rooms above, a loft above that.*

Kept by **Osna Creel**, a broad woman of fifty who survived two husbands and a river flood and gives the impression she could survive most other things too. She does not extend credit. She does not ask questions she does not want answered. She will, if pushed, admit she is worried about the dwarves — one of them left a deposit on his room for a full tenday and she does not like unused deposits.

**Rates:** 2 cp common room floor, 5 cp private room (includes one meal), 1 sp loft (exclusive use, fits six).

**Food:** Adequate. River fish, salt pork, black bread, a passable ale brewed on premises.

**What Osna knows:** She will share the following freely — the bluffs have been there always, most who go don't come back, the dwarves were professional and quiet and paid in advance, and there is a glyph or marking above the main entrance that her late husband once described as *wrong-looking* before he found reason never to return.

---

**B. CARGAULT'S TRADE POST**

*The factor's operation. Largest building in the Landing, positioned at the main dock.*

**Aldric Cargault** runs the post and controls most commerce within ten miles. He is thin, precise, and smells of ink and anxiety. He hired the dwarven salvage crew — outfit, supplies, and a 30% recovery fee on anything they brought out — and is now four days past their expected return with nothing to show for it.

He will hire the party on the same terms he gave the dwarves: outfit, supplies, and 30% of declared recovered value. He will attempt to negotiate for 40%. He will settle at 30%. He is not entirely honest about what the dwarves reported seeing on their first day before they went back up, which was: a skeleton in old garrison livery standing in the main corridor that watched them pass and did not attack.

**Supplies available for purchase:**

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Torch (each) | 1 cp |
| Flask of oil | 2 cp |
| 50 ft. hemp rope | 5 cp |
| Iron spikes (12) | 1 sp |
| Rations, trail (per day) | 5 cp |
| Lantern, hooded | 7 sp |
| Crowbar | 2 sp |
| Grappling hook | 8 sp |
| Pole, 10 ft. | 2 cp |
| Sack, large | 1 cp |

**Not available:** Weapons, armor, healing supplies beyond basic bandaging material, anything requiring a craftsman.

---

**C. THE SURGERY**

*A single-room structure behind the trade post. Herbs hanging from the rafters. Clean, by local standards.*

**Pell Vardus** calls himself a physicker. He was trained as an apothecary's assistant in a city he refuses to name and arrived in Cobb's Landing seven years ago without explanation. He is competent at wound treatment, moderately skilled at disease identification, and possesses a small stock of herbal preparations.

**Services:**

| Service | Cost |
|---|---|
| Wound treatment (stops bleeding, basic cleaning) | 3 cp |
| Fever treatment (2d4 days, uncertain outcome) | 1 sp |
| Restorative draught (heals 1d4 hp, tastes horrible) | 2 sp |
| Identification of poison or disease | 5 sp |

**Stock:** He has 4 restorative draughts currently. He will not sell more than 2 to any one party — he has seen what happens when adventurers buy everything and then half of them don't come back to need it.

**What Pell knows:** He treated one of the original dwarven crew — not a wound, a tremor in the hands the dwarf couldn't explain. He prescribed rest. The dwarf went back up the next morning. Pell is not optimistic.

---

**D. THE DOCKS**

Two boatmen operate out of Cobb's Landing. **Hen Scarrow** runs a flat-bottomed cargo barge and will transport the party to the base of the bluffs by river for 2 sp, which takes thirty minutes versus a two-hour road walk. He will wait four hours for 1 additional sp. He will not wait overnight under any circumstances and is not shy about explaining why. His cousin tried that once.

## PART TWO: THE VAULTS OF MORTHEK

### Approach & Entrances

The bluffs rise 60 feet above the Sullen's eastern bank. The rock face is grey limestone streaked brown with iron seep. Three approaches exist.

**ENTRANCE I — THE PORTICO** *(Main Gate)*

A switchback trail, carved into the bluff face, begins at a stone marker (the inscription defaced) and ascends 200 yards to a carved portico — two pillars, no door. The portico is visible from the river. Vegetation has been kept back by something; within ten feet of the entrance the ground is bare stone, the weeds dry and pulled. The dwarves did this. There are boot prints in old mud, four sets going in, two sets intermittently returning over multiple days.

The interior beyond the portico is dark. The smell is cold stone and something below that — not rot exactly, more like the absence of anything living.

**ENTRANCE II — THE RIVER SEAM** *(Low Entry)*

At the base of the bluff, accessible from the water, a vertical crack in the limestone — 2 feet wide at its broadest. A character in light encumbrance can squeeze through. Packs must be passed separately. The crack descends at an angle for 30 feet then opens into a natural chamber that connects to **Area 11** (the Kitchen drain). In wet season this passage is ankle-deep in cold water. In dry season it is merely damp. No traps. No light. Sounds echo strangely — voices from Level One are faintly audible as soon as the characters clear the crack.

**ENTRANCE III — THE SMOKE VENT** *(Top Entry)*

Atop the bluff, 80 feet from the edge, a stone cap sits over a circular shaft 3 feet in diameter. The cap is iron-banded stone, heavy but not fixed — two characters working together can shift it in one round with some noise. Below is a vertical shaft with iron rungs set in the stone. Every third rung is missing. The shaft drops 40 feet into **Area 11** (the Kitchen). Rope is effectively required. The shaft is not trapped, but it is narrow enough that combat while descending is impossible, and ascending while encumbered is a test of strength.

*A character who listens at the uncapped vent before descending will hear, very faintly, what might be rats.*

## LEVEL ONE: THE GARRISON WORKS

*General conditions:* Ceilings 8–10 ft. unless noted. Floors are cut limestone, worn smooth in the main corridor, rough in side chambers. Walls are mortared block. No light sources function that were left here — torches rotted, oil dried, sconces empty. The north wall of the complex seeps moisture in several areas; the floor there is damp and in some spots slick. Temperature is cool and constant, approximately 55 degrees.

---

**1. THE PORTICO**

*Outside air, stone pillars, open to the bluff face. Dim light even by day — the overhang cuts the sun.*

The portico is 20 feet wide, 15 feet deep, 12 feet high at the arch. The pillars are undecorated. The threshold between portico and interior is a single large flagstone, slightly discolored — lighter grey than those around it.

**The Welcoming Stone (Trap):** The threshold flagstone is a pressure plate. Any weight above 50 lbs. triggers it. A stone slab (4 inches thick, ceiling-mounted) drops across the portico exit in 1 round, sealing the entrance behind those inside. The slab has no exterior purchase — it cannot be lifted from the portico side without a crowbar and significant effort (2 turns, two characters). The release lever is in **Area 3**.

The trap does not harm anyone. It traps them.

**Referee:** A character who explicitly looks at the ceiling before crossing the threshold sees the slab and its track. Most players will not ask their characters to look up. Do not prompt them. The dwarves triggered it on their first entry, found the lever in Area 3, reset it, and triggered it again on their second entry. They have since learned to step around the edge of the flagstone, where the pressure mechanism does not reach — this trick is visible in the boot prints if anyone examines them carefully.

**Tracks:** Four sets of boot prints in old dust inside the portico. Recent. The prints are careful, not panicked.

---

**2. THE ENTRY HALL**

*20 x 30 ft. Two iron sconce brackets on the east wall, empty. A pile of refuse against the south wall — old camp debris.*

This room served as a guardroom in Morthek's time. The refuse pile is what previous explorers left behind: a rotted bedroll, three empty wine skins, a broken lantern, a gnawed beef bone (recent, dwarven rations). On the west wall, someone has scratched a rough map in charcoal — a partial layout of Level One rooms 1 through 8, with a skull drawn over what appears to be Area 9 (the Shrine).

**Behind the refuse pile:** A functional tinderbox and 3 usable torches that someone cached and did not return for.

**Door north (to Area 3):** Iron-banded oak, open, hinges intact.
**Door east (to Area 5, North Barracks):** Same construction, closed.
**Passage west (to Area 4, Main Corridor):** Open archway, no door.

**Referee:** The charcoal map is not inaccurate as far as it goes but misrepresents the distance between areas and omits the crawlspace entirely. The skull over Area 9 should be understood as a genuine warning.

---

**3. THE GUARD POST**

*15 x 20 ft. A heavy oak table, two stools (one collapsed). Arrow slits in the north wall looking out over the bluff approach.*

This room once processed visitors to the garrison. The table is thick-legged and immovable without effort (bolted to the floor). Under the table, behind a rotted curtain: a recessed iron lever, set horizontally into the wall at knee height. This is the release for the Welcoming Stone trap in Area 1.

**Lever mechanics:** Pulling it resets the slab to the ceiling (1 round of grinding noise, audible throughout Level One). Pushing it triggers the drop if the stone was already reset. The mechanism is simple and obvious once found.

**Contents of table:** Rotten wood surface, a bronze inkwell (empty, but the bronze is worth 5 sp), and a set of knucklebones — not carved, actual finger bones, small, worn smooth from use.

**North wall arrow slits:** Three, approximately 6 inches wide. Enough to see the approach trail and the portico. In Morthek's time, the guard posted here could harass anyone attempting to escape through the entrance.

**Skeleton:** A Warden Skeleton is assigned to this room. It stands in the northeast corner, motionless unless a living creature enters without passing through the Main Corridor (Area 4) first — it was instructed to allow people in through the front. It will attack anyone attempting to leave via the portico without Morthek's mark.

> **Warden Skeleton (1):** AC 7, HD 1, hp 6, #AT 1, Dmg 1d6 (iron-shod club), MV 12, Immune to sleep/charm/cold. Does not pursue beyond its assigned room.

---

**4. THE MAIN CORRIDOR**

*10 ft. wide, 80 ft. long east-west, with two branches — one north, one south. Ceiling 10 ft.*

The spine of Level One. The floor here is the most worn — the smooth groove of decades of garrison traffic still visible. Three iron torch brackets are set at even intervals; all empty.

**The Patrol:** Two Warden Skeletons walk a slow circuit of the Main Corridor and its branches — east to west, north branch, south, return. Their circuit takes approximately 10 minutes (1 turn). They ignore each other. They will attack any living creature they detect, pursuing the length of the corridor but not into side rooms.

**The North Branch:** 30 ft., connects Main Corridor to the North Barracks (Area 5), the Armory (Area 7), and eventually the collapsed section (Area 13). The floor here is slightly lower and damp.

**The South Branch:** 20 ft., connects to the South Barracks (Area 6) and, through it, eventually to the Shrine (Area 9) and Kitchen (Area 11). A section of the south branch ceiling is cracked — not dangerous but drops small debris if the skeletons walk through, creating a periodic soft pattering sound.

> **Warden Skeletons (2):** AC 7, HD 1, hp 5/7, #AT 1, Dmg 1d6 (iron-shod club), MV 12. Pursue length of corridor only.

---

**5. THE NORTH BARRACKS**

*40 x 30 ft. Eight wooden bunk frames, most collapsed into kindling. A wide fireplace on the north wall, cold and ash-choked.*

This housed Morthek's common garrison soldiers. The bunks have rotted to varying degrees; two are structurally intact. The fireplace chimney connects upward — a character who climbs into it (narrow, sooty, 2 ft. square) can eventually reach a horizontal duct that vents near the bluff face. The climb is 25 feet, requires Strength and generates considerable noise. There is nothing in the chimney worth the effort.

**Contents:** Beneath the ash in the fireplace, a iron cookpot (heavy, functional, worth 1 sp). Scattered on the floor among the debris: 3 cp, a bronze belt buckle (2 sp), a leather boot with something hard in the toe — a silver ring set with a garnet, worth 12 sp, hidden there by a soldier who did not survive to retrieve it.

**Under the third collapsed bunk frame from the east:** A skeleton in rotted leather — not undead, just dead. A soldier killed in whatever altercation followed Morthek's assassination. A corroded short sword lies beside him (non-functional) and a folded piece of vellum in a waxed case: a letter in old script, readable with literacy and ten minutes of effort. It is from a woman in a city two weeks' travel distant, asking the recipient to come home. It is dated eighteen months before Morthek died. It was never sent.

**Referee:** The letter has no mechanical purpose. Referees who read it aloud tend to find that their players remember this room longer than the ones with treasure.

---

**6. THE SOUTH BARRACKS**

*40 x 25 ft. Similar construction to Area 5 but with one significant difference — four of the bunks have been recently cleared and are in use.*

This is where the dwarven NPC party has made camp.

**The Dwarves:**

- **Bregga Holt**, crew leader, female, competent fighter, calm under pressure, has not been calm since they lost Durwick. She will talk before fighting and means what she says.
- **Ossin Vark**, the remaining salvage specialist — picks locks, evaluates goods. Nervous. Was not hired for combat. Has a broken wrist from the portcullis incident, splinted and wrapped.
- **Thumin**, a fighter, the youngest of the crew, twenty-two years old and deeply frightened and hiding it poorly.
- **Redvar**, fighter, oldest of them, says almost nothing, watches exits.

**Their situation:** They entered four days ago (seven members). The portcullis trap at Area 14 crushed two of them on the second day when they attempted to force it without understanding the mechanism — they subsequently found the trigger passage (Area 13, the crawlspace) and have not retried the direct route. A third dwarf, Durwick, went down the Throat on a scouting pass and did not return. That was two days ago.

**They know:** Full layout of Level One rooms 1-13 (minus the crawlspace's destination). The location of the portcullis trigger. The fact that something below is functional and dangerous. The patrol circuit of the corridor skeletons, roughly. They do not know about the ghoul in Area 9 — they have not entered the Shrine.

**Their stance:** Not hostile. Wary. They will not attack unless the players are actively threatening. They will negotiate for mutual benefit but will not discuss splitting a 50/50 share downward. They will lie about Durwick — they will say he is scouting, not that he is missing.

**Their intent:** To find Durwick, retrieve what they can, leave. Bregga is beginning to accept that Durwick is dead. She has not said this to her crew.

**Betrayal threshold:** If the players are clearly winning and clearly have more resources, Vark will suggest to Bregga that they could follow the players to Level Two, let them clear the path, and take the proceeds. Bregga will not agree to this unless the party has been actively hostile. Thumin will go along with whatever Bregga says. Redvar watches.

**Their camp:** Four bedrolls, two lanterns (full oil), a locked iron strongbox (Vark has the key — contains 40 gp of operating funds, Cargault's salvage contract, and two restorative draughts). An open pack shows rations, rope, tools.

**Referee:** The dwarves are the most useful NPCs in the dungeon and also the most dangerous, depending on how the players treat them. Do not play them as automatically friendly or automatically hostile. They are professionals in a bad situation.

---

**7. THE ARMORY**

*20 x 20 ft. Smell of rust. Three collapsed weapon racks along the south wall. Walls are bare iron hooks, most empty.*

The valuable contents of this room were taken by Morthek's surviving lieutenants. What remains is garrison surplus and damaged goods.

**Contents:**
- 14 short swords (all corroded, non-functional as weapons — blades will snap on first use but could be sold as scrap metal, 1 sp total for the lot)
- 6 spears (2 fully functional, 4 with split shafts that can be repaired)
- 3 shields (1 functional and sound, bears Morthek's mark — a stylized M above an eye. 2 are rotted through)
- A locked iron chest beneath the central rack

**Chest (locked, good quality):** Requires a thief's tools and skill, or a crowbar and noise (1 round of clanging). Contains: 40 sp in old coin (valid currency, the minting is just old), a whetstone in a leather case, a vial of fine oil in waxed cloth (keeps, useful for mechanisms), and a folded paper with names listed in two columns — a duty roster. The names in the right column are crossed through one by one. The last entry, uncrossed: *Harven. Vault watch. Does not return.*

**Referee:** Harven is the Wight in Area 24. Players who find this and make the connection later should feel clever. Do not point it out. If they ask what the roster means, tell them what it looks like — a duty record. Let them interpret.

---

**8. THE WEIGHT ROOM**

*15 x 15 ft. Accessed from the north branch corridor via a plain wooden door (unlocked, slightly warped).*

This room was used for equipment storage — crates, barrels, heavy goods. It is empty now except for what the trap contains.

**The Floor Trap:** The entire floor is a single balanced stone slab, pivoting on a central iron rod set in the east and west walls. Weight distributed evenly across the slab (characters entering from different sides simultaneously) keeps it level. Weight concentrated past the center point of either half causes that half to tilt downward at approximately 30 degrees, sliding anything on it into a 10-foot pit beneath.

**The Pit:** 10 feet deep, stone walls. Iron handholds are cut into the north face — this was a containment pit, not a killing trap. Anyone dropped in can climb out in 1 round. The pit contains:

- The skeleton of a previous explorer (no ID possible, older than the dwarves)
- A fine quality dagger in a rotted sheath — balanced, holds an edge, worth 25 sp to a weaponsmith, worth more to someone who needs a reliable blade

**Trigger mechanics:** A single character of average encumbrance (120+ lbs. loaded) crossing the center line alone will tip the slab. Two characters crossing from opposite sides simultaneously will balance it. The slab can be heard to flex slightly — a low creak — when any weight is placed on it. A character who explicitly listens before entering will hear this creak when a 10-foot pole is used to test the floor.

---

**9. THE SHRINE**

*30 x 25 ft. Vaulted ceiling, 14 ft. A stone altar against the north wall. Three niches in the east wall, empty. The smell of old incense buried beneath something fouler.*

Morthek co-opted a minor god of roads and transitions for his garrison's religious observance. The altar still bears the road-god's symbol — a carved wheel — though it has been scratched through, whether by Morthek's lieutenants or by something else is unclear. The niches once held offerings. Two are empty. The third contains a scattering of small bones — human finger bones, arranged in no pattern.

**The Ghoul:** The shrine's current occupant crouches behind the altar in the northeast corner. He was a freelance cleric who came to rob the vaults seven years ago and did not leave. He has had time to develop something approximating patience. He will hear characters entering and will speak before he acts.

He calls himself Mave. His voice is wet and wrong but the words are coherent.

*He wants to leave. He cannot tolerate daylight — even overcast sky causes him pain. He is looking for an arrangement.*

**What he knows:** Everything on Level One — every room, every trap, every skeleton's patrol. He knows the portcullis trigger (though the dwarves also know this). He knows that something below killed a dwarf two days ago. He knows the marked threshold in the Level Two antechamber (Area 16) — he crossed it once and the shriek sent the skeletons below into a frenzy that lasted an hour. He has not returned.

**What he will trade:** One piece of specific, accurate information for a credible promise. He understands credible means something binding — an oath, a written agreement, collateral. He has been lied to before.

**What he wants in exchange for full cooperation:** Transport downriver, at night, in a covered cart or closed boat, to somewhere he can find a population to sustain himself. He is not proud of this.

**If attacked or cornered:** He fights. He is not stupid — he will attempt to paralyze and flee rather than fight to the death in a room with multiple opponents.

> **Ghoul (1):** AC 6, HD 2, hp 11, #AT 3 (claw/claw/bite), Dmg 1d3/1d3/1d6, MV 9, Special: paralysis on touch (save vs. paralysis or paralyzed 1d6+2 turns). Immune to sleep and charm. Turned as ghoul.

**Behind the altar:** A leather satchel — Mave's original kit, mostly rotted. One item intact: a silver holy symbol of the road-god, worth 15 sp, functional as a holy symbol.

---

**10. THE GREAT HALL**

*50 x 30 ft. The largest space on Level One. A long stone table, fixed to the floor, with bench foundations along both sides. Three iron chandelier mounts overhead, all empty. Faded paint on the walls — geometric patterns in faded red and black.*

This was Morthek's garrison hall. Used for meals, for punishment, for the announcement of orders. The table seats thirty. Two of the bench slabs have been overturned — this happened a long time ago and nobody has bothered to right them.

**Two Warden Skeletons** stand at the far (east) end of the hall, flanking a raised platform where Morthek's seat once sat (the chair is gone). They are stationary unless living creatures enter the hall. They will pursue anywhere in Level One, unlike the corridor skeletons — this room was considered a secure interior space.

**On the platform:** The stone base of the chair remains. Carved into its underside (visible only if overturned, which requires two characters and a round of effort) is a name — not Morthek's. A woman's name, in careful letters: *Suelle.* Below it, a date in an old calendar format. Nothing else.

**Wall murals:** The painted patterns are geometric, Morthek's aesthetic — all right angles and concentric frames. Players who study them with torchlight and time (1 turn) can identify one section that is subtly different — a pattern that resolves, with effort, into a stylized eye. This matches the mark on the shield in Area 7 and, if anyone looks when they reach Level Two, the carvings throughout.

**South door (to Area 9, the Shrine):** Closed, iron-banded, functional.
**East passage (to Area 12 and onward to the Throat):** Open archway.
**West passage (to Main Corridor):** Open archway.

> **Warden Skeletons (2):** AC 7, HD 1, hp 4/8, #AT 1, Dmg 1d6 (iron-shod club), MV 12. Pursue throughout Level One.

---

**11. THE KITCHEN**

*35 x 25 ft. A massive iron stove against the south wall, cold. Two stone preparation tables, cracked. A drain channel cut in the floor running south.*

The kitchen is the most inhabited room on Level One. The Giant Rat colony has made it their center of operations. Signs are everywhere: gnawed wood, rat droppings, a smell of musk and old grease, several generations of nest material heaped in the corners.

**The Colony:** 3d6 Giant Rats are present at any time, with 1d6 more arriving from the drain channel or the smoke vent shaft (Area, above) within 1d4 rounds if combat begins. They will not pursue beyond the kitchen if the party retreats.

**The Drain Channel:** A channel 18 inches wide, 14 inches deep, cut south through the floor and into the rock. This is where the River Seam entrance (Entrance II) connects — a character who follows the channel south can squeeze through a narrowing passage to the crack in the bluff face. The channel floor is wet and slick.

**The Smoke Vent:** The shaft descends through the ceiling at the north end of the kitchen, 3 feet in diameter, with a rope or ladder required for the 40-foot climb. Every third iron rung is missing. This is Entrance III.

**Under the preparation tables:** The rats have been using the space as a larder. Mostly inedible debris, but amid it: 3 usable iron spikes, a pewter pitcher (cracked but worth 3 sp), and the personal effects of someone who died here — a leather purse with 11 sp and a copper ring.

> **Giant Rats (3d6):** AC 7, HD 1/2, hp 2 each, #AT 1, Dmg 1d3 + disease (save vs. poison or contract wasting fever — 1 point CON damage per day for 1d6+2 days, reduced by treatment). MV 12/6 swimming.

---

**12. THE STORAGE WARRENS**

*A series of three connected rooms, roughly 15 x 15 ft. each, northeast of the kitchen.*

These were dry goods storage. They are empty now — cleaned out two centuries ago. The rooms are notable only for what connects them:

**Collapsed Wall:** The east wall of the third storage room has partially collapsed inward, leaving a rubble-choked passage approximately 3 feet high. This is the entrance to the Crawlspace (Area 13). Rat tracks lead into and out of it.

**Water Seep:** The north wall of the second storage room drips steadily. A puddle covers most of the floor (1 inch deep). The floor here is slick — a character moving faster than careful walking pace must save vs. paralysis or fall prone. The skeletons in Area 4 avoid this room for this reason.

---

**13. THE COLLAPSED SECTION AND CRAWLSPACE**

*The crawlspace is approximately 60 ft. long, variable width (2–4 ft.), ceiling 3 ft. throughout.*

The crawlspace passes through natural fissures in the bluff rock, supplemented by some deliberate chiseling — someone cleared this passage intentionally, likely the man mentioned in the Cobb's Landing rumors who escaped this way years ago.

**Traversal:** Characters must remove all large packs and armor above leather to pass. Armor and packs can be pushed or pulled through but not worn. A character carrying a lit torch will find it awkward — the torch can be held ahead of them but the smoke pools at ceiling height (unpleasant but not dangerous for the 10-minute traverse). A hooded lantern is significantly better.

**At the far end:** The crawlspace exits behind the portcullis at Area 14. A character emerging here will find themselves on the south side of the portcullis, at the top of the Throat staircase, having bypassed the portcullis and its noise entirely.

**The dwarves know this route.** Bregga used it on days 3 and 4 of their expedition for her scouting passes. She has not mentioned this to the party.

**What is in the crawlspace:** At the midpoint, a skeleton — a rat, very large, dead of unknown causes. A faintly phosphorescent growth on the wall (not dangerous, merely strange — produces dim light equivalent to a candle in a 2-foot radius, fades to nothing within a day if removed). Near the exit, a dwarf's pack, left behind — it contains Durwick's personal effects, 3 days of rations, and a letter he was writing home that breaks off mid-sentence.

---

**14. THE THROAT**

*The Throat is a staircase and portcullis, not a room. It connects Level One to Level Two.*

The staircase descends 30 feet through solid rock in a straight line, 5 feet wide. One person can pass at a time without significant jostling; two can walk abreast with difficulty. The steps are even and sound.

**The Portcullis:** Positioned at the top of the staircase, this iron gate was functional in Morthek's time. The counterweight mechanism is jammed by two centuries of corrosion and a piece of broken stone from a crack in the housing. The portcullis can be:

- **Forced upward** by a character of 17+ Strength, or two characters cooperating — requires a round of effort and produces a groan and clang audible throughout Level One and into Level Two.
- **Triggered from the side passage** — a narrow niche in the east wall beside the staircase top conceals a hand-lever. A character searching the wall specifically will find it in 1 round. The lever releases the jam and allows the portcullis to be raised quietly (relatively — still a creak of iron). The dwarves know about this lever. If asked directly, they will admit to it, depending on their relationship with the party.

**Below the portcullis:** The staircase continues down. At the base, the Marked Threshold trap is in the Level Two antechamber (Area 15).

## LEVEL TWO: THE VAULT WORKS

*General conditions:* Ceilings 12–14 ft. Walls are dressed stone carved with obsessive geometric patterns — all right angles, concentric squares, and the recurring eye motif. The alchemical torch system Morthek installed persists: iron sconces throughout Level Two hold slow-burning alchemical wicks that produce light equivalent to a torch, without heat, and do not deplete noticeably in the party's presence. The effect is unsettling — the dungeon appears to expect inhabitants. Temperature is colder than Level One, approximately 45 degrees. Sound behaves oddly in the carved corridors — voices carry further than expected.

---

**15. THE ANTECHAMBER**

*20 x 20 ft. At the base of the Throat staircase. A single alchemical sconce on each wall, burning. The room is bare except for its floor.*

The floor of the antechamber appears to have been sanded or scraped at some point — the surface is unusually smooth. A thin layer of fine stone dust has settled across it since.

**The Marked Threshold (Trap):** A Glyph of Warding, invisible beneath the silt, covers a 5-foot band across the center of the room. Any living creature stepping into the band triggers it. The glyph does not damage — it shrieks. A sustained, high-pitched tone, audible throughout Level Two, lasting 1 full round.

**Effect of the shriek:** All creatures on Level Two who can hear are immediately aware that living creatures have entered. Random encounter check at +2 for the next 3 turns. The Wight in Area 24 (the Reckoning Hall) begins moving toward the antechamber at his standard move rate unless subsequently distracted.

**Detecting the glyph:** The silt layer means the floor appears clean but uniform. A character who explicitly examines the floor with a light source held low (angled to rake the surface) will see the silt is slightly disturbed along the 5-foot band — previous passage by something that does not trigger magical wards. Undead cross it without effect.

**The glyph cannot be disarmed without magical means.** It can be bypassed by stepping over the 5-foot band (requires a jump of modest length — not difficult, but gear must be balanced). It can be triggered deliberately as a distraction.

**Durwick's Remains:** In the northwest corner, a dwarf's body. He triggered the glyph on a solo scouting pass, was set upon by two warden skeletons who had been circuiting nearby, and did not survive. His short sword is beside him (functional). His boots are good quality. He has 7 sp on his person. He is identifiable to the other dwarves.

---

**16. THE APPROACH CORRIDOR**

*10 ft. wide, 40 ft. long, running north from the Antechamber to the Reckoning Hall. Alcoves at 15-foot intervals on both sides.*

Each alcove (six total) is 5 feet deep, 4 feet wide, with a carved niche at the back. The niches once held statuary that was removed — the pedestals remain, some with fragments of stone feet.

**Two Warden Skeletons** circuit this corridor as part of their standard patrol, which includes portions of Areas 17, 18, 19, and 20. Their circuit takes 15 minutes. Their interval can be timed with patience.

**The carvings here are denser** than on Level One — the geometric patterns cover every surface with no gaps. A cleric or magic-user who examines them for a full turn can determine that they constitute a ward complex, probably a containment or binding system, in an archaic tradition. The eye motif appears every 12 inches along the upper border.

**Referee:** Damaging the carvings in this corridor is equivalent to damaging the binding on the Wight. Each 5-foot section destroyed or significantly defaced reduces the Wight's effective level of binding — treat as progressively removing HD of the binding effect. After 3 sections are damaged, the Wight becomes aware and agitated regardless of location. After 6, he is fully unbound. Track what the players do to the walls.

---

**17. THE ANTECHAMBER CELLS**

*Three small rooms, 10 x 10 ft., accessed from the east side of the Approach Corridor.*

These were waiting rooms for those who required an audience with Morthek. Plain, no fixtures. Locks on the outside of each door.

**Cell A:** Empty.
**Cell B:** A wooden chair, collapsed into three pieces. Under the chair pieces: a brass button, a charcoal nub, and scratched into the floor — a rough map of the Reckoning Hall (Area 24), including the position of an altar or dais and something chained to it. Whoever made this map was brought here for an audience and survived long enough to record what they saw. They did not leave this room alive; there is an old stain on the south wall.

**Cell C:** Occupied. A Warden Skeleton sits on the floor with its back against the south wall. It is not on patrol. It was placed here and given no instructions except to wait. It has been waiting for two centuries. It will rise and attack when a living creature enters.

> **Warden Skeleton (1):** AC 7, HD 1, hp 5, #AT 1, Dmg 1d6. This one carries a hand axe rather than a club — a personal weapon, never replaced.

---

**18. THE LABORATORY**

*40 x 30 ft. The most cluttered room in the vault. Ceiling 14 ft. Three long stone tables in parallel. Open shelving on all walls, partially intact.*

Morthek's research space for his preservation work. The shelving was built into the rock and could not easily be removed — his lieutenants took the portable contents but left the infrastructure. What remains:

**On the shelves:** Dozens of clay jars and glass vessels, most empty, most cracked. 8 are sealed and intact. Contents (referee roll or select): 3 contain preserved organic material in clear fluid (no magical value, only curiosity); 2 contain alchemical reagents, still potent — one is an accelerant (poured on a fire, doubles its radius for 1d4 rounds) and one is unclear (black, oily, smells of copper — treat as a contact irritant, not dangerous but numbing); 1 contains a fine preservative oil with a genuine practical use for treating leather or wood; 1 contains an actual useful solution — a powdered coagulant, 3 doses, packs a wound and stops severe bleeding immediately; 1 is sealed with wax over a glass stopper and contains a rolled paper.

**The rolled paper:** Morthek's personal notation key. The cipher is simple — 8 symbols, each corresponding to a numeral 1-8. This is needed to decode the treasury lock (Area 25) without guessing. A character with literacy can work this out in 1 turn once they also find the sequence in Morthek's quarters (Area 22).

**The writing desk:** Stone-topped, built-in, south wall. Drawers have been forced open and emptied. The underside of the desk top, visible only when crouching beneath it, is scratched with 4 symbols in Morthek's notation. This is the treasury lock sequence. Without the key from the sealed jar, the symbols are unreadable. With it, the combination is clear.

**Referee:** The combination is deliberately split between two locations — the key in a jar and the sequence on the underside of the desk. Players who search the lab thoroughly can get both from this room alone. Players who rush will miss one or both.

**Two stone slabs** against the north wall, each with iron restraint brackets. Preservation work slabs. Clean. Morthek was meticulous.

**A Warden Skeleton** stands in the northwest corner, assigned to protect the research space. It will engage immediately when living creatures enter.

> **Warden Skeleton (1):** AC 7, HD 1, hp 8, #AT 1, Dmg 1d6 (iron-shod club).

---

**19. THE SPECIMEN CHAMBER**

*30 x 25 ft. The smell here is different from the rest of Level Two — drier, more chemical. Eight stone plinths, roughly body-length, spaced in two rows.*

Morthek's preservation subjects. Six of the eight plinths hold bodies in various states — each treated, sealed with an alchemical preparation, in a state between mummification and preservation. They are not animated. They are not alive. They are studies.

**The subjects** are individuals from various periods of Morthek's research, distinguished by the progression of his technique — the older ones are desiccated and sunken, the later ones (those on the northern plinths) retain an unsettling life-like quality. One of the later subjects appears to be wearing garrison armor. Another appears to be in merchant's dress.

**On the bodies:** A total of 22 sp and 4 gp in old coins distributed across visible pockets and pouches. One subject wears a gold ring (worth 30 gp) on a finger that is perfectly preserved. The ring can be removed without difficulty. It feels wrong to do so, but it can be done.

**Mechanical trap:** The southern door of this room (connecting toward the Cell-Crypts, Area 20) has a pressure mechanism on its upper frame — opening it too quickly brings a stone block down at face height, dealing 1d6 damage and requiring a save vs. paralysis or being stunned 1 round. The mechanism is visible on the door's interior face; on the exterior face it is not. The block must be propped up or removed (10 minutes) to make the door safe.

---

**20. THE CELL-CRYPTS**

*A corridor 10 ft. wide, 30 ft. long, with four alcoves sealed by iron doors, two per side. Ceiling 12 ft.*

These are where Morthek interred his most loyal soldiers. He called it an honor. He explained that their service would continue in a new form. He described it as a promotion.

The soldiers understood, in the abstract, that they would be preserved. They did not fully understand that preservation meant interment while still living, the alchemical preparation completed while conscious, and a long period of transition into something other than living. Three of them went willingly. The fourth did not, and the marks on the inside of that door are still visible.

**The Doors:** Iron, unlocked. Morthek intended his Zombie Soldiers to be deployable. The doors have not been opened in two centuries because no one has reached this room.

**Room 20A:** Zombie Soldier, standing in the center of the cell, facing the door. It will emerge when the door is opened and attack immediately.

**Room 20B:** Zombie Soldier, slumped in the corner. It will rise within 1 round of the door opening. It moves slightly slower than 20A — its left leg was damaged at some point in its existence.

**Room 20C:** Zombie Soldier, upright, in a far more aggressive posture — this is the one who did not go willingly. The marks on the inside of the door are deep gouges. It will emerge immediately and attack with a ferocity the others lack, pursuing further than the standard zombie 1-room range.

**Room 20D:** Empty. The fourth soldier escaped this room at some point — the marks on the inside of this door are different, a deliberate repeated impact in one spot, eventually sufficient to crack the door's lower hinge. The soldier is not on Level Two. It is not in the dungeon. The door shows how it got out. Where it went is unresolved, and will remain so.

> **Zombie Soldiers (3):** AC 8, HD 2, hp 12/9/14, #AT 1, Dmg 1d8 (weapon, still carrying garrison short swords), MV 6. Immune to sleep, charm, cold, and fear.

**In each occupied cell:** Basic garrison kit — belt, boots (rotted), scabbard. On the back wall of each, scratched: a name. Only the name. No other inscription.

---

**21. THE APPROACH TO THE TREASURY**

*A corridor, 10 ft. wide, 25 ft. long, running east from the Reckoning Hall to the treasury wing. Lined with the densest concentration of geometric carvings in the dungeon.*

The carvings here are nearly floor-to-ceiling and cover all four walls plus the ceiling with no gap. The eye motif is repeated so frequently that a character moving slowly with a torch experiences the sensation that the eyes are tracking them. This is an optical effect produced by the pattern, not a magical phenomenon. It is effective.

**At the midpoint of the corridor:** A second Glyph of Warding, identical to the one in Area 15. The silt here is thinner — this corridor is drier and cleaner, and the glyph is slightly more visible. A character actively checking the floor (having been warned by the first glyph or from the ghoul's information) will spot it automatically. The glyph's effect is identical — a shrieks, 1 full round, audible throughout Level Two.

**The False Vault (east branch of corridor):** A door identical to the treasury door, in a doorframe in the south wall. See Area 22.

**The Treasury (end of corridor):** The real treasury door is at the corridor's end. See Area 25.

---

**22. THE FALSE VAULT**

*20 x 20 ft. Appears identical to the treasury at first entry — same scale, similar goods visible immediately.*

Morthek's joke. He found this genuinely funny.

The room contains an assembled display of worthless goods dressed to appear valuable:

- Lead ingots, 12, painted gold — convincing to inspection, revealed by weight (far heavier than gold equivalent)
- Glass stones in a locked wooden chest — red, green, cut to resemble gems, flawless surface, no depth under close light
- Eight scrolls on a rack — rolled, sealed with Morthek's mark, written in dense script. Appear to be research records. Three are pure nonsense — invented words in Morthek's hand, grammatically plausible, meaningless. Four are actual research notes but pertain to subjects of no value — dietary observances during preservation preparation, maintenance schedules for the alchemical torch system. One is a genuine research summary of his binding methodology, in clear language, describing how the geometric carvings function. This one is actually useful and Morthek left it here because he could not resist.
- A portrait in a gilded frame, mounted on the east wall. A man in his middle years, severe expression, eyes too wide. He is wearing the same mark as the shields and carvings. Presumably Morthek. The frame is genuinely gilded — worth 15 gp — and the portrait itself may interest a collector (5 gp if found, more in the right market).

**The One Real Item:** Tucked behind the lead ingot stack, in a cloth pouch that has gone grey with dust: a ring of carved bone, extremely old, pre-dating Morthek's occupation of the vaults by several centuries. It is magical. Morthek found it and put it in the false vault because he could not identify it and that frustrated him, and it was too interesting to put in the real treasury where someone might take it as mere jewelry. The ring's effect: the wearer's presence makes undead creatures hesitate for 1 round upon first perceiving them (not paralysis, not turning — a momentary pause, as if uncertain). It provides no other protection. This effect works once per unique undead creature encountered.

**Referee:** The scroll describing the binding system is the most valuable item in this room to a clever party — understanding the carvings means they can choose to damage them strategically or leave them intact as a control over the Wight. Do not prompt players toward this scroll. It is there if they look.

---

**23. THE CORRIDOR CELLS**

*Three small chambers off the north wall of the Approach to the Treasury (Area 21), 10 x 10 ft. each, plain.*

Used for Morthek's personal guards during work in the treasury wing. Now empty of inhabitants.

**Cell A:** A broken sword — the blade is in two pieces, but the hilt is worked silver, worth 20 gp to a jeweler.

**Cell B:** A set of Morthek's personal papers — drafts, lists, observations. Readable with literacy and time (1 turn). Contains: an inventory of treasury items, dated, in coded notation that requires the cipher from Area 18 to read. If decoded, it gives a partial but accurate account of treasury contents. It does not mention the ring in the false vault. Also contains a personal entry — three sentences about a woman named Suelle (the name from Area 10), in a tone that does not fit the rest of Morthek's clinical writing. The entry ends in mid-sentence and the page is torn.

**Cell C:** Two Warden Skeletons, both in parade rest position, assigned permanently to treasury corridor protection. They have been standing here for two centuries awaiting an alarm. The glyph in Area 21 is that alarm.

If the glyph has been triggered, they are already moving. If not, they stand motionless and will engage the instant they detect living creatures.

> **Warden Skeletons (2):** AC 7, HD 1, hp 6/5, #AT 1, Dmg 1d6 (short swords, belted).

---

**24. THE RECKONING HALL**

*60 x 40 ft. The largest space in the dungeon. Ceiling vaulted at 18 ft. at its peak. Alchemical lighting from twelve sconces, giving the room a quality of false daylight. Every surface — floor, walls, ceiling — is carved.*

This was Morthek's ritual space. The carvings here are the apex of his geometric system — the pattern on the floor is a single continuous line, beginning at the south entrance, spiraling inward across the entire floor to a raised dais in the center. The dais is circular, 10 feet in diameter, 2 feet high. On the dais: a stone chair, fixed, facing south. Chains are attached to the chair's arms — loose now.

**The Wight** stands on the dais. He is in Morthek's captain's uniform, preserved by proximity to the ritual space. His name was Harven (see the duty roster in Area 7). He was interred here as a centerpiece — Morthek's statement about the possibilities of his research, and also a final safeguard.

He is territorial. His assigned space is the Reckoning Hall and the immediately adjacent corridors. He does not pursue to Level One.

He is aware. Not intelligent in the way a living person is intelligent, but aware — he recognizes that living creatures are present, that they are in Morthek's space, and that his purpose is to prevent that. If the binding carvings in Level Two are substantially intact, he moves at a measured pace, preferring to drive intruders back rather than attack immediately. If the carvings have been damaged, he is more aggressive.

**Bypassing the Wight:** He can be moved past at speed if the party is quiet and fast — he is not quick, and the Hall is large. If players approach openly he will engage. If players run across the Hall they may reach the corridor to Area 21 before he can prevent it. He will not pursue beyond the corridor.

**Interacting with the Wight:** He cannot communicate. The thing that was Harven is too far gone. He responds to Morthek's mark (the shield from Area 7 or the signet from the treasury) with a momentary pause — recognition, not deference.

**Treasure on the Wight:** His scabbard is worked leather with silver fittings, worth 25 gp. He carries a long sword +1, though there is no convenient moment to discover this. Players who defeat him will find it.

> **Wight (1) — Harven:** AC 5, HD 4+3, hp 26, #AT 1, Dmg 1d4 + energy drain (1 level on hit, save vs. death magic negates), MV 12, Immune to sleep, charm, cold, and all non-silver or non-magical weapons. Cannot be turned by a cleric of under 4th level. If destroyed, reforms in 24 hours unless the binding carvings are fully destroyed or the body is removed from the vault and burned.

**The Hall's exits:**
- South: the Approach Corridor (Area 16)
- East: the corridor to the treasury wing (Area 21)
- West: a passage to Areas 17, 18, 19 (laboratory and specimen chamber)
- North: Morthek's private passage to Area 26

---

**25. THE TREASURY**

*25 x 25 ft. Iron door, 3 inches thick, hinged to open inward. The lock is the Counting Lock. No other entrance.*

**The Counting Lock:** The door's face has a recessed panel containing eight symbols in Morthek's notation, each a pressure-point that depresses slightly when pushed. The correct sequence (four of the eight, in order) is derived from the key and the sequence found in Area 18. If the players have both, they can open the door in 1 round. If they are guessing, there are 1,680 possible sequences — tell them this if they ask how many combinations there are.

**Forcing the door:** Can be done. Requires: 18+ Strength (or multiple characters cooperating), 3 rounds of effort, and generates sustained metallic noise audible throughout Level Two and up the Throat into Level One. The Wight will immediately begin moving toward the sound if he is not already defeated.

**Treasury contents:**

The treasury was partially cleared by Morthek's lieutenants, but they could not open the counting lock — they took what was accessible in the outer rooms before sealing the vault. What remains is what Morthek personally placed here:

- **Coin:** 340 gp in old mint, 520 sp, 80 cp (in four iron strongboxes, each manageable by one person but heavy)
- **Gem lot:** 14 gems in a velvet case — garnets (×5, 10 gp each), a sapphire (value 200 gp), three pieces of amber with inclusions (30 gp each), two bloodstones (25 gp each), three pieces of polished onyx (15 gp each)
- **Objects of value:**
  - A silver dinner service, partial (6 of 12 pieces remain) — 120 gp as silver weight, more to a collector
  - Three small sculptures in alabaster — abstract, Morthek's aesthetic, 40 gp each
  - A painted miniature portrait in a silver frame, the subject the same woman as in the letter from Area 5 (Suelle). The frame is 30 gp. The miniature has no monetary value unless Morthek is somehow in a position to assign it one.
  - A locked mahogany case containing a wand — 9 charges, function unknown without identification. It is cold to the touch and smells faintly of copper. (Referee determination: the wand casts Cause Fear in a 10-ft. radius, save vs. paralysis negates, 1 round duration per charge. Morthek used it on his soldiers during training exercises. He considered this instructive.)
  - Morthek's signet ring — heavy gold, the eye mark, worth 60 gp as jewelry, recognized by the Wight as described in Area 24

**Weight considerations:** The coin alone is approximately 90 lbs. The full treasury contents run to 150+ lbs. Players cannot carry it all in one trip without planning. The dwarves will notice if the party leaves with heavily laden packs and less than full party strength.

---

**26. MORTHEK'S PRIVATE QUARTERS**

*Accessed via a narrow passage from the north side of the Reckoning Hall (Area 24). 30 x 20 ft. The passage door is closed, unlocked, and fits so precisely in its frame that it is not visible as a door from the Hall side unless specifically searched for.*

*The alchemical lighting here is brighter than elsewhere — more sconces per square foot. Everything is visible. There is no place for shadow.*

The room is spare: a stone sleeping platform with a decayed mattress, a writing desk (the one from Area 18's description is in the laboratory — this is a different, personal desk), a standing wardrobe in carved stone, a small circular table with two chairs.

In the stone wardrobe: two sets of robes in heavy wool, decayed but identifiable as formal garments, still bearing embroidery in silver thread (the thread retains value — 15 gp if carefully removed). On a stone shelf in the wardrobe: a metal coffer, small, sealed with a simple latch (not locked). Inside: a collection of personal effects — a lock of dark hair bound with a ribbon, three small sketches on vellum of a woman (Suelle, recognizable if the party has found other images), and a correspondence, twelve letters in and eight letters out, between Morthek and a woman in a city two weeks' travel distant, covering a period of seven years. The letters end. There is no indication why.

**Morthek** is in this room. He is seated in one of the chairs at the circular table, facing the passage entrance, in a state of absolute stillness. He is dressed in formal robes, identical to those in the wardrobe but worn. His hands are on the table. His eyes are open and opaque.

He is aware of the party as soon as they enter. He will speak within 1 round — his voice is dry, cadenced, slow, precise.

**Interacting with Morthek:**

He is not immediately hostile. His preservation ritual gives him periods of coherence interspersed with intervals of instinct-driven aggression. Players have approximately 3 questions (or equivalent conversational exchanges) before the instinct cycle reasserts.

*He will answer truthfully:*
- The location of the treasury and its lock (though not the combination — he no longer remembers it exactly, which frustrates him)
- The nature of the Wight (he calls Harven by name)
- What happened to him (he will describe his assassination clinically, with no apparent affect)
- What his research produced (the Zombie Soldiers, the Warden Skeletons, the preservation process — he considers all of these successes)

*He will lie about:*
- The contents of the treasury (he will describe it as mostly cleared, minimizing its value — old habit)
- The False Vault (he will deny its existence if asked about a second vault door)
- His current state (he will describe himself as functioning normally, which he believes)

*He cannot answer:*
- The combination to the treasury (genuinely forgotten)
- How long he has been here (time is not coherent for him)
- What happened to Suelle (the name causes him to go still in a different way, and the instinct cycle accelerates)

**When the coherence ends:** He rises from the chair. The transition from stillness to aggression takes 1 round — players who are watching will have warning.

> **Morthek (Mummy, modified):** AC 3, HD 6+4, hp 34, #AT 1, Dmg 1d12 + mummy rot (save vs. poison or contract rot — lose 1 point STR per day, no natural healing possible until Cure Disease), MV 6. Immune to sleep, charm, cold, and normal weapons. Fire deals double damage. *Modified:* he retains the ability to speak and a limited intelligence but this does not affect his combat behavior once the instinct cycle reasserts.

**After Morthek is destroyed:** Nothing notable occurs. The alchemical lights continue to burn. His body is fully combustible if the party has fire — doing so eliminates any possibility of his return. A cleric who examines his body can determine that the preservation ritual was genuine research, not corrupted by malice alone, and that the results were not what Morthek intended.

### Appendix C: Dungeon Ecology Notes

**The undead do not maintain the dungeon.** They do not repair, clean, or organize. Two centuries of slow entropy are visible in the garrison works in particular. The vault level is better preserved only because Morthek's alchemical systems continue to function and because nothing organic has been present to accelerate decay.

**The skeletons follow fixed programs.** They patrol routes, guard assigned spaces, and do not communicate with each other in any functional sense. A clever party can map their routines and exploit gaps.

**Morthek is the only thinking entity in the vault.** Everything else is operating on instinct or instruction. The ghoul (Area 9) is the only fully independent mind in the dungeon below Level One's surface, and he is not cooperative by nature.

**The dwarves are on a clock.** Bregga will not stay more than two more days before attempting to extract her crew by the crawlspace regardless of outcome. If the party delays their descent, the dwarves may clear Level One of accessible valuables while they wait.
