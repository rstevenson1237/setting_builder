# Genre

The answers to the genre questions in `patterns/Setting_Schema.md`, one per question, in its order.

- **Reference** - 1981 Basic/Expert Dungeons & Dragons, and the Expert-set wilderness module
  of a lost island: a hex-crawl of jungle, swamp and hill reached by sea, with tribes on its
  shores, dinosaurs and cave men in its interior, and an ancient evil in a stepped pyramid at
  the end of it. Play goes out from a friendly village and back to it, and the island is
  explored a day's march at a time.
- **Lethality** - High at the start and easing with experience: a first-level character
  falls to one or two blows, poison is save or die, and a dinosaur kills what it catches.
  Disease, the swamp and the sea kill as surely as any monster. Death is permanent at any
  level a party begins at.
- **Magic level** - Present but scarce. A few characters cast, each holding a spell or two
  a day that is gone once spoken until they rest. Magic items are found, never bought; most
  finds are a potion, a scroll or a weapon a little better than it should be, and anything
  greater is rare and remembered. The tribes' shamans and the snake people's priests work
  the island's only other magic, and neither sells it.
- **Authority** - Local and divided: each village answers to its own chief and elders, the
  two shores' villages to no common power, and no law reaches the interior, the swamps or
  the sea. A party is a guest where it is welcome and on its own everywhere else.
- **Allegiance** - No side is good except by what it does. The two tribes each want the
  other's land and the party's trade; the lizard men want back what was taken from them;
  the mer people want the pirates gone; the pirates and the treasure hunters want the gold.
  Only the snake people's cult is plainly evil. A stranger's side is read from the tongue
  they speak, the marks they wear, and what they do on meeting - never from a label.
- **Population density** - Settled only at the shores: villages a few miles apart along the
  north and south coasts, the swamp enclaves and the sea caves each a day from the nearest
  village, and between them days of jungle and hill with nothing but lairs and the beasts.
- **Decay** - Long past. The snake people's empire is fallen to a pyramid and the overgrown
  ruins of its towns, and those who threw it down live in villages on its edge. What was
  built is ruined and held by what came after; what is alive is new and growing over it.
- **Player characters** - Adventurers of the seven classes - fighter, cleric, magic-user,
  thief, dwarf, elf, halfling - come by ship with a partial map and the tale of a treasure,
  landed at the southern villages with what they carry. The ship sails home on a day set at
  landing. Nobody else will come for them.
- **Reward** - Treasure, carried back to the ship before it sails: the reef's wrecks, the
  pyramid's gold and what the lairs hold. A map of the island, guides and standing with the
  villages are worth as much as gold, and magic items are the rarer prize.
- **Naming** - Plain and descriptive, in the common tongue, for what the party names: the
  wreck coast, the hill jungle, the pirates' cove. Coined names, drawn from the tongues seeded
  in `setting/Language.md`, belong to the island's peoples - their villages, chiefs and
  gods - to the snake people's ruins, and to what a creature calls itself.
