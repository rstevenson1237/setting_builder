Truths of Khirengaard

- A boundary-stone does not ward against what lies past it; it only marks where a different law applies. Nothing stops a person from stepping across - only knowing which law now governs them.
- The wold keeps no count of days. A party that crosses a stone line and returns may find an hour gone, or a season, and never the one they expected.
- Boundary-cutters do not fell trees for lumber; their trade is reading which trees, stones, and deadfall still belong to the fringe and which have quietly become wold. A cutter's blessing is a survey, not a blessing in the priestly sense - though most in Khirengaard no longer make the distinction.
- Iron taken from inside the stone line and worked by a fringe smith holds an edge no fringe ore does, but it also draws the wold's notice - such a blade is prized and never carried lightly.
- The Marrgath left behind no graves. Whatever became of them, the wold does not surrender bodies, only the buildings the living left standing.
- Game animals native to the fringe will not cross a stone line in either direction, living or fleeing. A hunted beast that vanishes past the stones is gone for good, and a wolf-pack that dens against the line will never be found on the wold's side of it.
