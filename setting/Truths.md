Truths of The Ashwrack Coast

- No hold recognizes a king or council; the only authority anyone honors is whoever currently keeps a bronze diving-bell salvaged from a wreck, since ringing it underwater is believed to be the one sound the sea will answer.
- Widow-glass - corked bottles that periodically wash ashore, each sealed with a single voice trapped inside it. They can be broken open to hear a fragment of speech from someone long drowned, and the coastal holds trade them, hoard them, and interrogate them for buried secrets, never fully trusting what they say.
- Iron is treated as a debt, not a possession. Every smith's ledger records not who owns a blade but who still owes the sea for the ore it was pulled from, and a debt left unpaid too long is said to draw the tide's attention to its bearer.
- The coastal holds keep no calendar of years; time is measured in "tallies" - the count of days since a person's or a hold's last bargain with the deep - and a tally reaching triple digits is treated as an omen that a reckoning is due.
