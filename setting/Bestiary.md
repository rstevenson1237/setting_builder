Bestiary of Telar

Steppe Lion (Beast) - AD: 4d6 [MA: 5]
Description: A pale, heavy-maned cat that hunts the open ground on either side of the road,
built for a single fast rush rather than a chase.
Range: One per stretch of open country per region; a mated pair holds a kill-ground and
drives off any third. Eats grazing herd animals, and anything smaller that stands still too
long.
Sign: Flattened grass at a kill-ground, a half-eaten carcass dragged into cover, a single
low cough carrying across open ground at dusk.
Disposition: Watches from cover before committing. A party that keeps moving and stays in a
group is rarely tested; one straggler is.

Sand Viper (Beast) - AD: 1d6
Description: Thin, sand-colored, and coiled tight enough to vanish in a footprint's worth of
shade.
Range: Common wherever the ground is dry and open; the country supports as many as there
are places to hide from the sun. Eats anything small enough to strike and wait out.
Sign: A trail in soft sand ending at nothing visible; a dead rodent left where it fell.
Disposition: Strikes only when stepped on or reached for. It gives no warning display - the
first sign is the bite.

Carrion Vulture (Beast) - AD: 1d6
Description: A bald-headed scavenger bird that circles anything large enough to be worth
waiting for.
Range: Widespread; a dozen or more will gather over a single kill within a day. Eats
carrion exclusively - it kills nothing itself.
Sign: A slow circling shape overhead, visible for miles - the most common way a party learns
something died nearby before they find it.
Disposition: Indifferent to anything alive. A flock lifting off ahead of the party is a sign
something else got there first.

Ford-Eel (Beast) - AD: 5d6
Description: A river predator as long as a person is tall, more jaw than body, that lies
motionless in slack water until something crosses above it.
Range: One or two per crossing on a major river; the country supports few, because each
needs a whole ford's worth of prey to itself. Eats livestock, fish, and anyone who wades
rather than uses a bridge.
Sign: Livestock that goes missing only at the water's edge; a disturbance in still water
with nothing above the surface to explain it.
Disposition: Strikes only what enters the water. It will not follow prey onto dry land, and
a party that knows this can cross by a longer, drier route for free.

Wild Aurochs (Beast) - AD: 3d6
Description: A black-horned herd ox, taller at the shoulder than a person, that grazes the
verges of the trackless country in small herds.
Range: Moves in herds of six to fifteen across open ground; the country supports several
herds per region. Eats grass and low scrub, nothing else.
Sign: Cropped ground, a trampled wallow, dung fresh enough to still be steaming.
Disposition: Will not fight if it can run, and would rather run than posture. Cornering one,
or startling a herd toward a party, is the only way this becomes a problem.

Locust Swarm (Beast) - AD: 1d6
Description: Not one creature but a moving weight of them, visible from a distance as a
brown haze low over the ground.
Range: Seasonal and unpredictable; where it lands, it strips the ground bare within a day
and moves on. Eats standing crops, and nothing else it cannot also eat.
Sign: A darkening sky in daylight; a sound like rain with no rain in it.
Disposition: Cannot be fought in any useful sense and takes no interest in anyone not made
of crop. The problem it creates is entirely what it leaves behind - a settlement's granary,
emptied for the season, with nothing anyone can point a blade at.

Dune Scorpion (Beast) - AD: 2d6
Description: A hand-length scorpion, pale as the sand it hides in, with a sting that swells
badly rather than kills outright.
Range: Common under stones and dead wood anywhere dry; the country supports as many as
there is cover for. Eats insects and anything smaller than itself.
Sign: A trail like a narrow rake dragged through sand, ending under the nearest flat stone.
Disposition: Defensive only - it stings what disturbs its cover and otherwise stays hidden
through anything short of that.

Grey Wolf Pack (Beast) - AD: 2d6 [MA: 5]
Description: Lean, pale-eyed pack hunters that run down anything slower than themselves
across open or broken ground.
Range: One pack of four to eight per region, ranging the whole of it rather than holding
one ground; the same pack can be met more than once in a single traverse. Eats deer, wild
aurochs calves, and livestock left unwatched.
Sign: Tracks crossing a trail in single file; a distant, answered howl at dusk.
Disposition: Tests before committing - a pack will shadow a party for a stretch, gauging
numbers, before deciding whether to press or peel off.

Border Reaver (Man) - AD: 2d6 [MA: 5]
Description: A mounted raider in mismatched, functional gear, riding light and fast and
carrying more weapons than armor.
Range: Bands of six to twenty, holding no fixed ground and answering to no crown; several
bands work the road at any time. Takes what a caravan or an undefended crossing carries.
Sign: Hoofprints cutting straight across country rather than following the road; a burned
wagon left as a warning to the next one.
Disposition: Will parley for a toll before fighting for it - a reaver band wants the goods,
not a fight it might lose.

Garrison Soldier (Man) - AD: 2d6
Description: A conscript or hired regular in a border kingdom's colors, drilled enough to
hold a line and no further.
Range: Companies of ten to thirty per garrison point; one such point per region holding a
crossing or a wall. Draws pay and rations from whichever of the three kingdoms still funds
that garrison.
Sign: A watch-fire kept lit past dawn out of habit; boot-worn ground in a pattern too
regular to be foot traffic.
Disposition: Follows orders first and judgment second. A garrison soldier stopped alone,
off duty, is a different conversation than the same soldier on the wall.

Serpent Cult Initiate (Man) - AD: 1d6
Description: An ordinary person by day, marked underneath by a tattoo or a scar in the
shape of a coiled serpent that plain clothing hides easily.
Range: Cells of three to seven, hidden inside otherwise ordinary settlements; the country
supports as many cells as have gone undiscovered. Draws on whatever trade or office its
members already hold.
Sign: A shared mark, glimpsed and then covered; a person who reacts wrong to an ordinary
question about serpents.
Disposition: Will not act against the party without cover or numbers, and denies everything
if confronted alone.

Mercenary Sellsword (Man) - AD: 3d6
Description: A professional fighter under contract to whoever is currently paying, marked
by better gear than a garrison soldier and less loyalty than either side would like.
Range: Companies of five to twelve, moving between whichever of the three kingdoms is
hiring this season. Paid in coin, and gone the day it stops.
Sign: Gear that doesn't match any one kingdom's colors; a contract mentioned before a name
is offered.
Disposition: Will always talk before it fights - a sellsword's whole trade depends on
knowing which jobs are worth dying for, and this usually isn't one.

Man-Ape (Humanoid) - AD: 4d6 [MA: 4]
Description: Taller and heavier than a person, with a gait that reads as wrong before it
reads as dangerous - neither fully upright nor fully not.
Range: Bands of eight to fifteen holding a stretch of country humans do not settle; nobody
has counted how many bands exist. Eats what the country provides, unprocessed.
Sign: Knuckle-prints beside a footprint; bark stripped from a tree at head height, well
above where a person would reach.
Disposition: Will not strike first at anyone wearing worked iron above the elbow, per the
truce nobody now remembers the terms of - and reacts to its absence exactly as fast as
anything else that size would.

Nirguk, the Serpent-kin (Humanoid) - AD: 6d6
Description: Serpent-descended and scaled rather than skinned, speaking Essath fluently and
older tongues besides; Nirguk means "serpent-lords" in the dead tongue that names them.
Range: Small enclaves - a dozen or fewer - each holding one hidden place and never
leaving it undefended. Eats little, and rarely in front of anyone.
Sign: A shed skin, unnaturally long, left somewhere dry; a smell like cold stone that has
never seen sun.
Disposition: Territorial around whatever their enclave guards specifically, and otherwise
indifferent to anyone who has not gone looking for it.

Barrow-Kin (Humanoid) - AD: 3d6
Description: A pale, silent people who live among the burial mounds and standing stones,
tending grave-ground nobody asked them to tend.
Range: Small kin-groups, one per mound-field, that do not travel far from the ground they
keep. Eats what can be gathered near the mounds; takes nothing from a grave itself.
Sign: Offerings left at a mound that nobody living placed there; a path kept clear of
brush that the surrounding ground has otherwise reclaimed.
Disposition: Will not approach first, and does not explain itself if it does. Digging into a
mound they tend is the one act that reliably ends the silence.

Bound Dead (Undead) - AD: 3d6
Description: A desiccated body still upright, moving to a purpose it was given rather than
one it holds - Essath sorcery outlasting whoever cast it.
Range: Stationed singly or in small groups wherever an Essath binding still holds - a
ziggurat threshold, a sealed door. Eats nothing; it does not hunger.
Sign: A held position that never varies, even when unwatched; dust undisturbed in every
direction except the one it faces.
Disposition: Acts only against whatever its binding names - an intruder, a specific
threshold crossed, a thing removed. It is not hostile to anything the binding did not
anticipate.

Drowned Thing (Undead) - AD: 5d6
Description: What is left of someone who went into deep water and did not come back out
whole - bloated, patient, and slower to notice than to act once it has.
Range: One or two per flooded or submerged ruin; the drowned temple downriver holds at
least two, by what account survives of the companies lost there. Eats nothing living, but
pulls it under anyway.
Sign: Still water where a current should show; a smell of standing water somewhere with no
water in sight.
Disposition: Waits below the surface and does not pursue onto dry ground. It has never once
been reported to leave the water it drowned in.

Barrow Wight (Undead) - AD: 8d6+1
Description: What a mound's dead becomes when the mound itself is disturbed wrong - cold,
deliberate, and aware in a way the Bound Dead are not.
Range: One per disturbed mound, never more; it does not range beyond the mound it rose
in. Eats nothing, but leaves whatever it touches cold long after.
Sign: A mound with fresh disturbance and no digger's tools left behind; frost on a warm
morning in one place only.
Disposition: Wakes only when its mound is opened, and does not stop for anything short of
the opening being sealed again or the wight destroyed. This is not a fight to walk into
unprepared, and its Bestiary entry exists specifically so a referee can warn a party in
time.

Stone Ward (Construct) - AD: 9d6+1
Description: A statue in the shape of whatever it was carved to resemble, until it isn't
one anymore - Essath craft, not sorcery cast fresh, and it does not tire.
Range: One per site worth guarding permanently; never encountered away from what it was
made to protect. Eats nothing and needs nothing.
Sign: A statue with no dust on it in a room thick with dust everywhere else; tool-marks on
the floor where it has pivoted, over and over, to face the same door.
Disposition: Inert until its stated trigger - a threshold crossed, an object touched, a
word spoken. It gives no warning between inert and acting.

The Pit-Thing (Horror) - AD: 18d6+2
Description: Nothing about it resolves into a shape a person can describe twice the same
way; it is the thing the Pit of the Old Ones was sealed to keep in, not out.
Range: One. It does not travel, forage, or reproduce - it is a fact of one specific place,
and removing that fact is not this setting's business to offer.
Sign: Everything the Bound Dead, the Barrow Wight, and Nirguk's enclave all separately treat
as unthinkable to approach is treating the same one place that way.
Disposition: This entry is not meant to be beaten. Its value is entirely in what a party
does before ever meeting it - what they learn about the pit, and what they choose to leave
sealed.
