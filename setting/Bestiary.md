Bestiary of Fellward

Treeline Hounds (Beast) - AD: 1d6 [MA: 5]
Description: Lean, big-eared dogs gone wild generations back, running in shifting packs of
four to a dozen. Bay before they close, never after.
Range: Ranges the whole borderland side of the treeline rather than denning in one place -
a party can cross the same pack twice in a region and never find where it sleeps. Eats
anything smaller than itself, including each other's kills.
Sign: Fresh scat on the trail, a dead rabbit half-eaten and abandoned, distant baying that
does not get closer for an hour and then suddenly does.
Disposition: Circles and bays at a range rather than charging; breaks off entirely if met
by more than three armed people standing together.

Bramble-Back Boar (Beast) - AD: 3d6
Description: A heavy-shouldered boar whose back has grown a permanent mat of thorn and
bramble from years of wallowing in the same patch. Digs with real intent.
Range: Lives close enough to the keep's kitchen garden and grain stores to root through
them by night at least once a season - a standing grievance nobody has solved by killing
individual boars, since another always moves into the same wallow.
Sign: Gardens rooted up in a single night, a wallow packed with old bramble and boar hair,
a trail of overturned earth.
Disposition: Ignores anything that keeps its distance; charges anything that gets between
it and its wallow.

Ashmoth Swarm (Beast) - AD: 1d6
Description: Pale, dry-winged moths that rise in a cloud thick enough to blot out a lantern
when disturbed. Individually harmless; the cloud is not.
Range: Nests in dry thatch, old grain sacks, and abandoned rooms - anywhere still and dark
enough. Feeds on stored grain and cloth.
Sign: A patch of thatch or sacking gone pale and powdery, a smell like old dust, a single
moth loose in a lit room at the wrong hour.
Disposition: Rises as one cloud the instant its nest is disturbed, blinding and choking
anyone caught in it for as long as they stay; does no lasting harm on its own but panics
livestock and ruins whatever it nested in.

Longshank Wolf (Beast) - AD: 2d6 [MA: 5]
Description: Taller and leaner than a Treeline Hound, running alone or in mated pairs
rather than packs, built for distance rather than a quick kill.
Range: Ranges wide territories along the treeline's near edge, following game rather than
denning; the same pair can be met at either end of a region in the same season.
Sign: Long, evenly spaced tracks that don't waver, a kill stripped clean and left rather
than dragged off, a single distant howl answered by another far off.
Disposition: Watches from a distance before deciding whether to approach; will trail a
party for hours without attacking if there's easier game nearby.

Cave Adder (Beast) - AD: 1d6
Description: A thick-bodied, sluggish snake, patterned to disappear against rock, that
almost never moves unless something gets close to its clutch.
Range: Lairs in a single rock crevice or cave mouth near water and does not leave it - the
clutch of eggs buried there is the only reason it's dangerous at all.
Sign: A faint musky smell near a crevice, a shed skin, disturbed gravel at a den mouth.
Disposition: Motionless and easy to miss until approached within a few feet of the clutch,
at which point it strikes without warning.

Barrow Bear (Beast) - AD: 6d6
Description: An old, scarred bear that has made its den in an older ruin rather than a
natural cave, and has grown used to defending walls as much as ground.
Range: One bear per old ruin or barrow large enough to den in; does not range beyond it
except to hunt.
Sign: Claw-marks scored into old stonework at bear height, a den entrance packed with old
bedding and bone, a smell of rot and fur.
Disposition: Ignores anything that stays outside its den; treats anyone who enters it as an
intruder in its home, not as prey to be chased down elsewhere.

Quarry Lynx (Beast) - AD: 3d6
Description: A solitary, rangy cat that hunts by patience rather than speed, favoring high
ground and rockfall where it can watch a trail for hours.
Range: Holds a single stretch of high or broken ground as a hunting range, jealously kept
against other lynxes but not against people who don't linger.
Sign: A half-eaten kill dragged up onto rocks, claw-scored bark at head height, a low growl
from cover that stops the moment it's located.
Disposition: Stalks anything that lingers on its ground for more than a short while;
otherwise lets travelers pass without being seen at all.

Fen Auroch (Beast) - AD: 4d6
Description: A massive, dark-coated grazing beast that travels in small family groups
through the low wet ground near the treeline, placid until it isn't.
Range: Follows the wet grazing ground in a loose seasonal circuit; a herd's range overlaps
several locations across a region.
Sign: Flattened grass wide enough for a wagon, deep hoofprints filling with water, a low
territorial bellow carrying a long way.
Disposition: Grazes and ignores distant observers; a lone bull will charge anything that
approaches a calf or crowds the herd's path.

Deserter Band (Man) - AD: 2d6
Description: Former garrison soldiers who slipped off the roster rather than starve on it,
still carrying keep-issue gear gone unmaintained.
Range: Moves in small bands of four to eight along the supply road and its verges, living
off what they can take from travelers and, occasionally, the keep's own stores.
Sign: A cold camp with keep-pattern gear left behind, a supply cart stopped and looted
rather than burned, boot-prints in keep-issue tread.
Disposition: Would rather talk their way to a toll than fight for it, especially from
anyone who might recognize old faces; turns violent only if refused and outnumbering.

Road Toll Gang (Man) - AD: 4d6
Description: An organized band, never garrison-trained, that has simply decided the supply
road is theirs to tax and has the numbers to make it stick for now.
Range: Holds a stretch of road or a crossing point rather than ranging, moving on only when
pressed hard enough to make it not worth the trouble.
Sign: A felled tree or cart blocking a narrow stretch, a fresh-cut waymark demanding a toll,
recent hoofprints converging from several directions at once.
Disposition: States its price plainly before anything else happens; fights in earnest only
once payment is refused and they've committed to blocking the way.

Charter Agents (Man) - AD: 1d6
Description: One or two crown clerks and their guard, sent to inspect a holding's books
against its charter - thin, tired, and entirely unequipped for anything past the road.
Range: Travels the supply road between chartered holdings on a schedule nobody at Fellward
has seen kept in years.
Sign: A well-kept ledger case, a horse better fed than the garrison's, questions asked about
numbers that don't add up.
Disposition: Wants to talk, ask questions, and leave with answers written down; will not
fight and has no idea what to do if someone insists.

Old Tongue Grave-Robbers (Man) - AD: 5d6
Description: A disciplined, well-equipped crew who've made a trade of breaking into the old
foundations and barrows for whatever the builders left behind, and know what they're doing.
Range: Works a single dig or barrow at a time until it's exhausted, then moves to the next
one they've scouted.
Sign: A fresh-cut entrance shored with new timber, tools and rope left staged at a barrow
mouth, old-tongue fragments discarded as worthless.
Disposition: Avoids a fight it doesn't need, but defends an active dig and its haul with
real, coordinated force.

Barrow-Kin (Humanoid) - AD: 4d6
Description: Small family holds living in and around the older ruins beyond the treeline,
keeping customs about the dead that predate the keep and answering to no one but their own
eldest.
Range: Holds a scatter of family sites near the old foundations; does not raid or migrate,
only defends what's theirs.
Sign: A boundary marked in old-tongue-carved stone, a cleared and tended grave far older
than it should be, smoke from a hearth that isn't on any keep map.
Disposition: Avoids contact by default; will parley if approached openly and without
weapons drawn, but treats any disturbance of a grave-site as an act that ends the
conversation.

Trapper Clans (Humanoid) - AD: 2d6
Description: Small, self-sufficient groups who work traplines through the safer margins of
the wilds, moving lightly and knowing which ground to avoid.
Range: Works a network of traplines across a region, never settling in one place long
enough to be found twice in the same season.
Sign: A trap sprung and reset, a skinned carcass left for scavengers, a trail marker cut
into bark in no pattern the keep recognizes.
Disposition: Will trade what they know about safe ground for coin, salt, or iron; treats
anyone who damages a trapline as having declared themselves an enemy of it.

Threshold-Marked (Humanoid) - AD: 8d6
Description: People who went into the treeline and came back changed - skin, eyes, or
manner wrong in some way that never fully heals - and who are no longer welcome on either
side of the wire.
Range: Drifts along the treeline's edge in ones and twos, rarely more than a handful
together; belongs nowhere long.
Sign: Tracks that start human and don't stay that way, a camp abandoned mid-meal, a smell
that doesn't match anything that made it.
Disposition: Avoids people when it can; when cornered or approached too closely, its
reaction is unpredictable and can turn violent with no warning either side can read.

Wall-Bound Dead (Undead) - AD: 1d6
Description: The restless dead of anyone buried facing the wrong way - toward the keep
instead of the treeline - bound close to their own grave and little more than a cold
presence given shape.
Range: Fixed to the graveyard or grave-site where the burial happened; cannot travel beyond
sight of it.
Sign: A grave with disturbed, frost-killed grass regardless of season, an unexplained chill
that starts at a specific spot, animals refusing to cross the same ground.
Disposition: Does not attack and mostly cannot - but nothing will graze, sleep, or store
food well within its reach, and it is a standing reason the graveyard's layout matters.

Barrow Wight (Undead) - AD: 9d6
Description: What's left of someone the old foundations' builders buried with real
ceremony, still bound to guard whatever chamber holds them, long after anyone alive
remembers what that was.
Range: Bound to a single barrow or vault; cannot leave it under any circumstance.
Sign: Cold air moving the wrong way from a sealed passage, old-tongue warding marks still
intact and unbroken, a silence that swallows sound past a certain point in the ruin.
Disposition: Ignores anyone who does not breach the chamber it guards; anyone who does gets
no warning before it acts.

The Unrelieved (Undead) - AD: 3d6
Description: The shape of an officer, still in keep colors, who died waiting for orders or
relief that never came and has kept the watch since without noticing it's over.
Range: Walks a fixed patrol route between a watchtower and the keep gate, on a schedule
nothing living now keeps.
Sign: A watch-bell rung with no one at the rope, a patrol route worn into ground nobody
walks anymore, cold breath answering a challenge no one gave.
Disposition: Will exchange a password, a report, or a token exactly as the old watch
required, and reacts with real distress - not violence - to anyone who tells it what year it
is or that the relief isn't coming.

Old-Ward Sentinel (Construct) - AD: 6d6
Description: A jointed shape of the old foundation-stone, made rather than born, still
following whatever standing order the builders left it with.
Range: Fixed to a single passage, gate, or chamber in the old ruins; does not patrol beyond
its post.
Sign: Joint-seams in stonework that shouldn't be able to move, floor-stone worn smooth in a
pattern that implies pacing, a low grinding sound with no visible source.
Disposition: Inert and easy to mistake for statuary until its post is crossed against
whatever rule it still enforces, at which point it acts without hesitation and without
malice.

The Long Reach (Horror) - AD: 14d6+4
Description: Nothing at Fellward has seen the whole of it and lived to compare notes -
only ever a wrongness in the deep treeline, a sense of being reached for from further away
than anything should reach.
Range: Somewhere in the deep treeline, far past anywhere the keep's watch has ever mapped;
does not range toward the keep so much as the keep's people range toward it.
Sign: Trees fallen in a pattern that isn't wind, absolute silence where the wilds are never
silent, tracks that stop mid-stride with nothing after them.
Disposition: Not a fight - it does not chase, posture, or negotiate, and nothing the party
can bring to bear is written to stop it; the only working answer to it is not being where it
reaches.
