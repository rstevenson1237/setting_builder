Bestiary of Telar

Steppe Lion (Beast) - AD: 4d6+3 [MA: 1]
Description: A pale, heavy-maned cat that hunts the open ground on either side of the road,
built for a single fast rush rather than a chase.
Range: One per stretch of open country per region; a mated pair holds a kill-ground and
drives off any third. Eats grazing herd animals, and anything smaller that stands still too
long.
Sign: Flattened grass at a kill-ground, a half-eaten carcass dragged into cover, a single
low cough carrying across open ground at dusk.
Disposition: Watches from cover before committing. A party that keeps moving and stays in a
group is rarely tested; one straggler is.
Special: none. Its whole danger is in the first exchange - the modifier is above its size
because a rush that connects takes someone off their feet, and the MA below it because it
has hold of exactly one person while it does.

Grey Wolf (Beast) - AD: 2d6+0 [MA: 1]
Description: Lean and pale-eyed, running down anything slower than itself across open or
broken ground.
Range: Packs of four to eight, one pack per region, ranging the whole of it rather than
holding one ground - the same pack can be met more than once in a single traverse. Eats
deer, calves, and livestock left unwatched.
Sign: Tracks crossing a trail in single file; a distant, answered howl at dusk.
Disposition: Tests before committing - a pack will shadow a party for a stretch, gauging
numbers, before deciding whether to press or peel off.
Special: none. The flat modifier is the entry's point: one wolf is less than it looks, and
everything dangerous about wolves is in the Range line instead.

Ford-Eel (Beast) - AD: 5d6+4 [MA: 1]
Description: A river predator as long as a person is tall, more jaw than body, that lies
motionless in slack water until something crosses above it.
Range: One or two per crossing on a major river; the country supports few, because each
needs a whole ford's worth of prey to itself. Eats livestock, fish, and anyone who wades
rather than uses a bridge.
Sign: Livestock that goes missing only at the water's edge; a disturbance in still water
with nothing above the surface to explain it.
Disposition: Strikes only what enters the water. It will not follow prey onto dry land, and
a party that knows this can cross by a longer, drier route for free.
Special: Takes hold and rolls, putting whoever it has under the water rather than fighting
them on the surface - the fight becomes a drowning, and anyone going in after them enters
the water too.

Locust Swarm (Beast) - AD: 1d6-2 [MA: 4]
Description: Not one creature but a moving weight of them, visible from a distance as a
brown haze low over the ground.
Range: Seasonal and unpredictable; where it lands, it strips the ground bare within a day
and moves on. Eats standing crops, and nothing else it cannot also eat.
Sign: A darkening sky in daylight; a sound like rain with no rain in it.
Disposition: Cannot be fought in any useful sense and takes no interest in anyone not made
of crop.
Special: Strips a settlement's standing crop and stored grain in a day. The modifier is at
the bottom of the scale and the MA near the top for the same reason - it can reach everyone
present and hurt none of them, and the problem it creates is entirely what it leaves behind.

Border Reaver (Man) - AD: 2d6+2 [MA: 1]
Description: A mounted raider in mismatched, functional gear, riding light and fast and
carrying more weapons than armor.
Range: The rank and file Brekar fields, per `setting/Factions.md` - bands of six to twenty,
holding no fixed ground and answering to no crown. Takes what a caravan or an undefended
crossing carries.
Sign: Hoofprints cutting straight across country rather than following the road; a burned
wagon left as a warning to the next one.
Disposition: Will parley for a toll before fighting for it - a reaver band wants the goods,
not a fight it might lose.
Special: none.

Garrison Soldier (Man) - AD: 2d6+1 [MA: 1]
Description: A conscript or hired regular in a border kingdom's colors, drilled enough to
hold a line and no further.
Range: The rank and file all three crowns field - companies of ten to thirty per garrison
point, one such point per region holding a crossing or a wall. Draws pay and rations from
whichever of the three kingdoms still funds that garrison.
Sign: A watch-fire kept lit past dawn out of habit; boot-worn ground in a pattern too
regular to be foot traffic.
Disposition: Follows orders first and judgment second. A garrison soldier stopped alone,
off duty, is a different conversation than the same soldier on the wall.
Special: none.

Man-Ape (Humanoid) - AD: 4d6+3 [MA: 2]
Description: Taller and heavier than a person, with a gait that reads as wrong before it
reads as dangerous - neither fully upright nor fully not.
Range: Bands of eight to fifteen holding a stretch of country humans do not settle; nobody
has counted how many bands exist. Eats what the country provides, unprocessed.
Sign: Knuckle-prints beside a footprint; bark stripped from a tree at head height, well
above where a person would reach.
Disposition: Reacts to a party's approach exactly as fast as anything else that size would,
and does not posture first.
Special: Will not strike first at anyone wearing worked iron above the elbow, per
`setting/Truths.md` - the truce holds until the band is removed, lost, or seen through, and
it is owed to a person rather than to a party.

Nirguk (Humanoid) - AD: 6d6+2 [MA: 2]
Description: Serpent-descended and scaled rather than skinned, speaking Essath fluently and
older tongues besides; Nirguk is their own name for themselves, and means serpent-lords in
the dead tongue that names them.
Range: Small enclaves - a dozen or fewer - each holding one hidden place and never leaving
it undefended. Eats little, and rarely in front of anyone.
Sign: A shed skin, unnaturally long, left somewhere dry; a smell like cold stone that has
never seen sun.
Disposition: Territorial around whatever its enclave guards specifically, and otherwise
indifferent to anyone who has not gone looking for it.
Special: Reads and speaks Essath binding-script, so a seal, a ward or a waystone the party
cannot get past is something a Nirguk can simply walk through - and knows the cost of
spending a name, per `setting/Truths.md`, well enough never to spend one cheaply.

Barrow-Kin (Humanoid) - AD: 3d6+0 [MA: 1]
Description: A pale, silent people who live among the burial mounds and standing stones,
tending grave-ground nobody asked them to tend.
Range: Small kin-groups, one per mound-field, that do not travel far from the ground they
keep. Eats what can be gathered near the mounds; takes nothing from a grave itself.
Sign: Offerings left at a mound that nobody living placed there; a path kept clear of brush
that the surrounding ground has otherwise reclaimed.
Disposition: Will not approach first, and does not explain itself if it does. Digging into a
mound they tend is the one act that reliably ends the silence.
Special: Knows which mounds are safe to open and which are not, and will say so once, to
anyone who asks before digging rather than after.

Drowned Thing (Undead) - AD: 5d6+2 [MA: 1]
Description: What is left of someone who went into deep water and did not come back out
whole - bloated, patient, and slower to notice than to act once it has.
Range: One or two per flooded or submerged ruin; the drowned temple downriver holds at least
two, by what account survives of the companies lost there. Eats nothing living, but pulls it
under anyway.
Sign: Still water where a current should show; a smell of standing water somewhere with no
water in sight.
Disposition: Waits below the surface and does not pursue onto dry ground. It has never once
been reported to leave the water it drowned in.
Special: Whatever it takes under becomes another one. The companies lost fifteen years ago
are the reason there are two in the drowned temple rather than one.

Barrow Wight (Undead) - AD: 7d6+5 [MA: 1]
Description: What a mound's dead becomes when the mound itself is disturbed wrong - cold,
deliberate, and aware in a way a bound corpse is not.
Range: One per disturbed mound, never more; it does not range beyond the mound it rose in.
Eats nothing, but leaves whatever it touches cold long after.
Sign: A mound with fresh disturbance and no digger's tools left behind; frost on a warm
morning in one place only.
Disposition: Wakes only when its mound is opened, and does not stop for anything short of
the opening being sealed again or the wight destroyed.
Special: Its grip leaves a cold that does not lift with warmth, food or rest - only with the
mound being closed. It takes one person at a time and finishes with them, which is why the
MA sits below its size while the modifier sits well above it.

The Unlettered Dead (Undead) - AD: 1d6-1 [MA: 3]
Description: The nameless dead of the Essath rites, raised without a binding and without a
purpose - a body that walks because nothing in it was ever written down and told to stop.
Range: Wherever Essath buried in number rather than in ceremony: charnel pits, fill under a
floor, the ash of a cellar. Counted in dozens, never as individuals. Eats nothing.
Sign: Ash disturbed in a broad, aimless spread rather than a path; a sound underfoot in a
room with nobody else in it.
Disposition: Has no purpose and pursues none. It closes on whatever is warm and nearest and
keeps closing.
Special: none singly, which is the entry. One is a nuisance and the modifier says so; the
MA says how many of the party a dozen of them can be touching at once.

Bound Dead (Guardian) - AD: 3d6+2 [MA: 1]
Description: A desiccated body still upright, moving to a purpose it was given rather than
one it holds - Essath sorcery outlasting whoever cast it.
Range: Stationed singly or in small groups wherever an Essath binding still holds - a
ziggurat threshold, a sealed door. Eats nothing; it does not hunger.
Sign: A held position that never varies, even when unwatched; dust undisturbed in every
direction except the one it faces.
Disposition: Inert until its binding is met, and it does not warn. It is not hostile to
anything the binding did not anticipate.
Special: Acts only against whatever its binding names - an intruder, a specific threshold
crossed, a thing removed - and nothing else in the room will move it. A party that works out
what the binding names can stand in front of one and take everything else.

Stone Ward (Guardian) - AD: 9d6+5 [MA: 3]
Description: A statue in the shape of whatever it was carved to resemble, until it isn't one
anymore - Essath craft, not sorcery cast fresh, and it does not tire.
Range: One per site worth guarding permanently; never encountered away from what it was made
to protect. Eats nothing and needs nothing.
Sign: A statue with no dust on it in a room thick with dust everywhere else; tool-marks on
the floor where it has pivoted, over and over, to face the same door.
Disposition: Inert until its stated trigger - a threshold crossed, an object touched, a word
spoken. It gives no warning between inert and acting.
Special: Takes nothing from a blade it has not already taken from a hundred; only the
binding-script cut into its own base stops it, and reaching that means getting behind it.
Special: Returns to its post and to stillness the moment the thing it guards is put back,
whatever has happened in the meantime - a party that took and returned can walk out.

Threshold-Bane (Guardian) - AD: 5d6+2 [MA: 6]
Description: Not a body at all - an Essath working still sitting in a doorway, five hundred
years after the priest who spoke it stopped breathing. It is visible only as the line of
script along the lintel, and only while it is unspent.
Range: One per bound opening; the ziggurats hold several and the waystones are its outdoor
form. It does not move, feed or notice anything that does not cross it.
Sign: A lintel or a stone cut with unworn Essath script in a building worn everywhere else;
dust lying unbroken across a threshold that clearly gets used from one side.
Disposition: Does nothing whatever until somebody crosses without the word the binding wants,
and then does all of it at once.
Special: Strikes everyone across the threshold in the same instant, which is why the MA is at
the top of the scale on a middling count - a party that files through one at a time loses one
person, and a party that rushes it loses all of them.
Special: Spent once it fires, per `setting/Truths.md` - the crossing is then simply a
doorway, and the only party that gets the benefit of that is the one following the first.

Ash-Choke (Hazard) - AD: 2d6+0 [MA: 5]
Description: The fine grey ash of the Essath rites, packed into the lower chambers by
centuries and still loose underneath - it lifts at a footfall and does not settle for as long
as anyone is moving.
Range: Any chamber where the rites burned in quantity; thickest at the bottom of a ziggurat
and in any cellar that has not been opened. It is a condition of the room, not a thing in it.
Sign: A floor that takes a bootprint deeper than it should; a taste of old burning in a room
that has been cold for centuries.
Disposition: Does not act, pursue or stop. It is simply what happens to a room when somebody
walks into it.
Special: Fills the chamber within moments of being disturbed and blinds everyone in it -
light, tracks and each other all go, and the way out is whichever direction someone was
facing when it lifted.

Grave-Mould (Hazard) - AD: 3d6+1 [MA: 2]
Description: A grey-green growth that takes to worked Essath stone and nothing else, soft as
felt and cold to the hand.
Range: Sealed chambers, always - it dies within a season of being opened to moving air, so a
living patch means a room that has been shut since the priest-kings.
Sign: Stone that is warm-coloured everywhere except in one unbroken sheet; a smell of cut
earth in a dry room.
Disposition: Grows toward warmth and moisture at a pace a person can watch but not
comfortably outwait.
Special: What touches it carries it, and it takes to leather, cloth and rope as readily as to
stone - a party that brushes past a patch finds their own gear going soft days later, which is
the first time most of them learn it was there.

Sunk-Temple Horror (Horror) - AD: 10d6+4 [MA: 4]
Description: Something the flood let out rather than something the flood made - what came up
fifteen years ago when the river shifted and bared a temple nobody had accounted for.
Range: One, in the drowned temple downriver, and it has not left the water it surfaced in.
Two full trading companies went in after it. Eats what comes to it.
Sign: A stretch of river where nothing is fishing and the birds do not land; temple stone
above the waterline scoured clean of fifteen years of weed in a band nothing living explains.
Disposition: Does not hunt and does not need to. It is between the party and the thing they
came for, and it has been for fifteen years.
Special: Reaches further than it looks, and takes hold of more than one person at once - the
MA is above its average because what comes out of the water is not the whole of it.
Special: Cannot be killed in the water and does not come out of it. Everything a party can
actually do about it is done on dry stone, with what they can lure or carry there.

Marsh Wyrm (Wyrm) - AD: 11d6+3 [MA: 5]
Description: A great serpent the length of a causeway span, marsh-coloured and slow enough to
be mistaken for a fallen tree until the far end of it moves.
Range: One in Marfen's deep water, never seen whole by anyone who reported it. It does not
compete with anything else in the marsh, because nothing else in the marsh is a competitor.
Sign: Reed beds flattened in a single continuous line rather than a trampled patch; a
causeway stone shifted from the water side.
Disposition: Indifferent to a party on the causeway and immediately interested in one in the
water. It does not rush.
Special: Coils rather than bites - it takes hold along its length, so everyone in the water
together is taken together, and the modifier sits slightly below its size because no single
exchange is what kills anyone.
Special: The Serpent Cult treats it as owed rather than as a monster, and a cultist will go a
long way out of their way to prevent it being harmed - which is the most reliable way in the
setting to find out who is one.

The Pit-Thing (Horror) - AD: 18d6+6 [MA: 6]
Description: Nothing about it resolves into a shape a person can describe twice the same way;
it is the thing the Pit of the Old Ones was sealed to keep in, not out.
Range: One. It does not travel, forage, or reproduce - it is a fact of one specific place,
and removing that fact is not this setting's business to offer.
Sign: Everything the Bound Dead, the Barrow Wight and the Nirguk enclaves all separately
treat as unthinkable to approach is treating the same one place that way.
Disposition: This entry is not meant to be beaten. Its value is entirely in what a party does
before ever meeting it - what they learn about the pit, and what they choose to leave sealed.
Special: Every Essath binding in the setting is downstream of this one. A waystone defaced,
a threshold spent, a seal forced anywhere on the road is a fastening coming off the same
thing, and nothing in Telar tells a party that.
Special: Reaches everything in the chamber at once and has no stated limit past that.
Special: Nothing recorded has ever hurt it, and the priest-kings, who had five hundred years
and an entire tongue built for binding, sealed it instead.
