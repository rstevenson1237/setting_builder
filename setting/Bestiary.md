Bestiary of Brackvaen

Twenty entries. AD is pitched against party altitude from `setting/Outline.md` - two to four
d6 - and never against a region's difficulty die. Sign and Disposition are the fields that
let a party decide before they commit; they are written to be usable at the table without
the referee inventing anything.

---

Sump Leech (Beast) - AD: 1d6 -1
Description: A flat, fist-long feeder the colour of wet peat, with a ring of backward teeth that leave a clean circular mark. It does not hunt so much as wait in the warm shallows for something to stand still in them.
Range: Every stretch of standing water in Lindmarn and the flooded western pans. Thousands. It eats blood, and in a bad year it takes it from cattle and pan-hands equally.
Sign: The water is warmer than the water beside it, and there are no minnows in it.
Disposition: It attaches and is not noticed for some minutes. It never attacks in any sense a party would recognise as an attack.

Saltrat (Beast) - AD: 1d6 -2
Description: A grey, thin-furred rat that has adapted to the pans, drinking brine and voiding salt through pads on its feet, so that a runway shows white on the boards. Individually contemptible; a pan store loses a season to them.
Range: Anywhere salt is stored or crystallised, which means all of the working pans and every building in Torsgaard that holds stock. Hundreds to a pan. It eats stores, tallow, leather, and the glue in book bindings.
Sign: White powdery trails along beams and boards, and the smell of ammonia in a closed store.
Disposition: It flees, then comes back in numbers when the lamp goes out. A dozen of them in the dark will strip a pack.

Saltcrow (Beast) - AD: 1d6
Description: A heavy pied crow that has learned the pans - it drops shellfish on the drying floors to crack them, and it has learned the same trick works on eyes. It follows crews.
Range: The whole of Brackvaen, thickest over the eastern pans. Flocks of twenty to forty. It eats carrion, shellfish, and anything a crew leaves unattended.
Sign: Birds already circling somewhere you have not been yet, which is worth more to a party than anything else in this file.
Disposition: It watches, it follows, and it takes from the injured and the sleeping. It will not close with anyone standing.

Marsh Lymer (Beast) - AD: 2d6
Description: The holding's tracking hounds, gone feral over four or five generations - long-legged, grey, deep-chested, and still built for scent across water. They run in packs of six to ten and they still work as a pack rather than as a mob.
Range: The drier margins of Lindmarn and the causeway verges. Two packs the fen supports, no more. It eats hog, heron, and anything a crossing party has lost hold of.
Sign: Scat full of feather and bone on the causeway stones, and a single hound seen at distance that does not run.
Disposition: It circles at fifty yards and waits for the party to string out. It will not commit against a group that has stopped and closed up, and it knows the difference immediately.

Gaunt Heron (Beast) - AD: 2d6 +1
Description: A five-foot stilt-legged fisher, slate and white, with a bill like a boarding pike. It is not aggressive and it is not timid; it simply has no reason to move for anyone and a very effective objection if pressed.
Range: The channel edges throughout Lindmarn, one bird to a mile of channel, fiercely spaced. It eats eel, frog, and leech.
Sign: A single wet channel-side patch of trampled reed, and eel heads.
Disposition: It holds absolutely still and lets a party come to within a few feet. It strikes once, at a face, and then leaves.

Reedback Hog (Beast) - AD: 3d6 +1
Description: A fen boar with a crest of stiff bristle from poll to rump, caked in dried mud that hardens into a genuine armour across the shoulders. Bad-tempered in the way of an animal with no predators worth the name.
Range: The reed beds of Lindmarn and the abandoned western pans. Sounders of four to twelve, and a solitary old boar in perhaps three places. It eats roots, carrion, and the eggs of everything that nests on the ground.
Sign: Wallows, and reed cut off level at knee height over a wide area.
Disposition: A sounder moves off. A solitary boar does not, and it charges from about fifteen yards without any of the display an unwounded animal usually offers.

Brine Eel (Beast) - AD: 5d6 -1
Description: A nine- to twelve-foot eel, pale as a peeled root, that has come up out of the drowned stonework into the pans. It hunts along the bottom of the deeper cuts and it can cross forty yards of wet mud between them.
Range: The deep cuts of the eastern and western pans, and the flooded lower courses of Siwapelu. Perhaps a dozen in the whole of Brackvaen. It eats hog, hound, heron, and men, and it does not need to eat often.
Sign: A cut with no leeches and no minnows in it, and a smooth trough pressed across the mud between two cuts, three hands wide.
Disposition: It takes from below without any warning at all, and it takes the person at the back. This is the one entry in this file with no useful Disposition, and its Sign is therefore the whole of the party's chance.

Mire Aurochs (Beast) - AD: 5d6 +2
Description: A splay-hooved wild ox, black-brown, six feet at the shoulder, with horns swept forward rather than up. The fen supports very few and they are correspondingly ancient. Torsgaard does not hunt them, on the reasonable grounds that the meat is not worth the funerals.
Range: The firm ground and reed islands of Lindmarn. Three or four cows and a bull. It eats reed and sedge.
Sign: Hoofprints the size of a dinner plate sunk six inches into ground that will hold a man, and rubbed bark at chest height.
Disposition: It watches, it goes on feeding, and it tolerates a great deal. What it will not tolerate is being placed between a party and the water, and a party that does that has made the decision for it.

Salt-reever (Man) - AD: 2d6
Description: A pan-thief working the abandoned western pans, lifting stock from unwatched drying floors and selling it downriver as western salt. Ragged, competent on the water, and carrying a salt-debt that has already eaten everything they used to own.
Range: The western pans and the causeway, in twos and threes, perhaps twenty in the whole holding. They eat what they steal.
Sign: A drying floor swept too clean, and a boat drawn up where no pan is worked.
Disposition: They run, and they talk if cornered, and they will sell the party a route, a name, or each other at a very reasonable price.

Drowned-road Wrecker (Man) - AD: 2d6 +1
Description: Men working the causeway at its worst stretches - pulling stakes, shifting channel markers, and taking from whoever walks into what they have arranged. They are not a band so much as three or four families who do this in bad seasons and pan work in good ones.
Range: The middle stretches of the causeway across Lindmarn. Six to ten at a time.
Sign: A channel stake with the cord cut off it rather than untied.
Disposition: They do not fight a party that is watching for them. They rearrange the ground and take from whatever the ground catches, which means they are met after the disaster rather than before it.

Assize Serjeant (Man) - AD: 3d6 +1
Description: The Salt Assize's men, in grey wool sashes, carrying a boat-hook and a lead-sealed writ. Half constable and half surveyor - they measure pans, weigh debt, and put down trouble, in that order of enthusiasm.
Range: Torsgaard and the worked eastern pans. Perhaps thirty, in pairs and fours.
Sign: A lead seal with three notches nailed to a post, which means this pan has been measured and this month it is somebody's.
Disposition: They ask your business, and the answer determines everything after. They are not looking for a fight and they are extremely willing to have one about the boundary stones.

Faelhond Digger (Man) - AD: 3d6
Description: One of the Brined working the stair - salt taken into the skin to the elbow and past it, wrists wrapped in oiled leather. They go down at dawn, they come up before the gorm rises, and they are markedly hard to put down for men with no particular training.
Range: The stair head and the upper courses of Siwapelu, and lodgings in Torsgaard nobody rents to them officially. Perhaps forty.
Sign: A grey smear of salt on a door frame at hand height, which means one of them has called at this house.
Disposition: They ask what you are doing on the stair and they do not accept a vague answer. They are not hostile until the word 'seal' is used.

Vingash Reedwatcher (Humanoid) - AD: 3d6
Description: One of the fen people - lean, grey-skinned, a head shorter than a Marchman, with a bitter-iron ring through the outer ear. They hold the channels, they set the knotted cord that gives their depth, and they have been doing it since before Torsgaard was founded.
Range: The whole of Lindmarn, in watches of two and three. Several hundred in the fen altogether. They eat eel, hog, and waterfowl.
Sign: Knotted reed cord on a channel stake, freshly tied - which is both their mark and a genuine navigational aid to anyone who learns to read it.
Disposition: They are seen when they choose to be seen. A party that has not cut a cord or gone toward the stair is watched and let through; one that has done either is followed.

Vingash Channel-Elder (Humanoid) - AD: 4d6 +2 - MA: 2
Description: An old one of the fen people, iron through both ears and the reed cord of nine channels at their belt. They hold what the Vingash know about Nessekire and about what is under the pans, and they are the reason the fen people's position on the stair is a position rather than a reflex.
Range: Three of them in Lindmarn, each at a deep-water fastness the causeway does not go near.
Sign: An entire stretch of channel stakes tied in one hand's knotting, and no watchers visible anywhere in it.
Disposition: They will talk, at length, and they will trade knowledge for a commitment about the stair. They are the most valuable conversation in Brackvaen and they are not difficult to reach - they are difficult to satisfy.

Sump-Kin (Humanoid) - AD: 2d6
Description: A branch of the fen people who went into the flooded western pans during the Gell Year and did not come out again in any sense the Vingash recognise. Wet, silent, iron rings grown over. The Vingash will not speak about them and will not go west of the causeway.
Range: The flooded western pans and the drowned stonework beneath them. Perhaps sixty. They eat leech, eel, and what the pans catch.
Sign: Iron rings, one or two, left on a drying floor above the waterline. Nobody knows whether this is an offering, a boundary, or a count.
Disposition: They surface, they watch, and they do not approach a party that stays out of the water. Anything in the water is a different matter entirely.

Cured Man (Undead) - AD: 4d6
Description: A body the pans kept - skin like tallow-soaked leather, joints that work, salt standing in the creases. It does what it was doing. A pan-hand walks a pan circuit; a thief sunk for stealing goes on trying to get out from under its weight. It is not malevolent and it is not stoppable by anything short of dismemberment.
Range: The pans, both worked and abandoned, and any part of Siwapelu that has been dry long enough. Perhaps two dozen in the whole of Brackvaen, most of them still weighted and still where they were put.
Sign: A weight-stone with its rope cut or rotted through, and a shallow trench going away from it.
Disposition: It continues. If the party is not between it and what it is doing, it will pass within arm's reach and not engage at all - which is a thing a party can find out and use, and it is why this entry is 4 AD instead of a fight.

Nuthora Oathkeeper (Undead) - AD: 6d6 +2 - MA: 1
Description: Something the Odarath bound to a boundary stone and did not release. It is a dry, standing shape of salt and wrapped cloth, and it does not leave the stone's ground - it has perhaps thirty yards, and within them it is implacable and outside them it does not exist. There is one at each of the eleven Assize stones, which is why the Assize stones are obeyed.
Range: The eleven Assize stones through the pans, and an unknown number of Odarath stones still standing in Lindmarn and Siwapelu. One to a stone.
Sign: Ground within thirty yards of an inscribed stone that nothing has fouled, nested in, or grown over, in country where everything is fouled and overgrown.
Disposition: It does nothing at all to anyone who leaves the stone alone, and it is not hidden - a party can walk past eleven of these in a season and never learn what they were. Damage the stone and there is no negotiation and no pursuit beyond thirty yards.

Drowned Chorus (Undead) - AD: 9d6 +4 - MA: 3
Description: What Nessekire made of everyone who was in the low country when it came in - not individually, but as one thing, four hundred years cured and still arriving. It comes up a flooded course as a rising of dirty water and a sound like a crowd heard through a wall.
Range: The flooded lower courses of Siwapelu and, twice in living memory, the western pans on a spring tide. One. It does not eat.
Sign: The gorm thickens fast enough to put out a lamp in under a minute, and standing water begins to move against the fall of the ground.
Disposition: It fills the space it is in. It cannot be fought in a flooded course and it cannot follow a party up a stair faster than they can climb, and the whole of the encounter is that arithmetic.

Tuggik (Construct) - AD: 7d6 - MA: 1
Description: An Odarath salt-hauler: a stone-and-iron frame the height of two men, walking on four jointed legs, with a hopper on its back that has been empty for four centuries. It is still walking its route, from a working that is exhausted to a wharf that is forty feet under Lindmarn, and it has worn a groove in the gallery floor doing it.
Range: The lower courses of Siwapelu and, on its outward leg, one flooded channel of Lindmarn. There is one. It does not eat.
Sign: A polished groove in a stone floor, and a rhythmic knock carrying through the rock a long time before anything is visible.
Disposition: It walks its route. It does not perceive anyone as anything but obstruction, and it removes obstruction from the route with complete indifference and no pursuit whatsoever. Everything about surviving it is knowing where the groove goes.

Zorethanu (Horror) - AD: 14d6 +6 - MA: 5
Description: The thing the deepest course of Siwapelu was cut to hold, and which the Odarath's entire under-work is arranged around not disturbing - the drainage, the salt, the eleven stones, all of it. It is not described here because nothing that has seen it has come back to describe it, and what a party will actually meet is the arrangement rather than the thing.
Range: The lowest course of Siwapelu. One. What it eats is a question that assumes it has eaten recently.
Sign: Old Rede cut into every surface of an approach in a hand that gets worse and more hurried the further in it goes, and salt laid a foot deep across a threshold by people who were not mining it.
Disposition: **This is the entry the party is not meant to beat.** It has never left the lowest course and there is no reason to think it can. Nothing about the approach is concealed, hurried, or gated: the Sign runs for ninety feet, costs nothing to read, and is legible before anyone commits to anything.
