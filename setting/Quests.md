Quests of Brackvaen

The Weight on Sallow's Boots - given at A.1 Thrapstad, resolved at B.5 Naugfast, also cited at A.4 Faelvaen and B.1 Doraiath
- Wants: Halmen Weldling, weighmaster at the scales, who has recorded the weight against a dead man's kit and cannot get it settled. He will not go himself because he is fifty-one, has weighed at that gantry for nineteen years, and has never in his life been west of the drying floors - and because a weighmaster leaving his scales to recover goods he has himself valued is exactly the appearance the Assize would take an interest in.
- Object: the kit of a pan-hand called Sallow, drowned eleven days ago - rakes, a coat, and boots in reasonable order - lying under the silt in the bow of a swamped punt moored at the north-east corner of the drowned pan-house at B.5 Naugfast. The punt is still on its line and the line is still sound.
- Obstacle: the arithmetic before the fen. The weight recorded against the kit is more than the kit is worth, which is why nobody from the holding has walked nine hundred yards to collect it - and why anyone who does collect it takes up the weight along with the boots, per `setting/Procedures.md`. Then the fen: the punt sits inside standing walls where a Brine Eel lies in the scoured corner, and the tell is that there are no leeches within the building and the channel outside is thick with them. And then the marker at B.1 Doraiath, which is a strip of Assize serjeant's grey wool tied in a Vingash knot - a Marchman could not have tied it and a serjeant would not have left it, and whoever recovers the kit will eventually have to account for who was standing over Sallow when he went in.
- Terms: "Bring it up and I'll clear the board on it out of my own pocket, and I'll weigh honest for you for a year. I'm not asking you to carry his weight. I'm asking you to let me stop carrying his name."

Count the Stones - given at A.2 Rendfast, resolved at C.7 Nuthorath, also cited at B.4 Nuthora
- Wants: Gellen Torsling, an Assize head, who has read board eleven of the settlement and understands that the Assize's own document may grant it Siwapelu, or may grant Siwapelu something. He will not go himself because an Assize head descending the stair in person would answer the question publicly before he knows what the answer is, and because the two men who have read board eleven have agreed, without saying so, that neither will be the one who tables it.
- Object: a count and a comparison. There are eleven pillars in the domed chamber at C.7 Nuthorath, cut in Old Rede, and eleven stones set through the eastern pans that were copied from them letter for letter sixty years ago. He wants to know whether the pillars and the stones say the same thing, and he wants to know what stands at the twelfth position - which the boundary chart at Rendfast marks in open water with no pan drawn against it, and which is the stone at B.4 Nuthora.
- Obstacle: the Oathkeeper standing between the fourth and fifth pillars, which does nothing whatever to a party that reads, crosses the salt, and leaves - so the obstacle is not the fight but the temptation, since the twelfth socket is scraped clear and the fitting for it is six hundred yards out in open water inside another Oathkeeper's thirty-yard ring. Then the count itself: a party that does it properly has to be in that room long enough to be sure, and Gorm and the Danger countdown both run while they are.
- Terms: "Eleven stones, eleven pillars. Tell me they match and I'll pay you sixty and never mention it again. Tell me they don't and I'll pay you two hundred, and then I would very much like you to leave Torsgaard for a season."

What Came Up With Ferris - given at A.10 Ondtstad, resolved at C.10 Wosaora, also cited at A.7 Halmgaard
- Wants: Druva Halmling, who has kept the lodging house for twenty-two years and has left a bench end clear for three months. Ferris drank at that table every night for nine years, came up out of the stair Brined to the elbow, and has not been back in the room since, though he is still in the holding and still sitting outside his hut at Ondtstad answering every direct question with complete willingness and no useful detail. She will not go herself because she has not been off the rise in eleven years and because, as she puts it, she is not going to be the one who finds out.
- Object: what happened in the long low room on the second course at C.10 Wosaora - specifically Ferris's coat, folded and placed against the north wall out of the worn path, of recent Torsgaard make with the wrappings still in the pockets. It was set down deliberately by a man who then walked out without it.
- Obstacle: Odaranu, walking its thirty-one paces from arch to arch and back, nine hundred years dead and not finished. It is 4d6 and it is almost never a fight: a party that watches one circuit and stays off the centre line can pass within arm's reach. The obstacle is that the coat is against the north wall, out of the path, which means whoever left it had already worked that out - and a party that recovers the coat has to explain to Druva why a man would take off his coat, fold it, and set it down neatly in a room with that in it.
- Terms: "He sat there nine years and now he sits outside. I'm not asking what he is. I'm asking what he saw, and I'll keep you fed and bedded the whole season for the answer, and I'll not repeat it, which you know is worth something from me."
