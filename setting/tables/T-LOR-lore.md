Written works, statues and art, given as the thing itself. A referee reads an
entry to a player exactly as it stands. The `Form` column carries the physical
gate: what it is cut into and how a party comes to be looking at it. Two of
these are confidently wrong, and the error is traceable.

| ID | Entry | Form |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| T-LOR-01 | Seaward first, then inward, and never two in one watch. A road rung out of order is a road paid twice. | Cut into the underside of a bell frame crossbeam, upside down to a walker. |
| T-LOR-02 | The reeds were cut back a chain on either side by order, so that the tolling would carry and so that nothing could stand close and not be seen standing. | A ledger page, waxed, folded into a bell pin socket. |
| T-LOR-03 | We rang nine, and the ninth was not given back. Hang the tenth from the frame and leave the frame bare, that the fen may count what it is still owed. | Cut into a floor slab, and legible only from inside the shrine looking out. |
| T-LOR-04 | A figure in relief, waist-deep, holding a clapper above the waterline with both hands. The face has been cut away, and cut away carefully, with the surrounding stone untouched. | A relief panel, half of it below the present waterline. |
| T-LOR-05 | Stop at the sill. What lies under it was not made, and it is not ours to open. Peg the shaft and set a watch that will outlast us. | Cut into a shaft rim in letters a hand deep, the strokes uneven, the last word finished by a different hand. |
| T-LOR-06 | Third hold, closed the fourteenth day. Two hundred and six out, nothing left but the fixed things, door pegged from without. Count agreed both sides. | A tally board, hung on the outside of a pegged door, the wax still holding its notches. |
| T-LOR-07 | They asked what we were paying, and I told them the tides. Then they asked what the tides were paying, and I had no answer for them, and I have not found one since. | A letter, never sent, standing in a lamp niche with the soot wiped off the stone under it. |
| T-LOR-08 | The water has not come up. Set your mark on the third course and look again in the spring. | Scratched rather than cut into a hold wall, with three marks beneath it, the lowest of the three standing above the present water. |
| T-LOR-09 | A figure kneeling with both hands flat on the floor and one ear laid to the slab, cut at the size of a child and set where a person coming up a stair meets it at eye height. The stone around it is polished in two oval patches, at the span of a pair of hands, by other people kneeling down and doing the same thing. | A carved boss set into a floor at the head of a stair, worn smooth on the side a kneeling person would take. |
| T-LOR-10 | Whatever answers when you ring, it is not the bell. Do not say so anywhere it can hear you say it. | Cut backwards into the inside of a pin socket, and readable only in still water. |
| T-LOR-11 | The cutting order stands. It stood before us, it is not ours to lift, and it is not yours to lift either. | A standing order, cut on a post at the head of every cut lane in the Reach, in identical letters on every one of them. |
| T-LOR-12 | A tally of bells by road, ruled in two columns, the hand growing smaller and steadier down the page as the writer ran out of board. The last row reads *Bell Causeway* and the column beside it is empty, and the rule for the row below it was drawn and never filled. | A wax board, warped by heat, with the last row pressed in with a thumb rather than cut. |
