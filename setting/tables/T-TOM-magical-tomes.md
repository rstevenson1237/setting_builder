The written works that do something, as opposed to the written works that say
something. A tome is a device made of writing and it obeys the device rules: it
half-works, it costs something to read, and its instructions are wrong in one
particular. Half of these are spent by a single use. The object matters as much
as the text: what it is made of, what has survived, what a later hand has added.

| T-TOM-01 | The Ringing Order, part. Waxed bronze leaves on a pin, holding the sequence for eleven roads out of an unknown number. The leaves are read aloud in the order they hang: *Seaward first. Then inward. Never two in one watch, and never the ninth.* At a bare frame, the road named answers as though it had been struck. Reading costs the reader their voice until a full rest, and the eleventh leaf is in a different hand and is wrong. | {VALUE: 7000 cn} {WT: 3} {QUALITY: Masterwork} |
| T-TOM-02 | A settling book. A cutter's measure-book, columns of hand-marks by season, in which a column continues to be written while it is being read. What it writes is the next reading, and it has never yet been wrong. It is spent when the last page fills, and there are not many pages. | {VALUE: 5000 cn} {WT: 1} {QUALITY: Masterwork} |
| T-TOM-03 | The Stop Order, portable. A slate copy of the words cut into the deepest shaft rim, carried by whoever was set to keep the watch. The words are the ones cut in haste: *Stop at the sill. What lies under it was not made, and it is not ours to open.* Read at a shaft mouth, nothing comes up it for a watch. Read aloud anywhere else, everything within a chain that was standing still starts moving. | {VALUE: 6000 cn} {WT: 2} {QUALITY: Masterwork} |
