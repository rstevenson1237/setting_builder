Puzzles that recur, or that are built before they are placed. Every row states
the answer and states where the answer is findable, because a puzzle whose
answer is not written down is a referee improvising under pressure while four
people watch. Nothing here resolves on a roll: what a roll adjudicates is what
the time costs.

| ID | Entry | Answer | Clue |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| T-PUZ-01 | A bell frame with the pin seated and no bell on it. Struck in the wrong order, a road answers from the wrong direction, and something on it starts walking. | Sound the frames seaward first, then inward, and never two in one watch. | Distributed: T-LOR-01, cut under a crossbeam and readable by anyone who lies down beneath it. |
| T-PUZ-02 | A door pegged from the outside with the tally board still hung on it. The pegs are seated from inside the frame and cannot be drawn. | The board lifts off its hook, and the hook is the head of the peg. Taking the count down opens the door. | Proximate: every fitting on the door is green with age and the hook is bright. |
| T-PUZ-03 | A channel slab that lifts rather than swings, with a socket cut beside it at hand height and nothing in the socket. | Seat something of the right weight and shape in the socket. The slab stays up while it is in and drops when it is taken out. | Distributed: the same construction met earlier in the region and working correctly, with its counterweight still in place (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-09). |
| T-PUZ-04 | A votive shelf slotted along its outer lip, where nothing a party is carrying will sit in any slot. | The slots take clappers, not offerings. The centre slot is deeper because the road's own clapper stood in it. | Proximate: the centre slot is worn bright on the inside and the others are not worn at all. |
| T-PUZ-05 | A stair that turns four times and ends at dressed stone, one course thick, with no door in it. | It is a facing rather than a structure. One block sits a finger proud of the rest and will take a shoulder. | Proximate: the wear on the steps runs all the way to the wall and does not slow before it. |
| T-PUZ-06 | A tally post whose notches run out mid-column, with one fresh notch cut above the gap. | Somebody resumed a count they had abandoned. Counting back from the fresh notch gives the watch the gap opened, and the gap is the same length on every post in the lane. | Distributed: T-LOR-11, the standing cutting order, cut in identical letters at the head of every lane in the Reach. |
| T-PUZ-07 | A corridor with a tallow niche at shoulder height every forty paces, soot in every one of them, and one clean. | The clean niche is not a niche. It is the handhold of a shaft plug, and the plug turns. | Proximate: soot lies on every surface in the corridor, and something has been putting a hand in that one place. |
| T-PUZ-08 | A relief with the face cut away carefully and the stone around it untouched. | The face was cut off and kept. It is a lid, and it seats in a socket elsewhere on the same road. | Distributed: T-LOR-04, the relief itself, and the socket, which is cut to a shape nothing else in the Reach would fit. |
