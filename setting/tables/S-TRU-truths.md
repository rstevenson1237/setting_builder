The facts of the world that no in-world source states plainly. Referee-facing.
A player reaches these by inference and never by being told. The `Who could
state it` column is the inference route: it names who holds the fact and cannot
or will not say it.

| ID | Entry | Who could state it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| S-TRU-01 | The bells were never a warning. They were a payment, and the fen accepted them. | Nobody living. |
| S-TRU-02 | The water is not rising. The land is going down, a little each year, and always at night. | The Reedcutters, who measure it and call it settling. |
| S-TRU-03 | What the fen takes, it keeps whole. Nothing in it rots. | Anyone who has pulled something out, which is nobody twice. |
| S-TRU-04 | The level under the shafts was never dug. It was open before the Covenant reached it, and it is still occupied. | The last chapter, which no longer speaks, and whatever it went down to. |
| S-TRU-05 | The cutting order is what keeps the reeds off the roads, and the roads are what the payment is reckoned against. If the cutting stopped, the debt would be reckoned differently. | Nobody. The Reedcutters keep the order without knowing what it is for. |
| S-TRU-06 | The Covenant knew all of this and wrote none of it down, because writing a debt down is a way of agreeing to it. | The Drowned Chapter. |
| S-TRU-07 | The warmth under the cut stone is not the stone's. It comes up from the level below, and it is stronger the further down a road runs. | The people who lie down on it, who do not say so. |
| S-TRU-08 | The ninth bell was rung and was not given back. The tenth was hung and never rung, and the fen has been waiting on it since. | The Covenant Remnant, which holds the opposite: that ringing the tenth would finish the debt rather than open it. |
