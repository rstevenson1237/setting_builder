Layout: The forest trail (C.1) runs 12 miles north from the keep's postern (A.2) to the ravine's mouth (E.1), three watches' walk, single file between old pines and oaks; the other way in is the mere's north shore, at the mere landing (C.6) on the west. Its stops in order: the standing stones at 3 miles, seven stones ringing a rise beside the trail (C.5); the hare's foot post at 3.5 miles, where a track runs west to the outlaws' camp (C.2); the mere path at 5 miles, a worn path running 5 miles west to the mere landing (C.6); the hermit's path at 7 miles, running a mile east to the hermit's clearing (C.3); the old watchtower at 8 miles, beside the trail (C.4); and the ravine mouth at 12 miles, where the trail meets the stream path (E.1).

The standing stones (C.5), 3 miles north, are seven grey pillars each twice a man's height, ringing a low rise 30 feet across round a waist-high altar-stone, with a row of soot-smeared sticks pushed into the earth to the south; the trail runs beside them. The outlaws' camp (C.2), 5 miles north-west of the postern, lies under a lip of rock with lean-tos round a cookfire and a hare's foot nailed to each post, hidden from a hundred yards but for woodsmoke on the wind; the track west from the trail at 3.5 miles reaches it in 3.5 miles, and a second trail comes up to it from the mere path. The mere landing (C.6), 5 miles west of the trail at 5 miles north, is a rotting jetty 20 feet long in the reeds with a pole boat beside it, and half a mile of firm path through bog to the causeway (D.1). The hermit's clearing (C.3), 7 miles north and a mile east of the trail, is a clearing 60 feet across inside a thorn fence, with a turf hut, bean rows and a well, and a bell on the gate. The old watchtower (C.4), 8 miles north beside the trail, is a roofless tower of makers' stone, 20 feet across and broken at 15 feet, ivy-choked, its stair ending in rubble after the third turn and a cellar hole gaping at its foot.

The outlaws' cache (C.7), 6 miles north-west of the postern and a mile north-west of the camp by a game trail that climbs, lies in a pit 6 feet across and 5 deep under an uprooted oak; a party can notice turf cut over the pit with thorn cuttings laid between, and a hare's foot nailed to the trunk at head height.

Inhabitants: The Wood Outlaws (Faction: The Wood Outlaws) hold the wood. Four bowmen take a toll of 10 cn a head at a bend of the trail (C.1) and shoot those who refuse; twelve mend arrows round the cookfire at their camp (C.2), wary, talking before they fight, wanting to be left alone and knowing every path in the wood. Ansel the Hermit (Named Creature: Ansel the Hermit) tends his beans at the clearing (C.3) nine days in ten, wants news of the realm and knows what the stones answer. Five wolves den in the cellar hole of the watchtower (C.4) and circle then rush the weakest at dusk. Three bugbears of The Zurnaz (Faction: The Zurnaz) wait among the standing stones (C.5) by night, striking from hiding and never parleying, for the tithe to be paid in full.

Loot: The wood yields game and forage to a hunter and its outlaws' share in a pit. Rabbit and wild garlic, deer, boar and wild honey come to a party that Forages or Hunts within a mile of the trail, and bad mushrooms to one that is careless (Local Procedure: Forage and Hunt in the Wood); each day's rations found saves the 5 cn a head they cost at the General Store (A.5). The outlaws' share lies in the cache (C.7).

Secrets:
- The outlaws' cache lies in a pit under an uprooted oak, a game trail from the camp - the outlaws know; the hare's foot nailed to the trunk (C.7) brings it out.
- Bugbears gather at the standing stones on dark nights to take the tithe - Ansel knows; the soot handprints on the pillars (C.5) bring it out.

Leads:
- Rumour 8 - C.5 Stones, C.3 Ansel the Hermit.
