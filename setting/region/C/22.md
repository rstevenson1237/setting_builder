C.22 **Nekire** (low) - *pumps, resistance, failure*
A dead-end chamber holding four great **screw pumps** of oak and bronze set in stone housings, three of them **seized** and rotted through, and one of them **turning slowly** on its own with the water that runs past it.
*A room 40 by 25 feet, ceiling 12 feet to clear the pump housings, standing in a foot of running water. This was the drainage plant that kept the lowest course clear, and it is the reason the fourth course flooded when it failed. It is the only room in Siwapelu with a mechanical sound in it - a slow wooden groan on about a four-second interval, which carries a long way and which a party will have been hearing and misplacing for several rooms.*
**Screw Pumps:** Four Archimedean screws, each 12 feet long and 3 in diameter, oak flights in bronze-lined stone housings, set at an angle to lift water from the lower sump to the channel. Three have failed: the flights rotted, the bearings picked up, the housings cracked. Four hundred years of no maintenance did that, and it was gradual.
**Turning Pump:** The fourth still turns, driven by the flow, and it is lifting water - not much, but continuously, and it has never stopped. It is the last working part of the Odarath's arrangement and everything about the fourth course being flooded rather than drained depends on it and three others like it that failed.
**Socket Housing:** At the head of the working screw, a square-sectioned socket is cut into the bronze bearing plate, four inches across with a shallow taper, and there is an identical one on each of the three failed pumps. **Clue**: the sockets are the only part of this room without four hundred years of verdigris on them, because something has been fitted and withdrawn from them repeatedly; and the room is a dead end with a machine in it that somebody has plainly been maintaining. **Trigger**: fitting the stone rod from the workshop on the first course into the socket and turning it. **Payload**: the rod is the pump key. In the working socket it declutches the screw and the fourth course begins to fill further; in any of the three failed sockets it engages a bypass that puts flow through a sound section of housing, and a party that works out which is which can start draining the lowest course for the first time in four hundred years - which is exactly what the salt on the threshold at Zorethora was laid down to prevent (Keys: Peluora Fitting-Rod).
**Exits:** low corbelled arch in the south wall, water running in through it -> C.21 Taluora
