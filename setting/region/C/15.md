C.15 **Peluath** (high) - *working, exhausted, route*
A long hall with a **cut face** at the far end where the limestone has been quarried back forty feet and then stopped, and a **groove** worn down the room's whole length, deep enough to turn an ankle. A **hopper stand** of dressed stone squats in the centre, and something heavy is knocking out through the rock.
*120 by 30 feet, ceiling 20, cut as a working face rather than built. Loudest room in Siwapelu: the knock arrives through the stone long before anything does, and the cut face throws it back so it seems to come from the wrong end of the hall.*
**Groove:** Eighteen inches wide, six deep, polished, running dead straight the full 120 feet from the east arch to the west. Four jointed feet in the same four places for four hundred years.
**Tuggik:** One, passing through at intervals - a stone-and-iron frame two men high on four jointed legs, hopper empty, walking the groove east to west and back. 7d6. It registers only obstruction in the groove, removes it, and does not pursue or deviate.
**Cut Face:** The western forty feet is raw limestone in stepped benches, worked to a seam that pinches out. A half-freed block, nine wedge slots cut and never driven → the third from the left holds an iron feather-and-wedge set, unrusted → work it free, one action → (Treasure II, d20).
**Hopper Stand:** Dressed stone, 8 feet square and 4 high, centre of the hall on the groove's north side, cut with a shallow saddle where the hopper was filled. In the saddle, dry and undisturbed: grey salt, a foot deep. No stone.
**Exits:** corbelled arch in the east wall, the groove running out through it -> C.13 Teruimmo, low arch in the south wall, half-blocked by fallen bench stone, with water audible beyond -> C.17 Kireath
