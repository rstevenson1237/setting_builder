C.15 **Peluath** (high) - *working, exhausted, route*
A long hall with a **cut face** at its far end where the limestone has been quarried back forty feet and then simply stopped, and a **groove** worn into the floor down the room's whole length, polished to a shine and deep enough to turn an ankle. There is a **hopper stand** of dressed stone in the centre, and something very heavy comes through here at intervals.
*A rectangle 120 by 30 feet with a 20-foot ceiling, twice the height of the standard courses, cut as a working face rather than built. This was where the Odarath took the stone out, and it is exhausted - the face was worked to the end of the seam and abandoned, and everything since has been about the route rather than the room. It is the loudest room in Siwapelu: the knock of the Tuggik arrives here through the rock long before the thing does, and the cut face throws it back so the sound seems to come from the wrong end of the hall.*
**Groove:** A polished channel about eighteen inches wide worn six inches into the flagged floor, running the full 120 feet from the eastern arch to the western one, dead straight. It is the Tuggik's route and it has been worn by four hundred years of the same four feet in the same four places. The whole of surviving this room is knowing where the groove goes: a party standing off the groove is not perceived and is not interfered with, and a party in it is obstruction.
**Tuggik:** One, passing through at intervals - a stone-and-iron frame the height of two men on four jointed legs, hopper empty, walking from this exhausted face out through the flooded channel to a wharf that has been forty feet under Lindmarn for four centuries, and back. 7d6. It does not perceive anyone as anything but obstruction and it removes obstruction with complete indifference and no pursuit whatever. It cannot be reasoned with, it cannot be stopped, and it can be stepped around.
**Cut Face:** The western forty feet of the hall is raw quarried limestone rather than dressed courses, worked back in stepped benches to a seam that pinches out. The last cuts are unfinished - a half-freed block still attached at its base, wedge slots cut and never driven. The Odarath did not leave in a hurry; they left because there was nothing more here, and then they went on maintaining a route to it anyway for reasons the room does not explain.
**Hopper Stand:** A dressed stone platform 8 feet square and 4 feet high in the centre of the hall, on the groove's north side, cut with a shallow saddle where the Tuggik's hopper was filled. In the saddle, dry and undisturbed, is the last load that was never taken - not stone. Grey salt, laid a foot deep, the same as the floor at Nuthorath and the same as what a party will find across the threshold at Zorethora. The salt was not being mined here. It was being brought in and carried down, and this room is where a party can work that out.
**Wedge Slots:** Cut into the half-freed block at the face, a row of nine wedge slots, and jammed into the third from the left is an iron feather-and-wedge set, sound, that has been there four hundred years without rusting because the air here is salt and the salt keeps. Working it free takes an action (Treasure II, d20).
**Exits:** corbelled arch in the east wall, the groove running out through it -> C.13 Teruimmo, low arch in the south wall, half-blocked by fallen bench stone, with water audible beyond -> C.17 Kireath
