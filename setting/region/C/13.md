C.13 **Teruimmo** (low) - *three ways, divide, choice*
A triangular chamber with an arch in each of its three walls and the **groove** running in through one and out through another, so that two of the three ways are the route of something heavy and the third is not.
*A triangle about 30 feet on a side, ceiling 9 feet, cut deliberately three-sided which nothing else in Siwapelu is. It is a junction and was built as one. The three arches each carry the threshold character on the cap, and the room's own name is cut above the northern one - the only room in the complex that names itself.*
**Groove:** The polished band, six inches deep here, enters through the eastern arch and leaves through the western one, crossing the room in a straight line and taking a corner off it. Standing anywhere in this room that is not on that line is safe from the thing that uses it. A party that has seen the faint version of this groove up on the first course now knows what it becomes.
**Named Arch:** Above the northern arch, cut into the cap's face rather than its underside, the room's name in Old Rede. It is the only self-naming room here, and the reason is practical rather than ceremonial - this is the junction you tell somebody to meet you at.
**Third Way:** The northern arch is the one the groove does not use, and it is different in a way a party can read: its sill is clean where the other two are dusted with stone flour worn off the groove, and the air through it is still where the other two draw. Everything heavy in this complex goes east and west from here, and whatever is north of this room has been left alone for four hundred years.
**Exits:** corbelled arch in the east wall, groove running through it, stone flour on the sill -> C.12 Galuora, corbelled arch in the west wall, groove running through it, stone flour on the sill -> C.15 Peluath, corbelled arch in the north wall, its cap cut with the room's name, sill clean and air still -> C.16 Nesseora
