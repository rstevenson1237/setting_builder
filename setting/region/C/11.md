C.11 **Siwakire** (medium) - *tide, channels, timing*
A vaulted hall floored entirely with **open channels** of dressed stone running side by side from one wall to the other, with narrow **walkways** between them, and the water in them running visibly in one direction. A **sluice frame** stands against the far wall with its gates raised, and the whole room is a machine for moving water somewhere.
*A hall 55 by 40 feet, ceiling 12 feet on a barrel of corbelled courses, floored with nine parallel channels each 3 feet wide and 4 feet deep with 18-inch walkways between them. This is the drainage control for the whole complex, and it is the room that decides what is flooded and what is not. It is loud - running water in nine stone channels under a vault - and it is the only warm-sounding place in Siwapelu, which is a lie the room tells.*
**Open Channels:** Nine channels running north to south, water moving through all of them at knee depth. Which way it runs is the tide in Lindmarn forty feet above: on a making tide it runs south, on a falling tide north, and it turns over the space of about ten minutes. A party that watches the channels for that long has read the tide and knows what the third course is about to do, which is the single most useful piece of timing available in this region.
**Walkways:** Eighteen inches wide, wet, with no handrail, crossing four feet of moving water in nine places. Crossing the hall means eight of them. Wet stone, a laden pack, and a party moving quickly is a Damaging fall into a channel per `setting/Procedures.md` - no test, ordinary damage, and the current takes anything dropped straight into the wall culvert and gone.
**Sluice Frame:** An iron and oak frame against the south wall spanning all nine channels, with nine gates in it, all raised and pinned. Lowering them is possible by hand, one at a time, and each takes an action. Dropping the gates backs the water up into the second course and drains the third; raising them again floods the third and clears the second. **Clue**: the pins are bright where every other iron in the room is dulled, and the frame's timbers are new oak, not Odarath work. **Trigger**: examining the pins, or asking the Faelhond about them. **Payload**: the Faelhond replaced this frame two years ago and have been working the gates ever since, which is how they keep the second course dry through the morning - and it is also, though they do not know it, why the eastern water dropped four feet and why the stair uncovered at all.
**Exits:** corbelled arch in the north wall, wide, with the gallery beyond -> C.9 Doraianu, low corbelled arch in the west wall with fallen stone across its sill -> C.16 Nesseora
