Layout: The ravine is entered at its mouth on the south from the forest trail (C.1), and its stream path (E.1) runs 1 mile north along the stream to the head, between walls 80 feet high and 80 yards apart; the path is ten yards wide, a shout carries to every mouth, and a party on it is in bowshot of the ledges on both walls. A goat track runs the east rim above the caves, reached by a climb at the mouth. Its stops in order: the crack at 10 yards, a fissure in the east wall whose worn handholds climb 80 feet to the rim (E.11); the stepping stones at 450 yards, flat stones across the stream; the dry bed mouth at 1,400 yards, where the dry fork enters from the east (E.7); and at the head, 1,760 yards up, the maze door 20 yards west of the path's end (E.8) and the shrine stair 20 yards east (E.9).

Each mouth is a ledge its tribe watches, climbed from the path and entered by a passage 20 feet long. The kobold ledge (E.2), 300 yards north and 40 west, is a six-foot shelf a few paces above the path, grinning faces chalked on the rock above a crawlhole at its back, and a tin rattle faintly heard. The goblin ledge (E.3), 700 yards north and 40 west, lies 30 feet above the path, a wolf skull on a pole by an iron-barred kennel door and a notch above it for slingers, wolves baying behind. The orc ledge (E.4), 1,200 yards north and 40 west, the last on the west wall, lies 50 feet up: a wide shelf littered with bone, a cairn of skulls waist-high before the cave, drunken roaring heard from the path. The hobgoblin ledge (E.5), 600 yards north and 40 east, lies 40 feet above the path behind a built wall 10 feet high with an iron-bound gate and a red hand stencilled beside it, a horn call carrying from it. The gnoll ledge (E.6), 1,000 yards north and 40 east, lies 60 feet up, a narrow shelf with iron collars hung on pegs along its edge and laughing barks heard from below. The dry fork (E.7), 1,400 yards north and 40 east, is a dry stream bed 15 feet wide, level with the path, running 200 yards east under overhanging walls, with soot handprints on the rock higher than a man can reach and no sound in it. The maze door (E.8), 1,760 yards north and 20 west, is a door of iron-bound oak 7 feet high in a frame carved with script, set in the cliff's foot at the head; no light shows at its edges. The shrine stair (E.9), 1,760 yards north and 20 east, climbs fifty steps of makers' stone, 6 feet wide and stained black, to a landing with a gong, and a trodden path from each cave mouth meets its lowest tread. The owlbear's rim (E.10), 1,600 yards north and 42 east, is a shallow cave on the east rim, 80 feet above the path, with a nest of bones at its back and torn feathers on its floor; its hooting carries down the ravine at night, and it is reached only along the rim track (E.11).

The rim track (E.11), 1,000 yards north and 42 east at its hollow, is a goat track 2 feet wide along the brink of the east wall, 80 feet above the path, running 1,600 yards from the crack at the mouth to the owlbear's cave (E.10); loose stone at its edge gives under a heavy load, and a hollow trodden flat above the gnoll ledge shows every ledge on the east wall to a party lying in it for a watch (Local Procedure: Watching the Cave Mouths). The tithe niche (E.12), 1,760 yards north and 18 east, lies 6 feet deep behind a fallen slab leaning at the stair's foot; a party can notice fresh scrape marks at the slab's foot.

Inhabitants: Six Wolves hunt the stream path (E.1) at dusk, hostile, circling a party and rushing the slowest. The ledges are held by the tribes of the walls. On the west wall, three kobolds of The Gubak (Faction: The Gubak) squat in the rubble at E.2 with slings, wary, shaking the rattles and running to warn their warren; two goblins of The Zhakuk (Faction: The Zhakuk) watch from the notch at E.3, hostile, slinging at small parties and waiting for word of the next wagon; three orcs of The Rokmok (Faction: The Rokmok) lie drunk across the cairn at E.4, hostile, charging smaller parties. On the east wall, two hobgoblins of The Burgnaz (Faction: The Burgnaz) stand at the gate of E.5 in mail, wary, challenging and then shutting it; three gnolls of The Kurzun (Faction: The Kurzun) lounge on the ledge at E.6, hostile, hamstringing the slow and wanting captives to sell; two bugbears of The Zurnaz (Faction: The Zurnaz) lie along the overhang at E.7, hostile, dropping on the last in line, for the tithe to be collected. Three acolytes of The Black Altar (Faction: The Black Altar) stand at the landing of the shrine stair (E.9) with knives and a gong, hostile, striking intruders. An Owlbear sleeps on its nest at E.10 by day and attacks whatever enters its cave.

Loot: The ravine floor yields no game or forage that is not an Encounter, and its wealth lies inside the holdings (F to M). The tithe sacks behind the slab at E.12 hold each tribe's share in its own sack, and the keep's Moneychanger (A.7) pays 80% of value for what a party brings out, 90% for gems and jewellery.

Secrets:
- A fallen slab at the stair's foot hides a crevice where the tribes leave their tithe - the Zurnaz know; the scrape marks round the slab (E.9) bring it out.
- The rim track along the east wall looks down on every ledge on it - no tribe watches it; the crack near the mouth (E.1) leads up to it.
