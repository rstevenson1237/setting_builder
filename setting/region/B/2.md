B.2 **Immora** (landmark) - *stair-head, threshold, traffic*
A rectangular mouth of dressed stone standing open in the mud at the fen's eastern edge, with a **cut stair** going down into the dark and a **tide-line** of dried salt scum standing four feet up the stonework all round it, marking where the water used to be. There are **punts drawn up** on the mud beside it most mornings, and a **stack of gear** under a tarpaulin that belongs to people who are currently underneath you.
*A stone-lipped opening about 12 by 8 yards standing proud of the mud, with the stair descending inside it; the drop to the first landing is 40 feet. It sits at Lindmarn's eastern edge, four miles west of Torsgaard and two miles north of the causeway, and it is the busiest place in the region. It was an Odarath entrance and it is one again after four hundred years. The smell coming up out of it is cold and faintly sour, and the air movement is constant - it draws in at dawn and blows out by afternoon, which is the single most useful thing about this location.*
**Cut Stair:** Twenty-two double-height treads of dressed limestone with a shallow ramp beside them, going down forty feet to a landing, dry as far as the eye can follow. The treads are cut deep and even, and the ramp beside them is polished in a band about two feet wide down its centre - something heavy has been going up and down it for a very long time, and it is not the Faelhond. This is the way everybody goes into Siwapelu.
**Tide-Line:** A crust of dried salt and dead weed standing four feet up the outside of the stonework on every face, unbroken, marking the water level of three winters ago. It has not been washed off because the water has not been back up. A party that walks the whole circuit of the stonework will find the crust is broken in exactly one place, low down on the north face, where something the width of a punt has been dragged repeatedly through it into a reed channel - that is the way to Naugfast, and it is nine hundred yards.
**Stack of Gear:** Under a tarred canvas weighted with pan-scrap on the mud beside the opening: coils of line, lead diving weights, shuttered lanterns, and a wicker cage with a pan-finch in it, alive. This is Faelhond kit, cached openly because nobody in Brackvaen would take it. Three or four of them are below at any hour before noon. Whoever is at the head will ask a party's business and will not accept a vague answer; the word 'seal' ends the conversation and the word 'share' begins a different one.
**Draw:** The opening breathes. Cold air pulls down the stair from about an hour before dawn until mid-morning, then reverses and comes up sour and heavy for the rest of the day. The Faelhond work the drawing hours and are out before the reversal, and they will tell a party this for nothing because they would rather not be recovering bodies. A lit taper held at the lip shows which way it is going, and that is the whole of the technique.
**Exits:** dressed stone stair descending inside the opening, twenty-two treads, forty feet -> C.1 Immorael, a punt channel through the reed running north from the broken tide-line on the stonework's north face, nine hundred yards -> B.5 Naugfast, open mud and reed east toward the eastern flats and the rise beyond, four miles of wading and poling with no staked channel
