B.1 **The Washed-Out Ford** (landmark) - *supply road gone under, exposed crossing, half-day detour*
The old supply road drops straight into brown, moving water where the **Washed-Out Ford** has eaten the roadbed whole. Older paving-stones show as darker shapes under the current for anyone who knows to look.
*A crossing roughly 30 yards wide where the road meets a swollen streambed, the original paved verge crumbling into the bank on both sides; smells of wet clay and standing water, and runs louder after rain than the season alone explains.*
**Ford Stones:** Dressed paving stones from the original road, laid some ninety years back when Fellward's charter was still funded, now half-buried in silt and visible only as a straighter line of shadow beneath the current. Following that line keeps the footing sound.
**Silt Channel:** A soft-bottomed gap cut into the old roadbed just downstream of the true crossing, invisible until stepped in. Impact: damaging - it won't drown a careful party, but it costs a pack, a weapon, or an afternoon to get free and dry again. Pale, sun-bleached wood scattered on the near bank, the remains of at least one wagon that missed the stones, is warning enough for anyone who reads it.
**Detour Trail:** A footpath worn along the near bank leads upstream to a shallower, unpaved crossing - a half-day's walk round trip, and the only dry way across when the water runs high.
**Exits:** open ford, worn bank, south -> back along the road toward Fellward; broken roadbed, north bank -> onward along the supply road, deeper into the treeline; footpath, upstream bank, west -> the detour crossing and open country
