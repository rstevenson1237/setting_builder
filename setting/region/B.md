B Oerath

Overview: A party crosses Oerath to reach anything past the causeway at all - there is no
other way around it on foot or horse. The Ashward Riders consider the ground within sight
of the causeway theirs to answer for, and treat anyone digging or camping on it as
trespass rather than passage. What sets this steppe apart from open country elsewhere is
that it is watched, not empty - something is aware of every crossing within a day of
making it.

Ambiance: Wind never fully stops, and grass runs waist-high in every direction, broken
only by wind-carved gullies and the odd standing stone. What little is built here - cairns,
a horse-tribe way-shrine, a collapsed shepherd's hut - is dry-stacked stone with no mortar,
built to be rebuilt rather than maintained.

Terrain: Open rolling grassland, easy walking underfoot but offering no cover for a mile
in most directions. Wind-carved gullies cut across it randomly and can hide a rider or a
body equally well until you're on top of either. Crossing it at night without a fire is
how most people who die out here die.

Foraging: Wild onion and a bitter grey lichen the horse-tribes call "saddle-bread" both
grow abundantly and keep well dried. Game is Steppe Wolf's prey more often than a party's,
and a hunting party competing with a wolf pack for the same kill should expect company.
Nothing foraged here carries any healing value worth the name - that's a Glaswy claim, not
Oerath's.

Layout: Roughly eight named landmarks scattered across a day's ride from the causeway to
the Ashward camps, two to six miles apart. An action costs four hours: a move between
neighbouring landmarks, a search of one, a forage, a tracking attempt, or making camp.

Features: The causeway is the only reliable river-crossing for miles either way; everything
else is a ford that floods with the spring recession. Sound carries far in the open - a
shout or a shot is heard well before whoever made it is seen. Night drops hard and cold
even in warm seasons.

Dangers: Oerath is watched, not indifferent. The Ashward Riders patrol within sight of the
causeway constantly and treat unescorted digging or camping there as trespass; further out,
the land itself is merely lethal rather than hostile.

Creatures: Steppe Wolf packs range throughout (Bestiary); Sand Cat and the odd Horse-lord's
War Stallion turn up in broken ground and open range respectively. Border Raider bands work
the trade routes for toll rather than the Riders' own patrols, and the two are not the same
people, whatever a stranger assumes.

Secrets: Somewhere among the wind-gullies within a few miles of the causeway, the Riders
maintain a cache of tribute-silver taken in toll rather than raid - only a handful of them
know exactly where. What the Riders themselves guard the ground for, beyond tradition, is
Baldun's business more than Oerath's own.

Treasure: Mostly Treasure I scavenged debris left by past travellers and Treasure IV trade
goods lost off carts; a Rider's own gear, if won rather than stolen, runs Treasure II.
Treasure V-grade finds belong to Baldun, not here.

Tables: d6 Encounter table, rolled on each failed Difficulty roll
1. A Steppe Wolf pack shadows the party from a distance, testing before committing.
2. A Border Raider band demands toll for safe passage.
3. An Ashward Riders patrol observes openly from a rise and does not approach unless
   approached.
4. A sudden squall cuts visibility to a few yards for the rest of the four-hour action.
5. A lone Horse-lord's War Stallion contests the ground and has to be avoided or driven off.
6. Tracks cross the party's own path - recent, human, heading toward the causeway at a run.
