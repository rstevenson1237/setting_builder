B The Borderland

Overview: The Borderland is what stands between Greywatch's own walls and the ravine's
mouth: verge cleared by the keep nearest the gate, true wood beyond it that no patrol
holds. Traders too poor to wait for an escort, a raiding band working the road for its own
account, and whatever a household sends up from the ravine to watch or hunt all cross the
same ground for different reasons. It stays this empty because nothing past the cleared
verge answers to Greywatch's own writ, and nobody at the keep is spared to make it
otherwise.

Ambiance: Cleared verge nearest the gate gives way within a mile to true wood; wet timber
and standing rain on the open stretch, woodsmoke that isn't the keep's own fires further
in. The old-tongue boundary stones recur throughout, worn past reading by anyone at
Greywatch now, and every dry camp found off the road shares the same ring of cold
fire-stones, however long since it was used.

Terrain: Mixed ground - cleared verge and worn road nearest the gate, true forest further
out, and low fen ground breaking off the road's edge toward the ravine. Off the road,
footing is uneven and slows anyone not already keeping to a known path; the fen ground
won't bear weight at all without one.

Foraging: Fen-reed, cut from the wet ground, sold at Greywatch for thatching and cordage -
abundant, worth little. A numbing moss gathered from the region's own high broken ground is
locally claimed to dull a wound's pain when packed into it; nobody's tested the claim
against a wound that mattered. No forage here carries any working past GENRE's magic
level - what's found is plant and game, nothing spoken over.

Layout: WILD - four hours per action; a move, a search, a forage, or a tracking attempt
each cost one block, and the region die is rolled once per block moved through open
ground. The road runs from Greywatch's gate toward the ravine's mouth as a rough spine,
with the fen ground breaking off it to one side and the region's high broken ground rising
clear of the treeline on the other.

Features: An old ford crosses the region's one river, waded at low water and forcing a
detour when it isn't. Past dusk, the wood goes quiet all at once rather than gradually.
High ground clear of the treeline is the only place a party can see any real distance
across the region. Fire carries scent and sound both further here than in the true wood.

Dangers: Contested rather than uniformly hostile - a raiding band works the road itself
and states its price before anything else happens, and whatever a household sends up to
scout does not linger once it's been seen.

Creatures: Ravine Lynx (Bestiary) ranges the region's broken high ground and the ravine's
outer mouths both - watching. Herd-Biter (Bestiary) roots the tree line nightly and the
fringes of Greywatch's own fences - avoiding, unless it's already feeding. Road Raiders
(Bestiary) work the road in bands of four to eight - hunting, for whatever's carried
rather than a fight over it.

Factions: Greywatch Garrison claims only the road itself as a route kept, and nowhere past
its verge. No tribe in setting/Factions.md holds ground here outright, though Gorzgur
scouts the region's edge from the ravine side.

Secrets: Something buried in the fen ground before Greywatch ever stood is still watched
by whatever was set over it. One of the region's hermits is said to have served the shrine
himself before walking out on it, and hasn't said why to anyone at the keep. A second way
down into the ravine is known to whoever traps this ground for a living, and comes out
somewhere the watched road doesn't.

Treasure: Pelts and hides taken in the ordinary course of hunting or trapping here, and
old-tongue coin occasionally dropped or discarded by whatever's moving loot out of the
ravine. Draws on `setting/Treasure1.md` (Scavenged Loot) and `setting/Treasure4.md`
(Luxury and Trade Goods).

Tables: d6 Encounter table, rolled on each failed Difficulty roll
1. A Road Raider band has the way blocked ahead and states its price.
2. A Ravine Lynx watches from cover at range, and breaks off if challenged directly.
3. A Herd-Biter is already feeding nearby and charges if approached.
4. Fresh raider sign crosses the path - hoofprints at an angle no trader's cart would take.
5. The wood goes quiet all at once, well before dusk.
6. Something is watching from the high ground and breaks line of sight the moment it's
   noticed.
