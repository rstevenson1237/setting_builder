B Pelren

Overview: Pelren is the only way past Fellward toward anywhere else, and the point past
which the keep's reach actually ends - no bell, no roster, and no wall answers for anyone
out here. Every hour spent on open ground past the gate is an hour the region die gets
rolled against whoever's crossing it. The one thing true here that isn't true anywhere
else in the setting: the boundary stones marking the treeline's edge are the only fixed
thing in the region - everything else in it moves, including what's hunting.

Ambiance: Cleared verge nearest the gate gives way within a mile to true wood cover, cut
by the old paved supply road. Wet timber and standing rain on the cleared stretch; deeper
in, woodsmoke that isn't the keep's own fires. The boundary stones stand at irregular
intervals, old-tongue-carved and weathered past reading by anyone at Fellward now.

Terrain: Mixed ground - cleared verge and washed-out paved road nearest Fellward, true
forest cover further out. The road itself is washed out past the first mile, forcing foot
travel or a wide detour around the gap; off the road, footing is uneven and slows anyone
not already keeping to a known path.

Foraging: Fen-reed, cut from the wet grazing ground the Fen Aurochs graze, sold at
Fellward for thatching and cordage - abundant, worth little. Quarry-moss, gathered from
the high broken ground the Quarry Lynx hunts from, is locally claimed to slow bleeding
when packed into a wound - nobody's tested the claim against a real wound and lived to
argue about it either way.

Layout: WILD - four hours per action; a move between neighbouring points, a search, a
forage, or a tracking attempt each cost one block, and the region die is rolled once per
block moved through open ground. The road runs from Fellward's gate as a rough spine
roughly six miles to the boundary stones, with side paths breaking off it toward the
Fen Auroch wetlands and the Quarry Lynx's high ground.

Features: The washed-out stretch of road, roughly a mile past the gate, forces a choice
between fording exposed ground or a half-day detour through the trees. The boundary
stones themselves are a stated fact of this setting: no scent, human or otherwise, carries
more than a few hundred yards past them - anything tracked runs cold there without
exception.

Dangers: Contested rather than uniformly hostile - the Road Toll Gang holds a stretch of
the road and states its price before anything else happens; the Deserter Band works the
verges and would rather talk a toll than fight for one. Neither is alive to the region as
a whole; both react only to what crosses their own ground.

Creatures: Treeline Hounds range the whole borderland side of the treeline rather than
denning anywhere fixed. Longshank Wolf pairs follow game across the region's full width
and will trail a party for hours without attacking if easier prey is near. Fen Aurochs
graze the wet ground in a loose seasonal circuit; a Quarry Lynx holds the high broken
ground and lets anyone who doesn't linger pass unseen. The Deserter Band, the Road Toll
Gang, and Trapper Clans working a network of traplines all move through here, along with
Charter Agents traveling the road on a schedule nobody's seen kept in years.

Secrets: A barrow entrance the Diggers have already scouted somewhere off the road's far
stretch, not yet marked on anything the keep holds. The Barrow-Kin move their boundary
markers closer to the treeline's edge a little at a time, warning before they act on
anyone who ignores it. The Trapper Clans know a way around the washed-out road and will
sell it for coin, salt, or iron.

Treasure: Pelts and Fen Auroch hides taken in the ordinary course of hunting or trapping
here; old-tongue coin occasionally dropped or discarded by crews moving loot out of
Ingaraz. Draws on `setting/Treasure1.md` (Scavenged Loot) and `setting/Treasure4.md`
(Luxury and Trade Goods).

Tables: d6 Encounter table, rolled on each failed Difficulty roll
1. A Treeline Hound pack bays at range and closes over the next several minutes.
2. The Road Toll Gang has a felled tree across the road and states its price.
3. A Deserter Band asks for coin first, weapons only if refused and they outnumber.
4. A Longshank Wolf pair trails at a distance, watching rather than closing.
5. A Trapper Clan offers a known safe route around whatever's ahead, for a price.
6. A Diggers crew moves fast with a loaded cart, avoiding contact rather than risking it.
