B Lindmarn

Overview: Eighty square miles of standing brackish water, reed bed and floating peat, lying over the drowned grid of the Odarath's original pans - which means the ground under Lindmarn is not ground but a four-hundred-year-old ruin, cut into rectangles, and the fen's channels run where its cuts ran. Nobody has settled it and nobody will: it has no firm ground worth building on, it drowns anything left standing over a winter, and it is held - genuinely and completely - by the Vingash, who have watched it since before Torsgaard was founded and who make it passable or impassable by tying and untying cord. The Marchfolk built one causeway across it a hundred and twenty years ago and have never built a second. Run Lindmarn as the tax on everything: the party crosses it to reach Siwapelu, crosses it again carrying more than they came with, and it is the return crossing that the setting is really about. It is a d6 region because it punishes carelessness rather than boldness - six landmarks at a one-in-six bite, short and spiky, with exactly one encounter expected across a full traverse like every other die.

Ambiance: Flat to the horizon in every direction and enormous with sky, so that the only vertical things in sight are reed, channel stakes, and the occasional black stump of Odarath stonework standing out of the water at an angle. The reed runs eight and ten feet high in the beds and closes over a punt completely, so that most of a crossing is made in a green corridor with no view at all, which is the single fact that makes Lindmarn dangerous. It smells of brackish water, rotting reed, and the flat mineral smell of the pans; on a still hot day the gorm comes up off the water in the old cuts and the smell goes sour and faintly sweet. The sound is wind in reed - constant, loud enough to cover a boat at thirty yards - and wildfowl, and nothing else. In winter the beds go brown and lie flat and a party can see for miles, which is the season the Vingash prefer not to be crossed in. Anything built here is Vingash: reed and withy on staked platforms, lashed rather than pegged, and taken apart and moved rather than repaired.

Terrain: Fen - specifically, drowned worked ground, which behaves unlike natural marsh in one way that matters constantly. The old pan walls are still down there, so the water is not a gradient: it is a grid of chest-deep to head-deep rectangles separated by knee-deep walls, and the difference between a crossing and a drowning is whether you are on a wall or in a cut. This is what the channel stakes and the knotted cord are *for*. Off the staked channels, movement is a matter of poling a flat punt through reed at walking pace where the beds allow it, and wading where they do not; either way a party makes perhaps two miles in a four-hour action and arrives wet to the chest. The floating peat is worse than the water - it holds a man for four or five steps and then does not, and it does not look different. There is firm ground on perhaps a dozen reed islands, none of them large, all of them known.

Foraging: Abundant and easy, which is the fen's one kindness and the reason a party can stay out here longer than is good for them. **Eel** in every channel, taken in withy traps, and eel is what everything in Lindmarn eats including the Vingash. **Wildfowl** in enormous numbers on passage, netted or limed - a fowler's week here feeds Torsgaard for a month. **Reed** for thatch, withy for baskets and traps, and **bog oak** cut out of the peat, which is the best timber in Brackvaen and takes two men a day to get one length free. **Sedge-root**, locally *marn-bread*, edible, dull, and everywhere. Two things carry a price beyond their use: **fen-purse**, a swollen grey fungus growing only on Odarath stump-stone above the waterline, which the Vingash chew before going into gorm-heavy air and which genuinely works - it is uncommon, it does not keep more than a few days, and Torsgaard cannot buy it because the Vingash will not sell it; and **kkith-moss**, the pale weed growing in the western pans since the Gell Year, which is said to close a wound and does, and which is said to do so without the wound healing underneath, and does that too.

Layout: A rough oval about twelve miles east to west and eight north to south, with Torsgaard on the rise at its eastern end and open water and the old sea beyond its western edge. The **causeway** is the spine - a raised bank of stone and rubble, twelve feet wide, running the full twelve miles from Torsgaard west, built on top of an Odarath pan wall and consequently arrow-straight and in poor repair. Everything else is channel. Landmarks sit two to four miles apart, and there is no route between two of them that does not involve either the causeway or a channel somebody else holds. **An action costs four hours**, and one action buys: a move between neighbouring landmarks, a search of one landmark, a forage, a tracking attempt, or making camp. Four actions to a day, and a full traverse of Lindmarn is a two-day proposition each way for a party that knows where it is going and considerably more for one that does not. The stair-head into Siwapelu stands at the eastern edge, about four miles out from Torsgaard, where the water went out.

Features: **The channel stakes and the cord** are the region's central interactive fact - they are everywhere, they are readable, and reading them is a skill a party can actually acquire from the Vingash rather than roll for. Correctly tied cord gives the depth of the next reach; cord cut off rather than untied means Drowned-road Wreckers have been at it; no cord at all means the Vingash have withdrawn from this reach and a party should ask itself why. **The reed** cuts sightlines to nothing in summer and opens them to miles in winter, and this single seasonal swing changes how every encounter here plays. **The gorm** collects in the deeper old cuts on still days and a party wading one can walk into it without warning - the Gorm track in `setting/Procedures.md` runs in Lindmarn's cuts as well as underground. **From the causeway**, which is the only high ground, a party can see the whole region and be seen crossing it for twelve miles. **At night** the fen is completely impassable off the staked channels and the Vingash move freely on it.

Dangers: Watched, and mostly indifferent. Lindmarn will drown a careless party without any agency being involved at all - the cut, the peat, the cold - and that accounts for most of what happens here. What is not indifferent is the Vingash, who see every crossing, and whose response is graduated and almost never violent: cord tied correctly ahead of a party that has behaved, watchers made visible for a party that has not, and a reach retied wrong behind a party that has cut cord or gone toward the stair. That last is how Lindmarn kills people who could otherwise handle it. Below the Vingash sits the ordinary predation - the Wreckers on the causeway's worst stretches, the eels in the deep cuts - and west of the causeway the region stops being merely dangerous, because the Vingash will not go there and do not explain.

Creatures: **Vingash Reedwatchers** in twos and threes across the whole region, seen when they choose to be, and each carrying the cord of the reaches they hold - a party that takes one alive has taken the region's navigation. **Vingash Channel-Elders**, three of them, each at a deep-water fastness the causeway does not approach; they hold what the Vingash know about Nessekire, they will trade it, and their price is a commitment about the stair. **Brine Eels** in the deep old cuts, perhaps four of them in Lindmarn, hunting from below and taking the last person in a wading line - their Sign is the whole of the party's chance and it is available. **Reedback Hogs** through the beds in sounders, plus three solitary boars that will not give way. **Marsh Lymers**, two packs, working the causeway verges and waiting for a party to string out. **Mire Aurochs** on the reed islands, four of them, tolerant of everything except being put between a party and the water. **Gaunt Herons** at a bird to the mile of channel. **Sump Leeches** everywhere, unavoidable, and the reason nobody wades in Lindmarn who can pole. West of the causeway, in the flooded Gell Year pans: **Sump-Kin**, perhaps sixty, surfacing to watch anything that stays out of the water and taking anything that does not.

Secrets: What Lindmarn is hiding is mostly underneath it. The whole fen is a drowned working, and there are Odarath stones still standing in it - some of them cut, and at least one still holding whatever a Nuthora Oathkeeper holds, thirty yards of ground in the middle of open water where nothing grows or nests. Somewhere in the western pans is what the Gell Year actually was, which the Vingash call *Kkithik* and will not discuss and did not cause. The three Vingash fastnesses are real, reachable, and have never been found by Torsgaard. And the fen holds the other half of the Nessekire question: the barrier that failed four hundred years ago is still down there at the western edge, and it did not fail along its whole length.

Treasure: Lindmarn is poor and its rewards are mostly practical - what a party finds here is `setting/Treasure1.md`, salvage off the causeway and out of drowned punts, plus the foraged goods above, which are worth real coin in Torsgaard precisely because getting them across is the difficulty. The exceptions are worth knowing. Bog oak and prime pelts are `setting/Treasure4.md` and are the region's characteristic problem: heavy, valuable, and twelve miles from the scales. Anything come off an Odarath stone or out of a drowned cut is `setting/Treasure3.md` and is a completely different kind of trouble. The Vingash have no coin and do not want any; they trade in cord, passage, iron rings, and knowledge, and a party that grasps this is holding the cheapest currency in Brackvaen.

Tables: d6 Encounter Table - rolled on each failed Difficulty roll.
1. Cord. The next reach is staked and the knots are fresh and correctly tied, or the knots are wrong, or there is no cord at all - and which of the three it is depends entirely on what the party has done in Lindmarn so far. This is the region's most common answer and it is not an encounter so much as a verdict.
2. Sump Leeches, discovered rather than met - somebody has been carrying four or five of them since the last wade, and finds out when they take their boots off.
3. Marsh Lymers, a pack of eight, circling at fifty yards along a causeway verge and waiting to see whether the party closes up. They will not commit against a party that stops.
4. Vingash watchers, two or three, standing in plain view on a reed island at a hundred yards, making no approach and no attempt at concealment. They are counting the party and looking at what it is carrying.
5. A Reedback boar, solitary and old, on a stretch of wall too narrow to go around, which charges from fifteen yards without display.
6. The water is wrong. A cut that should be knee-deep is over a head, or the reach the party came in by three hours ago is gone, or the gorm has come up off a still cut and put a lamp out at noon. Something in Lindmarn has moved and the party's route home is not where they left it.
