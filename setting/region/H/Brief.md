Layout: The warren has one mouth on the ravine's west wall, the orc ledge (E.4), 1,200 yards up from the ravine's mouth and 50 feet above the path, with a cairn of skulls waist-high before it. The cairn mouth (H.1) opens 20 feet west of the ledge: a hall mouth 30 feet square and 40 deep, skulls heaped to a man's height beside its door, rib bones scattered across the floor and a notched tusk nailed above the arch at its back. The warren is gutted makers' halls of cut stone, ceilings 12 feet and rooms wide and square; light is torches in iron brackets, sound is lost in the big halls, the footing is slick with spilled brew and offal, the air reeks of smoke and meat, and a lit fire is no alarm.

The chief's cave (H.6), the one High location, lies 70 feet west of the feast hall (H.2) through a banded door, 130 feet west of the cairn mouth (H.1). It is a cave 40 feet by 40 with a ceiling of 14 feet, hung with skulls and axes, a fire pit at its centre and a bed heaped against the far wall, an iron-bound chest under the bed holding Hurk's Hoard (Hoard: Hurk's Hoard) and a rack of notched trophy axes of bone; Hurk the Brewmaster (Named Creature: Hurk the Brewmaster) drinks at the fire three nights in four with four orcs sprawled round him.

The feast hall (H.2) lies 60 feet west of the cairn mouth (H.1): a hall 80 feet by 40 with a ceiling of 12 feet, long tables down its length, spits turning at both ends, spoils heaped behind the high table and a notched tusk nailed over each of its five doors. The pens (H.3) lie 50 feet north of the hall (H.2): a stone passage 60 feet long with four plank pens along it, boar squealing in the first three and a stone trough in the last. The still (H.4) lies 40 feet south of the hall (H.2) through an archway: a low hall 40 feet by 30 with a ceiling of 10 feet, four copper vats on brick hearths along one wall fired from below with coals, a coil of tubing and a sump under the largest vat; a party can notice a sharp reek and scorched stone round the largest vat's tap. The walled stores (H.5) lie 50 feet west of the still (H.4) through a broken door: a store 50 feet by 10 with sacks stacked along both walls, a makers' door smashed from its frame at the near end and plain square-cut stone at the far end, a cold draught under the sacks.

Inhabitants: Three of The Rokmok (Faction: The Rokmok) sit on the ledge below, at E.4. Two Orcs drink on the watch at H.1 day and night, hostile, roaring and charging, wanting something smaller to hit; they know which captain relieves them. Ten of The Rokmok quarrel over the spits at the tables of the feast hall (H.2), hostile, brawling one another before they rush an intruder as one, wanting their captain in the chief's seat; they know when the goblins loose their wolves. Hurk the Brewmaster (Named Creature: Hurk the Brewmaster) is at his fire in H.6 three nights in four, hostile, bellowing for the hall and fighting to hold his seat, wanting the goblins broken; four Orcs round the fire pit guard him until he falls and then bolt.

Alarm: A roar at H.1 raises it. The feast hall (H.2) hears at once and brawls for a Round before it rushes the mouth; Hurk in H.6 hears a Round later, and his bellow brings the hall's orcs to his cave in two Rounds. The stores (H.5) hear nothing.

Loot: The warren's wealth is the ledge path's spoils and the chief's hoard. The largest prize is Hurk's Hoard (Hoard: Hurk's Hoard) in the iron-bound chest under the bed at H.6, with Hurk and four orcs over it and the trophy axes on the rack beside it, one of them not like the rest (Magic, d12); Treasure II lies behind the high table at H.2, Treasure I in the trough of the end pen at H.3, Treasure III in the sump under the vat at H.4 and Treasure IV among the sacks at H.5. The Moneychanger (A.7) buys it back at 80% of value.

Secrets:
- The largest vat holds spirit that bursts into flame near a fire - the Rokmok know; the sharp reek and scorched stone round it (H.4) bring it out.
- The lowest stones at the end of the stores lift out onto a makers' passage to the goblins' stores - neither tribe knows; the draught under the sacks (H.5) brings it out.
