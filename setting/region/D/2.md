D.2 **Upper Terrace** (medium) - *Black Ziggurat, Open Roof*
Open to the sky along its whole width, this terrace served as the priest-kings' own open-air oratory. The whole outer stretch of its **parapet** stands undercut where five centuries of weather have eaten the mortar out from behind it, and it is the only thing between a standing figure and a thirty-foot drop.
*An open-air oratory thirty feet across, its low parapet worn round rather than square. Smells of clean air, out of place this high in a place otherwise thick with old ash; wind is the only sound.*
**Undercut Parapet:** The waist-high basalt rail along the open edge is sound to look at and hollow behind - the mortar has washed out of the whole outer stretch, and the coping course sits on nothing but its own weight. Leaning, sitting or bracing against it puts the stone and whoever used it over the edge together (Damaging; putting weight on the outer parapet -> a thirty-foot fall to the tier below, taking a course of coping down with you).
**Worn Rest:** One stretch of the rail's top course is worn smooth where hands rested on it far more than anywhere else - and it is the one stretch still properly bedded, which is visible from directly above and from nowhere else.
**Loose Coping Stone:** Within the worn stretch, a single coping stone sits loose rather than mortared. Lifting it clear reveals a shallow cavity cut into the rail itself, packed and closed by whoever last stood here and never came back for it (Treasure I, d20).
**Exits:** vertical stair - basalt, narrow and open to the drop on one side, south side -> D.5 Collapsed Portico, vertical stair - basalt, eight steps down between two columns, west side -> D.4 Wind-Worn Colonnade
