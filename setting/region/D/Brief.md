Layout: The causeway (D.1), 3 miles long, is entered from the road at the causeway head (B.5), 9 miles from the keep gate, and from the wood at its north end, where a path of firm ground runs on to the mere landing (C.6) on the north shore. It runs north on black piles, a yard wide, with passing bays every hundred yards and no rails: a watch's walk on the planks, half that by pole boat. Its stops in order: the flat edge at half a mile, where tussocks lead east onto the sinking flat (D.4); the shrine channel at 1.4 miles, a reed channel running east to the drowned shrine (D.3); the boat landing at 2 miles, where pole boats wait and the stilt village lies a quarter-mile east (D.2); and the far shore at 3 miles, where the planks end at the north shore and the path to C.6 begins.

The stilt village (D.2), 2 miles north and a quarter-mile east of the causeway, is twelve huts on stilts round a central platform, joined by rope bridges, with smoked fish on reed racks and fish-bone points standing upright in the mud at the foot of every post; smoke rises over the reeds, and a party reaches it by pole boat from the landing at 2 miles. The drowned shrine (D.3), 2 miles north-east of the causeway's foot, is a hall 40 feet by 40 under black water, knee-deep at the pillars and over a man's head beyond, its green stone pillars standing out of the water with steps leading down and eight motionless figures among them; a party reaches it by the reed channel, 1.4 miles east of the causeway. The sinking flat (D.4), half a mile across, lies 0.5 miles north and 1 mile east of the causeway's foot: a level grey flat of mud and algae with gas bubbling in the shallows and tussocks of sedge a stride apart, crossed by the tussocks from the flat edge.

The hidden island (D.5), 2.5 miles north and a quarter-mile west of the causeway, lies 0.7 miles north-west of the village by pole boat and cannot be seen from the causeway: a dry knoll 30 feet across ringed by tall reeds, a cairn of white bones in a ring of stones at its top and a landing of stakes and planks on its south-east side. A party can notice the red thread binding the stones of the cairn, and the wake of the village boat running from the platform's north-west edge into the reeds.

Inhabitants: The Sellaca (Faction: The Sellaca) hold the mere. Four lizard-folk with fish-bone spears watch the causeway (D.1) from the reeds, wary, letting those who keep to the planks pass, wanting strangers kept to them and knowing who crosses and when; twelve live in the stilt village (D.2), mending nets on the platform, hostile, spearing anyone who lands unasked, wanting iron, salt and tools from the road and knowing which flats swallow a man whole. Eight Zombies stand among the pillars of the drowned shrine (D.3), hostile, walking toward the living and never stopping. The Sellaca run the crossing (Local Procedure: Crossing by Causeway or Pole Boat).

Loot: The mere yields no game, forage or treasure that is not an Encounter, and the nearest SAFE region pays nothing for what is in it. A pole boat with a spear's escort costs 30 cn; the dead at D.3 carry nothing a party can sell.

Secrets:
- The cairn on the hidden island is bound with red thread, and disturbing the bones brings boats from the reeds - the Sellaca know; the red-bound stones (D.5) bring it out.

Leads:
- Rumour 6 - D.1 Watchers, D.2 Villagers.
- Rumour 12 - D.3 Dead Men, D.5 Cairn.

Tables:
d6 Encounter
1. A pole boat slides from the reeds with two spears in it, silent, and falls in behind the party.
2. A plank gives under the lead crosser and drops them to the waist.
3. Fog closes over the water, and the causeway's edges vanish.
4. A pale shape stands motionless in the water, then walks toward the party.
5. Marsh gas bubbles up beside the planks, and a spark would set it alight.
6. A heron lifts from the reeds and the watchers ahead go still.
