A Roadsend Keep

Overview: A party comes to outfit, hire, sell what it carries home and hear what is said of the ravine. The Keep holds the walls, the Carters' Guild keeps a hall in the market yard, and the Black Altar has two agents living here unknown. Wagons have been lost near the wood's edge for two months, and the stores last the winter only if the next wagons arrive. If the Carters' Guild stops running convoys, market prices double within the month. If the commander is shown the Black Altar's hand, he sends a patrol into the ravine; none has gone yet.

View: First seen from the road three miles west: a grey stone hold on a bare hill some 60 feet above the plain. A curtain wall 20 feet high, walked along its top, runs about 160 yards by 120; twin towers of 30 feet stand over the west gate, and an inner wall 12 feet high cuts off the eastern third. The chapel's bell-cote shows over the wall, a blue pennant with a white tower flies from each gate tower, and a lantern is lit at the gate at dusk.

Approach: The main gate stands open from dawn to dusk. A Soldier asks a stranger's name and business and holds one who cannot answer; a stranger's blade is peace-strapped inside the walls. The postern opens on a Soldier's word, for patrols and for a party carrying the commander's pass. Both are barred at dusk, and whoever is outside camps under the wall.

Law: Drawn steel inside the walls costs 50 cn, or a night in the cells. Theft costs the goods returned and 100 cn, or the lash. A killing is held for the commander's judgement. A stranger in the outer yard after dusk is held until morning. The Keep's Soldiers enforce all of it.

Arrival:
- A.1 - A passage through the middle of the west wall. The clerk's ledger lies open on a desk there, and he turns to its wagon page for anyone who asks which wagons run when.
- A.2 - A narrow door in the north wall of the outer yard, the way to the forest trail (C.1).

Plan: The inner wall, with one gate at its middle, divides the outer yard, open to any visitor, from the inner ward. The chapel (A.9), the commander's hall (A.10) and the barracks (A.11) stand in the inner ward; the rest stand round the market yard (A.8) in the outer yard. The barracks' wall stair is the only way up to the wall walk.

Places:
- A.3 - Common room under granary beams, eight rooms above and a stable for twelve mounts: full on a wagon day, half empty between.
- A.4 - Bone dice rattle at the hearth table and stakes are chalked by the bar; the floor by the casks behind the bar is scraped.
- A.5 - Barrels, rope and jars to the roof, the shelves thinning before each wagon day.
- A.6 - Open to the yard, a rack of blades and shields by the anvil.
- A.7 - A barred counter and a chained scale before a strongroom of iron with two keys.
- A.8 - Stalls and carts, and wages chalked on the notice board by the carters' guildhall.
- A.9 - A cold nave open dawn to dusk, the old tower of Law cut into its altar and a sacristy door beside it; its bell rings at dusk.
- A.10 - A map table with the road drawn to the ravine's mouth and "number unknown" written beside the ravine, before a dais whose step holds a ring polished by hands.
- A.11 - Forty bunks, the wall stair at the far end and the alarm drum by the door.
- A.12 - A damp cellar under the Broken Wheel, lit by black candles, a torn ledger page under its table.
- A.13 - A tunnel 4 feet high from steps under the commander's hall, beneath the east wall to a rusted door in the hillside.
