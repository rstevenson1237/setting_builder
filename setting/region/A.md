A Carrdun

Overview: Every company that crosses the causeway stops here first, because nothing past
it sells food, buys salvage, or offers a locked door. The commander's own factors take a
cut of anything traded inside the walls, coin or salvage alike, and say so openly. Carrdun
is the only place in the region where a fight draws consequence faster than it draws a
crowd.

Ambiance: Sun-bleached mudbrick barracks and market stalls lean against a wall built from
unmistakably older, darker stone; smoke and dust hang over the market row by day, and
someone is always hammering iron somewhere in earshot. The air carries river-damp even
this far from the water, and grain-dust from the granary drifts on any light wind.

People: Soldiers, dig-crew hands paid off the books, and the handful of merchants who
supply both make up most of Carrdun's population. There is no resident priest, no
moneylender who extends credit past a week, and no smith who isn't already under contract
to the garrison. Newcomers are looked over rather than welcomed - everyone here is already
someone's business, and a stranger is nobody's yet - but coin and a working pair of hands
buy acceptance fast.

Situation: The commander has been selling excavation rights into Baldun for three years
without the crown's knowledge, and the digging crews now outnumber the garrison itself two
to one. Officers who ask too many questions about the arrangement get reassigned to gate
duty, and the quartermaster who first found the breach hasn't been seen giving orders in
months.

Layout: A single walled enclosure around a market row, barracks, the granary tower, and the
commander's hall, with one gate facing the causeway and a smaller postern facing the river
landing. About six places inside are worth a party's attention on their own account;
everything else is wall, barracks, or storage. SAFE is not time-bound here - hours aren't
tracked unless the Situation or an Event demands it.

Features: Prices run about a third higher than any river-town - Carrdun pays for its
remoteness. The gate closes at full dark and opens at first light; anyone still outside
waits until morning. The garrison will not extend its law past the causeway, no matter
what's asked.

Dangers: Carrdun is safe from the country around it and nothing else. Debt to the
commander's factors compounds weekly and is collected in labour if not coin; anyone caught
undercutting the Concern's digging contracts answers to more than just the commander.

Creatures: Man, overwhelmingly - Border Raider and Garrison Deserter both pass through
under different names depending which side of the law they're currently on (Bestiary). The
one exception is penned livestock kept in the granary's lower level, which a careful eye
will notice doesn't breed true.

Secrets: What the crown doesn't know is exactly how much the commander has taken in
off-book payment. The quartermaster who found the original breach is the only other person
who could prove it - if he's still willing to.

Treasure: What changes hands here is mostly Treasure I and Treasure IV goods - trade
stock, tools, food - at markup; Treasure II equipment can be bought or commissioned
outright rather than found. Carrdun's own coin is standard cn; drowned-coin turns up in
the market occasionally, taken only at a steep discount by traders who know what it is.

Tables: d6 Events table, rolled on entry and each week thereafter
1. A dig-crew argument over an undeclared find turns into a brawl in the market row.
2. A courier arrives asking pointed questions on behalf of the crown - the commander's
   factors intercept him before he reaches anyone else.
3. A shipment of tools and grain arrives late, and prices on everything spike for a few
   days.
4. An officer who asked too many questions about the digging contracts is quietly
   reassigned to gate duty.
5. A dead ox turns up in the granary's lower level, and nobody who feeds the animals will
   explain how.
6. A trader offers to buy drowned-coin at more than twice the going rate, no questions
   asked - and pointedly, no explanation offered either.
