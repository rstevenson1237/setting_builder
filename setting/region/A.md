A Torsgaard

Overview: A holding of some four hundred people on the only rise east of the fen that stays dry in a spring tide, built a hundred and twenty years ago by Marchfolk coming down out of the wold and living since on one thing: it is where salt is weighed. The pans are cut out in the flats below it and worked by crews who sleep here; the Salt Assize measures those pans, records what every crew owes, and is the nearest thing to a government the place has, which is to say it is a sixty-year-old settlement about boundaries being asked to do work it was never written for. Torsgaard exists in spite of Lindmarn rather than beside it - everything it does not make itself comes forty miles up a causeway that the fen closes at its own discretion - and what it has traded for that is any ability to be particular about who it lets in. Run it as the place where a party turns a haul into coin and a rumour into a name, and where every one of those transactions is recorded against them.

Ambiance: Low buildings of bog oak and pan-scrap, black-timbered and silvered where the salt has got at them, roofed in reed thatch weighted with netted stones against the wind off the flats. Everything is built up on staddles a foot or two above the ground and nothing is built of anything that rots, which gives the whole holding a stilted, temporary look it has had for six generations. It smells of brine, wet reed, tallow, and the ammoniac stink of the salt stores. The constant sound is water - not waves, but the flat trickle and suck of the pans working somewhere below the rise - and under it the knock of the weigh-house scales, which run from before dawn until the light goes. Salt stands white in every crack and seam in the place, and the older a thing is here the whiter it is.

People: Marchfolk, four generations in, weathered and undersized and civil to strangers in a way that is genuinely civil and not remotely warm. They will answer a question, sell you what they have, and not ask where you have been, and every one of those is a policy rather than a courtesy. **Present**: salters and pan crews, who are most of the holding; the Assize with its thirty serjeants and its clerks; a cooper, a ropewalk, two boat-builders, a tanner working hides nobody wants downriver; a weigh-house; a fowler; drovers who run the causeway when the Vingash allow it. **Conspicuously absent**: there is no smith worth the name - iron comes up the causeway and pan-scrap is worked cold by whoever needs it, and a party that breaks a weapon here does not get it mended. There is no healer, only a bone-setter who is also the tanner. There is no priest and no temple of anything, which visitors notice and nobody explains. And there is no lord, no reeve, and no garrison; the Assize is it, and the Assize's authority is a document. **Standing**: held by whoever the ledger says is owed, which currently means three or four families and one woman who has not left her own house in eleven years. **The Brined** are here in numbers now - perhaps forty - working, housed, drinking in the same places, and unable to inherit, be sworn against, or hold a pan in their own name. Nobody is cruel to them. Nobody has decided what they are, either, and the holding is running out of time to not decide.

Situation: **The eastern pans are producing again and the ledger cannot hold it.** Since the water dropped three years ago the eastern flats have taken salt better than they have in a generation, and the hauls coming up the stair on top of that are worth more than salt - so crews are working pans they do not hold, selling through people who are not the weigh-house, and settling debts in Odarath coin that the Assize has no column for. *Responsible*: nobody, which is the problem; it is a boom, and it is behaving like one. *Affected*: everyone, unevenly - the families who lost pans in the Gell Year are watching people who kept theirs get rich a second time. *Which rung it is on now*: the Assize has begun measuring pans it has not measured since the settlement and nailing fresh lead to them, and two crews have already had a season's work formally established as not theirs. *What the next rung looks like*: the Assize invokes the boundary stones publicly against a crew rather than a family - which has never been done, works for reasons nobody understands, and will be watched by four hundred people who have all quietly wondered what those stones actually are. This is true whether or not the party ever engages with it, and it moves a rung roughly each season.

Layout: A low oval rise perhaps six hundred yards across, ringed by the drying floors and the salt stores, with the weigh-house and the Assize hall at the top of it where the ground is driest. There are no walls - the fen is the wall, and everyone here knows exactly how good a wall it is. Three approaches: the causeway running west into Lindmarn, which is the only way anything heavy moves; the pan tracks running south and east down into the eastern flats, which are walkable in any season by anyone who does not mind wet feet; and the landing on the north side, where the shallow-draught boats come up from downriver eight or nine times a year and the holding briefly has anything at all. From the weigh-house to the furthest drying floor is about four hundred yards, and from the rise to the stair-head at Lindmarn's eastern edge is a full day. **SAFE is not time-bound: do not track hours in Torsgaard.** Nothing here is gated behind anything else - a party can go to any of the holding's locations directly, on arrival, in any order.

Features: **Law** is the settlement and nothing else - there is no criminal code, and what the Assize can actually do to a person is measure them out of their living. Violence between individuals is a matter for the individuals, which cuts both ways for a party. **Prices** run about half again on anything that came up the causeway (iron, oil, rope, cloth, wine) and are very cheap on anything the fen produces (salt, eel, fowl, reed, bog oak, hides). **The holding will not trade for**: Odarath coin at anything near its metal value, and a party trying to spend it in the open is making a public statement about where it has been. **The weigh-house scales** are the only ones anybody trusts and using them is not optional if you want silver rather than barter - which means the Assize sees every haul that becomes money. **Gate hours** do not exist; there is no gate. Outsiders may go anywhere except the salt stores and the Assize hall's back room, and are expected to sleep somewhere that has a name to give.

Dangers: Not the fen. Torsgaard's dangers are its arithmetic. **Debt** - salt-debt attaches to goods and a party that has taken a dead stranger's kit out of Lindmarn or Siwapelu is carrying weight it has not been told about yet, which the weigh-house will find. **The ledger** - the Assize does not need to accuse anyone of anything to ruin them, and it does not, and that is exactly why it is dangerous. **Being remembered** - four hundred people, one weigh-house, and every haul recorded; a party cannot do anything here twice without it being a pattern somebody has noticed. **Faction interest** - all three powers touch Torsgaard, and the Faelhond and the Vingash are both here in a form that does not announce itself. **Feud** - the Gell Year families and the families who kept their pans, eighteen years on and one bad season from arithmetic.

Creatures: Man, overwhelmingly, working. **Assize Serjeants** in pairs, measuring, and they will ask a party's business on the first day and not the second. **Faelhond Diggers** lodging in houses nobody officially rents to them, coming and going before dawn - the grey smear on a doorframe is visible all over the lower holding once a party knows to look. **Salt-reevers** are here too, indistinguishable from pan-hands until they have something to sell. Beyond Man: **Saltrats** in every store in the holding, which is a standing nuisance and the reason for the cats; **Saltcrows** over the drying floors in numbers, tolerated because they take the shellfish; and penned fowl and a few hardy fen cattle. Anything else in Torsgaard is either working, penned, or a problem, and the Brined are none of the three, which is the whole difficulty.

Secrets: The Assize holds the only complete text of the settlement, and at least two of its heads know it grants something they have never explained - the eleven boundary stones are not eleven because eleven pans needed dividing. Nobody in Torsgaard can read the stones and nobody will touch them, and the holding has an inherited horror of the old letters that it experiences as ordinary common sense. The Faelhond are an organised body operating in plain sight, and the Assize has them filed as a sickness and a labour supply; the day it works out otherwise is the day the stair is contested. Also: the water in the eastern pans did not drop for any reason anyone here can name, and the Vingash, who are the only people frightened by that rather than delighted, have not been asked.

Treasure: Little is found in Torsgaard and a great deal passes through it. **In stock**: rope, tarred line, lamp oil, canvas, boats, salt, cured eel, reed, bog oak, hides, and pan-scrap - `setting/Treasure1.md` is what the holding actually contains, and most of it is for sale rather than lying about. **Not in stock**: quality arms and armour, which come up the causeway rarely and go fast, so `setting/Treasure2.md` is what a party spends here rather than finds. Money is standard silver coin (cn), a pan-hand's week is 12 cn, and obligation is weighed salt cut on a board. Odarath copper circulates quietly at about a quarter of its face; Odarath silver and gold do not circulate at all, and a party holding them needs the landing and a downriver buyer rather than the weigh-house.

Tables: d6 Events Table - rolled on entry and each week thereafter. These happen *to* Torsgaard and are distinct from the Situation, which is already happening.
1. A crew comes back off the eastern flats one short, and the weigh-house takes the dead man's kit in settlement before his people can get to it. Whatever debt was on it is now looking for a new holder.
2. Boats at the landing - eight or nine times a year, and this is one of them. For four days there is downriver iron, wine, glass and cloth in the holding, prices on everything else move, and there are strangers who are not salters.
3. The Assize nails fresh lead to a pan that has been worked by the same crew for thirty years, and formally establishes that it is not theirs. No violence follows. Everyone watches what the crew does next.
4. A Faelhond comes up the stair alone, walks through the lower holding without stopping, and marks four doors that have never been marked before. The householders do not wipe the salt off.
5. The causeway is impassable - cord retied, stakes gone, a reach that was chest-deep last month now over a head. Nothing moves west for six to ten days and the price of oil doubles.
6. Someone the holding knows well turns up Brined - not a digger, not a pan-hand of thirty years, but a person who had no business being below at all, with the salt to the elbow and no account of where they have been.
