A Puluni

Overview: Three stockaded villages on a lagoon's shore, a great wall across the peninsula's neck behind them, and a beach where the party's ship lies. A party comes ashore with a partial map to trade for guides and canoes before the ship sails on the day set at landing. Raids by the lizard men (Faction: Theshik) round the wall's ends empty huts, and each night food is left at the gate for something huge (Named Creature: Kunotewa). If the next raid comes, canoes burn huts on the lagoon shore and take captives into the swamp; if the egg-stone leaves the chiefs' house, the chiefs close the gate and no guide or canoe is hired until it is back.

View: From the beach where the ship lies, 300 yards off, three stockaded villages curve along the shore with the wall on the ridge behind: palisades of sharpened logs, cook-fire smoke, canoes drawn up on the lagoon's sand, and the wall's gate-tower dark against the hills.

Approach: The great gate, from the hill jungle, swamps or wreck coast, has a warden and a watch day and night, and food at its foot at dusk; it takes the chiefs' word and a search, and a party refused is shut outside for the night. The lagoon beach, from the sea, has a beach watch and a drum; landing is free to traders. The wall's ends, by water from the swamp, have watch canoes, two by day and one by night; a canoe caught slipping by is turned back.

Law: Drawing a blade inside a stockade is held for the chiefs' hearing and a fine of 100 cn or a month's labour on the wall (the watch). Taking the food left at the gate puts the taker out through the gate at dusk with nothing (the warden). Entering the chiefs' house unasked stops hiring: no guide or canoe until the chiefs have heard the party (the chiefs' guard).

Arrival:
- A.1 - The warden wants every bundle shown; the chiefs' word is bought at A.10 and the gate opened after a search (Local Procedure: Passing the Great Gate). The night's visitor arrives by dark.
- A.2 - The party's ship's boats and master wait here; a beach watch and a drum meet every boat, and the ship sails on the day set at landing.
- A.3 - A wall-end tower with watch canoes, burned huts beside it and a dead raider laid out; the way out by water to the swamp channels.

Plan: In Puluni, at the lagoon's head, a street runs from the beach A.2 past the market A.5 and the chiefs' house A.10 to the watch house A.11, with lanes to the ancestor house A.9 and the guest house A.4; A.12 lies behind A.10's hearth. Naloni, two miles south on the lagoon, holds the canoe yard A.6 and the guides' hall A.7; Tiwani, on the east coast, the fish stores A.8. The gate A.1 stands 2.5 miles north-east; the wall runs 3 miles east to A.3.

Places:
- A.4 - Beds, meals and the keeper's talk; a deserter from the treasure hunters lodges here hoping for passage home.
- A.5 - Weapons and cooked fish by the portion; the market warden settles quarrels at the weighing-stone.
- A.6 - Canoes hired or bought, and paddlers; the builder wants iron.
- A.7 - Guides and porters, and a coast chart; no guide crosses the serpent posts (Local Procedure: Hiring a Guide and Canoes).
- A.8 - Rations, waterskins, torches and lamp oil; the keeper knows which huts burned.
- A.9 - The dead are fed at dusk; for 5 cn the keeper asks them to watch a party's journey.
- A.10 - Three council speakers sit here; one keeps the shirt laced to the throat. The chiefs' word costs 50 cn.
- A.11 - Forty spearmen turn out to the drum; a lizard man is held in a cell, an ochre band across the throat.
- A.12 - Scraped soot at the hearth marks a lifted stone; below, an old keeper sings beside a warm grey stone.
