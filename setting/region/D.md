D Baldun

Region kind: collection - a worked sunken quarter, count defaulting to 3x the die (roughly
eighteen locations), two entrances.

Overview: Baldun is worked because the commander is selling the right to work it, one
contract and one section at a time, faster than anyone can account for what's coming up.
Every crew that goes in pays in time against the Danger track, not against a clock - a
section can be cleared in an afternoon or cost a company its afternoon and half its number.
What makes Baldun unlike any other ruin in the region is that at least one power already
living in it wants certain sections to stay exactly as sealed as they are.

Ambiance: Cold and damp throughout, river-smell mixed with something drier and older
underneath it. Sound carries strangely - a voice in one chamber can be perfectly clear two
rooms away and utterly silent in the room between. The air gets no warmer with depth, which
nobody who's noticed it has an explanation for.

Architecture: Cut river-stone throughout, dressed far better than anything Carrdun's own
masons could manage today - the builders knew what they were doing and had the labour to
spare. A stepped, tapering arch shape repeats over every threshold that mattered to whoever
built it, distinct from the plainer square lintels over service passages. Ceilings run high,
ten feet or more in anything built for people to gather in; passages default to six feet
wide unless a room's own notes say otherwise.

Layout: A sprawling sunken quarter of roughly eighteen chambers and passages, worked from
two known entrances - the commander's reopened footing-shaft, and a second, unofficial way
somewhere past the causeway that only the diggers who use it can find twice. Time here runs
on the Danger table below rather than the clock; there are no hours to track, only how many
failed rolls a crew can absorb before the place notices them.

Features: Standing water pools ankle-deep in the lowest chambers year-round, deeper after
the spring recession. Old lamp-oil residue on several walls suggests whoever worked here
last left in a hurry rather than by plan. Loose stone underfoot is common enough that
careful footing is worth the time it costs.

Dangers: Most of Baldun sleeps - undisturbed sections answer nothing at all. What's awake
is specific: the sealed section the vanished work-gang was working two winters ago, and
anything within earshot of a Xoss working spoken to its end. Loud, careless work anywhere
else mostly just draws Tomb Jackals.

Creatures: Husk and Tomb Warden hold the older, deeper sections (Bestiary) - the Warden
speaks Nemhet and can be reasoned with on its own narrow terms, the Husk cannot be reasoned
with at all. A Ward-Statue guards at least one specific warded threshold. The Coiling, per
Bestiary, is what answers a completed Xoss working - not a creature to be fought, but the
reason certain sections stay marked off.

Secrets: What's revealed here answers History's oldest entry: this is where Nemhet's
priest-line actually died, not merely where the flood reached. The unofficial second
entrance is a hidden way known only to the Coil and whichever diggers they've placed on the
crews - the Truths of Carrdun's warded-stone rule is how a party would find and use it
without already knowing where it is.

Treasure: Treasure III gems and Treasure V caches both lean on Baldun specifically - this
is where a table-topping result belongs. Drowned-coin turns up here more than anywhere else
in the region, in quantities that mean something to the right buyer and nothing to a
moneylender.

Tables: d6 Danger table, counting down from 6 with each failed Difficulty roll
6. Something registers the disturbance - a distant sound stops, all at once, somewhere
   deeper in.
5. Dust sifts down from a ceiling that hasn't moved in six hundred years, undisturbed until
   now.
4. A previously sealed door, checked earlier, is now standing open.
3. Tomb Jackals converge on the noise from wherever they've been denning.
2. The temperature drops sharply throughout the section the party is in, with no draft to
   explain it.
1. Whatever noticed at 6 acts - a Husk rises, a Ward-Statue's warding gives, or something
   worse answers directly.
