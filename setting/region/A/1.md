A.1 **The Great Gate** (Gate) - *towering, barred, fed*
A square stone tower fills the wall's only gap, its single gate barred, with a **warden** at the foot beside **fish bowls** set out as the light fails.
*30 by 30 feet, the tower 40 feet high; the wall is stone and logs, 15 feet. The gate opens only on the chiefs' word and after a search. A party turned back is shut outside for the night, where Kunotewa comes.*
**The gate-tower:** a barred oak-and-iron gate under a parapet where spearmen of the watch stand day and night. The chiefs' word is bought at the Chiefs' House, and the gate is then opened and what a party carries searched (Local Procedure: Passing the Great Gate).
**Hunapawa:** the warden wears white clay stripes on the brow and cheeks, and greets every party with "Who keeps the wall?", answered "The wall keeps us." Hunapawa wants every bundle shown, and can say when the night's visitor comes and what its tracks showed at dawn.
**The fish bowls:** fish and fruit, set out at dusk at the gate's foot outside the wall. A person who takes food from them is put out through the gate at dusk with nothing.
**Kunotewa:** a forty-foot reptile on two legs comes down the Wall Ridge at dusk and eats from the bowls; its three-toed prints cross the cleared ground below the wall at dawn (Named Creature: Kunotewa).
**The wall:** stone and log, running east and west from the tower across the peninsula's neck. Beyond the gate only a trail climbs north to the ridge, with no road through the hills to the north shore.
**Exits:** a barred gate, opened on the chiefs' word -> C.1 The Shore Path, the same gate and an uphill trail -> D.12 The Wall Ridge
