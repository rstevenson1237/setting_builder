Layout: From the Burned Wagons (B.3), three miles west on the road, the keep shows as a grey stone hold on a bare hill some 60 feet above the plain, the wood dark beyond it to the north. Its curtain wall stands 20 feet high and runs about 160 yards east to west and 120 north to south, with twin towers of 30 feet over the main gate (A.1) in the west wall, each flying a blue pennant with a white tower, and a lower inner wall, 12 feet high, cutting off the eastern third. The slate roofs of the hall and chapel show over the wall, the chapel's bell-cote highest, with thatch and shingle crowding the outer yard. The road climbs the west slope to the main gate, open from dawn to dusk, where a lantern is lit at dusk: Sergeant Ada Brandt challenges all comers, the clerk Pell Orrin writes each stranger's name and business in a ledger, a stranger who cannot give them is held, and a blade goes in peace-strapped. The postern (A.2), in the north wall, is the way to the wood (C.1); Corporal Dag Venn opens it for patrols and for a party carrying the commander's pass. Both are barred at dusk, and whoever is outside camps under the wall.

The hold is a rectangle of wall about 160 yards east to west and 120 north to south, with the main gate (A.1) at the middle of the west wall, its passage 40 feet long and 12 wide. The inner wall runs north to south 110 yards east of the main gate, with one gate at its middle, and divides the outer yard from the inner ward. In the outer yard, measured from the main gate: the Market Yard (A.8), open and 80 feet across, is 40 yards east; the Traveller's Inn (A.3), 40 by 20 feet with a stable for twelve mounts, is 40 yards north against the west wall; the Broken Wheel (A.4), 30 by 20 feet, is 50 yards south against the west wall, with the cellar (A.12), 30 by 15 feet, under it, also 50 yards south; the General Store (A.5), 30 by 12 feet, is 60 yards north-east; the Armourer's Forge (A.6), 25 by 20 feet, is 70 yards south-east; the Moneychanger (A.7), 15 by 12 feet, is 80 yards east; and the postern (A.2), a passage 8 feet long, is 85 yards north-east in the north wall. In the inner ward: the Chapel of Law (A.9), a nave 40 by 20 feet, is 130 yards east; the Commander's Hall (A.10), 50 by 25 feet, is 150 yards east against the east wall; and the Barracks (A.11), 60 by 25 feet, is 140 yards east-south-east against the south wall, its wall stair climbing to the wall walk. The sally tunnel (A.13) starts at steps under the hall, 150 yards east, and runs 150 feet further east, 4 feet high, beneath the east wall to a rusted door in the hillside.

At the gate the clerk turns his ledger's wagon page for anyone who asks which wagons run when (A.1). The Traveller's Inn (A.3) keeps a common room under granary beams, eight rooms above and a stable for twelve mounts, full on a wagon day and half empty between. At the Broken Wheel (A.4) bone dice rattle at the table by the hearth, stakes are chalked by the bar, and the floor by the casks behind it is scraped. The General Store (A.5) is crowded with barrels, rope and jars, its shelves thinning before a wagon day; the Armourer's Forge (A.6) stands open to the yard with its rack of blades and shields; the Moneychanger (A.7) is a barred counter and a chained scale before a strongroom of iron and two keys. Stalls and carts fill the Market Yard (A.8), and wages are chalked on the notice board by the carters' guildhall. Inside the inner wall, the Chapel of Law (A.9) is a cold nave open from dawn to dusk, the old tower of Law cut into its altar and a sacristy door beside it. The Commander's Hall (A.10) holds a map table with the road drawn to the ravine's mouth and "number unknown" written beside the ravine, before a dais whose step holds a ring polished by hands. The Barracks (A.11) has forty bunks, a wall stair at the far end and the alarm drum by the door.

Inhabitants: The Keep (Faction: The Keep) holds the walls. Commander Roderic Hale, who wants the raiders dealt with and the garrison kept in, receives at his hall (A.10) with Lieutenant Cass Ferrow, his adjutant, and six of his guard in mail, who answer a bell within a turn. Sergeant-Major Bram Tolley, who wants no recruits led into the ravine, keeps twenty off-duty soldiers at the Barracks (A.11), out within two turns of the alarm drum. Sergeant Ada Brandt holds the main gate (A.1) and Corporal Dag Venn the postern (A.2), and four soldiers off duty drink at the Broken Wheel (A.4). The Carters' Guild (Faction: The Carters' Guild) waits in the Market Yard (A.8): Brenn Calloway, its guildmaster, after a guard for the next convoy, and four drivers waiting on the wagon day. Edda Marsh keeps the Traveller's Inn (A.3), where Jory Fenn, the ostler, wants work carrying packs into the ravine. Marta Voss keeps the Broken Wheel (A.4), where Lysa Torren wants her brother's party found, whatever it costs. Garrick Dunn, the trader (A.5), wants his strongbox back with its seal whole; Hild Ironhand, the smith (A.6), wants someone to test her blades in the caves; Selwyn Crake, the moneychanger (A.7), wants to see old coin before anyone else. At the Chapel of Law (A.9) Mother Hesper Lund wants the reliquary found quietly, Wenna Pryce, the acolyte healer, wants help carrying the wounded in from the gate, and Old Gil, the sexton, rings the bell at dusk. Pell Orrin, the gate clerk, sits at the ledger (A.1), and Brother Anselm, the chapel's sacristan, is seldom at the chapel by night (A.12).

Services: The keep's goods come on the fortnightly wagon, and with wagons lost for two months its shelves thin before each wagon day. A bed at the Traveller's Inn (A.3) is 10 cn a night in the common room or 25 cn in one of two private rooms with a bolt, and stabling 6 cn; the Broken Wheel (A.4) serves ale and a hot pie for pennies, sells strong spirits at 12 cn a skin and seats a player at knucklebones for 5 cn (Local Procedure: Knucklebones at the Broken Wheel). The General Store (A.5) sells rope, torches, rations and kit at the listed prices, lamp oil at 6 cn a flask and spikes, crowbars and sacks at 5 to 10 cn; the Armourer's Forge (A.6) sells spears, daggers and swords at 30 to 100 cn and shields and leather armour at 50 to 100 cn, and mends armour or sharpens a blade for 5 cn a piece. The Moneychanger (A.7) changes coin of any issue for 3%, holds up to 500 cn in a locked box for 5 cn a week, and buys what a party carries home at 80% of value, 90% for gems and jewellery (Local Procedure: Selling Loot to the Moneychanger). Porters at 10 cn a day, torchbearers at 15 and light mercenaries at 30 are hired in the Market Yard (A.8), six hirelings in all and two mercenaries at a time (Local Procedure: Hiring at the Market Yard). The Chapel of Law (A.9) clears Bloodied, Exhausted or Poisoned for 15 cn a Condition, and cures Diseased or lifts a Madness for 100 cn a rite. Magic is neither bought nor sold here, nor are horses or plate armour.

Secrets:
- Pell Orrin sells the wagon days to the Black Altar - he knows; the wagon page of his ledger (A.1) and the torn ledger page under the table in A.12 bring it out.
- The Black Altar's other agent is Brother Anselm, who meets Orrin in a cellar - Anselm knows; scraped boards behind the third barrel (A.4) bring it out.
- A stair under the dais leads to a tunnel and a rusted door to the wood - the commander and his adjutant know; the polished ring on the dais step (A.10) brings it out.
