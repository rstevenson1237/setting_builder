C The Goblin Cave

Overview: The Goblin Cave is a single holding: the home of the goblin tribe, cut into the ravine's far wall in workings older than the tribe, with its one mouth opening on the stream. A party comes for the gold the tribe has stripped from the road, and for whatever the first makers left in the rock.

Approach: One entrance, a low mouth in the ravine wall above the stream, reached by a ledge. Two goblins sit above it by day and four by night, and clack at a party they see; the rest of the tribe is a shout away.

Conditions: Every room is cut rock, wet and cold, ceilings about ten feet and passages wide enough for two abreast. Light comes only from what a party or the goblins bring; goblins see in the dark. Sound carries down the passages and is deadened by the cave's old doors. Footing is wet stone, and in places the old makers' stair-treads are worn smooth. The air is damp and smells of mushrooms and smoke, and a lit fire fills a passage with smoke for ten minutes.

Inhabitants:
- Goblin, thirty - spread through the cave's warrens and its old halls; eating, sleeping and keeping watch; want what the road carries and to be left alone in the dark; sound the alarm at the sight of a party and fight where they outnumber it, and run for the lower rooms when they do not.

Situation: The tribe has not yet found how far the old workings go below it, and it is hungry for more than the road gives. Next, goblins begin to go deeper than the warrens, and one does not return; this follows the keep's next lost wagon. After that, the tribe moves out of the cave onto the ravine itself, which follows a party killing a third of it.
