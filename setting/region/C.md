C Siwapelu

Overview: The Odarath's under-work: a gallery of cut stone running under the eastern pans, sunk nine hundred years ago for a purpose no living account explains, drowned four hundred years ago by Nessekire, and standing open since the eastern water dropped four feet three winters ago and left the cut stair clear. **Three occupancies.** The Odarath built it, and everything about its construction - the drainage, the salt laid a foot deep across certain thresholds, the eleven boundary stones above ground that are copies of the ones below - reads as a working arranged around containing what is in its lowest course rather than extracting anything. The water took it, and Nessekire's dead are still in the flooded lower courses as one thing rather than many. The Faelhond squat the upper courses now, going down at dawn and up before the gorm rises, and they are the only occupancy that knows it is the third. Run Siwapelu as a **collection** - a worked complex, 24 locations at three times its d8, so a full clear burns exactly three of the Danger track's six steps and anything inefficient eats a half-track that was never generous. Two entrances. Three HIGH locations, eight MEDIUM, thirteen LOW.

Ambiance: Cold, and the same cold everywhere - about the temperature of the fen in February, in August. Wet without dripping: every surface carries a film, and the humidity is total, so that lamps halo and nothing a party carries dries out again. It smells of brine and stone dust and, deeper in, of the sour sweetish gorm. Sound carries enormously and wrongly - the courses are cut straight and the stone is hard, so a boot scrape travels two hundred feet and arrives sounding like it came from a side passage, and a party consistently mislocates everything it hears. Under all of it, at irregular intervals, is a rhythmic knock coming through the rock from a long way off, which is the Tuggik walking its route, and which a party will hear for hours before it ever sees anything.

Architecture: Dressed limestone, cut dry and fitted without mortar, and the workmanship is superb - four hundred years underwater has not opened a joint anywhere in the upper courses. The motif that ties the whole work together is the **corbelled false arch**: every opening in Siwapelu is a flat-topped trapezoid formed by stepping courses inward, never a true voussoir arch, and every one of them has the Old Rede character for *immo* - gate, threshold - cut into the underside of its cap stone. A party that starts checking cap stones will find the character present on every threshold in the complex except three, and those three are the whole of what this region has to say. Typical ceiling height is **nine feet** in the courses and passages, dropping to six in the descents; typical passage width is **six feet**, wide enough for two abreast and cut with a shallow central channel for drainage that is now a trip hazard and a watercourse by turns. Rooms default to those unless their own Referee Notes say otherwise. The stairs are cut as double-height treads with a shallow ramp beside them, which is what the Tuggik walks and what tells a party the thing has always been here.

Layout: Two entrances, both from Lindmarn's eastern reach. **The stair** is the cut stair uncovered three years ago - broad, dry to about forty feet down, and the way everybody comes in. **The vent** is an Odarath drainage shaft opening in a reed bed half a mile north, a nine-inch-wide grating over a forty-foot drop that no adult can pass; what it is good for is air, sound, and dropping things, and the Faelhond know exactly where it comes out. The complex runs on four descending courses, each roughly level and each a grid following the pan walls above it, so the plan is rectilinear and comprehensible - Siwapelu is not a maze and does not need to be. The first course is dry. The second is dry at dawn and wet by afternoon. The third is flooded to the knee always and the ceiling is where the gorm sits. The fourth has never been seen by anyone who came back. **Time here runs on the Danger countdown, not in hours** - but the Gorm track from `setting/Procedures.md` runs alongside it and is a separate track, because the Danger countdown is what the place notices and the Gorm is what the air does regardless.

Features: **Air is the region.** Everything below the second course is gorm-bearing and the track advances on every action; a party that plans around the air is playing Siwapelu correctly and a party that plans around fighting is not. **Water** rises and falls in the second and third courses with the tide in Lindmarn above, about four feet of swing, and the drainage channels in the floor are how a party can read it before it happens - they run one way on a making tide and the other on a falling one. **Light**: open flame gutters and dies at Gorm 3, so lanterns are the only workable light, and the Faelhond use them shuttered to a coin's breadth because light carries as badly as sound does here. **Footing** is the drainage channel and the Tuggik's groove - both are worn, both are wet, both are where a running party goes down. **Sound** arrives from the wrong direction everywhere in the complex, consistently, and the referee should mislocate it deliberately and every time. **Fire** does nothing useful here: there is nothing to burn, and it takes the air.

Dangers: **Siwapelu is asleep, and it is not the sleeper that answers.** The place itself has no watchfulness - it is stone, and it has been stone for nine hundred years. What answers intrusion is the arrangement: the salt across the thresholds, the *immo* characters, the drainage that keeps the lowest course flooded. All of it is machinery for containment and all of it is four centuries past maintenance, and the party's actions degrade it whether or not they understand what they are doing. Moving salt, cutting a threshold stone, breaking a drainage run, or blocking the vent are each a step, and the Danger track is the accounting. What wakes is not the complex. It is what the complex was built around, and it wakes slowly, and every entry on the table below is it coming further up.

Creatures: **The Faelhond**, perhaps a dozen below at any time in the first and second courses, working the dawn window - they eat and drink what they carry down, they leave nothing, and they will ask a party's business at the stair-head and not accept a vague answer. They know the second course cold and they know where the gorm sits at noon. **Cured Men** on the first and second courses, perhaps eight of them, each still doing what it was doing - two of them are Odarath and have been walking the same errand for nine hundred years, and the rest are Faelhond and Marchfolk from the last three years, which the Faelhond have not mentioned to Torsgaard. **The Tuggik**, one, walking a fixed route between an exhausted working on the second course and a wharf that is forty feet under Lindmarn, out through a flooded channel and back; its groove is polished into the floor and the whole of surviving it is knowing where the groove goes. **Brine Eels**, three or four, in the flooded third course, come up out of the drowned stonework and entirely at home. **The Drowned Chorus** in the third course's flooded runs - one thing, four hundred years cured, four hundred years still arriving; it cannot be fought in a flooded course and it cannot climb a stair faster than a party can, and that arithmetic is the entire encounter. **Zorethanu** in the fourth. It has never left the lowest course and there is no reason to think it can. Its Sign - Old Rede cut into every surface of the approach in a hand that gets worse and more hurried the further in it goes, and salt a foot deep across a threshold laid by people who were not mining it - is available to any party that reads, and turning around while that is still an option is the skill this region rewards.

Secrets: Siwapelu answers the setting's central question and answers it in the architecture rather than in a document. The complex was not a mine. It was a lid, and the eleven Assize stones above ground are copies of eleven stones down here, cut by men who could not read them onto ground the Odarath had already marked - which is why they work, and why nobody in Torsgaard can say why. Three thresholds in the complex lack the *immo* character, and what that means is available to a party that has checked the others. The drainage was built to keep the lowest course flooded, deliberately, forever - which makes Nessekire look less like a catastrophe and more like a decision, and makes the Vingash's four-hundred-year position on drowning the place the most sensible thing anyone in Brackvaen believes. And the eastern water did not drop for any reason above ground: something opened a run down here, and the water went out through it.

Treasure: The reason anyone comes. Siwapelu leans hardest on `setting/Treasure5.md` and `setting/Treasure3.md` - Odarath silver and copper in quantity, grave-plate from a people who left no graves, unset stones, and gold that is good metal and unspendable in Torsgaard without the landing and a downriver buyer. `setting/Treasure2.md` appears in the form the salt made possible: four hundred years of immersion has left worked iron and mail down here in condition that nothing above ground can match. What is *not* here is anything bulky worth carrying - the Odarath took their goods out and the water took the rest - so `setting/Treasure4.md` is thin, and the region's characteristic problem is not weight but the Gorm track, the tide in the second course, and the fact that everything valuable is one course further down than the party wanted to go.

Tables: d6 Danger Table - counting down from 6 with each failed Difficulty roll. Entry 6 is the place noticing; entry 1 is the place acting.
6. **Noticing.** The knock of the Tuggik stops, somewhere a long way off, and does not resume for a count of thirty. Nothing else changes. Every Faelhond below ground hears it and starts moving toward the stair.
5. **The air.** The gorm comes up early - the Gorm track advances two steps instead of one for the rest of this descent, and the second course, which should be dry until afternoon, is not.
4. **The water.** The tide turns in the drainage channels and runs the wrong way for the time of day. The third course rises a foot in an hour and one route the party has been using is gone until it falls again.
3. **The Chorus stirs.** Standing water in the flooded runs begins to move against the fall of the ground, and the sound of a crowd heard through a wall arrives from a direction that is wrong even for Siwapelu. It is coming up. It is not fast.
2. **The arrangement fails.** Salt across a threshold somewhere in the complex is breached - by water, by subsidence, by whatever the party has already done - and something that has been held in one room for four hundred years is now in the course.
1. **Acting.** From the fourth course, a sound like stone being drawn across stone, once, and then the Old Rede characters on every cap stone in the party's line of sight are wet, and they were not wet a moment ago. Zorethanu has not come up. It has noticed that the lid is open, and after this the referee should run Siwapelu as a place that is being emptied from below.
