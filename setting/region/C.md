C Velar

Overview: Velar is the eastern market town the Nine Wagons trading company built its detour
operation on, and its warehouses have filled faster in the three months since the Sunk
Bridge closed than in the two years before it. Everything sold here carries the detour's
markup, stated openly at every stall rather than hidden in the price. Unlike Tolkar, Velar
answers to coin before crown - its own toll-house collects for whichever of the three
kingdoms currently pays the Wagons' factor the largest bribe, and that changes.

Ambiance: Stone-built and more prosperous-looking than Tolkar, with painted shutters over
every window that can afford them. The small brass bell tied to every Nine Wagons cart's
brake lever carries over the market from dawn, so the company is heard arriving before it is
seen.

People: A Nine Wagons factor runs the warehouse row and settles most disputes that touch the
company's own trade. A toll-house clerk on the east gate is known to take payment for
looking away from certain wagons - which ones changes with the season. A moneylender
finances the trading houses the ford's closure has left overextended. Velar keeps no
garrison of any single crown - its watch is a hired company, paid by the merchant guild
rather than any king. Guarded around anyone asking too plainly who actually owns what;
otherwise as open as any town that lives on trade has to be.

Situation: Every caravan not flying the Wagons' blue-grey now pays the company's own fee to
use the one warehouse row with river access, since the Wagons bought out the second-largest
trading house here the week the ford closed. Two smaller trading families are close to
selling out entirely; one has already sent word asking whether Brekar would deal with an
independent caravan directly, and nobody has answered it yet.

Layout: A town, walled, standing on drier ground east of the marsh - the reason it grew here
rather than closer to the river. One gate opens west onto the causeway road; the market
square and warehouse row run along the river frontage inside it, with the toll-house at the
west gate itself. SAFE, and not time-bound - nothing here is tracked in hours.

Features: Goods hauled the long way round the ford carry a stated markup of roughly a fifth
over the standard rate on setting/Treasure4.md, posted at the stall rather than hidden in
it. The west gate keeps no fixed hours - it opens and shuts on the hired watch's own
judgment, which is not always the same judgment twice.

Dangers: Debt and feud more than violence - an overextended house, a moneylender's ledger
called in at the wrong time, a bribe traced back to the wrong clerk.

Creatures: Man - a company of sellswords on a Nine Wagons contract serves as the hired watch
(setting/Bestiary.md), and caravan and warehouse livestock fill the yards behind the market.

Factions: The Nine Wagons hold the whole of Velar's warehouse row and, in practice, the
market itself - nobody undercuts them and keeps a stall past a season. Neither Brekar nor
any of the three crowns hold acknowledged ground here. Velar's old quarter, the original
town from before the Wagons rebuilt the rest, is where rumour holds the Serpent Cult still
meets - unconfirmed, and this region's Secret says why.

Secrets: Whether the old quarter actually hides a Serpent Cult cell goes unanswered in Velar
itself - what is certain is that three respected households there have not opened their
doors to a Wagons factor once in three months, unusual enough that people have started to
notice.

Treasure: setting/Treasure4.md (luxury and trade goods) is what Velar's own market leans on,
with setting/Treasure3.md for the finer goods the wealthier houses deal in.

Tables: d6 Events, rolled on entry and again each week.
1. A Wagons caravan arrives a day early, wanting the warehouse row cleared for it
   immediately.
2. One of the two struggling trading families quietly puts its warehouse up for sale.
3. A courier from Tolkar asks pointed questions about where Velar's toll money actually
   goes.
4. A caravan guard is caught wearing a strip of red-dyed cloth - Brekar's own passage-mark -
   and won't explain how he came by it.
5. The hired watch turns away a caravan at the gate over unpaid back tolls, in full view of
   the market.
6. Someone leaves an offering at an old-quarter doorstep before dawn - gone by the time
   anyone else notices it.
