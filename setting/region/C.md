C The Ravine

Overview: Cut and shaped nine hundred years ago by builders nobody now living can name,
abandoned two centuries after the working set into its deepest chamber, and only lived in
again for the last century - by a scatter of rival households now, and for the last six
years by a shrine at its head calling them toward one purpose. A party comes here for what
a household's own hoard is worth carried out, and pays for it in whichever household's
ground stands between the entrance used and the hoard itself. True here and nowhere else
in the setting: what the ravine's first builders bound into its deepest chamber eight
hundred years ago is still doing the job it was set, waiting on nothing any household now
living has the power to end.

Ambiance: Cold, wet stone throughout, air that moves the wrong way out of certain passages
against their own slope. A sweet, heavy smoke marks wherever a Grozzgur rite's been spoken
recently, thinning the further a passage runs from the shrine at the head. Sound behaves
differently by cut: it carries far down the old builders' own dressed halls, and goes dead
within a few strides in places nobody, tribe or builder, has ever explained.

Architecture: Dressed stone cut and squared to the same measure throughout the ravine's
oldest halls, heavier and more precise than anything any household's added since; a
bind-rune - a closed loop cut around a single stroke - repeats at thresholds the whole
ravine over, tying every old-cut chamber back to one builder's work regardless of who's
since moved in around it. Ceilings run low and passages narrow in the oldest cutting,
forcing single file with no room to swing a weapon with any reach; a household's own added
digging runs rougher and a little wider, and the line between the two is plain underfoot.

Layout: Collection - runs on the Danger countdown rather than the clock; every meaningful
action rolls the region's die, and a failure ticks the count down. About ten households
hold the ravine between them, each with its own entrance rather than sharing the two or
three a smaller place could get by on; the shrine's own halls lie deepest, and every
household's tribute travels up toward it along a route at least one other household can
watch.

Features: Old cisterns cut by the first builders still hold water in the oldest chambers,
fouled or not depending which household's since used them. Every passage is dark without a
carried light; a household's own halls are lit by whatever rite-fire or torch that
household keeps burning, and nothing further. Footing is broken and settled throughout the
old cutting, smoother in a household's own recent digging. Open flame draws a bat roost
before it draws anything with a mind to answer it.

Dangers: Mostly indifferent rather than watching for intrusion - a household answers what
happens at its own entrance and its own halls, not the ravine as a whole. Noise is what
wakes it: a fight, a forced door, or a dropped torch carries further down the old
builders' dressed halls than any household expects, and travels household to household
faster than a runner could.

Creatures: Khughik (Bestiary) hold a dozen small warrens throughout, feeding on whatever a
larger household won't finish and drawing their own water from whichever old cistern sits
nearest - too many to starve out and too small to hold ground alone. Gorzgur (Bestiary)
hold one organized household as a faction position (see Factions below), fed and watered
from stores they raid rather than forage, their own young kept furthest from any entrance.
Hukkgur (Bestiary) hold the least ground of any household by numbers, answering any
challenge to their strength directly rather than posting a real watch. The Shrine
(Factions) keeps its bound and undead servants on no upkeep at all - the Bound Thrall, the
Ravine Restless, and the Shrine Wraith need neither food, water, nor waste, and know only
the one post or round each was set.

Factions: The Shrine holds the ravine's deepest halls outright, and calls on every
household's tribute without holding their ground directly. Gorzgur holds one organized
household as its own, sentries posted at every entrance it fields, contesting whichever
neighbour presses it hardest for the ground between.

Secrets: Chisel-work in the oldest halls, in the tongue no living scholar reads, may be the
builders' own account of what they set the deepest chamber's working to do - if anyone
still bound to read it by rote can be found. The bind-rune repeating at every old
threshold is the same mark the ravine's oldest guardians both answer to, and neither has
been shown a false one yet. A way down into the ravine that skips the watched road
entirely comes out somewhere no household currently holds.

Treasure: Grave goods, worked stone, and old-tongue coin real enough that a moneylender's
doubt is worth less than the silver itself; a household's own hoard is kept whole in one
guarded chamber rather than scattered through its halls. Draws on `setting/Treasure3.md`
(Gems and Jewelry) and `setting/Treasure5.md` (Treasure Cache).
