C Ingaraz

Overview: Ingaraz is a field of older foundations and burial mounds sunk beneath the far
half of Pelren's treeline - raised by whoever cut the stone under Fellward's own cellar,
three centuries before the keep had a charter, and never claimed or cleared by anyone
since. The Barrow-Kin still tend part of it as their own dead, while the
Diggers and a rival crew of Old Tongue Grave-Robbers both work it now, racing each other
to a sealed chamber neither has actually found the entrance to yet. The one thing true
here that isn't true above ground: this place remembers exactly who crosses it, because
some of what its builders left behind is still standing its post.

Ambiance: Cold air moves the wrong way out of sealed passages, against any draft that
should be pulling it in. Past a certain point in the ruin, sound simply stops carrying -
no echo, no footfall, nothing. Smell of old wet stone throughout, cut in the active dig
sites by fresh-turned earth and new-cut timber shoring.

Architecture: Old-tongue foundation-stone, cut heavier and more precisely than anything
built at Fellward since. A carved warding-mark - a closed ring cut around a single old-
tongue glyph - repeats at chamber thresholds throughout, tying the whole field together as
one builder's work rather than a scatter of unrelated graves. Passages run barrow-scale
rather than hall-scale: shoulder-width, head-height, forcing single file and no room to
swing a weapon with any reach.

Layout: Runs on the Danger countdown rather than real time, per `setting/Procedures.md` -
entering or meaningfully searching a mound advances the track. The field holds no single
entrance; separate mounds break the surface at irregular intervals across Pelren's far
treeline, connected below ground by older sunken passages rather than joined into one
structure.

Features: Standing water pools in the lower chambers, old and still rather than running.
Footing throughout is broken by root growth and settled stone. Sound behaves as stated
above rather than uniformly - a fact worth testing before relying on either silence or an
echo to warn of anything.

Dangers: Watched, not asleep. What was left here to guard specific chambers still does -
the Old-Ward Sentinel holds its post until that post is crossed; the Barrow Wight ignores
anyone who doesn't breach the chamber it's bound to and gives no warning to anyone who
does.

Creatures: The Barrow-Kin tend a scatter of family grave-sites here and avoid contact by
default, but treat any disturbed grave as an act that ends any conversation already
underway. The Diggers and the Old Tongue Grave-Robbers each work a site at a time,
defending an active dig with real force if pressed. A Barrow Bear has denned in one of the
larger mounds and ignores anything that stays outside it. An Old-Ward Sentinel is fixed to
a single passage or gate somewhere in the field; a Barrow Wight is bound to whichever
chamber turns out to be the sealed one the Diggers are racing toward. Any grave here dug
facing the wrong way holds Wall-Bound Dead, fixed to that grave and no further.

Secrets: The sealed chamber itself - its location is not yet fixed to any single mound,
and finding the actual entrance before the Diggers or the Grave-Robbers do is the one
thing both crews want and neither has. Old-tongue coin recovered here is real silver,
whatever a moneylender at Fellward says it's worth.

Treasure: Old-tongue relics and worked stone the Diggers' fenced buyer pays for, real
silver coin unminted by any standing authority, and grave goods fine enough to draw the
Barrow-Kin's attention the moment they're sold openly. Draws on `setting/Treasure3.md`
(Gems and Jewelry) and `setting/Treasure5.md` (Treasure Cache).

Tables: d6 Danger table, counting down from 6 with each failed Difficulty roll
6. A spiral scratch-mark turns up fresh at knee height - the Diggers have already worked
   this stretch.
5. A closed-ring warding-mark, freshly re-cut, shows the Barrow-Kin have re-tended ground
   nearby overnight.
4. Cold air starts moving the wrong way out of a passage that wasn't sealed a moment ago.
3. Old Tongue Grave-Robbers are heard working close by, shoring timber and moving stone.
2. The Barrow Wight's chamber threshold is close enough that the silence stops carrying
   any sound at all.
1. Whatever stands the nearest post - Sentinel or Wight - acts, without warning.
