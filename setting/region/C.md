C Glaswy

Overview: A party comes to Glaswy to trade with the marsh-kin, to follow the river past
where Carrdun's own landing reaches, or because something drowned upstream has washed down
into it. The marsh charges in time, not danger - nothing here hunts a careful party, but
nothing here hurries for one either. What makes Glaswy unlike anywhere else in the region
is that the marsh-kin already know what's out here, and simply haven't said.

Ambiance: Reed stands taller than a person in most channels, and sound flattens to almost
nothing a few yards off any open water. The air is close and green-smelling, and never
fully dries anything left in it for more than a day.

Terrain: A slow river delta broken into channels, reed islands, and stilt-platform paths;
walking is often impossible, and poling a shallow boat is the only realistic way through
most of it. Distances mean little here - what matters is which channel you're in, since two
that look alike can lead very different places.

Foraging: Reed-root and a marsh berry the marsh-kin call "still-fruit" are both abundant
and edible; eel and river-fowl are plentiful for anyone who can fish quietly. A pale marsh
moss said to draw fever out of a wound is real, rare, and the marsh-kin will trade it only
for iron, never for coin.

Layout: About ten notable stretches and platforms across a reed delta a long day's pole
from Carrdun's landing to its outer edge, distances better measured in channels crossed
than in miles. An action costs four hours, same as elsewhere in the region: a pole between
neighbouring stretches, a search, a forage, a tracking attempt, or making camp.

Features: Fog sits on the water most mornings until well past dawn. The current shifts
channel positions after every flood season, so a route poled once is not guaranteed twice.
Anything dropped in open water here is gone for good unless someone marks exactly where.

Dangers: Glaswy is mostly indifferent - the River Serpent that holds this stretch does not
hunt anyone who stays out of the water, and the marsh-kin avoid conflict on principle. What
isn't indifferent is anything that's washed down from Baldun; the marsh-kin have their own
quiet way of dealing with that, and don't explain it to outsiders.

Creatures: River Serpent holds the deep channel (Bestiary) and will not leave the water to
pursue. River Ox herds graze the reed banks in the open stretches. Marsh-kin live throughout
in small platform kin-groups and are the region's only people worth naming.

Secrets: The marsh-kin know of a second drowned district further downstream than the
commander's diggers have ever reached, and haven't mentioned it to anyone from Carrdun.
What it would take to be told is trust, not payment.

Treasure: Treasure I and Treasure III turn up in what the current carries down from
upstream - scavenged debris and the occasional lost gem alike. The marsh-kin themselves
value iron and salt over coin outright, which is its own kind of exchange rate a party has
to work out.

Tables: d6 Encounter table, rolled on each failed Difficulty roll
1. Fog closes in mid-channel, and the party has to choose between waiting it out or poling
   blind.
2. A marsh-kin platform is sighted, tending its weir - approachable, if the party doesn't
   come in armed.
3. The River Serpent's wake crosses open water nearby, close enough to see, far enough not
   to threaten yet.
4. Something recognisably from Baldun - a carved stone, a scrap of grave-cloth - is caught
   in a reed-drift.
5. A channel that should be passable has silted shut since the last flood; the route has to
   be relearned.
6. A River Ox bull, alone and short-tempered, blocks the only dry crossing for a mile.
