Outline of Brackvaen

Regions: 3, coded A-C. The default mix - one SAFE, one WILD, one DANGEROUS - which is the
shortest shape a setting can take and still hold a complete loop. Brackvaen is about one
drowned working and the country between it and the only roof for thirty miles, so a second
SAFE region would give the party a second answer to a question the setting wants to keep
hard.

A - SAFE, d10 - Torsgaard, the rise-holding: the only place to sell salt, sleep dry, and hear who came back.
B - WILD, d6 - Lindmarn, the treacherous water: the fen crossing, which is not a road and does not stay put.
C - DANGEROUS, d8 - Siwapelu, the under-work: the Odarath gallery the water let go of three years ago.

**Why the dice are what they are.** A is d10 because a rise-holding that has stood 120
years should breathe - a party ought to be able to resupply without the table biting, and
ten locations give it room to hold three factions without any of them owning it. B is d6
because the fen punishes carelessness rather than boldness: six locations at a 1-in-6 bite
is short and spiky, the texture of a crossing you can lose people on, and it keeps the
expected encounters per full traverse at exactly one like every other die. C is d8,
baseline, and takes the size that follows - 24 rooms at three per die, spending three of
the Danger track's six steps across a full clear.

Party altitude: treasure hunters barely above commoners. A starting character carries 2
dice, both d6; one who has survived a season carries 3 or 4, of which one may be a d8. That
party handles 1-2 AD reliably, takes 3-4 AD with numbers, ground, or preparation, and must
avoid 5 AD and above. Creature AD is pitched against this line and never against a region's
die - see `setting/Procedures.md`.

Shortest loop: A Torsgaard (outfit, sell, hear a rumour) -> B Lindmarn (cross, and pay the
fen for it) -> C Siwapelu (go down, take what will carry) -> back through B to A. Every leg
is one region, and the return crossing is the one a party underestimates.
