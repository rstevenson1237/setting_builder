Procedures of Duen

## Tests and Consequences
Call for a test only when the outcome is uncertain and both success and failure would be
interesting - if there's no real chance of failure, or failure would just waste time, it
happens without one.

To test: roll a single die from the pool of Action Dice matching the ability in question
(the sharpest die that plausibly applies, if more than one could), and read the result:

- **1** - Failure. The situation gets worse, not just the same as before the roll.
- **2-3** - Success, but at a cost, or complicated.
- **4+** - Clean success.

This 1 / 2-3 / 4+ read is the one shared resolution curve in this framework - the same
shape governs a region's Difficulty die (see **Region Dice** below), even though what's
printed on the die means something different each time.

Never test the same attempt twice. A failed test changes the situation before a retry is
possible - the second attempt is a different test, against a different situation, or it
doesn't happen.

**Setting resolutions.** Swearing falsely, or breaking a kept oath, with a hand on quarried
Dutor stone never needs a test - it happens, and the stone marks the breaker on the spot.
Carrying something taken without consent across one of the old boundary markers *does* call
for a test: on a 1 the theft costs the carrier something physical immediately; on a 2-3 it
costs them once they're clear of the line; on a 4+ they're through before it catches up with
them. A standard silver coin (cn) is this setting's currency, minted originally by the
throne that chartered Duen; a laborer's ordinary week runs to roughly 15-20 cn (see
`setting/Treasure1.md`), and every value in the Treasure tables is priced against that.

## Traps
A trap is defined by its impact tier, not its mechanism - a swindle, a snare, and a
deadfall can all be the same tier wearing a different face:

- **Nuisance** - costs time, noise, or a consumable. Never forces a test on its own; it
  makes the next test in the area harder, or the party's presence known.
- **Harm** - drops one of the character's Action Dice a size (d12 to d10, d10 to d8, and so
  on), or forces a costly test to avoid that drop. A die dropped below d4 is lost from the
  pool until it's recovered in play.
- **Lethal** - can end a character outright with no test available, or only one grim
  enough that failing it costs the character rather than just their die. Reserve this tier
  for traps that are found, not sprung blind.

A trap's tier is set when it's placed and never inferred from its dressing - a tripwire and
a collapsing floor can be the same Harm trap.

## Searching
Searching a space a character can already see or reach costs no test and always finds
whatever isn't deliberately concealed - stating this plainly is what keeps searching from
becoming a tax on paying attention.

Finding something hidden costs one of two things, decided when the hidden thing is placed:
a stated action that reveals it without a roll (moving a specific object, asking a specific
question, being in a specific place), or a test, which costs time and may cost noise if the
result is a 2-3. A thing with neither - unfindable no matter what the party tries - isn't
hidden, it's absent, and shouldn't be written as though it's there to find.

## Time
Time is tracked differently by region rating, and that rating's Region Overview states
which applies in its Layout field:

- **SAFE** isn't time-bound. Scenes resolve in as much or as little time as the fiction
  needs; nothing here runs against a clock.
- **WILD** runs on 4 hours per action - a Watch. Travel, a thorough search of a landmark, a
  standoff with something that isn't fighting yet: each costs a Watch, and four Watches
  make a day.
- **DANGEROUS** doesn't run on real time at all. It runs on the Danger table's countdown
  (see that rating's pattern folder) - an action there costs a step on the Danger track,
  not a duration.

## Scaling
Three Action Dice scales exist in this framework. They look alike - all are pools of dice
- and they are not interchangeable. Never compare a number from one scale to a number from
another.

- **Players** carry 1-6 Action Dice, sized d4 through d12, d6 average. The pool is the
  character: more dice and bigger dice both mean more capable, and losing a die (see
  **Traps**, Harm) is losing capability, not losing a resource that regenerates on its own.
- **Creatures** carry d6 dice only, counted 1 to 18, with a bonus from -2 to +6. The count
  reads roughly like classic Hit Dice. Creature dice are never sized up or down the way a
  player's are - a tougher creature gets more d6s or a bigger bonus, never a d8.
- **Factions** carry d6 dice only, with no bonus at all, and their count means something
  only next to another faction's count - a faction with 4 dice isn't "faction-strength 4"
  in the abstract, it's stronger than one with 3 and weaker than one with 5, full stop.

Creature dice are never pitched against a region's Difficulty die - they're pitched against
**party altitude**, fixed once when the Bestiary is written. A region's die measures how
hard the region is to move through; a creature's dice measure how hard the creature is to
fight. A d8 region wanting a d8-equivalent creature is the mistake this framework is built
to prevent.

## Region Dice
A region carries one die, d4 through d12, and it is a **difficulty die**, not a power
level - read exactly like a test, 1 = failure, 2-3 = complication, 4+ = success, except
it's the region being tested against the party's passage rather than the reverse. Smaller
die, harder region: a d6 region fails a roll more often than a d10 one. **d8 is the
baseline**; d6 is a step tougher, d10 a step easier, and d4 or d12 are deliberate outliers
reserved for a region the setting wants to call out as such.

The die also sets how many locations a region holds, and that count isn't a convention -
it's what makes the difficulty math come out even. A WILD region built at N locations,
where N matches its die's face count, produces **exactly one** encounter per full traverse
regardless of die size. A DANGEROUS region built at 3N locations produces **exactly three**
of the Danger track's six steps per full clear. A SAFE region's count is looser, since
SAFE isn't run against the countdown the other two ratings are. Building a region at a
different count is a deliberate trade against this math, not a stylistic choice, and should
only be made with the cost in mind.
