## Player Overview

Sixty feet of open floor under a low roof, with hemp laid out along it and a wheel at each end. The floor is board over dressed slab. That is **the walk**, and it is worked from first light while there is light.

**The gauge** is pegged to the doorframe at the height a rope runs, bronze, cut with two sets of units, one on each edge. Somebody has marked the two against each other by hand in ink that has not lifted off the metal.

On a peg at the back wall hangs **the Covenant rope**, eleven fathoms in three plies, laid rather than twisted. There is a knot in it and it has been in it a long time and it is still soft.

By the door, **the offcuts** stand in a barrel, free to anybody, and the ropemaker will not let a party leave without taking some.

## Referee Overview

**Service:** Rope, laid rather than twisted, in any length, made here. Also tar, hemp by weight, and repair of anything corded while the party waits.

**Cost:** Coin, and the price is by the fathom and is the same for everybody. Repair is done for the price of the material and the ropemaker will not take payment for the work, and does not explain that either.

**Refusal:** He will not sell the Covenant rope. There are eleven fathoms of it on the back wall, laid rather than twisted, holding a knot wet and not shrinking as it dries, and it is not for sale at any price to anybody.

*The rule the house keeps.* Nothing goes out of here unmeasured. Every length is run through the gauge at the door in front of the buyer and the figure is said aloud, and the ropemaker does this to his own work as readily as to anybody's.

*Schedule.* The walk works from first light while there is light, and the door is barred at dark because the tar is a fire in a hold made of board.

*Where a question goes.* Nowhere. He answers what he is asked about rope and does not repeat anything, and the hold knows this, which is why the ropemaking house is where people say things.

*What the refusal is about.* The eleven fathoms came off a bell frame. He will say that much. He will not say which frame and he will not say who brought it in.

The knot in the eleven fathoms is a knot for hanging a bell. He will say the rope came off a frame. He will not say which frame, and he will not say who brought it in.

## Features

### The Walk

Sixty feet of open floor under a low roof, with the hemp laid out along it and a wheel at each end. The floor is board over dressed slab and the slab is warm.

The yard door gives back onto the weighing floor -> R01-L07, and boards cross the gap to the street -> R01-L10.

### The Covenant Rope

Eleven fathoms coiled on a peg at the back wall, laid in three plies, tarred, and better than anything made on the Reach since (TREASURE TABLE II, T-TR2-05).

{VALUE: 250 cn} {WT: 2} {QUALITY: Masterwork}

There is a knot in it. It has been in it a long time and it is still soft, and it is a knot for hanging a bell rather than for anything a ropemaker does.

### The Gauge

Bronze, pegged to the doorframe at the height a rope runs, cut with Covenant units on one edge and the hold's own on the other, and the two are marked against each other by hand in ink that has not lifted off the metal (TREASURE TABLE II, T-TR2-07).

Somebody worked out the conversion and cut it here, and it is correct, and it is the only written conversion between the two anywhere in Reedhold.

### The Offcuts

A barrel of ends by the door, free to anybody, and the ropemaker will not let a party leave without taking some. Among them is a coil of Covenant cord, tarred, stiff as wire, still holding a knot somebody else left in it (TREASURE TABLE I, T-TR1-01).

{VALUE: 20 cn} {WT: 1} {QUALITY: Poor}
