## Player Overview

**The scale** stands outside the door on a stone plinth under its own lamp, bronze, the beam worn bright at the pivot and nowhere else. The factor works it before anybody has offered him anything.

Inside, against the wall, **the back weight** is stacked four courses high, by weight rather than by kind. Salvage, goods, one instrument, and a bronze plate off a hold door.

**The chalk** on every piece carries a date and a name and it is renewed when it fades, which is a job somebody does.

On the counter, not locked and not on a shelf, **the list** lies open at the current page. Two columns: a weight, and then a name.

**The yard door** at the back of the weighing floor is open in working hours, and tar and hemp come through it.

## Referee Overview

**Service:** Covenant work bought by weight, without a question about which shaft it came out of, and paid for on the spot. The House also sells tallow, sealed lamps, oiled reed-cloth and passage seaward on a boat it does not own.

**Cost:** Coin, at a fair price, and a name. The name goes in the list beside the weight. Tallowcount does not explain the list and does not conceal it (NAMED CREATURES, T-CRE-04).

**Refusal:** He will not price anything a party has not put on the scale, and he will not price a thing twice in one visit. He will not sell the back weight, and he will not say which piece in it came off a hold door.

*Standing.* A party opens as an unentered name. Selling anything raises it, because a name with a weight beside it is a name he can use. Selling something whose owner is still alive and still on the Reach raises it further and lowers it with everybody else in the hold, and both happen the same afternoon. Refusing to give a name closes the scale, not the door: he will still sell, at a worse price, and he will not buy.

*Why the House is still here.* It is the only place on the Reach that converts what comes out of the Covenant's work into anything a party can spend. Nobody else will take it, because everybody else knows whose it was.

*The refusal with an answer.* The back weight is not for sale, and the answer that is not the sale is the chalk: he will let anyone read it, standing there, for as long as they like. Reading it costs a visit's worth of daylight and it costs him nothing, and it is how a party learns which of their neighbours has been out to which shaft.

*The second reading.* A party who has been to a hold and come back with a door plate's dimensions, or a hold's name in the older layer, can match a piece in the back weight to the door it came off (UNIQUE TREASURES, T-TRE-04).

Three of the names in the chalk are of people the hold buried, their pieces are still in the stack, and the dates on them are after the burials. Nobody has read the courses across.

## Features

### The Scale

Bronze, set on a stone plinth outside the door under its own lamp, with the beam worn bright at the pivot and nowhere else. It is the first thing a party meets and Tallowcount works it before anybody has offered him anything (PROCEDURES, T-PRC-08).

The weights are Covenant weights and the units cut into them are not the units anybody now uses. He converts in his head, aloud, and the conversion is correct.

The doorway behind the scale gives back onto the open ground and the post -> R01-L03.

### The Back Weight

Stacked against the inner wall by weight rather than by kind, four courses high, with a strip of slate chalked and hung on every piece (HOARDS, T-HRD-02). Salvage, goods, one instrument, and a bronze plate off a hold door that nobody has come asking about.

{VALUE: 6000 cn} {WT: 40} {QUALITY: Fine}

It stops being a hoard the moment somebody reads the chalk, which is the point of the chalk. Nothing here is hidden and nothing here is offered.

### The List

A bound book on the counter, not locked, not on a shelf, and open at the current page. Two columns: a weight and a name, in that order, because the weight is what he cares about. It goes back further than anyone alive on the Reach.

Asking to read it gets a yes. Asking why it is kept gets *Because it is worth keeping,* and asking a second time gets the same words in the same order (UNANSWERED QUESTIONS, T-UNQ-07).

A name spoken at the closed door at the end of the passage draws its peg, and the name is chalked on one of the pieces in the back weight (KEYS, T-KEY-07).

### The Chalk

Every piece carries a date and a name, and the chalk is renewed when it fades, which is a job somebody does. Read across the four courses, the chalk is a record of who on the Reach has been where, and it is legible to anybody who can stand still long enough.

Three of the names are of people the hold buried. Their pieces are still in the stack and still chalked, and the dates on them are after the burials.

### The Yard Door

At the back of the weighing floor, standing open in working hours onto tar and hemp -> R01-L09. The salt floor is thirty feet along the same frontage -> R01-L11.
