## Referee Overview

**Service:** None. Nothing is sold here, nobody keeps it, and the door has not been opened in living memory.

**Cost:** For the door to open, a name, spoken at it. The name is chalked on a piece four courses down in the factor's back weight and it can be read by anybody standing in his shop (KEYS, T-KEY-07), (HOARDS, T-HRD-02).

**Refusal:** The hold will not discuss it. Asked whose door it is, people give the name on the chalk without knowing that is where they got it, and then stop talking.

*Hours:* none. The passage is walked past at all hours by people going between the count house and the frontage and none of them looks down it.

*Extent:* a passage four feet wide and thirty long behind the frontage, ending at one door. Feet indoors, days.

*Nil:* no keeper. The peg is driven from the outside and the person who drove it is dead, and the factor's list says which day.

## Features

### The Door

Oak on a bronze frame, shut, with one peg driven through from the outside and seated from within (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-06). No forcing, no mark of a tool, nothing off a hinge, and no board on the hook beside it.

Every fitting on it is green with age. The hook is bright, and the hook is the head of the peg, which is the same construction as every pegged door in the Reach (PUZZLES, T-PUZ-02).

Prising it is a stated procedure, and doing it in a passage thirty feet from a room where two people agree numbers aloud is a thing the hold will hear (PROCEDURES, T-PRC-05).
