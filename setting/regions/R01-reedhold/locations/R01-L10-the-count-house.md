## Player Overview

Two people at a long table, and everything they do is said aloud before it is written and read back after. **The day count** is chalked on the wall in two columns, yesterday's on the left and today's on the right, and anybody may correct either in front of a witness.

**The counting frame** stands on the table, bronze, strung with beads worn flat on one side only.

**The wax board in the case** is kept underneath and taken out only for a question written as a count. It has not been taken out for some time.

**The doors** are: one open onto the post fifteen yards out, one across boards to the tar smell, one to a shuttered frontage, and one into a passage four feet wide that runs behind the row.

## Referee Overview

**Service:** Reckoning. Two people here will put any two counts against each other and say where they stop agreeing, in writing, on wax, for anybody. They also write letters, keep the hold's day count, and hold coin on deposit against a mark rather than a name.

**Cost:** For a reckoning, coin, and it is priced by how many rows it takes. For a letter, coin. For deposit, a share, stated in front of witnesses. For the hold's own count, nothing: it is public and it is read aloud at dusk.

**Refusal:** They will not reckon a count against a count they have not been given both of. A party that brings one column and asks what the other must be gets told, plainly, that a reckoning is not a guess.

*The rule the house keeps.* Everything is agreed aloud before it is written and read back aloud after. Nothing here is written down that both parties have not heard twice, and the room will stop and wait while a stranger works out whether they agree.

*Schedule.* Open from first light to dusk. At dusk the hold's day count goes out to the post and one of them goes with it.

*Where a question goes.* Straight out. What is said in this room is public by construction, because the room's whole method is saying things aloud until nobody disagrees.

*What is behind the passage.* A door nobody has opened, at the end of the back passage, with a peg driven from the outside and a name chalked on a piece four courses down in the factor's back weight (KEYS, T-KEY-07).

The passage ends at a door nobody has opened. What is behind it is not in this room's day count and never has been.

## Features

### The Counting Frame

Bronze, on the long table, strung with beads worn flat on one side only, so that the direction of every count it ever kept is still readable to anyone who looks at the wear rather than the beads (TREASURE TABLE III, T-TR3-06).

{VALUE: 800 cn} {WT: 2} {QUALITY: Fine}

It came out of a hold. They know which one. They will say so and they will not say who sold it to them.

### The Wax Board in the Case

Kept in a case under the table and taken out only for a question written as a count, which it answers in notches by morning (MAGICAL TOMES, T-TOM-04).

{VALUE: 8000 cn} {WT: 2} {QUALITY: Cursed}

It answers only what can be counted and it has never been wrong. It has begun including whoever wrote the question in totals they did not mean to be in, and the two people here have noticed, and they have stopped writing questions about people.

### The Day Count

Chalked on the wall in two columns and public. The left column is what the hold agreed yesterday and the right is what it has agreed so far today, and anybody may correct either in front of a witness.

The count of what has come up out of the Reach and who brought it is not kept here. It is kept at the factor's, and everyone in this room knows that, and they will say who to ask (RUMOURS, T-RUM-07).
