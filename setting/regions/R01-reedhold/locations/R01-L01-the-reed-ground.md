## Referee Overview

**Service:** The ground itself. Anything Reedhold does that is not done under a roof happens here: a bed inside the light for anyone who asks before dusk, a boat and somebody to work it, dried peat by the barrow, and directions given by a person who will walk the first hundred yards with you.

**Cost:** For a bed, coin or a day's work on the cut, and the hold prefers the day. For a boat, coin and a name at the post. For directions, nothing, and the person giving them will expect to be told afterwards whether they were right.

**Refusal:** Nobody will hire out as a guide past the fourth frame of the Long Cut at any price, and the refusal is flat rather than negotiable (RUMOURS, T-RUM-01). The cutters will sell a crossing and will not sell a return.

*The rule the ground keeps.* Nothing crosses the cut line uncounted, in either direction. A crew coming in is counted before it is greeted, and a party going out is counted as it goes. Nobody explains it and nobody is stopped by it.

*Schedule.* At first light the cutting gangs go out and the landing works. Through the middle of the day the ground is empty except at the post. At dusk every niche is lit at once, by whoever is nearest, and the count is reconciled aloud at the post. After dark the ground is walked but not worked, and anybody outside the last niche is treated as not being in Reedhold.

*Where a question goes.* To whoever is at the post. It costs nothing and it travels: by the following dusk the question has been repeated to everyone who came in off the water, because a question is news and news is what gets counted when there is nothing else to count.

*Extent.* Four hundred yards of cut ground from the water's edge to the last roofed building, and a chain of kept lane beyond it on every landward side. Nine miles of causeway inward to the Ashen Fen, walked in a day by somebody who leaves at first light.

## Features

### The Cut Line

A chain of cleared ground running the whole way round the landward side, reed taken to the root, the cut edge as clean as it was under the standing order (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-07). Past it the reed stands as high as a shoulder and goes on.

It is not a defence and nobody here calls it one. It is the line the hold counts across, and everything that has ever come into Reedhold came in over it in front of somebody.

The kept lane runs landward through it -> R01-L04, and the last wick stands at the far end of the lit ground -> R01-L05.

### The Niches

Cut at shoulder height every forty paces through the whole of the lit ground, soot in every one of them, a wick in every one at dusk (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-12). The spacing is exact and the hold paces by it without noticing.

Three niches on the landward arc are cut into stone that is not the hold's: dressed slab, laid true, running under the boards. The hold built on top of a road.

### The Post and the Landing

The tally post stands at the middle of the cleared ground with somebody at it -> R01-L03. The boards run down to the water and the boats -> R01-L02.

Under any cut stone anywhere on the ground, a hand laid flat finds it warm, and the top of the same stone cold (RUMOURS, T-RUM-03). Nobody here will discuss it and several of them sleep on it.

### The Road Out

The causeway lifts out of the cut ground on fitted slab and runs inward -> R02-L01. The peat stacks are on the landward side of the boards -> R01-L06.

The hold's own name is a living-layer compound and it is exact: a fortified place standing in reed, and the only one in the Reach still lit (NAMES, T-NAM-05).
