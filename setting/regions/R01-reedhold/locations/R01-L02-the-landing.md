## Referee Overview

**Service:** Boats and the people who work them. A crossing seaward, a crossing along the shore, hire by the day with a crew of two, and cargo taken off or put on at the factor's rate.

**Cost:** Coin for a crossing, and it is not cheap. Coin and a name at the post for hire by the day. Cargo is priced by weight at the House and the landing takes a share of that rather than a fee.

**Refusal:** Nobody sells a return. A crossing is out, and coming back is a separate bargain struck on the day, at the price on the day, and everybody states this at the point of hire so that nobody can say afterwards they were not told.

*The rule the landing keeps.* A crew is counted before it is greeted. Boat, then crew, then cargo, aloud, by whoever is standing there, and the count is corrected by whoever disagrees. A party arriving by water is counted the same way.

*Schedule.* Boats go out at first light and come in through the afternoon. The landing works while there is anything on it and stops when there is not. When a boat is late every niche on the landing is lit at once and lit early, which the hold does when somebody has not come back.

*Where a question goes.* To the crews, who have been further seaward than anyone else here and no further inland than the fourth frame. What they know about the Reach is a rumour and what they know about the water is a fact.

*Extent.* A hundred and twenty yards of driven piles and laid board, ten feet above the water at the outer end and level with the ground at the inner. Time in days.

## Features

### The Boards

Driven piles, then boards, then ground, and where the boards stop the peat starts and gives twice: once for itself and once for whatever is under it. The outer forty yards are new board on old piles and the piles are dressed stone rather than timber.

Every board is nailed with bronze, which does not rot in the fen and is worth taking, and nobody takes it.

The ground runs inland off the boards -> R01-L01, and a rutted barrow run leads to the peat stacks -> R01-L06.

### The Tally at the Rail

A slate hung on the outer rail, chalked with the boats out and the watch they went, wiped and rewritten daily. It is not a record: it is the current state, and the hold keeps no other.

A party who reads it at first light and again at dusk knows exactly who did not come back, which is not information the hold volunteers.

### The Cutter's Dog

Wet to the shoulder, working ahead of nobody, on the landing most mornings (BESTIARY, T-BES-04). It will take a party to the last place its people stood and it stops there and will not go past it, and nothing will make it.

Nobody on the landing owns it and everybody feeds it. Asked whose it is, they give a name and change the subject.

### The Salt Walk

A short walk up the slope from the outer end to a doorway with salt on the air -> R01-L11.

Salt comes in here and goes inland, priced everywhere and divisible anywhere (TREASURE TABLE II, T-TR2-04). {VALUE: 160 cn} {WT: 3} {QUALITY: Fine}
