## Overview

Piles first, then boards, then ground. Everything underfoot in Reedhold
has been driven, laid or cut by somebody, and where the boards stop the peat
starts and gives twice: once for itself and once for whatever is beneath it.
The cut line runs all the way round, a chain of cleared ground with the reed
taken to the root on the far side of it, and past that the reed stands as high
as a shoulder and goes on.

Niches at shoulder height every forty paces, soot in every one, and a wick in
every one at dusk. The light is the same colour as the light off the water and
it holds an hour longer at each end of the day than the sky accounts for.
Everybody indoors is inside the reach of a niche. Everybody outdoors is walking
between two of them and knows how far apart they are.

The hold counts out loud. Watches at the post, boats at the landing, weight on
the factor's scale, and the count is agreed by whoever is standing there and
corrected by whoever disagrees. A crew comes in and is counted before it is
greeted. Somebody keeps a list of what has come up out of the Reach and who
brought it, and the list is not secret and nobody says what it is for.

The air smells of rendered fat and of wet rope, and the rope smell is on ground
where there is no rope. Under any cut stone in the hold, put a hand flat: it is
warm, and the top of the same stone is cold. Seaward there is open water. Every
other direction is reed, and one raised road going inward through it.

## Fields

**Extent:** Four hundred yards of cut ground from the water's edge to the
last roofed building, and a chain of kept lane beyond that on every landward
side. Nothing outside the cut line is Reedhold, and the hold says so.

**Rate:** The whole hold is crossed on foot inside a quarter of a day, on level
cut ground that has been kept level. The road out is nine miles of causeway to
the Ashen Fen and is walked in a day by somebody who leaves at first light.

**Time:** Days. The hold counts in days and the cutters count in watches, and
the two are reconciled aloud at the tally post at dusk, out loud, by whoever is
standing there.

**Light:** Tallow, in niches cut at shoulder height every forty paces, lit at
dusk and let burn out. The lit ground is the hold. A party standing outside the
last niche is not in Reedhold any more and everybody here treats them as though
they are not.

**Goods and services:** Everything the Reach has. Tallow, oiled reed-cloth,
rope, dried peat, salt fish, a bed inside the light, a boat and somebody to work
it, and a factor who will price Covenant work by the pound without asking where
it came from. What Reedhold does not stock, it can send for.

**What the difficulty measures here:** The size of the settlement and what can
be got in it. Reedhold is the best-supplied place in the Reach and the easiest
region in the setting, and everything a party fails to buy here they will be
without for the rest of the Reach.

**Refusals:** Nobody will hire out as a guide past the fourth frame of the Long
Cut, at any price, and the refusal is flat rather than negotiable. The
reedcutters will sell a crossing and will not sell a return.

**Firm ground off the cut:** None, and the hold will state it plainly to anyone
who asks. Reedhold stands on driven piles over peat, and the only ground here
that holds a foot for a full watch is ground somebody is keeping.

## Connections

The Reed Ground is the spine. Every road, lane and landing in Reedhold
runs off the cleared chain the hold keeps around itself, and the hold's whole
claim is that the chain stays cut (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-07). A party
crosses from the ground into the Tallow Houses by going under a roof, and there
is no other boundary: the houses are the part of Reedhold that buys, and the
ground is the part that is kept.

Between the two stands the tally post, notched by watch down one face and up the
other, and a party that has not read it does not know which count it is standing
on (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-08). Both containers are walked at the road
rate on level cut ground, and the decision Reedhold asks is not how to cross it
but how long to stay inside the light.

The Long Cut leaves from the seaward end of the reed ground, and it is the only
way out of the hold that does not involve a boat.
