## Referee Overview

**Reactions:** *Reading the order aloud at this rim:* nothing comes up the shaft for a watch. *Reading it aloud anywhere else:* everything within a chain that was standing still starts moving (MAGICAL TOMES, T-TOM-03). *Standing on the slab:* nothing, and the slab does not move. *Drawing a peg:* nothing here, and one rim further along, something that was pegged is not. *Putting the light out:* the letters are still legible, and they are legible by touch, which is what they were cut deep for.

**Gate:** The rim is not a gate and this is the only opening in the Deep Sill that is not. What it holds is the instruction, and the instruction is the region's whole content: stop at the sill (LORE, T-LOR-05). The answer that is not obeying it is the shaft, which is pegged, sound, and goes down past the point the order names. Both branches are priced. Obeying costs the party everything below, which is the only thing down here worth anything. Going costs what going has cost everybody who went.

*Approaches.* From the pegged rim seaward, which shows the letters first and the standing slab not at all. From the wide shaft inward, which comes at the rim from below the letters and shows the second hand before the first. From the dressed shaft, which comes out beside the sockets and shows that the slab has been moved.

*Why it is still here.* Because it is a slab and a rim, and nobody has ever wanted either. Nine parties have read it. Everything that has been taken out of the Deep Sill came out past this stone and none of it was this stone.

*What comes out.* The order itself, cut into a slab made to stand upright at a shaft head with the sockets for its feet cut in the rim (TREASURE TABLE V, T-TR5-03).

{VALUE: 9000 cn} {WT: 20} {QUALITY: Masterwork}

It is the Covenant's own last instruction, in the hand that wrote it, and it is the only object on the Reach that anybody from any faction would agree is worth what it is worth.

*Departure.* A party carrying the slab cannot go down. It is a lift for three on level ground and there is no level ground below this rim, and the shafts are pegged for a load rather than for a body.

## Features

### The Rim

Cut square, pegged all the way round, with the order cut into it in letters a hand deep (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-05). The strokes are uneven and they get worse across the line, which is what haste looks like in stone.

*Stop at the sill. What lies under it was not made, and it is not ours to open. Peg the shaft and set a watch that will outlast us* (LORE, T-LOR-05).

The pegged rim runs seaward -> R06-L01, and the wide shaft inward -> R06-L03.

### The Second Hand

The last word of the order is cut by somebody else. The strokes are even, the depth is right, and the spacing is correct, and all three are wrong for the hand that cut the rest of it.

The word is *us*. Whoever finished it finished it after the person who started it had stopped, and they finished it correctly, and then they pegged the shaft.

### The Standing Slab

Upright at the shaft head, chest high, cut on both faces with the same words, so that it reads to somebody arriving and to somebody coming up (TREASURE TABLE V, T-TR5-03).

{VALUE: 9000 cn} {WT: 20} {QUALITY: Masterwork}

It is out of its sockets by the width of a hand, and it is out on the down side. Something moved it from below and put it back and did not put it back straight.

### The Sockets

Two, cut into the rim to take the slab's feet, square, exact. The stone in the near socket is polished at the bottom and the far one is not.

A slab that has stood in a socket for four generations does not polish it. A slab that has been lifted out and set back repeatedly does.

The dressed shaft runs inward past the sockets -> R06-L08.

### The Slate Copy

Lying in the lee of the slab where the pegs are stacked: a slate copy of the same words, carried by whoever was set to keep the watch (MAGICAL TOMES, T-TOM-03).

{VALUE: 6000 cn} {WT: 2} {QUALITY: Masterwork}

It is face down. Turning it over is the named action, and it is the only thing at this rim that has not been rained on, which cannot be true of anything that has lain face down under open sky for four generations, and there is no open sky over this rim.
