## Player Overview

**The slope** falls about a hand every ten feet across three hundred feet, in steps of about a hand each, regular, running the whole way across without a break. Nothing else here is regular.

**The cold from below** comes up through the soles on dry stone, and it is worse the further down the slope a party goes.

**What turns** is here in numbers, and it turns toward a lit wick together and stops when the wick goes out.

**The count from above** is the party's own count, one number behind, and on this floor it comes from overhead. There is nothing overhead: the roof is forty feet up and it is the same unmade stone as the floor.

**The ways off** rise toward the shaft foot, the warm floor, the counting ground, and dry ground inward.

## Referee Overview

**Reactions:** *Lighting a wick:* everything within reach of it turns toward it together, and here that is more than anywhere else in the region. *Putting it out:* everything stops. *Lighting it again:* what stopped is closer, and it is closer by the distance it could have walked. *Counting aloud:* the answer comes from above rather than from ahead, which happens nowhere else. *Going on downward:* the floor keeps sloping and the region's Fields say the extent is unmeasured, and they mean it.

**Gate:** None. There is no door in the Deep Sill and this is the furthest a party can get without leaving the map. What gates it is the light and what the light costs.

*Job for the region.* It is the floor, in both senses. Everything in the deep water drains toward it, the region's own darkness is at its worst here, and it is the one room in the setting where the answering count comes from overhead.

*Cost in turns.* Three to cross, at ten feet a turn, and the rate is set by the light rather than by the footing.

*What is true that the Overview withholds.* The slope is not a slope. It is a series of shallow steps, each one about the height of a hand, running the whole way across, and they are regular. They are the only regular thing in the deep water and they are not cut: the stone was formed that way, or something wore it that way, and both readings are available and neither is settled anywhere in the corpus.

## Features

### The Slope

Falling about a hand every ten feet across three hundred feet, in steps of about a hand each, regular, and running the whole way across without a break.

Nothing else in the Deep Sill is regular. A party who has been reading this region as unmade ground meets the first thing in it that has a measure.

### The Cold From Below

Coming up through the soles on stone that is dry, out of the level below rather than out of the air, and it is worse the further down the slope a party goes (HAZARDS, T-HAZ-09) {TEST: Constitution} {WOUND: Frost} {CONDITION: Numb-footed / footing cues are not felt until they are stood on / until a fire}.

On a slope made of hand-high steps, not feeling the footing is the whole of the danger, and the tell is that a party stops being able to say how many steps they have come down.

### What Turns

Whatever is here is here in numbers, and it turns toward a lit wick together and stops when the wick goes out (BESTIARY, T-BES-05).

### The Count From Above

The answering count in the party's own voices comes from overhead on this floor and from ahead everywhere else. There is nothing overhead: the roof is forty feet up and it is the same unmade stone as the floor.
