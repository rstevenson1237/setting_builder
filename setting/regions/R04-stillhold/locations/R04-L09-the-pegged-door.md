## Player Overview

**The door** is oak on a bronze frame, shut, with two pegs driven through the frame from outside. No forcing anywhere on it, no mark of a tool, nothing off a hinge.

**The plate sockets** stand empty at head height, cut square, with the stone round them torn where something was levered rather than unpegged.

**The board** hangs on a hook beside it at the height a hand falls, waxed, the notches still crisp, the count running to the bottom of the face and continuing on the reverse.

Every fitting on the door is green with age. The hook is bright.

**The recitation** is given aloud, from memory, in the same order every time, by anyone in the Order who is asked, and it takes most of a turn and they do not hurry it.

## Referee Overview

**Service:** The count of what is behind it, recited item by item, by anyone in the Order, to anyone who asks. It is the most complete inventory of a closed hold anywhere on the Reach and it is free.

**Cost:** An undertaking, given aloud, that the door stays shut. They write down who gave it and the day. Nothing else is asked and nothing else is accepted.

**Refusal:** It is not opened. Not for coin, not for the Remnant, not for a reckoning, and not by them. The refusal is not defended and not explained, and there is no lock on the door at all.

*Why it is still shut.* Because the peg is on the outside and the count is on the hook, and everybody who has come this far has taken the count instead. That is not a trick. It is what the Order is for.

*The gate and both prices.* The board lifts off the hook and the hook is the head of the peg, so taking the count down draws the peg and opens the door (PUZZLES, T-PUZ-02), (KEYS, T-KEY-04). It cannot be rehung: the wax has set to the hook's shape and the hook comes away with it. The answer that is not the board is prising, which keeps the count on its hook, takes a turn an attempt, and splits the frame often enough that the Order will find it either way (PROCEDURES, T-PRC-05).

*Neither is the mistake.* Taking the board opens the door cleanly and destroys the count, which is the only record of what a hold held before it was closed, and the Order has been keeping it against a reckoning they expect. Prising preserves the count and announces itself. The Order does not say which they would rather, and asking them produces the count again.

*Departure.* Whatever comes out of here is recognised. Everything behind this door is on a list two hundred and six people agreed at both sides of it, and the Order will recite the list to anybody, including to whoever a party tries to sell to.

The Order reads the board, not the door, and the board is intact. They have not noticed the plate is gone, and they will know within the hour of its being shown to anybody.

## Features

### The Door

Oak on a bronze frame, shut, with two pegs driven through the frame from outside and seated from within so they cannot be drawn (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-06). No forcing anywhere on it, no mark of a tool, nothing off a hinge.

The frame is set into a wall two courses thick, and the sockets where a bronze plate was pegged stand empty at head height, the holes torn rather than cut.

### The Board

Hung on a hook beside the door at the height a hand falls, waxed, the notches still crisp. The count runs to the bottom of the face and continues on the reverse: two hundred and six out, across four years, the fixed things left, the count agreed at both sides of the door (LORE, T-LOR-06).

Every fitting on the door is green with age. The hook is bright, and it is bright because it is the peg head and the peg was driven last.

### The Recitation

Anyone in the Order will give the count aloud, from memory, in the same order every time, starting with the fixed things and ending with the tally. It takes most of a turn and they do not hurry it and they do not stop if the listener leaves.

What is behind the door is a bench, a counting frame, bronze fittings still pegged, a barrel of tallow gone hard, and one item the recitation names without describing (HOARDS, T-HRD-01).
