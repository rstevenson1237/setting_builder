## Player Overview

**The slipway** is cut into the Covenant slab at the water side, worn in two grooves at the width of a boat's keel. The grooves are worn deeper than any boat now on the Reach would cut.

At its head, cut peat is stacked in courses to dry. The ground beside the stacks is a shade darker than the ground round them.

The water at the foot of it is ankle deep and does not move at all.

## Referee Overview

**Service:** The landing, and the peat that comes off it. Boats work the still water on the hold's water sides, bring in cut peat from the covenant ground, and take nothing out.

**Cost:** For peat, a share of the day's cutting or coin, and the hold prefers the cutting. For a crossing, nothing, because there is nowhere on the still water to cross to.

**Refusal:** Nobody will take a boat out past the wall's corner. The still water goes on and the hold does not go on with it, and the refusal is stated as a rule of the landing rather than as anybody's decision.

*Hours:* worked in the season the peat is cut and empty the rest of the year. A party arriving out of season finds the boats drawn up and nobody on the slipway.

*Extent:* forty yards of slipway and a stone landing, ankle-deep water at the foot of it and not moving. Yards outdoors, days.

*Nil:* no tide. The water at the foot of the wall does not rise, fall or move at all, and the hold has never seen it do any of the three.
