## Player Overview

Forty yards square inside the landward wall, slab underfoot, with two people standing in the one gap in the stonework.

**The opening** is eight feet wide and there is no gate in it and no gate was ever fitted: the sockets one would hang in were never cut. The passage through the wall is long enough to be dark at midday.

**The book** lies open on a stand under the wall's lee, weighted with a stone: a name, a direction, and the day.

**The written word** is issued on waxed board carrying the hold's mark, and it is written in a lettering a party may not be able to read.

**The warden of the third door** is here most afternoons, and she states the count of what is behind her door at a party before they have asked her anything at all.

**The chapter door** is the one door in the inward wall, opened from inside or not at all, with nothing hung beside it.

## Referee Overview

**Service:** Entry, and the written safe conduct that goes with it. Two of the Order stand in the opening at all daylight hours and they write down every person who comes in or goes out, with the day and the direction.

**Cost:** A name and a destination, said aloud and written. Nothing else, and there is no toll. A party who will not give a destination is admitted anyway, and the refusal is written down instead.

**Refusal:** Nobody is refused entry. What is refused is the chapter door, which is opened from inside or not at all, and which is not opened for anybody who has not first been written into the yard's book.

*The rule the yard keeps.* Everything crosses in front of two people and is written down by one of them, and the writing is read back. It is the same discipline as Reedhold's count and it produces the opposite thing: Reedhold's count is agreed aloud and forgotten, and this one is kept.

*Schedule.* Open at first light, shut at dusk, and the shutting is a bar rather than a door. Between the middle ringing and dusk the supply from the seaward lane is unloaded here and the yard is full.

*Where a question goes.* Onto the board. A question asked in the yard is written down with who asked it, and the Order will say so before answering, every time, and a party who does not want that has been told.

*Extent.* Forty yards square inside the landward wall, slab underfoot, one opening out and one door in. Time in days.

The book goes back four years and no further. The page before the first is torn out rather than cut, and the tear is old.

## Features

### The Opening

The only way through the wall, eight feet wide, with no gate in it and no gate ever fitted: the sockets a gate would hang in were never cut. The wall is two courses thick here and the passage through it is long enough to be dark at midday (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-10).

The dry lanes leave from it, inward -> R04-L05 and seaward -> R04-L06.

### The Book

On a stand under the wall's lee, open, weighted with a stone, written up as people pass. Two columns: the name and the direction, and a third, narrow, for the day.

It goes back four years and no further, and the page before the first is torn out rather than cut, and the tear is old.

### The Written Word

Waxed board carrying the hold's mark and the day, honoured by the Covenant Remnant anywhere on the Covenant Ground. It also states where the bearer said they were going, in the Covenant's layer, and a party who cannot read it cannot tell that it does (LORE, T-LOR-02).

### The Warden of the Third Door

She is in the yard most afternoons, and she states the count of what is behind her door at a party before they have asked her anything at all (NAMED CREATURES, T-CRE-02). She is not stationed here and nobody sends her.

She does it to strangers only. The hold has heard it.

### The Chapter Door

One door in the inward wall, opened from inside, nothing hung beside it -> R04-L08. The open ground of the hold runs seaward -> R04-L01, and the ration door is sixty yards along it -> R04-L04.

Selling anything out of Covenant work is not done here and the Order will say where it is done, and how many days away, and that they will write down that they said so (PROCEDURES, T-PRC-08).
