## Referee Overview

**Service:** The counts. Every door in the hold has its board hanging here in copy, and the register that cross-rules them is on the table at the end. Anyone in the Order will read any of it aloud to anybody, and will lend nothing.

**Cost:** For a reading, nothing at all. For the register, an undertaking that whatever is read is not acted on inside the hold, given aloud, and written down beside the reading.

**Refusal:** Nothing leaves this passage. Not a board, not a copy, not a rubbing. A party who wants a count out of here memorises it or writes it themselves in front of somebody, and the Order will lend the wax and watch them do it.

*The rule the passage keeps.* A board and its register agree or the discrepancy is written up as its own row, dated, and left standing. There are nineteen such rows and not one of them has been resolved, and the Order regards leaving them unresolved as the correct thing to have done.

*Schedule.* Lit only when somebody is working in it, which is most of the day. The wicks are counted out and counted back.

*Where a question goes.* Into the register, as a row. Asking about a door is itself an event and it is written down under the door's name, so a party asking about the third door twice has made a pattern.

*Extent.* Eighty feet of passage, six feet wide, boards along one side, dressed slab underfoot. Feet indoors, and the passage is crossed in a few minutes and read in most of a day.

## Features

### The Boards

One to a hook, waxed, the notches still crisp, hung in the order the doors were closed rather than in the order they stand (LORE, T-LOR-06), (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-06).

Every hook here is a plain hook screwed into the wall. On the doors themselves the hook is the head of the peg, which is a fact the Order has never stated and every one of them knows (PUZZLES, T-PUZ-02), (KEYS, T-KEY-04).

### The Register

On a table at the inward end, bound, cross-ruled, and open. Boards down the left, register rows across, and nineteen discrepancy rows at the back in a hand that changes four times.

A wax board of the kind that answers a question written as a count sits beside it and is not used any more (TREASURE TABLE I, T-TR1-06). {VALUE: 15 cn} {WT: 1} {QUALITY: Poor}

The reason it is not used is written in the register as the twentieth row, and the twentieth row is scratched out.

### The Nineteen Rows

Each names a door, a board figure and a register figure, and the two differ by between one and four items. They are spread across eleven doors and four years.

Read together they are not random: every discrepancy is a shortfall, every shortfall is in bronze, and the total is the weight of a bell.

### The Doors Off It

The chapter house proper is at the seaward end where a ration is being stated aloud -> R04-L08. One door has the only board nobody takes down -> R04-L09. A bare cell with a bed and no wick is offered to anybody who asks -> R04-L11.
