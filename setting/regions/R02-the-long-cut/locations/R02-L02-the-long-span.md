## Player Overview

Three miles of fitted slab running straight inward, level to within a hand, wide enough for two abreast and no wider. That is **the straights**, and there is nothing on them nearer than the next frame.

**The courses** are visible from the cut on both sides: five of them at the seaward end and eight here, each a hand thicker than the one above it.

**The crossing places** are four dips where the slab takes a hand of water for twenty yards. The stone at each is polished smooth along the middle and rough at the edges.

A shout dropped here does not come back. Two people at opposite ends of the same straight cannot call each other.

## Referee Overview

**Approach:** Sound stops coming back. A shout dropped flat at a chain, so that two people at opposite ends of the same straight cannot call each other (HAZARDS, T-HAZ-12). It begins about a mile out of Reedhold and it does not let up again for nine miles, and a party notices it when the first person tries to call to the last.

**Terrain:** Fitted slab, laid without mortar, each course a hand thicker than the one above so the road grows heavier as it goes down (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-01). Two miles a watch on it. Half a mile a watch off it, and the reed closes behind at three paces.

*What has to be read.* The road's own state. Between the frames the span is a long straight and the straights are not the same: three of them are laid on made ground and one of them is laid on nothing, and the one on nothing gives a different note underfoot. It is not dangerous. It is where the road will go under first, and it is going under at a hand a generation.

*Both routes.* On the span a party moves at the road rate and is visible from one bend to the next. Off it they move at a quarter of that, unseen and unable to see, and a party strung out in the cut cannot call each other back {TEST: Sanity}. Neither is the mistake and the road never becomes the danger.

*Table effects.* Everything on the Encounter table can reach a party in the cut and nothing can reach one on the middle of a straight inside a watch. Weather that lays the smoke flat halves the rate on the span and does not change it in the cut, because the cut is already the slow rate.

The third straight rings hollow underfoot for two hundred yards. It will carry a party at any pace and it will not carry a bell.

## Features

### The Straights

Three miles of unbroken road between the first frame and the bend, level to within a hand over the whole length, and the slab is the same slab that runs under Reedhold's landward niches.

The third straight rings hollow under a boot for two hundred yards. There is nothing under it: the made ground it was laid on has gone and the road is spanning. It carries a party at any pace and it will not carry a bell.

The span runs seaward to the first frame -> R02-L01 and inward to the bend -> R02-L03. The cut edge is a step off the stone -> R02-L07.

### The Courses

Each course down is a hand thicker than the one above (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-01). Where the road is above water the count of courses is visible from the cut, and it is five at the seaward end and eight here.

A hand laid flat under any of them finds the stone warm, and warmer the further inward it is laid, and the top of the same stone cold (RUMOURS, T-RUM-03).

### The Crossing Places

Four points on the three miles where the slab dips and takes a hand of water for twenty yards, and the crossing is a stated procedure (PROCEDURES, T-PRC-01). The slabs at each are polished smooth along the middle and rough at the edges, and the wear says where to walk to anybody who reads it before stepping on.
