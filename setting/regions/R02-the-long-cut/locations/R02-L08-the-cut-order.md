## Referee Overview

**Approach:** The cutting changes. For a chain either side of this lane the reed is taken to the root rather than to the water, and a boot goes through stubble rather than through stems. It is felt underfoot a hundred yards before the post is visible over the reed heads, and a party that turns back there has lost nothing.

**Terrain:** Kept lane, a chain wide, dry to the ankle, walked at a mile a watch because the stubble takes a boot. The reed either side stands as high as a shoulder and closes behind at three paces.

*What comes out of it.* A post. It is sunk four feet, it weighs what a bell weighs, and it is the original: every standing order cut on every lane head in the Reach was copied from this one, letter for letter (UNIQUE TREASURES, T-TRE-06). It is worth what a buyer will give and it is worth everything to the Reedcutters, who believe without exception that the cutting stops if it goes.

{VALUE: 10000 cn} {WT: 18} {QUALITY: Masterwork}

*Why it is still here.* Every cutter on the Reach knows where it is. None of them guards it, because it has never occurred to any of them that somebody would want a post.

*The gate and both prices.* Getting it out is the gate and the gate is weight. Four people and a full watch of digging takes it, and the digging is visible for a mile in every direction across cut ground. The answer that is not the digging is the copy: the letters can be taken off in wax in a turn, standing up, and a wax pull sells at the Tallow House for a fraction and tells a reader everything the post says. A party that takes the pull arrives without the post and without having been seen digging.

*Departure.* A party carrying the post cannot walk the cut and has to use the lane, and the lane comes out at the road, and the road is where the cutters are. A party carrying the wax walks out through the reed and nobody counts them.

## Features

### The Post

Oak, squared, sunk four feet, standing at the head of the lane where every other lane head in the Reach has one. The letters are cut deep, straight, and by one hand, and the last line of them runs slightly downhill where the cutter ran out of face (LORE, T-LOR-11).

It is the only one on the Reach whose letters are not a copy, and the difference is visible to anybody who has read two: every copy reproduces the downhill last line exactly, including the fault.

### The Copies

Three spare faces lie stacked in the stubble beside the post, cut and never driven, oak, and weathered grey. Somebody was making replacements and stopped, and stopped mid-face: the third is finished to the last word.

Counting the words on the unfinished face against the post gives the point where the copying stopped, and that point is the same watch the gap in the tally posts opens (PUZZLES, T-PUZ-06), (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-08).

### The Spoil Heap

Cut reed piled and left, not stacked and not burned, going down to peat at the bottom of the pile. It has been added to recently, and the recent cutting is not the same length as the old.

Turning the heap over, which takes a turn and makes a smell that carries, finds a cutter's hook ground back to nothing on one side and re-hafted twice (TREASURE TABLE I, T-TR1-02). {VALUE: 10 cn} {WT: 1} {QUALITY: Poor}

### The Old Lane Head

A second lane leaves the post, narrower, with the reed closing over it from both sides -> R02-L10. Nobody has cut it since before the fault in the count, and the cutters walk past its mouth without looking down it.

The lane back to the cleared chain and the road runs the other way -> R02-L07.
