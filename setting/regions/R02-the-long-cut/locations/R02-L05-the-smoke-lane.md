## Referee Overview

**Approach:** The smell, a full mile out and upwind, and it is peat rather than reed. The smoke is visible as a flat line along the reed tops long before it is stood in, which makes this the one hazard in the region a party can decline at no cost.

**Terrain:** On the span, two miles a watch normally, one mile a watch through the smoke, because the road has to be read by foot. *Rate in the cut:* half a mile a watch, unchanged, because the smoke lies above the cut rather than in it.

*Decision:* through it on the road, which halves the rate and blinds the party to everything past arm's length; or down into the cut, which is the same rate as the smoke and is not blind, and which arrives having lost the frames they were counting by (HAZARDS, T-HAZ-04) {CONDITION: Smoke-blind / the party moves at half the stated rate and reads no cue further off than arm's length / until wind or open ground}.

*Table effects:* nothing that hunts by sight can work the road while the smoke is on it. Everything that works by sound can, and a party reading the road by foot is making a great deal of it. Wind off the sea clears the whole stretch inside a watch and the weather table says whether it is blowing.

*Carrying a light through it* is a stated procedure and it is the one thing here that can go badly rather than slowly (PROCEDURES, T-PRC-06).

## Features

### The Smoke

Lying at knee height along a mile and a half of span, level along the top, not moving, and it does not thin at the edges: it has a face (HAZARDS, T-HAZ-04).

It is not from the peat cut, which is four miles seaward and downwind. Nobody has established where it comes from and the cutters do not regard the question as interesting.
