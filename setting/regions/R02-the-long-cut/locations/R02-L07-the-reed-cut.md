## Player Overview

A boot goes from ringing slab to giving peat in one step, and the peat gives twice: once for itself and once for whatever is under it.

**The cut edge** runs a chain out from the road on both sides, reed taken to the root along a line as clean as a blade, and the stubble is even where it is being kept.

**The reed** past it stands as high as a shoulder and closes behind at three paces. From inside it the road cannot be seen and the frames cannot be counted.

**The lanes off it** leave inland at three points: one to a post with letters cut a hand deep, one to peat stacked in courses, and one that runs into standing reed and stops.

## Referee Overview

**Approach:** The ground. A boot goes from ringing slab to giving peat in one step and gives twice: once for the peat and once for whatever is under it. That is the whole warning and it arrives at the moment of committing, which is why the decision to leave the road is made on the road.

**Terrain:** Cut reed to the root, standing peat under it, shin-deep water within a chain of the span and waist-deep beyond. Half a mile a watch, and the pace does not come back by pushing. The reed closes behind at three paces and the road cannot be seen from inside it.

*What has to be read.* Where the cut is being kept and where it is not. A kept chain is cut to the root and the stubble is even. An abandoned one is cut to the water and the stems are closing over at the base. The difference is visible from the road, in the colour of the cut edge, and it is what says whether a party in the reed can be found by anybody.

*Both routes.* The cut is the only way to move on this region without being visible from the span for a mile in both directions, and it costs four times the time and the ability to count the frames. Reed cuts through cloth are fine and slow to close and the cutters wear oiled reed-cloth and a party usually does not (HAZARDS, T-HAZ-10) {WOUND: Piercing} {CONDITION: Fen-fever / one watch in six is lost to shaking / until a full rest inside the light}.

*Table effects.* Reed-hounds work the shallows in threes here and give ground the moment metal is struck and come back the moment the sound stops, which makes noise a resource rather than a mistake (BESTIARY, T-BES-02). Nothing that needs open ground can reach a party in the reed.

A kept chain is cut to the root and the stubble is even; an abandoned one is cut to the water and the stems are closing at the base. The difference is visible from the road in the colour of the cut edge.

## Features

### The Cut Edge

Reed taken to the root along a line as clean as a blade, a chain out from the road on both sides, kept by the standing order and by nobody in particular (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-07), (LORE, T-LOR-11).

Standing on the line, a party is in the open. One pace inside it they cannot be seen from the road, and one pace outside it they can be seen from a mile.

### The Reed

Shoulder high, closing behind at three paces, and it does not part the same way twice. Movement through it is audible at a chain in still air and inaudible at twice that, because the water takes a sound and gives none of it back.

The oldest word anybody out here still uses daily, and it decomposes on sight (LANGUAGE AND ROOT VOCABULARY, T-LNG-14).

### The Lanes Off It

The cut lane to the order post runs inland -> R02-L08, and a worked lane goes to the peat stacks -> R02-L09. The reed face where the cutting stops is a chain further out -> R02-L11.

The road stands a foot above the cut and can be rejoined at three points on this stretch -> R02-L02, -> R02-L04, -> R02-L01.
