## Player Overview

Smoke going up rather than along, which is the only vertical smoke on this road, and it can be seen from the span for two miles. Downwind it smells of burning green reed.

**The pit** is dug into the peat and lined with slab that came off the road, cracked and replaced twice.

**The lying-up ground** beside it is flattened reed under a cover of cut stems, big enough for a gang, and it is dry. A coat in oiled reed-cloth hangs on a stake beside it.

**The gang** is here or has just gone. When they are here there are eleven of them, working the chain on a schedule nobody has written down.

**The ways out** are the road a hundred yards off across cut stubble, and a worked lane running to the stacks.

## Referee Overview

**Approach:** Smoke, and it is not the lying smoke. A cutting gang burns spoil in a pit here and the smoke goes up rather than along, which is the only vertical smoke in the region and is visible from the span for two miles. What arrives first, downwind, is the smell of burning green reed, which is not the smell of peat.

**Terrain:** Trodden ground in the cut a hundred yards off the road, dry to the ankle, and it is the only ground off the span that a party can lie down on. Half a mile a watch to reach it through the reed, or a straight hundred yards from the road across cut stubble in full view.

*What has to be read.* Whether the gang is here. The pit is warm for a day after they leave and cold after two, and a hand in the ash gives the answer. A party that reads the pit before walking in knows whether they are about to meet eleven people or nobody.

*Both routes.* Straight across the stubble from the road takes a quarter of a watch and is seen by anything on the span for two miles. Through the reed takes a watch and a half and arrives from a direction the gang does not watch, and arrives with reed cuts.

*Table effects.* This is the one place in the region where the cutting gang Encounter row is not a chance: they are here or they have just gone. Nothing that closes while nobody is facing it comes within a chain of the pit while it is burning.

## Features

### The Pit

Dug into the peat, lined with slab that came off the road, and it has been in use long enough that the slab has cracked and been replaced twice. Spoil burns in it green and slow and the smoke goes straight up.

The slab lining is Covenant work and it is the only place in the region where a party can see a course of the road in section: a hand thicker at the bottom than at the top, which is what the whole nine miles is doing under them (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-01).

### The Gang

Eleven of them when they are here, working the chain on a schedule nobody has written down, and they will trade a watch count and where the peat holds for news from seaward (NAMED CREATURES, T-CRE-01).

They will not be on the road past the second watch and they will not say why and they will not stay to be asked (RUMOURS, T-RUM-01). They go into the cut rather than back to the hold, which nobody who has not watched them do it would guess.

Reading the lane's posts back with them to find where a count broke is a stated procedure and costs a watch (PROCEDURES, T-PRC-03).

### The Lying-Up Ground

Flattened reed under a cover of cut stems, big enough for a gang, and it is dry. A cutter's coat in oiled reed-cloth hangs on a stake beside it, which turns fen water for a full watch and not one hour longer (TREASURE TABLE II, T-TR2-01).

### The Ways Out
