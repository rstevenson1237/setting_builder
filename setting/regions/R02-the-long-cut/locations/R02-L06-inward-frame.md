## Referee Overview

**Approach:** The ring goes out of the road. Nine miles of slab give a boot a ring and for the last two hundred yards it flattens to a knock. It is felt through the sole a hundred yards before the water is visible, and turning back costs the watch.

**Terrain:** On the span, two miles a watch to the last frame, then one mile a watch for the last half mile, because the slab is under a hand of water and the footing has to be read. *Rate in the cut:* half a mile a watch, and the cut ends here: the standing order stops where the road goes under.

*Decision:* go on into the fen, which is the region's whole point and is a different region's problem; or stop at the last frame, which is the last dry ground with anything built on it for four miles, and which is where every cutter on the Reach turns round.

*Table effects:* the cutting-gang row cannot happen past this frame, because the order stops here. The bell-carrier row is likelier: this is where the carrier turns round too, and it turns round on the span rather than at the frame.

*Nil:* no tally post. The frames are counted from the sea inward and this is the last one, and the post that should stand at its foot was never driven — the socket is cut and empty (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-08).

## Features

### The Last Frame

Two uprights and a crossbeam, the pin seated, the socket clean, standing with its feet in a hand of water (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-02).

Waxed and folded into the pin socket is a wax board: a tally of bells by road, ruled in two columns, the hand growing smaller and steadier down the page as the writer ran out of board (LORE, T-LOR-12).

The last row reads *Bell Causeway* and the column beside it is empty, and the rule for the row below it was drawn and never filled.
