## Referee Overview

**Approach:** The footing changes. Coming out of Reedhold a party walks off board and cut ground onto fitted slab, and the sound of their own steps goes from a knock to a ring inside three paces and stays a ring for nine miles. It happens at the last niche and it is the last certain thing on the road.

**Terrain:** Fitted slab, two abreast, laid without mortar, at the road rate of two miles a watch. The cut runs a chain either side at half a mile a watch through reed and standing peat, and the pace does not come back by pushing.

*What has to be read.* The count. This is the first frame out of the hold and it is where the road's number is available: the post at its foot is notched by watch down one face and up the other, and cut into its head above the notches, in the Covenant's layer, is which road this is counted from the sea. Every post on the region carries the same line and this is the one a party passes first.

*Both routes.* The span is the fast road and it is visible from every other point on it for a mile in both directions. The cut is half the rate, it cannot be seen from, and it cannot see the frames it is counting by. A party that wants to reach the fourth frame unobserved pays two watches and arrives unable to answer the question waiting for them.

*Table effects.* Nothing on the Encounter table that works the open cut can reach a party standing at this frame inside a watch, because the cleared chain here is doubled: Reedhold keeps its own cut line and the road keeps its own, and they overlap for the first mile.

## Features

### The Frame

Two uprights and a crossbeam, the first of twelve, the pin seated and the socket clean (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-02). Nothing hangs from it and the rope on the pin is not new.

Cut into the underside of the crossbeam, upside down to a walker and right way up to anybody lying beneath it, is the ringing order: seaward first, then inward, never two in one watch (LORE, T-LOR-01).

Sounding it is a stated procedure and the note carries inward much further than seaward (PROCEDURES, T-PRC-02).

### The Post

Driven at the frame's foot, notched by watch, the top notches fresh (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-08). Above the notches, in letters nobody in Reedhold reads, is the road's count from the sea.

The count on this post and the count on Reedhold's post are the same number. Every post inward of here is one lower, and the numbering runs down rather than up, which is how the road knows where the sea is.

### The Doubled Cut

For the first mile out of the hold the cleared chain is a chain and a half rather than a chain, because two standing orders overlap here and neither has been rescinded (LORE, T-LOR-11). Beyond the mile it narrows to a chain and stays there.

The overlap is visible from the road as a change in the height of the reed at the cut edge, and it is where the cutters stop being Reedhold's and start being nobody's.

The road runs back to the hold's light -> R01-L01 and inward along the span -> R02-L02. The cut edge runs beside it -> R02-L07.
