## Overview

Fitted slab, two abreast, laid without mortar and running straight to
the limit of the light. Each course down is a hand thicker than the one above
it, so the road grows heavier the further it goes, and it is the only ground out
here that is reliably ground. Either side of it the reed has been cut back a
chain and kept cut, and the cut edge is as clean as a blade.

Frames stand at the bends and at the crossings: two uprights, a crossbeam,
nothing hanging. The pin is still seated in every one and the socket in every
one is clean. On some of them the rope is new. At the foot of each stands a post
notched down one face and up the other, and the notches at the top are fresh.

Sound goes out and does not come back. A shout drops flat at a chain, so that
two people at opposite ends of the same straight cannot call each other, and a
struck note carries further inward than it carries seaward and further than it
should carry at all. Peat smoke lies along the reed tops at knee height and does
not lift.

Step off the stone and the reed closes behind at three paces. From in there the
road cannot be seen and the frames cannot be counted, and the ground gives twice
under every step. The reedcutters do not go past the fourth frame after the
second watch, and they will not say why, and they will not stay to be asked.

## Fields

**Extent:** Nine miles of raised span, seaward to inward, from the last
tallow niche at Reedhold to the point where the slab goes under at the Ashen Fen.
The cut runs a chain wide on both sides of it for the whole length, and open
reed stands beyond that as far as anyone has walked.

**Rate on the span:** Two miles a watch, walking, with the road above water. The
span is level, fitted and clear, and nothing on it slows a party who stays on
it.

**Rate in the cut:** Half a mile a watch through reed and standing peat, and the
pace does not come back by pushing. A party that leaves the span and rejoins it
has lost the watch and cannot make it up before dark.

**Time:** Watches, six to the day. Every frame on the span carries a tally post
notched by watch down one face and up the other, and the count is accurate
because the cutters keep it and correct each other aloud.

**Light:** No niches past the fourth frame. What a party carries is what they
have, and there is no windbreak on nine miles of raised road.

**What the difficulty measures here:** The treacherousness of the terrain and
what leaving the road costs. The span itself is not the danger and never
becomes it. Everything this region charges, it charges for stepping off.

**Water:** Shin-deep in the cut within a chain of the span, waist-deep beyond
it, and unmeasured past the reed line.

**Frames:** Twelve, at the bends and at the crossings, counted from the sea
inward. Every pin is seated and every socket is clean, and nothing hangs from
any of them.

## Connections

The Long Span is the spine and it is the region. Everything in The Long
Cut hangs off nine miles of fitted slab running seaward to inward, laid without
mortar and growing a course heavier the further down it goes
(ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-01). The Reed Cut is the cleared chain either
side of it: the same standing order Reedhold keeps, held here by nobody, and
kept anyway (LORE, T-LOR-11).

Crossing between the two containers is a step off the stone and it is the only
decision the region asks. On the span a party moves at the road rate and is
visible from one bend to the next. In the cut they move at a fraction of it, they
cannot be seen, and they cannot see the frames they are counting by
(ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-02).

The region has three ends. Reedhold at the seaward end, the Ashen Fen where the
span goes under, and a lane branching inward towards Stillhold at the fourth
frame. The branch is the second route into the Covenant Ground and it is longer,
drier and watched.
