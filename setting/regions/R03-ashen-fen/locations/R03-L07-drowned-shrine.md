## Player Overview

The water shallows over a floor that is not the fen's. Dressed slabs, laid true,
run out from the end of the sunk span and hold at a depth of a hand for thirty
yards in every direction.

At the near edge, standing clear of the water, is a **votive shelf**, its outer
lip cut with slots. Beyond it the roof of the **inner cell** breaks the surface
like the back of something lying down, its lintel square and whole.

A **bronze ring** is set into the shelf where a hand would fall. Everything here
is green with age. The ring is not.

The reeds stop thirty yards out on every side and do not begin again.

## Referee Overview

**Approach:** The sound goes first. A party coming east along the sunk span
stops hearing its own wading a hundred yards short of the shelf, and the silence
is complete rather than muffled. There is a full watch of open span behind them
at that point, and turning back costs only the watch.

**Terrain:** Dressed slab under a hand of still water, walked at the road rate
of two miles a watch and no slower. The floor is level and it is not slick. Off
the dressed floor it is open mere with no peat under it and no bottom found.

This is the region's landmark and the thing the causeway exists to reach. It is
worth the walk, and it is not a trap. Everything here can be looked at, handled
and left, and a party that takes nothing is not pursued.

The inner cell is a gate, and it has two answers, both priced. The content
behind it is the last bell, which is what the whole region is holding its breath
on (RUMOURS, T-RUM-02).

A fen-wight stands in the shallows north of the shelf and has stood there long
enough for reeds to close around its legs. It lets a party pass inward. It
places itself between them and the span when they turn to leave
(BESTIARY, Fen-wight).

Straight instances funding the reversal here: the bell frame at
(ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-02) and the shelf at
(ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-03) are both exactly what they appear to be
and do exactly what they were built to do. This shrine is the one place in the
region where the Covenant's own construction is turned against its purpose.

## Features

### Votive Shelf

Waist-high, dry on top, cut along its outer lip with a row of slots. The slots
are shallow and narrow and nothing a party is carrying will sit in them
(ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-03). The centre slot is deeper than the rest
and worn bright inside.

The bronze clapper from the causeway frame seats in that centre slot exactly
(KEYS, T-KEY-01).

Cut into the floor slab immediately in front of the shelf, and legible only to
someone standing inside the cell looking out, is the Covenant's own account of
what was owed and what was not returned (LORE, T-LOR-03). A party that never
gets inside never reads it, and a party that gets inside reads it on the way
out.

The span runs back west from the shelf's near corner to the causeway
-> R03-L03.

### The Inner Cell

A single square chamber, lintel above the waterline, doorway below it. The
doorway is closed by a slab that sits in a channel and lifts rather than swings.

Seating the clapper in the centre slot lifts the slab. That is the priced
answer: it costs the party the clapper, which stays in the slot while the slab
is up and drops the slab when it is taken out.

The answer that is not the key is the east wall, which has come down and left a
gap at the floor. It is a longer route, it is under water the whole way, and it
is dark. {TEST: Constitution} {WOUND: Frost}
{CONDITION: Chilled through / halves the stated rate / until a full rest}

Neither route is the mistake. The clapper route leaves the party without the
clapper, which is the only thing in the region that opens anything. The wall
route leaves them inside, wet, with whatever light they were carrying spent.

Inside, hung from a beam that is still sound, is the tenth bell.
{VALUE: 1200 cn} {WT: 30} {QUALITY: Masterwork}

### Bronze Ring

Set flush into the shelf where a hand falls when a person leans on it. Green age
covers every other bronze surface here. This ring is clean, and it is clean
because it has been used, and used recently.

Hauling on it, which no amount of looking will substitute for, brings up a chain
from the mere on the shelf's blind side. On the end of the chain is a second
clapper, older, and cut with a name that decomposes into the shrine root
recorded in the registry.

The ring is the region's proximate clue that somebody is still working here, and
the fresh rope on the causeway frame is the same fact stated a mile away.
