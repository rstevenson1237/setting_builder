## Player Overview

The road lifts out of the water on a spine of fitted stone, wide enough for two
abreast and no wider. Where it bends, a **bell frame** stands against the sky,
two uprights and a crossbeam with nothing hanging from it. Fresh rope is knotted
at the pin.

Ahead the stone dips and the water closes over it. **The sunk span** runs
shin-deep for forty yards before the road comes up again, and the slabs under it
are polished smooth.

West of the bend the reeds have been cut back to open ground and kept cut, so
that **the cut stand** lies bare for a chain in every direction. Peat smoke
crosses it at knee height, flat, going nowhere.

## Referee Overview

**Approach:** A dull flat knocking carries up the road from a quarter of a mile
out, rope-end against a bare crossbeam. It is audible well before the frame is
visible, and a party that turns back at the sound loses nothing but the road.

**Terrain:** Fitted slab above the waterline, two miles a watch. The sunk span
halves that. Off the road it is reed and peat at half a mile a watch, and the
pace does not come back by pushing.

The causeway is the region's spine and it is not itself dangerous. What makes it
expensive is that it is the only ground, and everything on it is visible from
everything else on it for a mile in both directions. A party that wants to do
something unobserved here has to leave the road, and leaving the road is the
cost.

The reedcutters use the cut stand and will be off the causeway by the second
watch. They do not explain, and pressing them produces a shorter answer, not a
longer one (RUMOURS, T-RUM-01).

## Features

### Bell Frame

Two uprights and a crossbeam, the pin seated in its socket and the socket clean
of verdigris in a way nothing else here is. The rope is new: somebody tied it
inside the day and did not stay.

Cut into the underside of the crossbeam, upside down to anyone walking past and
right way up to anyone lying beneath it, is a line about ringing order
(LORE, T-LOR-01).

Reaching up into the pin socket, or climbing the upright to look into it, finds
a bronze clapper wedged inside, a hand and a half long, green everywhere except
where a hand has held it (KEYS, T-KEY-01). {VALUE: 90 cn} {WT: 1}
{QUALITY: Fine}

Sounding the frame with anything at hand is a stated procedure and it carries
much further inward than seaward (PROCEDURES, T-PRC-02).

### The Sunk Span

Forty yards of road under shin-deep water, cambered a finger toward the open fen
so that a slip goes outward rather than onto the stone
(HAZARDS, T-HAZ-03). The crossing is a stated procedure
(PROCEDURES, T-PRC-01).

The span runs east and comes up again on dressed floor that is not the road's,
and that floor is the shrine -> R03-L07.

### The Cut Stand

A chain of cleared ground west of the bend, reeds cut to the root and kept that
way. A cutter's tally post stands at the centre with the current watch count
notched into it, and the notches are fresh.

The stand was cleared so that nothing could stand close to the road unseen
(ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-01). It still works, and standing in it makes
the party the thing that is being seen. Anything already in the shallows at the
stand's edge has been passed before it is noticed
(BESTIARY, Fen-wight).
