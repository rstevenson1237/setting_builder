## Player Overview

The water goes still inside the wall, and the temperature reverses over about twenty yards: warmer than the air outside, not warmer inside.

**The hold wall** stands two courses thick above the waterline and one below it, and the step where the thickness changes runs the whole way round at the surface. Scratched above it, with three marks beneath, is a line about the water.

**The pegged doors** run along the inner face, eleven of them, every one shut and pegged from outside, no forcing anywhere. Every fitting on all eleven is green. Every hook is bright.

**The third door's board** is the one whose count runs past the bottom of its face and continues on the reverse. The plate that named the hold is gone off the door and the peg holes it left are torn rather than cut.

**The fixed things** stand where they were left, in order: bronze fittings still pegged, a bench, a counting frame, a barrel of tallow gone hard, and a case cut for two gauges holding one.

**The pegged passage** runs inward from the landward corner, dry to the knee, with a squared opening at the end of it.

## Referee Overview

**Approach:** The water goes still. Inside the hold wall there is no movement on the surface at all, not from wind and not from wading, and a party notices it at the doorway rather than before. What warns earlier is the temperature: the water on the approach is a shade warmer than the air over it and inside the wall it is not, and that reverses over about twenty yards.

**Terrain:** Dressed floor under a hand of water, level, swept, and not slick, walked at the road rate. The wall stands two courses thick above the waterline and one course thick below it, which is where the builders stopped expecting to be seen (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-10).

*What is here.* Everything two hundred and six people judged not worth carrying, left where it stood, in the order they left it, with the board on the door agreeing to the last item (HOARDS, T-HRD-01), (LORE, T-LOR-06).

*Why it is still here.* Nothing was hidden and nothing is portable without tools. The hold went under whole and the fen keeps what it takes whole, so the doors are sound, the pegs are sound and the wax on the boards is still holding. Two parties have been in. Both read the boards, took the small bronze, and left the rest, because the rest is a bench and a counting frame and a barrel of tallow gone hard.

*The gate and both prices.* Every door here is pegged from the outside, the pegs seated from inside the frame and undrawable, and the board hangs on a hook beside each one. The hook is the head of the peg: taking the count down opens the door (PUZZLES, T-PUZ-02). That costs the board, which cannot be rehung, and a hold with its count on the floor is a hold the Closed Order will hear about. The answer that is not the board is prising, which is a stated procedure, keeps the count on its hook and splits the frame about as often as it does not (PROCEDURES, T-PRC-05).

*Departure.* The pegged passage inward is the only way on from here and it is not the way a party came. Anything taken out of the hold goes up a shaft rather than back through the fen, or it does not go out.

## Features

### The Hold Wall

Two courses thick above the waterline, one below it, running unbroken except at the one doorway. The change in thickness is visible from inside, at the waterline, as a step in the wall face a hand deep, and the same step is in every hold the Covenant built.

Scratched rather than cut into the inner face, above the present water, are a line about the water not having come up and three marks beneath it, the lowest standing clear (LORE, T-LOR-08).

The flooded doorway is the only opening -> R03-L14.

### The Pegged Doors

Eleven of them along the inner face, every one shut, every one pegged from outside, no forcing anywhere, no broken frame, nothing off a hinge (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-06). Each has a hook beside it and a board on the hook, and the wax in every board is still holding its notches.

The fittings on all eleven are green with age. Every hook is bright.

### The Third Door's Board

The one board whose count runs past the bottom of its face and continues on the reverse: two hundred and six out, the fixed things left, door pegged from without, count agreed both sides (LORE, T-LOR-06).

The plate that named the hold and the day is gone off the door itself, and the peg holes it left are torn rather than cut (UNIQUE TREASURES, T-TRE-04). The Closed Order does not know, and it is chalked and stacked four days away in a factor's back weight.

### The Fixed Things

Bronze fittings still pegged, a bench, a counting frame, a barrel of tallow gone hard, and a bell gauge in a case cut for two (HOARDS, T-HRD-01), (TREASURE TABLE III, T-TR3-04).

{VALUE: 600 cn} {WT: 1} {QUALITY: Masterwork}

The case is cut for a pair and holds one. The other is somewhere the Covenant used it, which is anywhere a pin had to be set true, and without a gauge a pin cannot be set true and a socket cut to take a set pin will not give up what is in it (KEYS, T-KEY-05).

### The Pegged Passage

Running inward from the hold's landward corner, dry to the knee, with a squared opening at the end of it pegged over and an order cut round the rim -> R03-L20.
