## Player Overview

Two miles of fitted slab, dry, level, and the easiest walking in the region. There is no frame on it, no post and no niche.

**The road** runs straight inward, eight courses deep, and a shout on it goes out and does not come back.

A note carries up it from a long way ahead, struck, much clearer than it should be. There is nothing to strike a note on for two miles in either direction.

A long way up the road somebody is walking inward at a steady pace and is not gaining.

## Referee Overview

**Approach:** A note, struck, from ahead and a long way off, carrying much further inward than it should. There is nothing to strike a note on for two miles in either direction. It comes from the frames at the bell ground, and it means somebody is at them, and it is heard about a watch before they are.

**Terrain:** Two miles a watch on the road, dry, level, and the easiest walking in the region. Off it, open fen at half a mile a watch, and there is nothing off it worth reaching on this stretch.

*Decision:* keep pace with what is walking the road ahead, which arrives at the frames behind it; or overtake it, which means leaving the road into open fen for half a mile at a quarter of the rate and rejoining ahead of it. Neither is the mistake. It stops when it is watched and it does not object to being followed (BESTIARY, T-BES-03).

*Table effects:* the bell-carrier row is not a chance on this stretch: it is here, walking inward, at a steady pace, and the only question is where the party meets it. Nothing else on the table approaches while it is on the road.

*Nil:* no frames. Two miles of road with no frame, no post and no niche, which is the only such stretch in the region, and it is why the note from ahead carries so clearly.

## Features

### The Road

Fitted slab, eight courses, dry the whole way, running straight inward with the warm stretch beginning at its far end (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-01).

A shout goes out here and does not come back, and a struck note carries further inward than seaward and further than it has any business carrying (HAZARDS, T-HAZ-12).

The carrier walks it inward at a steady pace, sets its bell down when it is watched, and asks which road this one is (RUMOURS, T-RUM-08).
