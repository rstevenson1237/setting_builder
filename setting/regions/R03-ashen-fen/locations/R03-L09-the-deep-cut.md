## Player Overview

Cut reed on the air, green and sharp and entirely unlike peat smoke, from half a mile downwind. There is no cutting gang within nine miles.

**The lane** runs inland from the road toward open water, a chain wide, cut to the root and cut to the standing order's own depth. The first half mile of stubble stands white, the second grey, and the third is closing over.

**The spoil** lies in windrows along both edges rather than piled or burned, which is how it is done when somebody means to come back for it. None of it has been taken.

**The head of the lane** is where the cutting stops, and the last four paces are cut and the fifth is half cut on both sides.

## Referee Overview

**Approach:** Cut reed. The smell of it is green and sharp and entirely unlike peat smoke, and it carries half a mile downwind. There is no cutting gang within nine miles of here and there has not been for a generation, and the smell says otherwise. Turning back costs the watch spent walking to it.

**Terrain:** A cleared lane, a chain wide, cut recently and cut wide, running inland from the road with the spoil still lying on the edges. Firm to the ankle for the first half mile and then not. One mile a watch on it, half a mile a watch off it.

*What has to be read.* How old the cutting is. Reed cut this season stands white at the stubble and greys in a month. The first half mile of this lane is white. The second half mile is grey. The third is closing over. Somebody is cutting inward at about a chain a week and has been for a season, and the lane's head is where they have got to.

*Both routes.* The lane is the fast way to the mere and it is cleared and visible along its whole length from the road. The reed either side is half the rate, unseen, and comes out at the mere edge a quarter mile north where the ground is not cut and gives twice (HAZARDS, T-HAZ-01) {TEST: Constitution}.

*Table effects.* The reedcutter-working-alone row is not a chance on this lane: it is who is cutting it, and they are a chain further inward every week. Reed-hounds work the stubble here in threes because the cutting brings out what lives in the reed (BESTIARY, T-BES-02).

The blade is still in the reed at the half-cut pace. It is a cutter's hook and the haft has been in the fen a season.

## Features

### The Lane

Cut to the root, a chain wide, running inland from the road toward open water. It is cut to the standing order's own width and to the standing order's own depth, and it is cut correctly (LORE, T-LOR-11), (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-07).

The standing order applies to lanes beside roads. There is no road at the far end of this one.

The road runs back to the frames -> R03-L12, and the lane comes out at open water -> R03-L05.

### The Spoil

Cut reed laid in windrows along both edges rather than piled or burned, which is how it is done when somebody means to come back and take it. None of it has been taken.

Under the third windrow from the road, where a party turning the spoil finds it, is a cutter's tally post lying flat, notched, and unsunk. It was cut for the head of this lane and never driven (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-08).

The notches on it run from a number and stop. Counting back from the number gives the same watch the gap opens on every post in the Reach (PUZZLES, T-PUZ-06).

### The Head of the Lane

Where the cutting stops, which is not where the cutter stopped: the last four paces are cut and the fifth is half cut, on both sides, and the blade is still in the reed.

It is a cutter's hook, and the haft has been in the fen a season.
