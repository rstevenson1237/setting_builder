## Player Overview

The cold comes up through the soles standing in the same water the party has been walking in for a mile.

**The stair** turns four times, dressed, wide enough for two abreast, the steps worn deepest along the outside edge. The wear runs all the way to the bottom and does not slow before it. The water deepens by about a hand a turn.

Set into the floor at the head, where a person coming up meets it at eye height, is **the boss**: a figure kneeling with both hands flat on the slab and one ear laid to it, cut at the size of a child. The stone round it is polished in two oval patches at the span of a pair of hands.

**The tide plate** is pegged to the wall at the second turn, bronze, graduated in hands. Every mark below the current line has been scratched through and every mark above it has not.

**Above and below**, the stair joins level floor at one end and dry ground at the other.

## Referee Overview

**Approach:** The cold. Not the water's cold, which is a shade warmer than the air over it everywhere else in the fen. This comes up through the soles from below and it is worse where the stair runs deepest (HAZARDS, T-HAZ-09). It is felt at the stair head, standing in the same water the party has been walking in for a mile.

**Terrain:** Dressed steps turning four times, worn deepest along the outside edge where something wide was carried down (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-11). The water deepens by about a hand a turn: ankle at the head, chest at the last turn. One turn a watch going down and slower coming up.

*What has to be read.* How far down the water goes. It does not go to the bottom: the stair comes out at a squared rim that stands dry, and the water stops two steps short of it. The tell is on the wall, where a tide plate is pegged and graduated in hands, and it reads the same today as it read when it was pegged (TREASURE TABLE III, T-TR3-03). The water has not risen. The stair has gone down.

*Both routes.* The stair is the way to the rim and it costs the wading and the cold {TEST: Constitution} {WOUND: Frost}. The answer that is not the stair is the pegged passage from inside the hold, which is dry the whole way, which costs getting inside the hold first, and which arrives at the rim from the wrong side.

*Table effects.* Nothing on the Encounter table follows a party below the second turn. What is below the second turn is not on the Encounter table.

The plate reads what it read when it was pegged. Kneeling at the boss and laying an ear to the slab is the named action, and what is heard through it is counting.

## Features

### The Stair

Four turns, dressed, wide enough for two abreast, the steps worn deepest along the outside edge where something wide was carried down repeatedly by people going carefully (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-11).

The wear runs all the way to the bottom and does not slow before it. Whatever was carried down was carried the whole way.

### The Boss

Set into the floor at the head of the stair, a carved figure kneeling with both hands flat on the slab and one ear laid to it, cut at the size of a child and set where a person coming up meets it at eye height (LORE, T-LOR-09).

The stone round it is polished in two oval patches at the span of a pair of hands, by other people kneeling down and doing the same thing.

Doing the same thing is the named action. Through the slab, at the depth the stair reaches, something is counting.

### The Tide Plate

Bronze, graduated in hands, pegged to the stair wall at the second turn and meant to be read at the same watch every day (TREASURE TABLE III, T-TR3-03).

{VALUE: 700 cn} {WT: 2} {QUALITY: Fine}

It reads what it read when it was pegged. Every mark below the current line has been scratched through and every mark above it has not, which is what a person does when they are recording that nothing has changed and cannot understand why the stair is wetter.

### Above and Below

The stair comes up onto the tier's level floor -> R03-L13, and onto the cold floor at the second turn -> R03-L18. It ends at a squared rim -> R03-L20.
