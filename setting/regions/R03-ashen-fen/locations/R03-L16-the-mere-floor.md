## Player Overview

Over the floor a wick shows the stone. A pace past **the straight edge** the light does not come back at all.

The edge runs about half a mile and the courses at it are finished rather than broken. The last course is dressed on its outer face, where nobody would ever see it.

The tier was laid to this line, over water that already had no bottom.

## Referee Overview

**Approach:** The light in the water. Over the floor a wick shows the stone; a pace past the edge the light does not come back at all, and the change is at a straight line and is visible from twenty feet in flat light and invisible under wind.

**Terrain:** Not walkable past the edge. On the floor it is the road rate; a pace past the edge there is no bottom and no measurement, and this is the drowned tier's outer boundary.

*Decision:* work along the edge, which is the only route between the swept floor and the cold floor and takes a quarter of a watch; or go back across the tier, which is a watch and passes the shrine. The edge is not dangerous unless something is trailed in the water (BESTIARY, T-BES-09).

*Table effects:* the mere-eel row is not a chance on the edge: it works this line, and a party wading with rope out loses the rope and whatever the rope was attached to. Nothing that needs ground can reach a party here.

*Nil:* no bottom, sounded or otherwise. The Covenant's own soundings for this edge are recorded as unmeasured, in the same hand that recorded the mere, and it is the only measurement they gave up on (HAZARDS, T-HAZ-02).

## Features

### The Straight Edge

Where the dressed floor ends, square, running about half a mile, and the courses at the edge are finished rather than broken (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-04).

The tier was built to this line. Nothing fell away: the floor was laid to end here, over water that already had no bottom, and the last course is dressed on its outer face where nobody would ever see it (LANGUAGE AND ROOT VOCABULARY, T-LNG-13).
