## Overview

Flat water to the horizon, broken by reed stands and by one raised road running
seaward to inward across the middle of it. The road is the only ground that is
reliably ground. Everything either side of it holds a foot for as long as it
takes to decide to trust it.

The reeds have been cut back a chain on both sides of the causeway, and they
have stayed cut. Peat smoke runs flat along the tops of them and does not lift.
There is a bell frame where the road bends, and its crossbeam is bare.

Sound behaves wrongly here. A shout goes out and does not come back, and a
struck note carries further than it should, and further in one direction than
the other.

## Fields

**Extent:** Nine miles seaward to inward along the causeway, and open fen for
about four miles either side of it before the ground firms.

**Rate on the road:** Two miles a watch, walking, with the road above water.

**Rate off the road:** Half a mile a watch through reed and peat, and the pace
cannot be recovered by pushing.

**Time:** Watches, six to the day. The reedcutters count them out loud and will
count for a party that asks.

**Water:** Shin-deep over the sunk spans, waist-deep within a chain of the road,
and unmeasured beyond that.

**Light:** An hour longer at each end of the day than the sky accounts for, the
colour of weak tea.

**Firm ground off the road:** None. State it plainly to a party that asks, and
state that the reedcutters agree.

**What the difficulty measures here:** The treacherousness of the terrain, and
what it costs to leave the road. The road itself is not the danger.

### Weather

| Roll | Weather |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Still and clear. Sound carries a mile and the water gives nothing back. |
| 2 | Flat peat smoke at knee height, and nothing visible past a chain. |
| 3 | Warm rain, straight down, filling the sunk spans a hand deeper. |
| 4 | Wind off the sea, which lifts the smoke and lays the reeds flat. |
