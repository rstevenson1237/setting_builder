## Overview

The road goes under. Nine miles of fitted slab run seaward to inward and
somewhere in the middle of them the water comes over the top, and after that the
road is there in stretches and is not there in stretches, and which is which is
a thing to be read rather than remembered. Over the sunk spans the water stands
at a hand and the slab shows through as a colour. Between them it does not.

Flat water to the horizon in every direction, broken by reed stands and by one
raised line of stone. The reeds stop dead along a cut edge a chain out on both
sides, and past the cut edge they go on as far as the light holds. Peat smoke
runs flat along the tops of them. Bell frames stand at the bends with bare
crossbeams and clean sockets, and the rope on one of them is new.

Inward, the ground under the water goes square. Dressed floor, laid true,
doorways whole with their lintels a hand below the surface, and beyond them
floors running on into water nobody has found the bottom of. Nothing down there
has rotted. Everything the fen took, it took whole, and it is lying in the order
it went down in.

A shout goes out here and does not come back. Put a hand flat under any cut
stone: it is warm, and it is warmer the further inward the stone is laid, and
the top of it is cold. The light holds an hour past what the sky accounts for
and it is the colour of weak tea.

## Fields

**Extent:** Nine miles of causeway seaward to inward, and open fen for
about four miles either side of it before the ground firms. The Drowned Tier
stands under open water across roughly a mile at the inward end, and its edges
have not been walked.

**Rate on the causeway:** Two miles a watch, walking, where the road is above
water. Over the sunk spans it is one mile a watch, because the footing has to be
read before it is trusted.

**Rate off the causeway:** Half a mile a watch through reed and peat, and the
pace cannot be recovered by pushing.

**Time:** Watches, six to the day. The reedcutters count them out loud and will
count for a party that asks, and the count from Reedhold arrives here already
two roads out of true.

**Water:** Shin-deep over the sunk spans, waist-deep within a chain of the road,
and unmeasured beyond that. Over the Drowned Tier it is a hand deep across
dressed floor and then it is not measured at all.

**Light:** An hour longer at each end of the day than the sky accounts for, the
colour of weak tea.

**What the difficulty measures here:** The treacherousness of the terrain and
what it costs to leave the road. The fen is harder ground than the Long Cut
because the road here is not continuous, and the gaps are where the region does
its work.

**Sound:** A shout goes out and does not come back. A struck note carries
further inward than it carries seaward, and further than it has any business
carrying.

**Firm ground off the road:** None. State it plainly to a party that asks, and
state that the reedcutters agree.

## Connections

The Causeway is the spine and every location in the Ashen Fen hangs off
it. The container holds the road itself, the frames along it and the cut ground
either side; the Drowned Tier holds what stands under open water at the inward
end, square where it has no business being square
(ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-04).

A party crosses from one container to the other by going into the water. There
is no dry route between them, the sunk spans are the only ground that holds, and
what is walkable at one hour is not at another. That is the whole decision the
region asks, and it is asked once, at the point where the causeway stops being a
road.

The count the Covenant kept runs out here. The last row on the tally names the
Bell Causeway and the column beside it was never filled (LORE, T-LOR-12).
Inward of the Drowned Tier the fen opens onto Bellgate, and downward it opens
onto the Deep Sill, and neither of those is reached without crossing the
tier.
