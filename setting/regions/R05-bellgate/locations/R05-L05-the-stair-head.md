## Referee Overview

**Reactions:** *Standing at the head:* the air moves downward past the party all day and upward at dusk, and it carries wet rope and cold bronze. *Dropping something down:* it is heard hitting water at the fourth turn and not before. *Lighting a wick here:* it draws sideways into the stair and gutters, and it does not go out. *Going down without a light:* the niches below the last turn are spaced exactly enough to pace by and the count comes out right, and this is true.

**Gate:** The stair is a gate on light and on time. It costs a party turns going down and more coming up, and every wick they are carrying is burning against a count that starts at the rim. The answer that is not the stair is the flooded cut east, which is chest deep, dark, and arrives in the shafts rather than at the stair foot (R05-L06).

*Job for the region.* This is the throat. Everything that goes below the gate ground goes through here or through the flooded cut, and this is the one a party can carry a load down.

*Cost in turns.* One turn to the first standing water. Three to the foot at a walk, and the last two are in the water. Four coming back up carrying anything.

*What is true that the Overview withholds.* The wear on the steps is on the outside edge, which is where a person walks when they are carrying something wide between two of them and cannot see their feet. It runs the whole way down and it does not slow at the water: whatever was carried was carried into standing water, repeatedly, by people who knew the water was there.

## Features

### The Steps

Turning four times, dressed, wide enough for two abreast, worn deepest along the outside edge (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-11). Sixty feet a turn on dry slab, half that once the water is over the boot.

The inside edge is unworn. Nobody ever came up these stairs empty-handed and hugged the newel, which is what a person does when they are tired.

### The Boss at the Head

Set into the floor where a person coming up meets it at eye height: a figure kneeling with both hands flat on the slab and one ear laid to it, cut at the size of a child (LORE, T-LOR-09).

The stone round it is polished in two oval patches at the span of a pair of hands, by other people kneeling down and doing the same thing. Doing it is the named action and what is heard through the slab is counting, a long way down and not directly below.

### The Draw

Air goes down all day and up at dusk, and it reverses inside about a turn. The reversal is what carries the shafts' smell onto the gate ground, and it is the only clock below the rim that is not a party's own count.

A wick held at the head shows the direction before a hand does.

### The Water at the Foot

The last two turns are wet, ankle then knee then thigh, and the slabs under it are polished slick and cambered a finger outward, so a slip goes off the stair rather than down it (HAZARDS, T-HAZ-03) {TEST: Constitution} {CONDITION: Footsore / halves the stated rate / until a full rest}.

The gate face stands above -> R05-L02, and the stair goes down to silted floor -> R05-L11.
