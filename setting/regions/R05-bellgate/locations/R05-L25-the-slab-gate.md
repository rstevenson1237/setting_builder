## Referee Overview

**Reactions:** *Turning the slab with a bar:* it lifts a quarter of its height and stops, and the stop is deliberate. *Levering it:* it comes out whole and cannot be put back. *Seating something in the socket:* it lifts fully and holds while the thing is in. *Taking that thing out:* it drops, at once, and it drops whether or not anybody is under it. *Standing in the channel:* the party is standing in the thing that carries the water, and the channel is dry and has been for a long time.

**Gate:** The socket at hand height beside the slab, cut to take a clapper, and the slab lifts rather than swings (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-09), (PUZZLES, T-PUZ-03), (KEYS, T-KEY-03). The clapper is two rooms inward in a cell that will not give it up until a pin is set true. The answer that is not the socket is the flooded cut, which arrives in the tier from the shafts, wet, three turns later, and past a room a party cannot see into first.

*Job for the region.* It is the region's second gate and the one a party meets after they have already learned the construction. Bellgate's own socket is the same cut, and a party who has stood at the face has seen this mechanism from the outside without knowing what it was.

*Cost in turns.* One to cross when it is up. Three to work it with a bar and get the quarter-turn stop. Two to lever it out and destroy it.

*What is true that the Overview withholds.* This one has its counterweight. A stone the weight of a clapper is seated in the socket's back, behind a plate, and it is what makes the quarter-turn stop happen rather than the slab simply falling. The plate can be lifted and the counterweight seen. That is the distributed clue for Bellgate's socket, which has no plate and no counterweight, and which is why the face is down.

## Features

### The Channel

Cut down the middle of the floor, a foot wide and a foot deep, level, dry, running from the tier's open floor to the slab and stopping at it (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-09).

It was cut to take water away and there is no water in it. Everything above this level stands in water and this channel is dry, which means the water is not getting here, which means something above is taking it before it arrives.

### The Slab

Seated in a groove cut to take it, flush, with a slot across its face for a bar. It lifts rather than swings and it weighs what a road course weighs.

Levered rather than turned, it comes out whole and cannot be put back, and the way between the shafts and the tier is then permanently open, which is a thing a party may want and cannot undo.

### The Socket

Beside it at hand height, cut to a clapper: a hand and a half long, tapered, with a shoulder two thirds down. Empty.

Behind it, under a bronze plate that lifts off, is the counterweight: a stone cut to the same weight as the clapper it expects. Bellgate's socket has the same plate sockets and no plate and nothing behind it.

A drawing plate lies in the channel where the water would run if there were any, pulling nothing (TREASURE TABLE IV, T-TR4-03).
