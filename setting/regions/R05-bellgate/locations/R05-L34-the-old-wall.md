## Referee Overview

**Reactions:** *Following it:* the wall runs eighty feet and thins from two courses to one at shoulder height. *Striking the thin part:* it gives the note of a thing with nothing behind it, and there is a room behind it.

**Gate:** The wall itself. One course thick above shoulder height, and it can be gone through with a bar in three turns, loudly, in a level where something is listening to numbers. The answer that is not the wall is the pegged door two rooms round, which is quiet and costs the count on its board.

*Cost:* one turn to cross. Three to go through the wall.

*What is true:* the thinning is where the builders stopped expecting to be seen, which is the same construction as every hold wall on the Reach at the waterline, applied here four hundred feet down where there is no waterline (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-10).

*Nil:* no fittings and no soot. An eighty-foot wall with nothing on it and nothing ever hung from it.

## Features

### The Thinning Wall

Two courses to shoulder height, one course above it, running eighty feet without a break, and the change is visible as a step in the face (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-10), (LANGUAGE AND ROOT VOCABULARY, T-LNG-49).

Scratched rather than cut into the thin part, above the step: a line about the water not having come up, with three marks beneath it (LORE, T-LOR-08).

There is no water at this depth and there never has been, and the marks are set at the same heights as the ones in the holds.
