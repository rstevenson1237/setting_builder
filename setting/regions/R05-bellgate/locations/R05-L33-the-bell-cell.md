## Referee Overview

**Reactions:** *Looking up:* there is a pin socket cut into the ceiling and nothing hanging from the pin. *Reaching into the socket:* there is a clapper in it and it will not come out. *Setting the pin true with a gauge:* the socket releases and the clapper comes down into a hand. *Breaking the pin:* the clapper comes down, the socket is ruined, and it will not take a set pin afterwards. *Striking the clapper while it is seated:* the note goes inward, and the ranks two rooms inward turn to face this wall.

**Gate:** The pin. Everything in this cell is available to a party who has a bell gauge and nothing is available to a party who has not, except by breaking it (TREASURE TABLE III, T-TR3-04), (KEYS, T-KEY-05). The gauge is one of a pair and the case for it is in a hold four miles seaward and under water. The answer that is not the gauge is breaking the pin, which works, which cannot be undone, and which leaves the region's only spare clapper in a socket that will no longer hold one (KEYS, T-KEY-03).

*Job for the region.* This is the key room. The clapper here is what opens the slab gate, and the gauge that opens this room is in another region, which is the setting's longest key chain and is deliberate.

*Cost in turns.* One to cross. Two to find the socket, which is in the ceiling and is not in a party's eyeline. Four to set the pin with a gauge. One to break it.

*What is true that the Overview withholds.* Green age covers every bronze surface in this cell, including the pin. The clapper in the socket is clean. It was put in after the cell was closed, by somebody who came back, and the score marks running out of this room and across the floor slab run one way only.

## Features

### The Pin Socket

Cut into the ceiling at the room's centre, bronze-lined, with the pin seated in it and nothing hanging from the pin (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-02).

It is not in a party's eyeline. It is found by looking up, which is the named action, and nothing in the room points at it except that the floor beneath it is worn in a circle at the span of a person's feet.

### The Clapper

Seated in the socket beside the pin, longer than a forearm, cut along its length with the name of the road it belonged to (TREASURE TABLE V, T-TR5-05).

{VALUE: 8000 cn} {WT: 4} {QUALITY: Artifact}

Everything else in this cell is green. It is not. It came down here after the water and after the closing, and it is the only clean bronze below the rim.

### The Pin

Bronze, seated, out of true by about the width of a knife, and the socket will not release while it is out of true. Setting it takes a gauge, and the gauge is one of a pair (TREASURE TABLE III, T-TR3-04).

Cut backwards into the inside of the socket, readable only in still water lying in the worn circle after the floor is wetted, is a line about what answers when a bell is rung (LORE, T-LOR-10).

### The Score Marks

Parallel, in the floor slab, running out of the cell through the west doorway and across the tier. They run one way: out. Nothing has been dragged in here.

The tier's open floor is north -> R05-L24. The flooded cells are inward -> R05-L27. The score marks lead west -> R05-L35.
