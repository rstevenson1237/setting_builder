## Player Overview

**The mouth** is cut square into the slab at the ground's east side, six feet across, with no rim, no lip and no warning cut anywhere on it. The water in it goes from ankle to chest inside twenty feet.

**The dressed wall** runs both sides the whole way, joint to joint, with a course line running level at chest height that a hand can follow in the dark. At three points it is interrupted by an empty socket at hand height.

**The flat water** is dead still and gives nothing back. A shout drops at a chain and the cut is longer than a chain.

**Either end** is daylight or the shafts, and neither is visible from the middle.

## Referee Overview

**Reactions:** *Entering:* the water goes from ankle to chest in twenty feet and the party's own wading stops coming back to them. *Lighting a wick:* it shows twenty feet of cut and no further, because the water surface is dead flat and gives nothing. *Putting it out:* the cut is navigable by hand along the wall, and the wall is dressed the whole way. *Trailing a rope:* what is in the water takes what is trailed rather than what is standing, and whatever the rope was attached to comes with it (BESTIARY, T-BES-09).

**Gate:** This is the answer that is not the socket, and it is the region's second route below the slab. It costs the cold, the light, and everything a party is carrying above chest height {TEST: Constitution} {WOUND: Frost}. It arrives in the Bell Shafts rather than behind the gate, which means a party who takes it has bypassed the face and has not opened it, and will have to come back up the stair or go on down.

*Job for the region.* It is the redundancy. Bellgate is a gate with a key nobody has, and this cut is the reason the region is playable without one.

*Cost in turns.* Three turns from the gate ground to the water stair, wading, and it cannot be hurried. Five coming back against the flow.

*What is true that the Overview withholds.* It is not a flooded passage. It is a channel, cut, with a fall, and it was cut to take water away from the gate ground and out under the slab. It still works. The reason the gate ground stands in ankle-deep water rather than knee-deep is that this cut is draining it, and a party wading up it is wading up a drain that is doing its job.

It is not a flooded passage. It is a channel with a fall, cut to take water off the gate ground and out under the slab, and it still works. The reason the ground above stands in ankle-deep water rather than knee-deep is that a party wading up this cut is wading up a drain that is doing its job.

## Features

### The Mouth

Cut square into the slab at the gate ground's east side, six feet across, with the water in it going from ankle to chest inside twenty feet as the fall takes hold (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-09).

There is no rim, no lip and no warning cut anywhere on it. The Covenant did not expect anybody to be standing in it.

### The Dressed Wall

Both sides dressed the whole way, joint to joint, with a course line running level at chest height that a hand can follow in the dark. It is the only navigable feature in the cut and it does not stop.

At three points the course line is interrupted by a socket cut at hand height, and the sockets are empty. They took the boards that regulated the flow.

### The Flat Water

Dead still, giving nothing back, and a party strung out along the cut cannot call each other (HAZARDS, T-HAZ-12) {TEST: Sanity}. A shout drops at a chain and this cut is longer than a chain.

A drawing plate lies on the bottom halfway along, which pulls standing water off a floor into itself a hand at a time and must be emptied into running water (TREASURE TABLE IV, T-TR4-03).

{VALUE: 2500 cn} {WT: 3} {QUALITY: Masterwork}

Emptied anywhere else it gives all of it back at once. Somebody carried it in here to empty it and did not get out.

### Either End

The gate ground is up and back -> R05-L01, the standing water that will not drain is off the north side -> R05-L04, and the cut comes out at a stair running with water -> R05-L21.
