## Referee Overview

**Reactions:** *Standing at the rim:* nothing, and the air comes up rather than down. *Lowering a light:* it is visible to the bottom, which is true of no other shaft here, and something at the bottom stops moving. *Putting the light out:* whatever stopped moving starts again, and it is closer when the light comes back. *Calling down it:* the shaft carries a voice both ways and the answer is the party's own, later than it should be. *Taking the pack:* nothing at all, then or later.

**Gate:** Not a door. The shaft is a two hundred foot drop on rope with no pegs in the wall, and going down it is a stated procedure (PROCEDURES, T-PRC-07). The answer that is not the rope is the angled shaft off the west side, which is dressed, pegged, twice as long, and comes out at the same floor having cost the party a watch and their reading of what is at the bottom.

*Approaches.* From the stair foot across silted floor, which shows the rim first and the pack not at all. From the angled shaft, which comes up beside the pack and shows it before the rim. From the warden's rim to the east, which shows the whole shaft mouth in section and is the only angle from which the wear on all four sides is visible at once.

*Why it is still here.* Because somebody came up it, set a pack down on the rim, and did not pick it up again (HOARDS, T-HRD-06). Soot has settled on it evenly since, which takes years, and nothing has touched it. Everyone who has been this deep has read that as a warning and left it, and the reading is correct and is not the whole of it.

{VALUE: 7000 cn} {WT: 5} {QUALITY: Masterwork}

*The extraction.* Instruments, a lamp, and one slate leaf out of a set of nine (MAGICAL TOMES, T-TOM-06). The leaf is the extraction and the rest is weight: nine leaves read the depth of the road under a reader through the palm, and one leaf reads a ninth of it, which is a number nobody has been able to make sense of until they have the rest of the set.

*Departure.* Anything brought up this shaft comes up on the rope the party brought, and the rope is what they will want at the tier. A party that leaves the rope hanging here can find this shaft from below, which is the only thing in the Bell Shafts that can be found from below.

## Features

### The Rim

Cut square, twelve feet across, worn on all four sides to the same depth, which no other rim in the region is. Bells went down here, and they went down slung and turned, and the turning is what wore the fourth side (ARCHITECTURE AND TERRAIN, T-ARC-05).

Cut into the rim on the inward face, in letters a hand deep and cut without haste, is the road this shaft served and the count of bells that went down it. The count is eleven. Nine are hanging in the fen and one is behind a gate, and the eleventh is not accounted for anywhere.

The silted floor at the stair foot lies west -> R05-L12.

### The Pack

Standing upright on the rim where it was set down, canvas over a frame, dry, the straps still buckled. Soot lies on it as evenly as it lies on the stone, which is what says how long it has been there.

Inside: a sounding line knotted by fathom with the lead cut hollow (TREASURE TABLE III, T-TR3-01), a pin-lamp whose plate says it burns brighter with depth and which dims to nothing at the sill (TREASURE TABLE IV, T-TR4-01), and one slate leaf, laid flat between two boards.

{VALUE: 500 cn} {WT: 1} {QUALITY: Fine}

Nothing in it is spoiled and nothing in it is disturbed. Whoever set it down was going back for it.

### The Lowering Gear

Pegged to the rim on the east side: two ring bolts, a bronze sheave still turning, and the stub of a rope cut clean rather than parted (TREASURE TABLE V, T-TR5-01). The gear is sound and would take a bell today.

{VALUE: 12000 cn} {WT: 30} {QUALITY: Artifact}

The cut end of the rope is on this side of the sheave, which means the cut was made from up here, by somebody standing where a party is standing, while something was on the other end.

### The Slate Leaf

One of nine, meant to be laid flat on slab and read through the palm rather than the eye (MAGICAL TOMES, T-TOM-06). Read alone it gives a ninth of the depth of the road under the reader and takes a watch to do it.

{VALUE: 4500 cn} {WT: 4} {QUALITY: Cursed}

The reader is a hand lower at the end of it than they were at the start, and the leaf does not say so, and the ground does not either.

### The Shaft Itself

Two hundred feet, plumb, dressed the whole way, no pegs in the wall. A lowered light reaches the bottom, which nothing else here does, and what is at the bottom is a flat pale shape that fits a gap rather than a body (BESTIARY, T-BES-05).

{AD: 3, +1} {TYPE: Horror} {WOUND: Poison}

It comes up at the pace of a walking man and it stops the moment a light goes out. The angled shaft runs west off the rim -> R05-L17, and the warden's rim lies east -> R05-L19.
