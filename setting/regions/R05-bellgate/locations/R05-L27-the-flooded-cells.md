## Player Overview

The water deepens, and there are shapes standing in it that do not move when the light does.

**The ranks** stand in their order, facing the way they were facing, the water at their chests. Forty-one of them. Nothing has rotted and every face is a face.

**Ashenpin** is in the front rank, apart, in the deepest of the cells, holding the loop something hung from. It names everyone present, correctly, before anybody has spoken.

**The clapper** is in its hands, longer than a forearm, cut along its length with a road name, and it is warm to the hand at this depth.

**The folded page** lies flat on the floor slab under a hand of water, waxed and unspoiled, listing names in order.

**The beat** is the interval between the two words a rank is turned on, and whatever the room does it does together and on it.

## Referee Overview

**Reactions:** *Entering:* nothing. They are facing the way they were facing. *Speaking:* nothing, unless a name is spoken. *Speaking a name off the folded page:* the one it belongs to turns and faces the speaker, and stays facing them. *Taking anything:* the whole room turns together, on the same beat, the way a rank turns. *Putting the light out:* nothing changes and nothing is heard, and the light comes back on a room that has not moved. *Telling them the ringing is finished:* the officer asks who says so, and the answer has to be somebody with the standing to say it.

**Gate:** There is no door here and no lock. The gate is the standing itself: everything in this room is available to a party who can answer one question and nothing in it is available to a party who cannot (NAMED CREATURES, T-CRE-06). The answer that is not the standing is taking, which works, and which turns the room. Both are real and the text does not say which is the mistake.

*Approaches.* From the flooded cut, which comes in at floor level and shows the ranks before anything else, at eye height, at ten feet. From the bell cell, which comes in behind them and shows the folded page on the floor first. From the sill door inward, which comes in above the waterline along the wall and shows the officer alone, front rank, apart from the rest.

*Why it is still here.* Because they are still here. Two parties have reached this room. Both of them stood in the doorway, read what was standing in the water, and went back up, and both of them were correct to.

*What is here.* A flooded cell of the last chapter in their order, each still carrying what they carried down (HOARDS, T-HRD-04).

{VALUE: 15000 cn} {WT: 25} {QUALITY: Artifact}

They are not guarding it. They have been waiting a long time and what they are waiting for is a sentence.

*Departure.* A party who was given what is here walks out past a room that has turned to watch them go and does not follow. A party who took it walks out with the room turning behind them at walking pace, in their order, and the unrung have no answer that is a fight (BESTIARY, T-BES-12).

*Funding for the reversal.* There is none, because there is no reversal. This room is exactly what it appears to be, and it is the one place in Bellgate where that is the hardest thing about it.

The page names forty-two. Read as a roll, each name and then a pause the length of a breath, the one it belongs to turns and faces the reader on their own name.

### The Clapper

### The Beat

Whatever the room does, it does together and on one beat, and the beat is not the party's. It is the interval between the two words a rank is turned on, and it is the same interval as the pause between names on the page.
