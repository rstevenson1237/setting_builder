Lore of The Ashwrack Coast

The Niche Inscription (*inscription*) - found at C.6 Second Niche Row
A crude tally scratched into the stone from the inside, not carved by whoever sealed the niche - the count runs to ninety-one days before breaking off mid-mark. Beneath it, barely legible: a single name, and the coast's word for "unpaid." It confirms the tally-custom is old enough to predate even the fishing holds' founding, and that at least one of the barrow's dead was sealed away still owing, never reckoned. A party who carries the name to Saltmere's Tally-Keeper (A.3) may find it still listed, generations later, as a debt no descendant has ever paid off.

The Vault Ledger (*ledger fragment*) - found at C.22 The Rusted Vault
A single brittle page, its ink long since bled into the fiber, but a few lines still hold: a record of iron pledged against a debt too large for one hold to cover alone, cosigned by three family names. The vault held no treasure - it held pledged iron itself, kept in trust until the debt was settled or the sea called it in first. Whether it ever was settled isn't recorded here. One of the three cosigning names is uncomfortably close to a hold-family still active on the coast today.

The Tribute Record (*vellum fragment*) - found at C.10 The Sealed Niche of Tribute
A few words survive, in a hand generations older than anything else legible in the barrow: a record of the bell being carried here deliberately and sealed away as tribute to the Drowned Court - not hidden from thieves, but hidden from the very hold that once rang it, so it could never be rung again and draw the Court's attention back to the coast. It directly contradicts Saltmere Hold's current belief that the bell was stolen, and confirms the Cairn's tribute predates that theft rumor by generations.

The Elders' Letter (*letter*) - found at A.1 The Bell Hall
A single folded letter, sealed with a hold-elder's signet, admits what the council has never said publicly: years ago, someone found proof the diving-bell wasn't stolen at all, and the council chose to let blame fall on the Tallow Fleet rather than explain why it was truly gone. The letter never says who found the proof or where, only that revealing it would "wake more debts than it settles." Whoever wrote this knew - or suspected - something closer to the truth than they wanted known.

The Cosigned Pledge (*ledger entry*) - found at A.3 The Tally-Keeper's Shed
A single ledger page, older and more brittle than the others on the shelf, records an iron pledge cosigned generations back by three names - one of which matches a hold-family still carrying tallies today. The debt was never marked paid, and the ledger doesn't say what became of the pledge itself. A reader who's also seen the fragment recovered from the Hollow Cairn's rusted vault (The Vault Ledger, above) would recognize the same hand and the same debt, split across two records never meant to be read together.

The Boundary Symbol (*inscription*) - found at B.9 The Old Boundary Stones
A rubbing of the worn carving shows a spiral coiling around a closed eye, a symbol matching nothing in Vael's own carvings found elsewhere on the coast - if anything, it looks older, as though Vael's road-builders inherited this boundary rather than raised it. Nobody currently on the coast recognizes it, though a patient search of the Hollow Cairn's own carvings might turn up something similar.
