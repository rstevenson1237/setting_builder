Lore of Brackvaen

The Settlement of the Salt Assize (*oak boards in a lead case*) - found at A.2 Rendfast
Eleven boards of planed oak, cut both faces, held in a lead case on the top shelf of the Assize hall's back room; the only complete copy, and the source every other version in Torsgaard is an abridgement of. Boards one to nine are exactly what the holding believes them to be: a division of the eastern pans between eleven parties, pan by pan, with the boundaries fixed against eleven cut stones. Board ten records the cost of cutting those stones, and it is where an attentive reader stops - the stones were not cut for the settlement. They were *copied*, and the copying is itemised: eleven men paid for eleven days, "at the under-work, from the stones as they stand". Board eleven is a single clause in a different hand, and it grants the parties to the settlement the ground the stones stand on "and so far beneath as the same stones run". Nobody in Torsgaard has ever thought to ask how far beneath a boundary stone runs. Gellen Torsling has read board eleven and has worked out that the Assize's own document gives it Siwapelu, and that the same clause gives Siwapelu whatever the stones were holding - which is the reason he has never tabled it, and the reason the Assize's position on the stair is that it has no position yet. Connects the Salt Assize of sixty years ago to the eleven pillars at C.7 Nuthorath, and explains why boundary stones cut by illiterate men are obeyed by things that are not people.

Vethgaard Tally, Fourth Board (*cut tally board*) - found at A.9 Vethgaard, cited at A.1 Thrapstad
A single board of split oak from the fourth tier of Vetha Naugling's rack, the tier holding debts taken up from the dead rather than issued to the living. It is longer than a board needs to be and it is cut on both faces and along one edge. The face records forty-one weights and the names of the goods they attach to - a boat, a pan-crew place, a set of rakes, a coat, boots. The reverse records what happened to each: settled, forfeit, still standing. Along the edge, in a much smaller hand, is a second count that does not match either face: eleven entries, all within the last three years, all against goods that came up out of Siwapelu rather than off the pans. She has been keeping a private tally of the stair's traffic since it opened, and she is the only person in Brackvaen who has the numbers. The board's own value is that it is honest bookkeeping by somebody with every reason to falsify it, which is a fact about her the party will need before they decide what to do with what they learn at her door. Plants the hook that the debts she holds have all been taken up rather than lent, which is the arithmetic that undoes her.

Channel Reckoning of Nine Reaches (*knotted reed cord*) - found at B.3 Ghutrrun
Nine lengths of knotted reed cord on a ring at Zhakur-of-Nine's belt, one for each channel she holds - the Vingash record, and a genuine navigational instrument rather than a symbol. Each cord carries the depth of its reach at intervals of about forty yards, in a knotting that is teachable in an afternoon and useless without being told the convention. It is not a map; it is a set of soundings, and it is kept current, which is the point: the fen moves and the cords are retied. Two of the nine are not current. They record a reach west of the causeway that has not been sounded in eighteen years, and they are kept on the ring anyway. Their last knots record the water getting deeper on each of the final three soundings, and then stopping. Zhakur-of-Nine will teach the convention to a party she has decided about, and once they have it the cord on any staked channel in Lindmarn becomes readable - which is the single largest practical gift available in the setting. Connects the Gell Year to the flooded pans at B.6 Kkithik, and gives the fen's central hazard a solution the party earns rather than rolls for.

The Mezeora Course (*inscription cut in nine ruled bands*) - found at C.6 Mezeora
Nine ruled bands of Old Rede running round all four walls of the room, and the longest continuous text in Brackvaen. It is a maintenance record. Band by band it records the condition of the under-work's drainage, inspection by inspection - which screws were turning, which housings were sound, what the level stood at in the lowest course, how much salt was laid at which threshold and by whom. It is dry, technical, and entirely without ceremony, and it is written by people who plainly expected to be read by their successors rather than by anyone else. What it is wrong about is the future: every band assumes the next one will be cut. Read in order, the bands show the interval between inspections lengthening from monthly to yearly to whatever was convenient, the salt at the thresholds being renewed later and later, and the phrase for the lowest course changing from a measurement to a formula. The ninth band is ruled and empty. Connects the pumps at C.22 Nekire, the salt at C.23 Zorethora and the stones at C.7 Nuthorath into one system, and reveals that Siwapelu was never a mine - it was a maintained containment, and it was maintained by bored professionals for a very long time before it stopped.

Hand of the Last Warden (*inscription, deteriorating*) - found at C.23 Zorethora
Ninety feet of Old Rede cut over every surface of the approach passage, and it says one thing, repeated, for its whole length. Rendered plainly: *the salt is to be renewed, the water is to be kept over it, and the gate is not to be marked.* That is all. It is repeated some four thousand times. The interest is entirely in the hand: at the passage head it is the same careful unhurried cutting as the pillars at Nuthorath, and by the foot it is gouged. One person cut all of it, working downward, over a period the deterioration suggests was not short, and they were doing it alone - there is no second hand anywhere in the ninety feet. The last six feet are cut over an earlier line of the same text, because they had run out of unmarked surface and would not stop. Whoever this was did not leave with the others, and the instruction they were repeating was addressed to successors who never came. Connects the blank cap stones at C.7, C.23 and C.24 - *the gate is not to be marked* is why three thresholds in Siwapelu carry no character, and it means the absence is deliberate rather than unfinished. It also states, without ever explaining it, that keeping the water over the salt is the whole of the arrangement, which is the fact a party needs before they touch the pumps at C.22.
