Lore of Fellward

The Ring's True Ledger (*ledger*) - found at A.2 The Quartermaster's Stores
Weyland Cross's own hand, not the Ring's official one - a private tally of exactly what's been skimmed before the posted count, kept because a man who cheats still needs to know the true number even while showing everyone else a false one. It admits what the Ring's real state is against A.1's chalk tally coming up a fifth short: no accident, no clerical error, just grain routed around the books. Weyland notices its absence within the day, and the Ring hears of the theft before he does - whoever holds it holds enough to move him, or to move against the Ring directly.

The Burial Register (*ledger*) - found at A.6 The Old Graveyard
Recorded in a hand that isn't Ness's - whoever kept this register before him logged every grave and its facing for generations back, until the earliest pages simply stop. What survives already treats the facing-custom as old when the recording began: not a keep rule, something older that Fellward's own dead were made to follow regardless. Ness believes either his predecessor took the missing pages, or that something beyond the treeline wanted them gone, and won't say which he actually believes on a given day.

The Boundary Line (*carved inscription*) - found at B.2 The Boundary Stones
Cut by the same hands that squared the foundations under Ingaraz, not anything raised in the keep's own three centuries. Essir names only what a boundary-marker is; it says nothing about why crossing this exact line cuts every scent traveling with it, hound or human, cold and total. Frost holds longest in these carved letters of anywhere near Fellward, as though whatever keeps winter's line here is remembering something older than any season.

The Threshold Warning (*carved inscription*) - found at C.2 The Warded Gatehouse
Cut beside the Sentinel-Niche in the builders' own hand: whoever carries the matching seal passes unchallenged, no further test asked. Whoever wrote it took for granted the seal would never leave the barrow field at all - not that a keep commander would carry it into open air and die with it twelve years and a full region away, leaving the promise unreadable to the one seal it was written for.
