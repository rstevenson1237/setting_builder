Lore of Carrdun

The Grain-Tower Tally (*form - stone tablet*) - found at A.2 The Granary Tower
A steward's tally of grain set aside "for Werkamu's household, that the count may not end
at the count" - Nemhet's granary was never a common store but a funerary offering-vault,
feeding the dead's journey rather than the living. The steward who cut it recorded weights
and dates with obsessive care, certain the offerings' endless freshness was a mark of
favour; he says nothing about breeding or hatching, because no one in his day was foolish
enough to raise anything living inside a house built for the dead. Ties the Truths of
Carrdun's stone-preservation rule to its origin, and answers, without meaning to, why
Nesta's pens keep coming up wrong.

The Senametu Marker (*form - carved kerbstone*) - found at B.1 The Causeway
Cut in old Nemhet legal formula, the marker names the crossing and exempts temple servants
from toll "passing to the Sun-Court, four thousand paces east" - a destination that appears
on no map Carrdun's diggers have ever made. Whoever cut it assumed the temple's authority,
and the Sun-Court itself, would still be standing to enforce the exemption; both drowned
with everything else. It is the oldest confirmed reference to a Nemhet holding beyond
Baldun's known extent - if the Sun-Court exists at all, it lies somewhere further out than
anyone has dug.

The Weir Tally (*form - carved tally post*) - found at C.6 The Elder's Platform
Notches and glyphs cut over uncounted generations record marsh-kin births, floods, and
deaths - and one night, marked heavier than any other, when "the water sang before it
rose" and swallowed the stone city downstream in a single count of the moon. The keepers
who cut it blamed the river's own anger, never suspecting - or never saying - that
anything under the water might have wanted it to happen. It complicates Carrdun's own vague
dating of Nemhet's fall, and it is the reason the marsh-kin still watch the water before
trusting a night's calm.

The Second District Panels (*form - carved wall panels*) - found at C.10 The Second District
Carved panels name eleven officials of the temple household and state they departed
"toward the sunset hills, three days ahead of the water," carrying the temple's own
regalia with them - contradicting the assumption, encouraged by the epitaph in the
Priest-King's own rest, that the whole priest-line drowned. Whoever carved this was proud
of the departure and expected it to be read as a founding record, not a footnote; they
were wrong only in believing anyone from Carrdun would ever read it before the marsh-kin
let them. It is the clearest evidence in the setting that Nemhet's bloodline, or at least
its wealth, survived the flood and went somewhere - a somewhere nobody at Carrdun has ever
looked for.

The Failed Warding Note (*form - scrap of hide*) - found at D.7 The Ward-Threshold
Scratched in charcoal and wedged under a flagstone where the next person through would
find it: "Don't touch the letters. Tarrick did and it took him standing up. Go around if
you can, seal it if you can't, and for the love of anything don't say what's written on it
out loud." Whoever wrote it survived long enough to leave the note and never came back to
add anything else. It confirms the Ward-Threshold has already claimed at least one Concern
digger, by name, and it is the only source anywhere in Baldun for the word "Tarrick."

The Last Tally Page (*form - torn manifest page*) - found at D.17 The Sealed Gallery
A torn page from the work-gang's own day-tally, the handwriting increasingly cramped
toward the bottom: rations counted, hours logged, then - "the sound is coming from the
floor now, not the walls. K. says it's singing. it is not singing." The tally stops
mid-sentence. Whoever wrote it was recording an ordinary shift until the last two lines,
and never considered that stopping to listen was the mistake. It confirms half of
Carrdun's rumour mill at once - the crew heard something, and "singing" was the wrong word
for it.

The Priest-King's Epitaph (*form - carved plaque*) - found at D.18 The Priest-King's Rest
Carved plainly beneath the river-god mural: "Werkamu, last to hold the temple's word,
sealed himself here rather than be carried by the water as his city was. What could not be
saved was sealed; what could be saved was sent ahead." Cut, evidently, at his own order in
his final hours - a proud, deliberate account rather than a survivor's panic. He was wrong
only in assuming "sealed" meant safe: the Ward-Threshold he raised to protect his rest is
the same warding a careless digger nearly died defacing three years ago. Read against the
Second District's own panels, it confirms the temple household split in two - one part
sealed here with their king, the other sent ahead toward the sunset hills.

The Temple Roster (*form - work-roster scroll*) - found at D.4 The Scribes' Hall
A roster of temple staff by name and duty - scribes, granary-keepers, a granary-keeper's
household of four, guards posted "at the sealed door" in rotating shifts. It lists no
soldiers and no priest-king's own guard by name, only service staff, suggesting it
predates whatever crisis put a Ward-Statue on that door at all. The scribe who kept it was
meticulous about rotations and hopeless at dates - not one entry is dated, leaving
Carrdun's diggers no way to place it against the flood at all. It does confirm the sealed
door already existed, staffed and mundane, before it became the Ward-Threshold - somebody,
later, made it what it is now.
