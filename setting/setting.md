## Overview

Flat water from one horizon to the other, and raised road across the middle of
it. The road is fitted slab, wide enough for two abreast, and it is the only
ground that is reliably ground. Either side of it the reeds have been cut back a
chain and kept cut, so that the cleared lane runs ahead as far as the light
holds, and past the cut edge the reeds stand as high as a shoulder and go on.

Every road carries frames. Two uprights and a crossbeam, at the bends and at the
crossings, and the pin is still seated in every one of them and the socket is
clean. Nothing hangs from any of them. On some of them the rope is new.

Peat smoke runs flat along the reed tops and does not lift. The water takes a
sound and gives none of it back, and a note struck out here carries further
inward than it carries seaward. Put a hand flat under any cut stone and it is
warm, and it is warmer the further down the road it is laid, and the top of the
same stone is cold.

Under the water the ground is square where it has no business being square.
Doorways stand whole with their lintels a hand below the surface, and beyond
them the floors run on into water nobody has found the bottom of. Nothing down
there has rotted. Everything the fen took, it took whole, and it is all still
lying in the order it went down in.

## Style

**Units.** Yards and miles on open ground, feet indoors, hands for water depth
and for how far the land has gone down. Time in watches, six to the day, and
the reedcutters count them out loud. A chain is the width of a cut lane and it
is the distance at which sound stops carrying.

**Register.** Plain nouns and stated measurements. A thing is described by what
it does and what it is made of, and never by what it means. The prose withholds
conclusions rather than detail: it states what is there in full and leaves what
that implies to the table. Nothing in the Reach announces itself, nothing warns,
and the only sound that carries is one the party makes.

**Names.** Two layers, and which layer a name belongs to tells a referee who
named the place and when. The living layer is plain compounds of ordinary words
and decomposes on sight. The Covenant's layer is built from the recorded root
vocabulary, it is older, and it is often wrong about what the place is now.
Stone before water is a Covenant name; water before stone is older than the
Covenant. Where a place carries a name in both layers the two rarely agree, and
that disagreement is worth writing.

**Numbers.** The Reach counts everything and agrees the count out loud. Roads
are counted from the sea inward, watches are counted at the post, holds were
emptied against a tally agreed at both sides of the door. A count that has
stopped, or two counts that disagree, is the most reliable sign in the setting
that something happened here.

**What the prose refuses.** It does not say what a place means, what it was
really for, or what a party ought to conclude. It does not name a feeling. The
Covenant's own records state their conclusions freely and two of them are
wrong, which is what makes them worth reading.
