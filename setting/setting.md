## Overview

A country of raised roads over standing water, walked at a stated rate and
counted in watches. The roads were built to be rung, not to be travelled: every
causeway carries a frame, every frame carried a bell, and the bells were sounded
seaward to inward in an order that nobody now holds the whole of. The bells are
gone. The frames are not, and they are bare, and the pins are still seated and
still clean.

The water sits flat and gives no sound back. Under it the ground is square where
it has no business being square, and the stone is cold on top and warm beneath.
Nothing here has rotted. Everything the water took, it took whole, and it is all
still down there in the order it went down in.

## Style

Plain nouns and stated distances. Yards on the roads, miles between them, time
in watches, six to the day. A thing is described by what it does and what it is
made of, and never by what it means.

Names are built from recorded roots and decompose cleanly. Stone before water is
a Covenant name; water before stone is older than the Covenant.

The register is quiet. Nothing in the Reach announces itself, nothing warns, and
the only sound that carries is one the party makes.
