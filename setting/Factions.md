Factions of Brackvaen

Three powers. The dice are d6 only, no bonus, and they mean nothing except relative to each
other - they resolve Faction Turns and nothing else. None of the three is Torsgaard's
government, because Torsgaard does not have one: it has an Assize, which is a settlement
about salt that has been asked to do the work of a government for sixty years and is
visibly not built for it.

---

The Salt Assize - AD: 4d6
- Resources: The written settlement of sixty years ago and the eleven boundary stones that carry it; the measuring of every worked pan in the eastern flats and therefore the recording of every salt-debt in the holding; about thirty serjeants; the drying floors, the weigh-house, and the only scales in Brackvaen anybody trusts. Coin is not what they have. What they have is the ledger, and in a place where debt is weighed and inherited, the ledger is most of what there is.
- Knowledge: Every debt in the holding and who it will fall to when the current holder dies - which is to say, a complete map of who in Torsgaard is desperate and how soon. They also hold the only surviving copy of the settlement's full text, and at least two of the senior heads know it grants something they have never explained to anybody.
- Tactics: They measure. Their characteristic move against anyone is to arrive with a boat-hook, a rule, and a writ, and to establish formally that something the target relies on is not theirs - a pan, a boat, a drying floor, a place on a crew. They prefer to make a problem insolvent rather than fight it, and they are patient enough for that to work.
- Reactions: Noticed - a serjeant asks the party's business and a lead seal appears on something they were using. Crossed - the party's salt is refused at the weigh-house, which cuts off the only way to turn a haul into coin. Injured - they invoke the boundary stones, publicly, and let Brackvaen's own settled dread of the old letters do the work; this has never once failed and none of them can tell you why it works.
- Identity: Grey undyed wool, worn as a sash by serjeants and as a band on the sleeve by clerks. A device of three notches in a row - cut into the boundary stones' bases, stamped into the lead seals nailed to measured pans, and burned into the weigh-house tallies. A party learns to read three notches on a post before anyone tells them what the Assize is.
- Goals: Get the eastern pans back to full working, because the Gell Year cost them the western flats and the ledger has not balanced since. Establish, formally and in writing, that the stair and everything under it fall inside the settlement - which requires deciding what the settlement actually granted, which is the one thing they will not do. Determine what the Brined are, in law, before the question is decided for them by there being enough of them.

The Faelhond - AD: 2d6
- Resources: Perhaps forty people who can work below the gorm line longer than anyone else alive, and who are consequently the only reliable way anything comes up out of Siwapelu. No ground, no coin, no standing. What they have instead is that every other party going down the stair needs one of them, and they know it.
- Knowledge: The upper courses of Siwapelu in genuine detail - which courses flood on which tide, where the grooves in the floor go, where the gorm sits at noon. Also this: they know that being Brined stops at the elbow for pan-hands and does not stop at all for people who go below, and they have not told Torsgaard, because the day Torsgaard works that out is the day the stair is sealed.
- Tactics: They get there first. Their characteristic move when contested is not violence but arrival - they are already in the course you were going to, they have already moved what was in it, and they are entirely willing to discuss terms now that you are here. Against an actual threat to the stair they obstruct: stakes pulled, a course flooded, a way marked wrong.
- Reactions: Noticed - one of them attaches themselves to the party as a guide, for a share, and is genuinely useful. Crossed - the party's own guide is gone at the point where they most need one. Injured - they mark the party's lodging with the grey smear, and after that no house in Torsgaard will hold their gear and no Faelhond will take them below, which in practice ends any attempt on the deep courses.
- Identity: Wrists and forearms bound in oiled leather in all weathers, which everyone in Torsgaard reads correctly and nobody comments on. A smear of coarse grey salt at hand height on the door frame of any house they have called at, left deliberately and never wiped off by the householder. Among themselves they use Old Rede words for the courses, which they pronounce wrong and inherited from the stones.
- Goals: Keep the stair open. Establish that the Brined can hold property, because a category of person who cannot inherit cannot own the thing they are the only people able to reach. Find out what is in the lowest course, which about half of them want for the obvious reason and the other half want because they have started to suspect it is the reason they are what they are.

The Vingash - AD: 3d6
- Resources: The whole of Lindmarn - several hundred fen people holding every channel that crosses it, and the knotted cord that is the only accurate record of where the water is deep this season. They can make the causeway passable or impassable without touching it, simply by what they do or do not tie. They also hold three deep-water fastnesses the Marchfolk have never reached and mostly do not believe in.
- Knowledge: Nessekire. They know the barrier did not fail, they know roughly who opened it and why, and they know that the water going into the low country was the point rather than the accident. Four hundred years on, the practical form this knowledge takes is a settled conviction that Siwapelu was drowned deliberately and must stay drowned, held by a people who cannot explain it to Torsgaard in terms Torsgaard would accept.
- Tactics: They move the water. Their characteristic contested move is to retie or remove cord across a whole reach of channel, so that a crossing that worked last month drowns a cart this month - deniable, unattributable, and devastating to anyone whose living depends on the causeway. Direct violence is a third or fourth resort and is done by watchers in twos and threes at night.
- Reactions: Noticed - cord appears on stakes ahead of the party, correctly tied, which is both a courtesy and a demonstration. Crossed - the party is followed openly by watchers who make no attempt at concealment. Injured - the reach they came in by is retied wrong behind them, and Lindmarn is a d6 region for reasons the party is now going to learn on the way home.
- Identity: Bitter-iron rings through the outer ear, one for each channel a watcher holds - an elder wears nine. Knotted reed cord on channel stakes, in a knotting that is genuinely readable and genuinely teachable, which makes it the most useful thing in the setting to be given. Ghaunt spoken among themselves and never in front of a Marchman, which is not secrecy so much as a settled judgement about who is worth speaking to.
- Goals: Keep Siwapelu drowned, and failing that, keep anyone from reaching the lowest course. Get the eastern water back up - the four-foot drop three years ago frightened them more than anything since the founding, and they do not know what caused it either. Have the founding arrangement with Torsgaard put in writing at last, on their terms, before the last elder who remembers what was actually agreed is dead.

---

**Who knows about whom.** The Vingash and the Assize have dealt with each other for a
hundred and twenty years and understand each other tolerably well, which is not the same as
trusting each other. The Assize knows the Faelhond as individuals - it has thirty or so of
them in the ledger - and does not know they are a body at all; it files them as a sickness
and a labour supply, and the day it stops doing that everything about the stair changes.
The Faelhond and the Vingash do not know one another exists in any organised sense: the
Faelhond take the fen people for hostile locals, and the Vingash have not connected the
grey smears on Torsgaard doorframes to anything.

They want the same thing for opposite reasons and neither has any idea. A party that works
this out is holding the most valuable single fact in Brackvaen.
