History of Sundfall

Six centuries ago, or thereabouts - the Old Rede diked this delta and raised Durgath, a
sea-wall built to drain it for grain, and cut grain-ports along its landward face.
Left: Durgath's dressed coping-stones, still standing in unbroken runs, cut with the
builders' own welder-mark - [pending 4d]

A hundred-some years after that - the Old Rede withdrew from the delta without a fight
anyone here remembers, and their grain-ports stood empty a generation before the first
Kelder-speaking fisher-holds squatted the drained ground; they never rebuilt the wall,
only patched what failed.
Left: a fisher-hold's founding boundary-stone, set flush against an older Old Rede pier
without disturbing it - [pending 4d]

Forty years ago - a spring tide breached Durgath's western span in a single night, and the
grainland behind it has not drained since; old panners still argue whether treasure-diggers
tunneling for an Old Rede vault undermined the span, or whether the tide itself has simply
been coming in higher every year since.
Left: the breach itself, a quarter-mile gap in the wall's western span, its broken
coping-stones scattered into the new tide-flat - [pending 4d]

Fifteen years ago - a failed catch-season left half the delta's panners bonded to a single
ledger-holder for the first time, and every thin season since has bonded more of them to
fewer hands.
Left: the ledger-holder's counting-house, standing apart from every fisher-hold it holds
paper on - [pending 4d]

Within the last year - panners working the new tide-flat past the breach have started
coming back with old grain-sacks stamped in a hand no living mill uses, and two who went out
during the low water after a red dusk have not come back at all.
