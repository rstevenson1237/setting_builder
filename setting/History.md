History of Brackvaen

Nine hundred years ago - the Odarath cut the first pans along what was then a shore and
sank Siwapelu beneath them, for a purpose no living account explains. Nothing survives of
them but the stone, the letters on it, and the fact that their pans are still the best pans.
Left: cut stonework and Old Rede inscriptions, and no grave anywhere - C Siwapelu entire,
B.4 Nuthora, the milestone at B.1 Doraiath

Four hundred years ago - Nessekire, the breaking-water. The barrier between the pans and the
open sea failed in a season and the country behind it went under; whether it failed or was
opened is not a question the Marchfolk think to ask.
Left: eighty square miles of standing water over a drowned grid of pan walls, and the breach
itself - B Lindmarn entire, C.20 Nessekirel

A hundred and twenty years ago - the Marchfolk came down out of the wold, found the rise
above the eastern pans dry, and built Torsgaard on it. They did not find the country empty,
and the arrangement they reached with the Vingash has never been written down.
Left: the holding, the causeway, and a border that exists only in what both sides remember -
A Torsgaard, B.1 Doraiath, B.3 Ghutrrun

Sixty years ago - the Salt Assize. After two generations of quarrels the holding's heads
settled who owned which pan in writing, and cut the settlement into eleven boundary stones
copied letter by letter off the Odarath work by men who could not read a word of it.
Accounts differ on what was granted: the Assize holds that it divided the pans, and there
are families who hold that it also sold something.
Left: eleven obeyed stones through the pans, and the only complete text on oak boards in a
lead case - A.2 Rendfast, C.7 Nuthorath, B.4 Nuthora

Eighteen years ago - the Gell Year. Over one summer the western pans came up yellow, what
crystallised was sour, and forty pans were abandoned and have not been reworked. Half of
Torsgaard says the sea shifted; the other half counts the year against the Assize and notes
which pans soured and which did not.
Left: forty flooded pans standing full, and eighteen coarse yellow bands at the top of a
nine-hundred-year deposit - B.6 Kkithik, C.21 Taluora

Three years ago, and still going - the eastern water dropped about four feet over one winter
for no reason anybody can name, and stayed down. Some who go down the stair it uncovered do
not come back, which was expected; some come back with the salt taken into their skin, which
was not.
Left: the stair standing open, and three winters of readings four feet below every year
before them - B.2 Immora, the tide board at A.5 Druvmarn
