History of Brackvaen

Nine hundred years ago, or near enough that nobody argues the figure - the Odarath cut the
first pans along what was then a shore, and sank Siwapelu beneath them: a gallery of worked
stone running under the salt, for a purpose no living account explains. Nothing survives of
them but the cut stone itself, the Old Rede letters on it, and the fact that their pans are
still the best pans. No story, no line, no grave anyone can point to.

Four hundred years ago - Nessekire, the breaking-water. The barrier between the pans and
the open sea failed in a season and the country behind it went under. The shore became fen.
What it left is the shape of Brackvaen itself: eighty square miles of standing brackish
water over a grid of drowned stonework, and a coastline that is now nowhere near the coast.
Whether it failed or was opened is not a question the Marchfolk think to ask.

A hundred and twenty years ago - the Marchfolk came down out of the wold, found the rise
above the eastern pans still dry, and built Torsgaard on it. They did not find the country
empty; they found the Vingash already in it, and the arrangement they reached with them has
never been written down, which is the whole of the problem with it. What it left is the
holding, the causeway, and a border that exists only in what both sides remember.

Sixty years ago - the Salt Assize. After two generations of quarrels over which pans
belonged to whom, the holding's heads settled it in writing and cut the settlement into
eleven boundary stones set through the pans, in Old Rede, copied letter by letter off the
Odarath work by men who could not read a word of it. The stones have been obeyed ever
since, including by people the Assize has no power over, and nobody in Torsgaard has a
comfortable account of why. Accounts differ sharply on what the Assize actually granted:
the Assize holds that it divided the pans, and there are families who hold that it also
sold something.

Eighteen years ago - the Gell Year. Over one summer the western pans came up yellow and the
salt would not take; what crystallised was sour, and the meat packed in it went off inside
a month. Forty pans were abandoned and have not been reworked. Half of Torsgaard says the
sea shifted. The other half counts the year against the Assize and notes which pans soured
and which did not. What it left is the western pans, standing empty and full, and a
grievance with a date on it.

Three years ago, and still going - the eastern water dropped about four feet over one
winter, for no reason anybody can name, and stayed down. It uncovered a cut stair at the
edge of the eastern pans going down into Siwapelu. People have been going down it since the
first thaw. Some do not come back, which was expected. Some come back with the salt taken
into their skin to the elbow, which was not, and there are enough of them now that
Torsgaard has had to decide what they are - and has not.
