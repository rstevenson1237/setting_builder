History of Duen

Uncounted years ago - Before any charter, whoever cut the first crossing of the gorge also
walled it: a line of dressed stone markers running along the rim, each face carved in a
tongue nobody here still reads. Nothing else of them survived.
Left: the boundary stones, Accalur-inscribed, still standing along the gorge rim - [pending 4d]

Six generations ago - A throne now unnamed chartered a keep to hold this crossing, and Duen
was raised on the gorge lip from stone cut out of its own foundation. The quarry gallery
opened for that first building is still called the Old Cut.
Left: Duen's keep itself, and the Old Cut gallery beneath it - [pending 4d]

Nineteen years ago - The garrison broke through into an older working two levels below the
Old Cut and pulled the crews out within the week. Veterans of that crew disagree on why: a
cave-in, a sickness that took three of them, or something that followed the last man out
and was never seen again above ground.
Left: the sealed lower gallery door, and the three names cut into the guardroom lintel -
[pending 4d]

Five years ago - Letters from the throne stopped arriving, and no one agrees whether the
kingdom fell, forgot Duen, or simply stopped answering its post. The garrison still levies
tolls, swears musters, and judges disputes in the charter's name, exactly as before.
Left: the charter itself, read aloud at every muster - [pending 4d]

Last spring - The low ford, the easier crossing three miles downriver, dropped into the
gorge in a single night with no storm to explain it. Every cart, herd, and column bound
across the Durad now funnels past the reopened Dutor quarry road instead.
