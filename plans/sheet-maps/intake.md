## How each clause is read

| Clause | Read as |
|---|---|
| "a module overview on the brief" | A new node in `BRIEF.md`'s `setting` block: the sheets the setting is drawn on, each with its scale and extent, and which regions it holds; each region's ground (forest, marsh, hills cut by a stream) and its place against its neighbours, by direction and distance. Region adjacency moves here from `setting/region/Connections.mmd`. |
| "a big picture terrain map against that" | One terrain map per sheet, generated by code. The overview sheet is the play surface for every WILD region on it. |
| "anything needed by the brief gets written and the rest is algorithmic" | The brief states only what the story needs. Heights, streams, slopes and the ground between places come from the generator, and are facts the writing reads and must not contradict. |
| "regions are placed and if the map doesn't work then a new one is generated until all regions fit" | Agreed recommendation: placement first, from the overview's directions and distances; terrain is then generated with each placed region as a requirement (forest here, a cut through hills there). A check names any requirement unmet. Reseeding is the fallback, not the method. |
| "wild regions can use a zoomed in cut out in place of the mermaid diagram" | A WILD region's map is a crop of its sheet, at the sheet's scale unless the overview gives it a sheet of its own. |
| "the setting is drawn as a single wild region with keyed locations while we have multiple regions" | The reference draws one map and keys places on it; we keep regions as units of writing. A region's map is a crop of the shared sheet, so regions never disagree at their edges, and the sheet is printed whole as the play map. Whether adjoining WILD regions should instead merge is a `spec.md` Decision. |
| "dangerous regions create a dungeon map ... a single room cut per location" | A DANGEROUS region is drawn per block (below). A location's cut is a crop of its block's map around its room. |
| "for the ravine ... contours on a 10 yard grid and scale to fit" | The ravine is its own terrain sheet at 10 yards a square, rendered to fit the page rather than at a fixed size per square. |
| "separating and just keying the entrances on the ravine map" | The ravine sheet marks and keys each lair's entrance only. Interiors are separate dungeon maps. A passage between two holdings is an exit on each map, labelled with the code it reaches. |
| "blocks as a table that points to a list of locations" | A region table, one entry per block, naming its locations. Not a field on Location. It replaces the subgraphs in each DANGEROUS `Connections.mmd`, which are the only place blocks are recorded today. |
| "the block being our indivisible dungeon collection, no verticality ... draw per block" | A block is one level, drawn as one dungeon map. A change of level is a connection between blocks. A single holding is usually one block; a collection is several. |
| "the majority of the caves really should be a single block" | A single holding is one block unless it changes level. Most of F to L become one block, replacing their current two- and three-part groupings. |
| "retiring mermaid ... inserting any needed information into the location tables" | Every `.mmd` file and template goes. What they carry that no table does - blocks and region adjacency - moves to the Blocks table and the overview. Connections between locations are already in `Connections.md`. |
| "we own the artgen project and can build to target both quality and token expense" | Missing pieces are built in artgen, not worked around here: constrained generation, crops, walls and towers, terrain under a dungeon plan, and a text summary of each map for writers. A writing step reads that summary, never a rendered image. |
| "plays similar at the table" | The target is the reference's play, not its look: a party chooses routes on one grid in squares; the ravine shows which mouths are higher and nearer; a lair's interior is a numbered plan with doors, traps and secret ways marked; the keep is numbered buildings inside walls on a 10 ft grid. |

## Agreed before drafting

- Sheets own terrain and height. Tables own locations, their footprint and their block.
- Location gains footprint (`size`, and `shape` where not rectangular). Today a footprint
  exists only in `Brief.md` prose, so nothing can draw a room to size.
- A sheet's extent bounds generation, and the validator flags what falls outside it.
- Height in feet comes from the terrain and reaches the tables, so a Layout can say how high
  up a slope a mouth is.
- SAFE regions start from the dungeon tool with no rock hatching over terrain, and get walls
  and towers added to artgen. A market town is out of scope.
- artgen's map and dungeon engines are taken in as a package of their own, without the
  pixel-art pipeline, its `CLAUDE.md` or `.claude/`.

## Evidence from the evaluation

artgen 0.10 was installed in a scratch repo and driven from the setting's own tables through
`tools/site_common.py`'s `positions()` and `route_stops()`.

- The Kobold Warren (F) drew cleanly once room sizes were typed in from its `Brief.md`.
  Connection types mapped to doors, `Lock` to locked, `Secret` to secret, `Hazard` to a trap
  mark.
- The Bugbear Den (K) drew as a strip: its K.1 "The Dry Bed" repeats E.7 "The Dry Fork" (same
  tags) and puts its caves 180 yards from their entrance. K.4's "120 feet up" to M.14 was
  flattened. Both are generation issues.
- The Ravine (E) and the Dark Wood (C) placed every location, but the ground had to be
  painted by hand: no table states any. The ravine, a mile by 80 yards, came out 11 by 78
  squares.
- Regions carry no position against one another, so no overview could be drawn.
