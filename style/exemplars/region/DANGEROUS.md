X Slagfoot

Overview: Iron was smelted here for a century, and whoever came after cut the iron out of it. What was left is behind cold floors built to be hot, and behind the slag the strippers tipped into the ways. The Ash Road men hold the upper works now and take a third of anything carried out.

Ambiance: Burnt bone and wet ash throughout, sharpest under an open hood-vent where the air still draws. Sound goes up the flues instead of along the ways, so a voice on one level arrives on another. Cold and dry above the water line, cold and dripping below it.

Architecture: Coursed sandstone with firebrick at every hearth and flue, well cut and kept better than the ironwork it carried. Every opening is corbelled, and the same three-lobed mason's mark is cut into each springing block. Ways run six feet wide and nine to the ceiling, except in the casting halls, which take the hood height.

Layout: A collection: eighteen worked locations on three levels cut back into the hillside, with two ways in. The charging yard on the crown and the tail-race at the foot are half a mile apart by the road round the hill. The Kiln Hall (X.1) stands at the head of the middle level, and the hoist shaft runs from the cage head (X.9) down to the sump. Time here runs on the Danger countdown and not in hours.

Features: Standing water on the lowest level, knee to waist, and it moves. Air draws up the flues wherever a hood-vent is open above, so a fire lit anywhere sends its smoke to the crown. Casting-hall floors are sand over plank and have rotted from beneath. Firewood stores burn readily and go up entire; stone, slag and wet plank take no flame.

Dangers: The works sleep. What wakes them is the hoist chain: a drop or a lift rings the length of the shaft, and brings the Ash Road men to the head of it inside a turn. The rest of the place takes no notice of a party at all.

Creatures: Ash Road toll crews work the upper level in fours, boil bone for glue at the Kiln Hall fire, and draw water from the header tank on the crown. They sleep in the charging yard and foul the tail-race below it. Rock doves nest in the open flues and can be netted at the vents. Something in the sump takes the doves that fall down the shaft, and the feathers come back up the tail-race.

Factions: The Ash Road holds the crown and the whole upper level, in ash-grey cloth, and keeps its glue pots at the Kiln Hall (X.1). What it wants is the road under the hill kept its own, and the works are where it sits to watch the road. No other faction holds ground here.

Secrets: The strippers walled their own caches into the works as they went, in stretcher bond against three sides of cut rock (X.12). The lower level has a way out to the tail-race, and no Ash Road boot-print goes past the water line. Three hood slots are shuttered and pinned to a three-lobed profile. Nothing in these eighteen locations turns that profile; do not place one here.

Treasure: Scavenged tools, sound rope, and cut iron stacked at the loading door and still stacked. The works lean on Table I on the lower levels and Table II where the strippers worked. One charge basket in the shaft still holds what it was carrying.
